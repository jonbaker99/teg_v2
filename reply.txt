# Order Restored on the Silver Coast: Baker Leaves the Mid-Pack Behind

For three consecutive tournaments, Jon Baker occupied fourth place in the Trophy standings. It was an exquisitely specific kind of purgatory, comfortable enough to avoid the indignity of the Spoon yet sufficiently distant from silverware to feel like an administrative oversight. On the windswept dunes of the Lisbon coast, that bureaucratic ceiling was thoroughly demolished.

Baker produced a four-day clinic of relentless scoring, carding 166 Stableford points to claim the Trophy by an 18-point margin and adding the Green Jacket at +67 gross. Behind him, the field largely disintegrated into the Portuguese scrub. David Mullin mounted a brief mid-tournament charge before settling for two distant silver medals, John Patterson endured a statistical rollercoaster of historic proportions, and Gregg Williams surrendered his grip on the Jacket to collect the Wooden Spoon.

## Round 1 — Patterson Sets the Pace at Praia D'El Rey as Alex Baker Finds Every Dune

The tournament began on Saturday at Praia D'El Rey, where the Atlantic breeze swiftly started separating intention from execution. John Patterson negotiated the initial skirmish best, piecing together 39 Stableford points to establish a one-point advantage at the summit of the leaderboard. Lurking directly behind him was Jon Baker, whose first-day 38 points provided a quiet foundation for what was to come.

In the gross competition, Gregg Williams launched his title defence with an assured 93 (+21) to take a three-shot lead. The lower reaches of the field, however, were already staging an exhibition in damage limitation. Alex Baker spent his afternoon conducting an exhaustive archaeological survey of the coastal dunes, eventually signing for a 114 gross. It was a new course worst across 24 visits to the layout, anchored by a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 5th, one of the hardest holes on the property. He sat at the foot of the table on 27 points.

## Round 2 — Sunday Shockwaves: Baker Takes the Lead as Patterson Runs Up 116 at West Cliffs

The field moved to Cynthia Dye's penal layout at West Cliffs on Sunday, a course designed to punish optimism with immediate geometric cruelty. Jon Baker wasted no time, taking the outright Trophy lead on the 1st hole and dismantling the track with a majestic 44 points. His gross 86 represented a 15-stroke improvement on his previous visit to the venue.

David Mullin staged an extraordinary assault of his own. Assembling a 12-hole sequence from the 1st to the 12th without dropping a single net shot, Mullin posted an 82 gross (+10). It slashed six shots off the previous course record and handed him a three-shot cushion in the Green Jacket race.

At the other extreme, Patterson suffered a complete operational collapse. Battling the coastal brush with grim determination, he ran up a 10 at the par-5 13th before carding an 11 at the par-5 15th, an assignment considered one of the easiest on the card. His round of 116 gross (+44) set an unwanted West Cliffs record, returning just 22 points and dumping him squarely into the Spoon position.

## Round 3 — Seve's Layout Conquered: Jon Baker's Course-Record 86 Breaks the Field

Monday brought the championship test of Seve Ballesteros's Royal Óbidos. The gross competition briefly flared into life when Jon Baker opened with a double bogey at the 1st, allowing Mullin to seize the outright gross lead by the 6th. The rebellion was brutally short-lived.

Baker drew level at the 7th and unleashed a devastating 12-hole stretch to the clubhouse without surrendering a net stroke. Backed by birdies at the par-5 11th and the par-3 13th, the friendliest hole on the course, Baker carded a course-record 86 gross (+14). The accompanying 44 Stableford points blew his Trophy advantage out to 11 points and established an eight-shot cushion in the Jacket.

Elsewhere, the scorecard haemorrhaged numbers. Alex Baker recorded a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 14th. On the very same hole, Williams saw his tournament tilt permanently sideways. Returning just 26 points, Williams dropped into solitary possession of last place in the Spoon standings.

## Round 4 — Procession at Praia D'El Rey: Baker Completes the Double by 18 Points

The final round on Tuesday returned to Praia D'El Rey as a coronation rather than a contest. Baker played with unblinking composure, carding 40 points and a gross 92 to complete an emphatic sweep of both major prizes. His 18-point winning margin over Mullin in the Trophy stood as an unanswerable statement.

The minor placings offered late turbulence. Patterson's volatile week concluded with a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 15th, one of the sternest challenges on the course. Yet his total of 135 points was comfortably enough to avoid the basement.

Williams could muster only 29 points in response, confirming his descent to the foot of the table on 123 points. The man who had entered the tournament wearing the Green Jacket left it holding the Wooden Spoon.

## How it was decided

* **Trophy:** Decided on Sunday at West Cliffs, where Jon Baker took the outright lead on the 1st hole of Round 2 and never looked back. Back-to-back rounds of 44 points in Round 2 and Round 3 extended the margin to unassailable proportions. Baker won on 166 points by 18 points over David Mullin (148 points).
* **Green Jacket:** Decided on Monday at Royal Óbidos, where David Mullin took the lead on Round 3 hole 6 before Jon Baker drew level on hole 7 and pulled clear with a course-record 86 gross (+14). Baker won at +67 gross by 13 shots over Mullin (+80 gross).
* **Wooden Spoon:** Decided on Monday at Royal Óbidos, where Gregg Williams slumped to 26 points and dropped into last place outright on Round 3 hole 14. Williams finished bottom on 123 points, 12 points behind John Patterson (135 points).

## Player-by-player summary

* **Jon Baker**: Ended his streak of three straight fourth-place Trophy finishes with a devastating performance, firing back-to-back 44-point rounds and a course-record 86 at Royal Óbidos to secure the Trophy and Green Jacket double.
* **David Mullin**: Set a magnificent West Cliffs course record of 82 gross in Round 2 and briefly led the Gross race on Monday before settling for a pair of distant runner-up finishes.
* **Alex Baker**: Recovered from an opening course-worst 114 gross at Praia D'El Rey and survived multiple double-figure hole scores to finish comfortably clear of the Wooden Spoon in third on 138 points.
* **John Patterson**: Led the field after Round 1 before weathering a tumultuous campaign featuring a course-worst 116 at West Cliffs and three separate double-figure blow-ups to take fourth on 135 points.
* **Gregg Williams**: Led the Green Jacket after the opening round but endured a ruinous second-half collapse of 26 and 29 points to finish bottom on 123 points and claim the Wooden Spoon.
