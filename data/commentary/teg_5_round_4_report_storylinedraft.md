# Williams Crowned As Baker's Late Charge Falls Short

## Gregg Williams holds off a fast-closing Jon Baker to win the TEG 5 Trophy by two shots
<!-- storyline: race -->

Gregg Williams took the final round of the TEG 5 Trophy at Palmares to hold on for the title, surviving a stretch that could easily have cost him the lead. Between the 10th and 13th he dropped ten shots, running through a triple bogey, two doubles and another triple, and then added a quintuple bogey 9 at the 16th. Even so, the round left him level with his overnight cushion on net score, and the gap to the field closed only from five shots to two by the finish.

The closing pressure came from Jon Baker, who shot a personal-best gross round of 90, four better than his previous course mark. Baker piled up five shots to par across the first four holes and later added a quintuple bogey 8 at the short 5th, but pulled himself back into contention with two straight birdies at the 14th and 15th. It was not quite enough. Williams's overnight advantage proved just durable enough, and he took the Trophy by two shots as David Mullin's overnight edge kept him clear at the top of the Green Jacket race.

## David Mullin wins the Green Jacket while finishing bottom of the Wooden Spoon table in the same round
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin's fourth round in the Algarve produced a strange split scorecard. He held onto top spot in the Green Jacket race, extending his overall command even as his margin there narrowed from 14 strokes to 12. But everywhere else, the day went wrong. In the Trophy, he fell from first place after round one all the way to fifth. And in the Wooden Spoon, a quadruple-bogey nine at the par-5 6th sent him from fourth to bottom of the table, swapping places with Henry Meller, with the gap back up to the field cut from eight strokes to just two.

The round had its moments of recovery. After a triple bogey at the 9th, a bogey at the 10th and a double bogey at the 11th, Mullin birdied the 12th and followed it with another at the par-5 13th, going two holes without dropping a gross shot before the damage elsewhere caught up with him.

## Henry Meller's personal-best round, net and gross, on TEG's first visit to Palmares
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller produced a personal-best round at Palmares, TEG's first visit to the course, finishing at -3 on the net and +17 gross — his best figures on both counts. It was far from a clean card. Meller had gone into the day propping up the Wooden Spoon race, and after a stretch of holes 9 through 11 without a single net par — a triple bogey on the 9th followed by two double bogeys, bleeding seven gross shots in all — that struggling position looked secure. A double bogey on the 15th even dropped him to the bottom of the Spoon standings outright, from third to fifth.

But Meller rallied down the stretch, playing the 16th and 17th without dropping a shot, and the round held up as his best of the week by some distance. It also lifted David Mullin into the Wooden Spoon's last place instead, with the gap to safety cut from eight strokes to two — turning what had looked like a lost day for Meller into a quietly significant one.
