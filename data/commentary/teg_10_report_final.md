# Second, Second, First

Alex Baker landed in Lagos with a ledger that read like a caution notice. Second in TEG 8. Runner-up in TEG 9, behind the man who would spend this tournament at the bottom of the field. Last in the Green Jacket in each of the last three TEGs, and a Wooden Spoon already gathering dust at home. He had never set foot on Boavista.

Four days later he had played the finest round in the history of TEG.

That is the destination. The journey opens somewhere else entirely, because on the first afternoon in the Algarve — TEG's fourth visit to this coast — David Mullin was taking a golf course apart with the calm of a man dismantling a shed. Behind him, John Patterson, the defending Trophy champion, had already found the bottom of the field and was settling in for a long stay.

## Round 1 — Mullin Rewrites Boavista at the Eleventh Attempt

Saturday, and Boavista for the eleventh time in TEG's history: Atlantic views on one half, something rather more agricultural on the other, and a course the regulars believe they know hole by hole.

Mullin went round it in 75 shots, four over par. The previous best anyone had managed there, across fifty prior player-visits, was 82. It was his own eleventh visit to the place, and he beat that mark by seven. It was also the finest gross round ever recorded in TEG, and worth 49 Stableford points, which stood as the second-best round in TEG history.

By teatime he led the Jacket by fourteen and the Trophy by eight.

The Trophy lead had, admittedly, changed hands five times before he took it — Alex Baker level at the 6th, Jon Baker outright at the 9th, Mullin level at the 10th, Alex Baker level at the 11th. This is what a bunched field does on a first morning and it means nothing. Mullin took the lead at the 12th and held it for the next thirty holes.

Meanwhile the closing hole began its work. Stuart Neumann made 10 at the par-4 18th, a sextuple bogey, arrived at with what witnesses describe as commitment. Gregg Williams made 9 at the same hole. Between them, in the space of an hour, they handed back most of a decent round.

Patterson's day found its shape at the par-5 8th, SI 2 and one of the hardest holes on the property, where he signed for 10. He had dropped to the bottom of the field four holes earlier. He would not see daylight again for the remaining sixty-eight holes of the tournament.

Alex Baker, seeing all of this for the first time, made 7 at the par-3 2nd, which is one of the easiest holes on the course, and 9 at the 8th. He signed for 41 points and second place, eight adrift. Nobody wrote it down as significant.

## Round 2 — New Ground, New Personal Bests — and a 10 at the First

Sunday at Palmares, where Robert Trent Jones Jr has twenty-seven holes and TEG chose two nines nobody in the field had ever played: the seaside Praia spliced onto the parkland Alvor. Unfamiliar ground normally punishes this group. Instead it handed out prizes.

Williams posted 48 points, a personal best, at +14 gross, also a personal best. Alex Baker posted 45 points and +23, both personal bests. Jon Baker went round in +10, the third-best gross round in TEG history.

Patterson opened his account at the par-4 1st with a 10.

There is a particular economy to that. Most men need several holes to establish the tone of a round; Patterson had his in one, before anyone had properly finished their coffee.

Neumann's contribution came later and in a cluster. He dropped thirteen shots across three holes: 10 at the par-5 13th, 9 at the 14th, 7 at the 15th. He then arrived at the 18th and made another sextuple-bogey 10 — a different hole on a different course from the one that ruined his opening round, but the same number, and by now something close to a habit.

Mullin, going quietly round in +11, watched his Trophy lead shrink from eight to five. His Jacket cushion sat at thirteen, which is another way of saying the Jacket was already over.

## Round 3 — Thirty Holes in Front, Undone by a Par

Monday at Palmares again, this time Alvor spliced onto Lagos: pure parkland, and once more new ground.

The Trophy lead was passed round all morning. Alex Baker drew level at the 4th; Williams drew level at the 4th. Then at the par-4 6th, Alex Baker made a par, took four points, and led outright.

That was it. That was the moment. Mullin's reign, which had begun at the 12th hole of the opening round and survived thirty holes of everything the Algarve could offer, ended not with a blow-up but with another man making a four. The record 75 had bought him a day and a half.

Mullin drew level again at the 7th, briefly. At the par-5 13th, Williams made bogey for three points and took the lead outright. At that same hole, moments apart, Alex Baker made 10.

A quintuple bogey ought to end a man's afternoon. Baker drew level again at the 15th and signed for 47 points at +22 gross — both personal bests, both set on a card carrying a 10. He finished the round eleven clear.

Patterson, elsewhere, made 10 at the par-5 8th, which was his second such visit to an 8th in three days. He then produced the tournament's most sustained piece of work: nineteen shots dropped across holes 11 to 15. A 7 at the 11th, the second-hardest hole, was defensible. An 8 at the par-3 12th, the easiest hole on the course, was less so. Then 8 at the 13th, 7 at the short 14th, 9 at the 15th. Twenty-seven points for the round.

Mullin made 8 at the 18th, went round in +23, and led the Jacket by ten. The closing hole was still taking its cut.

## Round 4 — Fifty-One: The Best Round Ever Played in TEG

Tuesday, back at Boavista, twelfth TEG round at the venue and the place where the tournament had begun.

Williams birdied the par-4 5th and drew level with Baker at the top. He was never that close again.

What Baker did next was not spectacular so much as relentless. From the 5th to the 10th he took twenty-one points from six holes: bogey at the 5th, par at the 6th, par at the 7th, bogey at the 8th, par at the 9th, par at the 10th. Three points, four, four, three, three, four. The 8th is SI 2 and the 10th SI 3; the 9th is the easiest hole on the course and he treated all three identically, which is to say without visible emotion.

He signed for 51 points at +17 gross. The 51 is the best round ever played in TEG. The +17 was another personal best, his fourth of the tournament. His four rounds read 41, 45, 47, 51: better every single day, which is not a thing golf normally permits.

The total came to 184 points and the Trophy by eleven, with a personal-best gross aggregate of +90 alongside it. Runner-up twice, and now first.

Williams, whose three Trophies gave him every right to expect otherwise, made 7 at the par-3 6th and 8 at the 18th. He finished second by eleven with a personal-best gross total of +76 and a new Boavista best of 93, ten shots better than his last visit here. The closing hole, one last time, had taken its fee.

Mullin came home in +13 for +51 and the Green Jacket by twelve — an eighth Jacket, and a third in succession. Jon Baker was runner-up with a personal-best gross total of +63 and a Boavista best of 86, nine better than his previous visit. That makes four Jacket runner-up finishes in five TEGs. He is becoming very good at the view from there.

Patterson made 11 at the par-5 8th, his worst score at a par 5 to date, then 9 at the 18th for good measure. Twenty-one points, his lowest round ever, and 112 for the tournament.

Neumann, on his eleventh visit to Boavista, matched his previous score there exactly and remains sixteen shots adrift of the 82 he made in TEG 5. He escaped the Spoon he has held twice by the simple method of scoring more points than John Patterson.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy.** Mullin led by eight after Round 1 and five after Round 2 with Baker second on both nights. Baker's par at the 6th in Round 3 ended Mullin's thirty-hole reign and took the lead outright. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th that afternoon and drew level again at the 5th in Round 4, but never led outright thereafter. Baker's 21 points from holes 5 to 10 and his record 51-point round settled it: 184 points, winning by eleven.
- **The Green Jacket.** Decided on day one. Mullin's 75 at Boavista was a new course record, seven better than the 82 that had stood across fifty player-visits, and the best gross round in TEG history. It opened a fourteen-shot cushion. He led after every round and the only two lead changes came inside the opening eighteen holes, neither of them outright. Final: +51, by twelve from Jon Baker.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Patterson dropped to the bottom at the 4th hole of Round 1 and stayed there for the remaining sixty-eight. Rounds of 29, 35, 27 and 21. The nineteen shots bled over holes 11 to 15 in Round 3 and the 21-point close reduced it to arithmetic: 112 points, and the defending Trophy champion holding the Spoon within a single TEG.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Alex Baker** — Arrived with two runner-up finishes, three straight last places in the Jacket and a Spoon on the record, and had never seen Boavista. Improved in all four rounds, took the lead with an unremarkable par at the 6th in Round 3, survived a 10 at the 13th that same afternoon, and closed with the best round TEG has recorded. First Trophy, by eleven.
- **David Mullin** — Broke a course record that had stood across fifty visits and the TEG gross-round record inside one afternoon, then led the Trophy for thirty holes before another man's four took it off him. Consolation: an eighth Green Jacket, a third in a row, by twelve.
- **Gregg Williams** — Personal bests at Palmares in Round 2 with 48 points at +14, a new Boavista best of 93, and the outright lead twice. A 7 at the 6th and an 8 at the last on the final day left him second by eleven, with a personal-best gross total of +76 to file away.
- **Jon Baker** — Went round Palmares in +10, the third-best gross round in TEG history, added a Boavista best of 86 and a personal-best gross total of +63. Jacket runner-up again, which is now four times in five TEGs without the win.
- **John Patterson** — Defending Trophy champion, and bottom of the field from the 4th hole of the first round to the final green. Tens at the 8th in Rounds 1 and 3, a 10 at the opening hole in Round 2, an 11 at the 8th in the last round, and a closing 21 points. The Spoon, on 112.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Twice a Spoon-holder, and spared this time only by out-pointing Patterson. Donated sextuple-bogey 10s at the closing holes of two different courses on consecutive days, plus a third 10 at the Palmares 13th, and matched his previous Boavista score to the shot.
