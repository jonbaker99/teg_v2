# The Stadium Gives In

## David Mullin, bottom of the Trophy and holding the Wooden Spoon at breakfast, shoots 84 gross for a new PGA Catalunya Stadium course record and 44 Stableford points
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin began Monday at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course at the bottom of the 2025 TEG Trophy and holding the 2025 Wooden Spoon, and finished it holding the course record. His 84 gross is the lowest round recorded here in 28 visits, beating the previous mark of 89, and it is comfortably his own best at the course: his prior best over five visits was 91, and he was eight shots better than on his last trip round it.

The shape of it was steadiness rather than fireworks. He opened with three straight pars, including one on the third-hardest hole, for nine points, and added two more pars at the fifth and sixth. The best stretch came at the turn: 10 points across the eighth, ninth and tenth, with a bogey on the hardest hole on the course still worth three points and a birdie at the tenth.

The 44 points were the best Trophy score of the day in a field of five, and the Spoon passed to Jon Baker.

## Alex Baker shoots 100 gross — seven worse than his last visit here — and still takes 44 points and extends his Trophy lead
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker signed for 100 gross at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course on his sixth visit, seven strokes worse than he managed here last time, and still walked off with 44 points — the best Trophy return in the field of five and enough to push his lead in the 2025 TEG Trophy from 11 to 12.

The gross number was built almost entirely in one stretch. From the 6th through the 10th he dropped 12 shots: a triple bogey at the 6th, then doubles at the 7th, 8th and the 9th, the hardest hole on the course, then another triple at the 10th. Five holes yielded eight points.

What followed was the opposite. Holes 11 to 16 brought 21 points — pars at the 11th, 13th and 14th, bogeys at the 12th, 15th and 16th, with the 13th and 14th played without a dropped gross shot. The handicap absorbed the wreckage; the scorecard did not.

## Europe's elite championship test hands out three personal bests and four scores of 43 points or more in a single afternoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

On its sixth visit to the Stadium Course at Camiral, a Championship parkland layout that had never previously yielded much, the field went low. The best Stableford return of the day was 44 points, against a worst of 28 in a five-man field.

Two of the scorecards were the best either man has produced here. David Mullin signed for 84 gross, beating his previous best at the course by seven and his last visit by eight. Gregg Williams went round in 85, seven better than his last time out and a course best as well.

Both got going immediately. Mullin parred the first three holes, including the 1st, one of the hardest on the course, for nine points. Williams was even more productive: four consecutive pars from the 2nd through the 5th, worth 12 points, none of them costing him a gross shot.

Williams's round also took him to the front of the 2025 Green Jacket race, eight clear of the field.

## Jon Baker hands the TEG 18 Green Jacket lead to Gregg Williams at the 2nd and drops to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race on the same hole
<!-- storyline: race -->

Two things happened to Jon Baker at the 2nd on Monday, and both of them cost him. His triple-bogey 7 returned no points and dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon race, displacing David Mullin, who had been two clear of him; by the end of the round the gap back to the next man was 14. On the same hole, Gregg Williams made a routine par and took outright possession of the TEG 18 Green Jacket lead from Jon Baker, turning a two-shot deficit into an eight-shot advantage. Williams's 85 was his best gross round at the Stadium Course, seven strokes better than his previous visit.

Jon Baker's round did not recover. A 9 at the par-4 4th followed, and he closed with four straight double bogeys from the 15th, bleeding eight shots and managing a single point on each. His 28 points was 11 fewer than his 39 in round two, and the lowest Trophy score in the field.
