# Forty-Two at Monte Rei

## Jon Baker's 42 points at Monte Rei, built on three opening pars, surviving a seven at the par-3 seventh, and closed out with four straight pars for 14 points from the 15th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker went round Monte Rei in 42 points on Sunday, the best anyone managed, and he did it with a scorecard so plain it hardly looked like it was trying. The course is a Jack Nicklaus design and ranks third hardest of the 28 the group has rated, so turning up and simply making pars there counts as a statement.

He opened with three of them for 10 points, including a four at the 3rd, one of the hardest holes out there. Then came the par-3 7th and a quadruple bogey 7, which scored precisely nothing and handed the 2026 TEG Trophy lead to Gregg Williams. The two of them passed it back and forth after that, Jon Baker in front again at the 12th, Williams ahead once more at the 13th.

The finish was where he put an end to the argument. Four straight pars from the 15th, a mid-length par 4 with a lake down its flank from tee to green and stroke index 1 against its name, for 14 points in four holes. The par at the 16th took the Trophy lead outright. He signed for 88 gross and a cushion of five, and the field will be talking about that closing stretch for some time.

## Alex Baker's 114 at Monte Rei: nines at the first and second, a ten at the par-3 seventh, a birdie at the eighth, an eight at the seventeenth, and a fall from second to third in the Trophy
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker signed for 114, and the trouble arrived before he had properly settled. The opening par 4 drops from an elevated tee to a fairway doglegging right between two large bunkers, and it cost him a 9. The 2nd cost him another, a quadruple bogey. A 7 at the 3rd and a 6 at the 4th followed, leaving him 14 over par after four holes with not one net par to show for any of it. That is bad golf, and there is no gentler way to put it.

The 7th is the hardest of Monte Rei's par 3s, over 230 yards, with deep hollows either side of a narrow entrance. He took 10 there, the worst hole of his TEG career relative to par. Then he birdied the 8th, straight away, which must have been either very cheering or completely maddening depending on where you were standing.

Nine more shots went over the 9th, 10th and 11th, three holes that nobody involved will be framing. A neat run of three from the 12th brought in nine Stableford points, so the golf was in there somewhere. An 8 at the 17th, another of the hardest holes on the course, finished things off. He began the round second in the 2026 TEG Trophy and ended it third, on 67 points.

## Monte Rei's five par 5s and the 18 shots David Mullin and John Patterson gave away on four of them
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Monte Rei offers five par 5s, which on a layout of over 7,200 yards from the back tees ought to be the only place where a shot or two comes back to you. David Mullin and John Patterson between them gave away 18 strokes on three of them.

The 6th took two: Patterson a 10, Mullin a 9, and neither of them worth a single Stableford point. Mullin added a quadruple bogey 9 at the 16th. Patterson rounded it all off with a 10 at the 18th, a reachable par 5 that bends round a lake which is in play off the tee, on the second shot and on the third, so there was no shortage of opportunity.

Mullin got round in 95, Patterson in 111.

## The day from the vantage of those who lost ground: Gregg Williams twice held the Trophy lead and finished five behind, Alex Baker slid from one off the lead to fourteen, and John Patterson's 25 points opened a nine-point gap at the bottom
<!-- storyline: race -->

Gregg Williams opened with two bogeys and then birdied the 3rd, which is about the fastest anyone turned a morning around all day. A par at the 7th carried him to the outright lead in the 2026 TEG Trophy, and he still had it at the 11th. He lost it at the 12th, took it straight back at the 13th, and gave it up for good at the 16th. He finished five points adrift on 76, consoled by a four-shot lead in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket.

Alex Baker was a single point off the lead after R1. He is now fourteen behind.

John Patterson's 25 points were the lowest of the five. A triple bogey at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon, and the 9th left him down there on his own. Holes 11 to 15 then took 12 shots off him, five holes of golf that nobody enjoyed, closing with a triple bogey at the 15th. He trails Mullin by nine.
