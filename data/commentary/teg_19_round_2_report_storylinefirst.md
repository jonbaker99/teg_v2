# Monte Rei Gives With One Hand

## Jon Baker's 44 points at Monte Rei, the best round of the day, containing two eagles, a quintuple-bogey eight and a closing burst of 19 points
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's Sunday at Monte Rei was the best round of the day. Forty-four Stableford points, eight clear of the field's worst, and eleven better than the 33 he posted in Round 1. Steady, it was not. An eagle three at the 2nd announced a man in command of his faculties. Three holes later he had dropped seven gross shots: doubles at the 3rd and 4th, a triple at the short 5th. The 7th was worse. A quintuple-bogey eight there handed the 2026 TEG Green Jacket lead to Gregg Williams, a lead Jon Baker had held for 20 holes since the fifth hole of Round 1. Twenty holes of stewardship, surrendered in one hole. He had it back by the 9th. Possession, in this company, is a short-term arrangement.

A birdie at the 8th stopped the run. A second, at the 14th, opened the stretch that made the round: 19 points across the last five holes, including a second eagle at the 16th and pars at the 17th and 18th. He finished as though the first seven holes had happened to somebody else.

## The par-3 7th at Monte Rei, where all five players failed to make par and gave up twenty shots between them
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The par-3 7th at Monte Rei carries stroke index 8. Nothing on the card marks it as a problem hole. On the Sunday round it took twenty shots off the field. Not one of the five made par. Not one of the five took a single Stableford point from it. Jon Baker and Alex Baker both ran up 8s, quintuple bogeys apiece. John Patterson made 7. David Mullin and Gregg Williams both took 6. The hole collected its levy from each man in turn, politely, without discrimination.

For two of them it was merely the final instalment. David Mullin dropped 15 shots across holes 4 to 7, including an 8 at the par-3 5th. John Patterson dropped 13 over the same stretch. They arrived at the 7th already ruined. The 7th did not ask questions.

It also changed a competition. Jon Baker had led the 2026 TEG Green Jacket since the 5th hole of Round 1, twenty holes in front, and his 8 handed the lead to Gregg Williams. He took it back two holes later, at the 9th, and finished the round two strokes clear.

## Five eagles on Monte Rei's par fives in a single round, two of them by David Mullin in the middle of an otherwise ruinous day
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Monte Rei's par fives gave up five eagles in the second round, and three of the five players in the field collected at least one. The 2nd and the 16th did most of the giving. Jon Baker holed out for a 3 at both. Gregg Williams matched him at the 16th. David Mullin took a 3 at the 2nd and followed it with a par at the 3rd, the second-hardest hole on the course. At that point he looked like a man about to have a very good day.

His other eagle was the strangest of the five. Between the 4th and the 7th he went quadruple bogey, quintuple bogey, triple bogey, triple bogey. An 8 at the par-4 4th. An 8 at the par-3 5th. Fifteen shots dropped in four holes, with not one Stableford point banked. He then walked to the 8th and made his second eagle of the day, which is either resilience or a complete failure to notice what had just happened.

It did not save the round. Mullin signed for 94, finished last in the TEG 2026 Trophy standings, and took over the Wooden Spoon by four.

## Alex Baker's ten-point Trophy cushion cut to four, and David Mullin's fall from second overnight to last and sole possession of the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker kept his grip on the 2026 TEG Trophy at Monte Rei. He did not keep his cushion. He dropped nine shots in three holes early: a triple bogey at the 3rd, one of the hardest on the course, a double at the 4th, and a quadruple bogey at the short 5th. He added a quadruple bogey 8 at the 10th. Then, at the last, he made an 11. Six over par on a single hole, the worst he has scored relative to par on any hole in his career. There is a certain grandeur in signing for that and still leading a competition. He also slid from second to fifth in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. Jon Baker, ten adrift at one point, finished the day four behind.

David Mullin managed worse. Second in the Trophy after the opening round, he played the 4th through the 7th in fifteen over: 8, 8, 8, 6. He ended the day fifth. His quadruple bogey 8 at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course, dropped him to the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race on his own, four clear of Gregg Williams, who had held that place for all of three holes. Williams must have thought the worst was behind him. Mullin was already on his way.
