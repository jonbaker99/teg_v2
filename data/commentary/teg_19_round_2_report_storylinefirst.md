# Records Fall on a New Course

## Gregg Williams's +3 gross at Monte Rei is the lowest gross round in TEG history to date, paired with a personal-best 50 points and a birdie at the 16th that took him to the top of the Green Jacket standings
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei was TEG's first visit to the Nicklaus course. It belonged to Gregg Williams. His 75 came in at +3 gross. No one in the history of TEG has returned a lower gross round. It was also comfortably the best of the five cards handed in. The worst of those was +18, which tells you roughly what the rest of the field made of the place.

The middle of the front nine did most of the work. A birdie at the 5th, then an eagle three at the 6th. Holes 4 to 8 yielded 18 points. He closed with the same unhurried menace, dropping no gross shots at all from the 13th to the 16th. That included a par at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course. Thirteen points across four holes.

The birdie at the 16th was the decisive one. It moved Williams from second to outright first in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, displacing Jon Baker. He ended the day one shot clear, 15 over par to Jon Baker's 16. One further detail deserves the record. The Wooden Spoon place Williams held after Round 1 had already passed to David Mullin. A man can improve.

## John Patterson's 51 points, the third-best round in TEG history and the best of his own 34, built on an eagle two at the par-4 15th, Monte Rei's stroke index 1
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei handed John Patterson the third-best round in TEG history to date. Fifty-one Stableford points, from a gross 82, ten over par. It was the best of his own 34 rounds, which is a long apprenticeship to sit through.

The scoring arrived in two bursts. Through holes 10 to 12 he made three straight pars and collected 11 points, the handicap strokes quietly converting respectability into a haul. Then came the 15th, the hardest hole on the North Course. He holed out for a two. An eagle on a par 4, worth six points on its own, and part of a two-hole stretch through 14 and 15 in which he gained two shots on the card.

It changed nothing about the shape of the 2026 TEG Trophy. Patterson moved up to second at the 16th hole. Alex Baker's lead, over the same round, grew from nine points to 13. Patterson sits on 98, level with Jon Baker. The third-best round in TEG history bought him a share of second and no ground whatsoever. Gregg Williams, meanwhile, posted a personal-best 50 points on the same day, which rather crowded the occasion.

## What Monte Rei did to the three chasing positions: Jon Baker surrendered the Green Jacket lead by a single shot, David Mullin fell to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, and Alex Baker's worst day of the trip still widened his Trophy lead from nine to thirteen
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei reshuffled every position behind the leader. Jon Baker's day is best read as a chronicle. He had taken the 2026 TEG Green Jacket lead on the last green of R1. He lost it at the 2nd, when Alex Baker eagled the par-5. He drew level again at the 3rd. He was outright in front once more after a birdie at the 5th, one of the easiest holes on the course. He held on until the 16th, when Gregg Williams went past. Williams had picked up three shots on par across the 4th to the 9th, with a birdie and an eagle in that stretch. Williams now leads on 159 to Jon Baker's 160. A single shot. Jon Baker spent the entire day defending something he does not now own.

David Mullin dropped to the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon at the 2nd and stayed there for the duration. He did mount a finish: fourteen points across 15 to 18, including a par at the hardest hole on the course and a birdie at the last. It left him seven behind Williams. The Spoon is unmoved by late enthusiasm.

Alex Baker's 49 points was 13 down on his R1 62. His 2026 TEG Trophy lead grew from nine to thirteen. There is no justice here, only arithmetic.
