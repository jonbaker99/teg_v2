## Alex Baker turns a record 62-point opening round at Quinta da Ria into a wire-to-wire, 16-point Trophy win and the highest total TEG has recorded
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**Alex Baker's 62 at Quinta da Ria set up a record Trophy**

Alex Baker won the TEG 19 Trophy with 213 Stableford points. He finished 16 clear of John Patterson. No one in the history of TEG has posted a higher total.

The work was done on the first day. David Mullin held the lead after the opening hole at Quinta da Ria, and Alex Baker took it outright at the 4th. Sixty-eight holes remained. For competitive purposes they were ceremonial. His 62 points that round is the best single round TEG has recorded, and the +5 gross that produced it is the third-best gross round in the tournament's history. The scoring arrived in a burst from the 10th: 29 points across seven holes, built on a birdie at the 12th, a par at the 15th, a birdie at the par-5 16th, and an eagle two at the par-4 14th.

He added a second eagle at Monte Rei's par-5 2nd in Round 2. Two eagles in a single TEG is an all-time record, as are his 10 birdies or better across the four days. His closing 52 points at Monte Rei was the third-best round in TEG history.

The gross figures ran the other way. Alex Baker's +55 total was a personal best, and it left him fifth of five in the TEG 19 Green Jacket, which Jon Baker won at +43. Last of the field in one competition, untouchable in the other. That is what a handicap is for.

## Two new Algarve courses surrender the TEG record book: seven eagles, four personal-best gross totals, and the gross-round record broken twice in two days
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**The record book takes a beating**

Two courses neither of these players had seen before. Quinta da Ria for the opening round, Monte Rei for the other three. Between them they rewrote more of the TEG ledger than any previous visit to the Algarve.

Start with the eagles. Seven across the field over the four days. Alex Baker, Gregg Williams and John Patterson each made two, an all-time TEG record apiece. Four of them came in the opening round at Quinta da Ria, including John Patterson's two at the par-4 18th and Jon Baker's three at the par-5 11th. David Mullin went one better still with a two at the par-5 1st. An albatross, on the first hole of the trip. The standard was set before anyone had properly loosened up. At Monte Rei in Round 2, Gregg Williams eagled the par-5 6th and John Patterson the par-4 15th, a different hole on a different course from his Round 1 two.

The gross-round record fell twice in two days. Jon Baker's +4 at Quinta da Ria was the best gross round TEG had seen. Gregg Williams's +3 at Monte Rei the following day beat it. The record lasted a day. Alex Baker's +5 in Round 1 stands third all-time.

Alex Baker's 62 points in Round 1 is the best round in TEG history, with Jon Baker's 53 second and John Patterson's 51 at Monte Rei third. The three best rounds ever played were all played here. Jon Baker's 41 pars or better and the 10 birdies-or-better recorded by both Alex Baker and Gregg Williams are also records. Four players posted personal-best gross totals: Alex Baker (+55), David Mullin (+45), Gregg Williams (+45) and John Patterson (+47).

## John Patterson plays the best scoring golf of his TEG career, with personal bests, a record-tying birdie streak and two eagles, and is beaten by 16
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Patterson's best ever, and still 16 short**

John Patterson has never scored like this in a TEG. It still was not enough. His TEG 19 Trophy total of 197 points left him second, 16 behind Alex Baker's 213.

The scoring began at Quinta da Ria on the Saturday, where Patterson eagled the par-4 18th. He repeated the trick a day later at Monte Rei, eagling the par-4 15th, the stroke index 1 hole, on his way to 51 points. That is the third-best round in TEG history to date. The same round produced a personal-best gross of +10, an 82, his lowest round of the trip. Two eagles in one TEG is an all-time record.

Round three brought another burst: 21 points from holes 4 to 10, built on five pars and two bogeys that all returned three points apiece. There is no shame in a three-point bogey. There is a handicap system, and he used every inch of it. In the final round he made birdies at the par-5 6th and the par-3 7th, back-to-back. That ties the all-time TEG record for a birdie streak. The record is two. Across holes 5 to 8 he took 15 points and dropped only two gross shots, gaining on par at two of them.

The par-3 9th then cost him a triple bogey and a blank, ending the run. His final gross total of 335, +47, was a personal best, and placed him fourth in the TEG 19 Green Jacket.

## Jon Baker, the reigning Wooden Spoon holder, records the lowest Gross total in TEG history and survives David Mullin's closing charge by two
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Jon Baker's +43 is the lowest Gross total in TEG history, and he needed all of it**

Jon Baker won the TEG 19 Green Jacket with a Gross total of 331, +43. It is the lowest figure recorded in TEG history. The margin was two strokes.

He set the terms on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria, going round in +4 gross, at the time the best Gross round TEG had seen, and 53 Stableford points, the second-best round in TEG history. An eagle at the par-5 11th was the highlight. He led the Jacket from the 18th hole of that round.

It did not stay his. The thing then changed hands like a suspicious parcel. Alex Baker took the lead at the 2nd in Round 2, Jon Baker recovered it at the 5th, then Gregg Williams went ahead at the 16th, helped by a +3 gross round at Monte Rei that in turn became the best in TEG history. Jon Baker took the lead back at the 12th in Round 3, part of a run of 13 points across holes 10 to 13 without dropping a gross shot. A double bogey at the 14th ended it.

David Mullin, who had trailed by as many as 11, closed to two on the final day. He opened Round 4 with eight straight holes of par or better, tying the all-time TEG record. The streak ended at the 9th, in the manner these things do, with a triple bogey. He finished with 49 points, a personal best, and a personal-best Gross total of +45. It was not quite enough.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross round in TEG history on Sunday and still collects the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon by seven
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**Gregg Williams: record round, Wooden Spoon**

Gregg Williams finished the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon on 168 Stableford points, seven behind David Mullin on 175. He took the trophy nobody wants in the same tournament in which he played the best gross round in TEG history. Both facts are true. Neither cancels the other.

That round came in Round 2 at Monte Rei: +3 gross, a figure no one has bettered, and 50 Stableford points, a personal best. The engine was the front nine. Six holes without dropping a gross shot from the 4th to the 9th, including a birdie at the 5th and an eagle at the par-5 6th, with 18 points banked across holes 4 to 8 alone. By the 16th he had taken the outright lead in the TEG 19 Green Jacket.

He held it until the 12th hole of Round 3, when Jon Baker moved back ahead. From there the Spoon race caught up with him. Triple bogeys at the 15th and 16th at Monte Rei in Round 3 put him at the bottom. A bogey at the 1st and a double at the 3rd in Round 4 kept him there, which was tidy work for a man with nothing left to protect. An earlier Round 3 wobble of bogey, bogey, double from the 4th had at least been halted by a birdie at the 7th.

The rest of the ledger reads rather better: a personal-best gross total of +45, second in the Jacket alongside Mullin, plus all-time TEG records of two eagles and ten birdies or better. He leaves with the record round, the records, and the Spoon.
