# The Book Was Thin, And Mullin Rewrote It Twice

## How David Mullin won the TEG 2 Trophy: two record rounds either side of a wobble, while Jon Baker's seven-shot lead evaporated in five holes
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**Mullin's record bookends deliver the TEG 2 Trophy**

David Mullin won the 2009 TEG Trophy at Boavista with rounds that framed the tournament at either end. Three rounds over Howard Swan's parkland layout in Lagos, and Mullin opened with a net level-par 0 — at the time the best round in TEG history, and his +20 gross the best gross round recorded to that point.

Jon Baker matched him for much of the way. Baker's +4 in round one and +1 in round two both stood as the second-best rounds in TEG history to date, and after 36 holes it was Baker who held the Trophy lead, with Mullin ahead in the TEG 2 Green Jacket.

Both then found the closing holes at Boavista brutal. Mullin dropped ten shots across 16 to 18 in round two — triple, triple, quadruple, finishing with an eight at the last — and went three holes without a net par. Baker fared worse over the same stretch plus the 15th, bleeding 13 shots with quadruple bogeys at both 17 and 18. His round three was decided early: five straight holes without a net par to start, 12 shots gone by the turn at the 5th, and later another eight at the 15th.

Mullin's response was a net -2, a new best round in TEG history, and an 89 gross — his lowest of the week. He took the Trophy at +6, seven clear of Baker.

## The par-three 14th at Boavista collected an 8 or worse from three different players in R1, then took another 8 off Stuart Neumann in R2
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**The 14th at Boavista, and the damage it did**

TEG 2 was played entirely at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort in Lagos, a Howard Swan parkland course none of the field had seen before, and the 14th made the strongest first impression. It is a par three off stroke index 7, and in Round 1 it took an 8 or worse from three separate players.

The worst of them belonged to Gregg Williams, who signed for a 9 — a sextuple bogey, and his worst score on any par three. It came in the middle of a closing run in which he dropped 22 shots across holes 12 to 18, including a second quintuple bogey at the 17th. Henry Meller also made 8 there, his fourth big number in a five-hole sequence from the 11th that cost him 20 shots. Stuart Neumann's 8 sat inside a five-hole stretch from the 13th that leaked 14.

Neumann then went back the following day and did it again, another 8 at the same par three in Round 2. Both of his were quintuple bogeys, and both stand as his worst efforts on a par three.

All three players recorded their best gross of the week in Round 3 — 91 for Meller, 92 for Neumann, 105 for Williams — which suggests the course, and the 14th in particular, was learned the hard way.

## Gregg Williams's tournament measured in nines: one at the 14th, one at the 17th and one at the 12th, spread across R1 and R3
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Gregg Williams's tournament measured in nines**

Gregg Williams made three 9s at Boavista, and two of them came in the same round. The first arrived at the par-three 14th in the opening round, a sextuple bogey that remains the worst hole he has played relative to par. The second came three holes later at the 17th, a par four, for a quintuple bogey — and this on the easiest hole on the card, stroke index 17.

Those two holes sat inside a closing stretch that unravelled completely. From the 12th to the 18th in round one, Williams played seven holes in 22 over par, with a double bogey or worse at every one of them.

Round two was steadier only by comparison: a quadruple-bogey 7 at the par-three 6th, 13 shots dropped across the 11th to the 15th, and a quadruple-bogey 8 to finish at the last.

The third 9 came in the final round, at the 12th — a par four and the third-hardest hole on the course — for another quintuple bogey. That it landed in his best round of the week says something about the rest of it: Williams signed for 105 on the Monday, his lowest score of the three days. TEG 2 was his first visit to the Algarve course, and Boavista collected its dues in a small number of very large numbers.

## David Mullin's gross procession to the TEG 2 Green Jacket, and Stuart Neumann's chase from nine behind, then eight, then eleven
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

David Mullin took the TEG 2 Green Jacket at Boavista with a gross total of +66, eleven clear of Stuart Neumann. Across three rounds on the same Howard Swan parkland course in Lagos, Mullin never gave the tournament a shape other than his own.

He opened with +20, the best gross round in TEG history to that point. He beat it in the final round with +18, an 89 and his lowest score of the week. In between came a +28, the third-best gross round recorded at the time. Three rounds, three of the strongest gross scores anyone had produced.

Neumann's problem was that he played well and still lost ground. His opening +29 was the second-best gross round in TEG history to date, and worth 11 Stableford points — the third-best round then recorded — yet it left him nine behind. The damage came in a five-hole stretch from the 13th, where he dropped 14 shots and failed to make a net par, including a quintuple-bogey 8 at the par-three 14th, his worst return on a par three. His second round of +27 matched the second-best gross mark and pulled a single shot back, to eight. Then he made 8 at the 14th again.

By the closing round the gap had stretched to eleven. Neumann did at least halt a run of bogeys or worse with a birdie at the 11th, and signed for 92, his best of the week.

## Henry Meller was bottom of the field from the third hole of R1 and never moved, then produced his best golf a day too late
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**Henry Meller**

Henry Meller took the TEG 2 Wooden Spoon, finishing the week at +39 net against par at Boavista, the Howard Swan parkland outside Lagos that hosted all three rounds of the 2009 trip.

The damage was done early. In the first round Meller played holes 11 to 15 in twenty shots over par: a triple bogey at the short 11th, quadruple bogeys at the 12th, 13th and 15th, and a quintuple-bogey 8 at the par-three 14th. Across the five holes he did not make a single net par, coming up ten net shots short of the mark. The 15th, the hardest hole on the card, cost him four.

The recovery, when it came, came too late to matter. Meller's second round was worth +13 net; his third was +2, an improvement of eleven shots between rounds. He birdied the par-four 5th and parred the 6th on his way round, and signed for a gross 91 — +20 for the round, and the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to that point.

It was also his best score of the three days at Boavista, a course none of the field had seen before this week. With the Spoon already effectively settled by the first afternoon, the final round served only to show what Meller had been capable of all along.
