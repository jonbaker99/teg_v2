# Forty-Two at Monte Rei

## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time, a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve that only two rated courses can beat for sheer difficulty. Jon Baker opened with three pars for 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there. Arriving at a course you have never seen and parring the first three is, by any sane measure, showing off.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead went back and forth all afternoon. Gregg Williams moved outright ahead at the 7th with a par, Jon took it back at the 12th with one of his own, and Williams reclaimed it at the 13th while making a bogey, which takes a certain cheek. Jon drew level again at the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that follows a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the course, with a par worth four points. He went clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a single gross shot. Four closing pars, on a course handing out nines to everyone else, is the finish you dream about on the drive over. He signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round, and turned a one-point overnight lead into five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course on the rated list, and the nines, eights and sevens it took from all five players
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Monte Rei made its TEG debut here, and the Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve, with water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes, turned up as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker got the worst of it immediately: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, another 9 at the 2nd, and then a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. Two nines before anybody had properly woken up is bad golf, and there is no gentle way of phrasing it. Jon Baker was not let off either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's trouble came in bunches. A 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, and a second 9 arrived at the 16th.

Mullin did at least find an ending. After a long spell without beating a bogey, he made a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. Finishing a day like that with a birdie is the work of a man with an unusually strong constitution.

## Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25: every gap in the TEG 19 standings widened, not a single leader changed
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks as the third-hardest course in TEG's rated set, and the bottom of the field felt every yard of it. Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes: a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 on the course, he signed for a 10, the worst he has made on a par 3 anywhere. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. Then came bogey, bogey and par from the 12th for nine points in three holes, genuinely fine work in the circumstances, before a quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, shut the recovery down. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th started things off. A triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him there on his own, five shots gone across two holes. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th. Nobody at the top moved, but the margins stretched: Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one point to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
