# Forty-Two at Monte Rei

## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round carried TEG to Monte Rei for the first time. It is a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve, and it concedes nothing. Only two rated courses play harder for golfers of this standard. Jon Baker opened with three pars worth 10 Stableford points. The third of them was a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there.

Thereafter the 2026 TEG Trophy lead passed back and forth with considerable civility. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with another. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th, and did so with a bogey. At the 15th, Jon drew level. That is the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green, and the hardest hole on the course; his par there was worth four points. He moved clear at the 16th.

He then parred the last four holes for 14 points, without dropping a gross shot among them. It is a fine way to finish a round on a course of that severity. He signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round was Monte Rei's first appearance in TEG. The Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve has water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes, and it entered the records as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker was attended to early. A quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, a second 9 at the 2nd, and later a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. Eleven shots to par across those three holes, and poor golf by any honest reckoning. Jon Baker was not spared either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's troubles arrived in company. A 9 at the par-5 6th began a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, which was a thoroughly unhappy quarter of an hour. A second 9 arrived at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a long stretch without beating a bogey. He followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th, and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. It was the only genuinely good news on offer.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks as the third-hardest course in TEG's rated set, and the bottom of the field had the fullest acquaintance with it. Alex Baker stood 14 over par after four holes: a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 here, he signed for a 10. It is the worst he has made on a par 3. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots, and those were dismal holes. A run of bogey-bogey-par from the 12th brought nine points in three holes, which was handsomely done. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, closed the matter. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th began it. A triple at the 8th took him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him there alone. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, a passage of golf with nothing to recommend it. He closed with another 10 at the 18th. No leader changed, though the gaps did. Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
