# An Eleven, a Ten, and the Trophy: Alex Baker Wins in Catalonia

## Alex Baker's Trophy: 169 points, two field-best rounds, and a course-worst gross that cost him nothing

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It is the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of his own career. He led the Stableford count after each of the final three rounds and, having taken the front after R2, was never headed again. The victory is unarguable. The golf is another matter entirely.

The scoring arrived in bursts, as though rationed by an unseen authority. On the Tour Course in R1 he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run assembled almost entirely from bogeys at generously indexed holes, then added 13 more across 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-five 15th. His finest sustained passage came in R3 on the Stadium Course: 21 points from holes 11 to 16, six consecutive holes without a blank. For those six holes he looked like a man in complete command of his own destiny.

The gross figures decline to corroborate any of this. In R2 he made an 8 at the par-three 16th, which is a considerable amount of golf for one short hole, and then an 11 at the par-five 18th. Both were personal worsts against par. He signed for 106. That is a new PGA Catalunya Tour course-worst, surpassing the previous mark of 105, which had stood across 22 prior visits. To set a course-worst requires a certain refusal to be discouraged, and he found it. R4 brought a 10 at the 14th and an 8 at the 17th, delivered with the serenity of a man who had already checked the Stableford column. His best gross of the trip was 98 on the Tour and 100 on the Stadium, against prior bests of 92 and 93. It cost him the Green Jacket, where he slid from second after R1 to fifth. On Stableford, it cost him precisely nothing, which is the only currency that was being counted.

## David Mullin: an 11 at the 15th, then the lowest gross round ever played at the Stadium

**David Mullin**

Mullin visited both extremes available to a golfer, and the first of them was waiting at the 15th on the Tour Course in Round 1. The par five took him eleven strokes. A sextuple bogey, no Stableford points, and the worst score he has ever recorded on a par five in his golfing history. The consequences extended beyond the card: the hole shifted him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, which is to say the very bottom of that particular contest.

Two days later, on the Stadium Course, he did something the tournament had never previously witnessed. His 84 gross in Round 3 is a new course record at the Stadium, beating the previous mark of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was also a substantial personal revision: his own best there had been 91 across five earlier visits, and this was eight strokes better than his last outing on the course. Somewhere in that round he stopped playing carefully and simply forgot to make a mess of anything.

Round 4 arrived to clarify that the record had not remade the man. Through the closing stretch he went four holes without a net par: a double bogey at the 14th, a triple at the 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. Nine shots gone in four holes, and with them five Stableford points he was entitled to consider his own. A tournament, then, containing one number nobody will forget and several he would prefer everybody did.

## John Patterson's second place, and the eight holes on the opening morning

John Patterson's tournament was defined, and very nearly buried, in a single stretch of the opening morning on the Tour Course. At the 3rd he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, and then declined to stabilise: holes 3 through 10 cost him 22 shots to par, including triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th. Across the seven holes from the 3rd to the 9th he did not manage a single net par. By the 4th hole he had reached the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, dropping from fourth to fifth. It was, by any reasonable measure, a morning to be quietly written out of the official history.

What came next is the reason anyone was still discussing him on the final afternoon. Later in that same round he took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th: three pars and a bogey at the par-five 15th. In round two he produced another eight at the 4th and leaked 11 shots between the 6th and the 10th, before answering with 16 points across the 11th to the 15th. That round yielded a 94, his best gross on the Tour Course, bettering his previous mark of 96. He is, apparently, incapable of a bad round without also having a good one inside it.

The pattern held to the close. After a triple at the 14th and a double at the 15th on the Stadium, he arrested the slide with a bogey at the 16th and a birdie at the 17th. Second place, constructed entirely out of the recoveries, with the collapses left in plain sight for the record.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: reigning Wooden Spoon holder to +66 and a 14-shot win / Jon Baker's Wooden Spoon: the defending Trophy champion finishes 23 points adrift of the next man

**Round 1, Tour Course.** Gregg Williams opened in the manner the holder of the Wooden Spoon is contractually obliged to open: a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 7th, another quadruple bogey at the 17th for an 8, and a bleed of dropped shots running from the 2nd all the way to the 14th, taking in triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th. A birdie at the par-5 15th finally halted proceedings. He signed for 31 points. Jon Baker, meanwhile, had held the Green Jacket gross lead since the 5th hole of that same round, and looked entirely comfortable there.

**Round 2, Tour Course.** Baker's tenure at the summit lasted 20 holes. A bogey at the 7th on Sunday handed the gross lead to Williams. Baker responded in the manner of a man who resents eviction: a birdie at the 14th launched a run of five holes without dropping a gross shot, worth 16 points to the 18th, and by the close of play he was Jacket leader again, with Alex Baker heading the Trophy. Williams mounted his own surge over the identical closing stretch, taking 13 points from the 13th to the 16th with four consecutive pars and not a gross shot dropped, and posted 42 points. An improvement of 11 on his opening round, and the first evidence that the Spoon holder had other plans.

**Round 3, Stadium Course.** The Jacket changed hands for good, and it happened early. Williams parred the 2nd and took the outright gross lead. Baker's round then disintegrated at the 4th, where a 9 on a par 4, a quintuple bogey, was recorded as a blank. By nightfall the boards read Alex Baker in the Trophy and Williams in the Jacket, and they did not read anything else thereafter.

**Round 4, Stadium Course.** Williams birdied the par-3 5th, parred the 6th, and gathered nine points across the 8th, 9th and 10th, closing out an 84 that stands as his best gross score in seven visits to the Stadium, eight shots better than his previous time round it.

The two ledgers closed in opposite corners of the room. Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin: the sixth-best gross total in TEG history and comfortably his own finest. Jon Baker, the defending Trophy champion, finished on 126 Stableford points, 23 adrift of the next man, and accepted the Wooden Spoon. A title defence conducted, in the end, from underneath the field.
