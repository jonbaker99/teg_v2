# Monte Rei Collects Its Dues

## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time. It is a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve, and it concedes nothing. Only two rated courses play harder for golfers of this standard. Jon Baker opened with three pars worth 10 Stableford points, the third a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there. They were struck with the quiet authority of a man renewing his car insurance a month early.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead changed hands with the decorum of a minor ministry in a reshuffle. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with another. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th despite a bogey, which tells you what the course was doing to everyone else. Jon drew level at the 15th. That is the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green, and the hardest hole on the course. His par there was worth four points. He moved clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a gross shot. He signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round was Monte Rei's first appearance in TEG. The Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve has water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes. It introduced itself as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker got the worst of it early. A quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, then another 9 at the 2nd. That is not a slow start. That is a surrender, filed in triplicate before the dew had lifted. A quadruple-bogey 7 followed at the 11th. Jon Baker was not spared either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th, four over on a hole that asks for one decent shot. David Mullin's damage came in clusters. A 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par. A second 9 arrived at the 16th. Lessons, one assumes, will be learned.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a stretch without beating a bogey. He followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. It was the only genuinely good news the course handed out in these beats.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks as the third-hardest course in the TEG's rated set. The bottom of the field can confirm it. Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes: a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. That is not a start. That is an incident report. At the 7th, the course's longest and meanest par 3, he signed for a 10, the worst he has made on a par 3. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. A run of bogey-bogey-par from the 12th brought nine points in three holes, which was properly done. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, closed the door. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th started it. A triple at the 8th took him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon. A double at the 9th left him there alone. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th. Two tens in a single round stops being bad luck. It becomes a policy. No leader changed, but the gaps did. Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
