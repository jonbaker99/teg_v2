# Two Rounds, One Record

## David Mullin and Stuart Neumann both card 82 gross to break the Boavista course record, with Neumann's net -9 standing as the best round in TEG history to date
<!-- storyline: round -->

At Boavista Golf & Spa Resort, both David Mullin and Stuart Neumann carded 82 gross in round two, breaking the course record of 83 that had stood across 40 prior visits. Neumann's round, worth -9 net, stands as the best round in TEG history to date, while his +11 gross matched Mullin's own +11 as the best gross round recorded. For Mullin it was also a personal best, his -4 net eclipsing anything he had managed before. The score marked a course personal best for both men: Mullin had never beaten 83 at Boavista in eight prior visits, while Neumann's previous best there had been 89, an improvement of 15 strokes on his last visit. Mullin remained atop both the 2012 Trophy and the 2012 Green Jacket standings, extending his leads to three and fourteen shots respectively. At the other end of the table, Henry Meller took over the last-placed spot in the 2012 Wooden Spoon race from Neumann, now trailing by 15.

## Henry Meller's three-hole collapse from the 14th to the 16th hands him the Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

At Boavista Golf & Spa Resort in round 2 of the 2012 TEG, Henry Meller's card was undone by a single stretch. Across holes 14 to 16 he bled 12 shots without a single net par, a sequence that started with a quadruple-bogey 7 on the par-3 14th, continued with a triple bogey on the punishing par-4 15th (stroke index 1), and ended with a quintuple-bogey 10 on the par-5 16th, his own worst hole of the round. He added a further quadruple bogey at the 18th for good measure. Oddly, his back nine still finished 12 shots better than his front, suggesting the outward holes had been grim in their own right. The round produced a gross 30 over par, the worst score recorded by anyone in the field that day. It was enough to blow Stuart Neumann out of the Wooden Spoon cellar and drop Meller into last place by 15 shots, a collapse as sudden as it was complete.

## Gregg Williams' career-best round and Stuart Neumann's leap from last both vault past a level-par Jon Baker in the Trophy chase, while Henry Meller's collapse flips the Wooden Spoon race entirely
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Boavista in TEG 9's second round, Gregg Williams posted a career-best round, finishing six-under-par net for the day. It was enough to leapfrog Jon Baker, whose level-par effort suddenly looked ordinary as the TEG 9 Trophy chase reshuffled.

Stuart Neumann matched the move stroke for stroke, following a poor first-round 6-over with a stunning nine-under second round — a 15-shot swing that also carried him clear of the Wooden Spoon cellar he had occupied overnight.

That last-place spot passed instead to Henry Meller, whose double bogey on the par-5 3rd sent him from fourth to last in the TEG 9 Wooden Spoon standings, flipping the race entirely. Overall, David Mullin retained his TEG 9 Trophy lead, though the gap to the chasing pack widened from one shot to three.
