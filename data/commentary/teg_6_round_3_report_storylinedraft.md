# Fifty-One Holes Undone in a Single Round

## Stuart Neumann's flat, error-free round is the best golf played all day, and quietly rescues him from bottom spot in the Trophy standings.
<!-- storyline: round -->

Round three at El Prat's Rosa course produced the best golf of the day, and it came quietly from Stuart Neumann. While others carded wayward numbers around him, Neumann settled into a flat, unspectacular rhythm, at one point running two holes without dropping a gross shot, a par on the fifth followed by a par on the tricky, short sixth. Nothing about the round shouted for attention, and that was rather the point.

The effect on the standings was significant. Neumann had gone into the round anchored to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon table, just a single shot off safety. His round of 94 shifted the picture entirely: Henry Meller dropped into the Spoon's last place instead, and the gap back up to Neumann now stood at eight shots. Neumann's day did not trouble the Trophy leaderboard, where Gregg Williams remained out front, but it was exactly the sort of error-free, forgettable-looking round that rescues a TEG from the wrong end of the table.

## Henry Meller's round of double-figure horrors, two tens and a nine, drops him from mid-table into commanding last place.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Henry Meller's round at El Prat - Rosa came apart in a single ugly stretch. Having gone strong in Round 2 (-5), he swung 20 shots the wrong way to finish this round on 15 over. The damage was concentrated between the 7th and 10th, four holes that bled 14 gross shots and produced not a single net par: a triple bogey, a sextuple bogey, a double bogey and another triple in succession. The sextuple came at the 8th, one of the hardest holes on the course, where he ran up a 10. He had already opened with a 10 of his own at the 1st, a quintuple bogey on the par-5 that stood as his worst hole of the tournament relative to par, and later added a nine at the 17th, the hardest hole on the course.

The cumulative effect pushed him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, a spot he had not occupied before this round, and left him a commanding eight shots adrift of the next-worst player.

## El Prat's Rosa course, new to TEG and designed by Greg Norman, punishes the same stretch of holes across nearly the entire field on its debut.
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Real Club de Golf El Prat's Rosa course made its TEG debut in round 3, and Greg Norman's design left a mark on the same stretch of holes for almost the whole field. The 7th, 8th and 9th — with the 8th ranked the hardest hole on the course — proved the wrecking ground.

Jon Baker took a quintuple bogey 9 at the 8th. Gregg Williams made a quadruple bogey at the 7th, then followed with further bogeys at 8 and 9, bleeding 8 shots across the three holes. Henry Meller suffered worst of all: a triple bogey at 7 was followed by a sextuple bogey 10 at the 8th, then more dropped shots at 9 and 10, 14 shots lost across the four holes.

The damage reshaped the standings. Williams kept his 2013 Trophy lead but saw it trimmed to 8. Baker's meltdown didn't stop him snatching the Green Jacket lead from David Mullin, cutting a five-shot gap to one. Meller's collapse was costliest of all, dropping him into the Wooden Spoon lead ahead of Stuart Neumann by 8 shots.

## David Mullin's fifty-one-hole grip on the Green Jacket collapses at El Prat, handing Jon Baker a one-shot lead as the Wooden Spoon changes hands too.
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin's hold on the TEG 6 Green Jacket had lasted since the third hole of round one, 51 holes of unbroken leadership through Costa Brava and PGA Catalunya. At El Prat's Rosa course it finally gave way. Mullin carded his worst gross round to date, a 29, dragging him down from a strong second round that had him at -1 to a closing 11 over. The damage came in bursts: a quintuple bogey nine at the 15th, his worst hole of the TEG, followed by a quadruple bogey nine at the 18th, the round's final blow.

While Mullin unravelled, Jon Baker closed with three shots gained to par across the last three holes, a par at the 18th enough to move him into the outright Green Jacket lead by a single stroke. Elsewhere, Gregg Williams retained his grip on the TEG 6 Trophy, and the Wooden Spoon changed hands as well, with Henry Meller dropping to last place from Stuart Neumann.
