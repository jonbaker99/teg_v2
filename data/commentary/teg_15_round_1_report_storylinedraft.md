# Williams Bookends Disaster With Brilliance On Estoril Debut

## Gregg Williams's Estoril debut, bookended by two quadruple bogeys either side of the best golf of the day
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams's first round at Estoril Golf Club opened with a quadruple bogey 8 at the 1st, one of the hardest holes on the course. He steadied himself immediately, though, with a bogey at the 2nd before a birdie at the 3rd stopped the bleeding. That sparked the best stretch of his day: back-to-back birdies at the 3rd and 4th took him to the outright lead in the TEG 1 Trophy, and by the 11th he had also moved into the outright lead for the TEG 1 Green Jacket. He kept things tidy through the middle of the round, pairing par with par at the 8th and 9th and again at the 13th and 14th. But the round came apart at the very end, with a second quadruple bogey 8, this time at the 18th, closing out the day in the same fashion it had begun.

## Jon Baker's share of the Trophy lead evaporates in twin quintuple bogeys
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Jon Baker built his way into a share of the lead in the 2022 TEG Trophy through the front nine at Estoril, closing it out with pars on the 8th, 9th and 10th to pick up nine points across that stretch and draw level at the top after ten holes. It capped a front nine of 31 points that stood well clear of his back-nine form.

The turn undid all of it. The 11th, one of the hardest holes on the course, brought a quintuple bogey that snapped his run of pars, and the very next hole produced a second quintuple bogey to match it. Two holes had wiped out the momentum he had spent the first ten building. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, on the easiest hole on the course, added further damage before the round was out. Baker's overall score of 96 stood as his best gross of the TEG so far, but the round told two stories: a sharp front nine and a back nine that let the shared lead slip away.

## David Mullin and Alex Baker share TEG 15's opening Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Estoril in TEG 15's Round 1, David Mullin and Stuart Neumann both found their way to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race. Neumann's trouble came early: a quintuple bogey 9 at the 3rd dropped him into last place, and matters worsened at the 15th, where a sextuple bogey 10 stood as his heaviest blow against par of the trip. He recovered well down the stretch, picking up 9 points over the closing three holes with a bogey and two pars. Mullin's collapse came later in the round. A quadruple bogey 8 at the 12th, one of Estoril's harder holes, was followed by a triple bogey at the 17th, the easiest hole on the course, and that finish was enough to send him to the foot of the Spoon standings in Neumann's place. Both men were making their first visits to Estoril, and both spent part of round one occupying the cellar of TEG 15's Wooden Spoon race before the tournament had even reached its second round.
