# The Man Who Was Never Headed: Alex Baker's Long Walk to Catalonia

## Alex Baker's wire-to-wire Trophy win from the least likely CV in the field

**Baker takes the Trophy on 169**

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 Stableford points, finishing eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. The total ranks seventh on the all-time list and is the second-best of Baker's own career.

The route there was not a smooth one. His scoring came in bursts, the first arriving in the opening round at PGA Catalunya's Tour course, where he collected 17 points across holes 8 to 12 — three bogeys and two pars, all of them worth full value against his handicap allowance. The best single stretch of his week came in round three on the Stadium course, where holes 11 to 16 yielded 21 points from three pars and three bogeys.

Between and after those runs came the damage. In round two he signed for a gross 106, a new worst score recorded at PGA Catalunya - Tour across 22 prior visits, beating the previous mark of 105. That round ended with an 11 at the par-five 18th, a sextuple bogey and the worst score Baker has made on a par five. Round four brought two more blow-ups on the Stadium course: a 10 at the par-four 14th and an 8 at the 17th, both blanks on the card.

He still held the Trophy lead after round four, level at the top on Stableford, with Gregg Williams leading the Jacket.

## The Stadium course record broken and immediately rendered pointless — David Mullin's 84

**Mullin's record round, and what surrounded it**

The single best round of the week belonged to David Mullin. His third-round 84 gross at PGA Catalunya's Stadium course set a new course record, beating the previous mark of 89 — a figure that had stood across 27 prior visits. Lowering a course record by five shots is unusual in any context; doing it on the Stadium, the harder of the two tracks in use, made it the standout individual performance of the tournament.

It did not change the outcome. Gregg Williams won the Green Jacket at +66, finishing 14 shots clear of Mullin in second.

The rounds either side explain the gap. In the first round on the Tour course, Mullin ran up an 11 at the par-five 15th, a sextuple bogey that returned no Stableford points and dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings. In the final round, back on the Stadium, he lost the tournament's closing stretch entirely: a double bogey at 14, a triple at the par-five 15th, and further doubles at 16 and 17. Nine shots went in four holes, and he failed to make a net par across any of them.

The record stands in the books. The scoreboard, by then, had long been settled.

## The brothers Baker at opposite ends of the same tournament

**The brothers Baker**

For a while, both Bakers were in front. After two rounds at PGA Catalunya's Tour course, Alex Baker led the Trophy on Stableford points and Jon Baker led the Green Jacket gross competition — one family, two trophies, both apparently in hand.

Jon's half of that arrangement did not survive the day. He had held the gross lead for 20 holes, stretching from the fifth hole of round one to the seventh of round two, before a bogey six at the par-five seventh handed the Jacket to Gregg Williams. Round three on the Stadium course offered no recovery. At the par-four 4th, Jon ran up a nine — a quintuple bogey worth no points at all.

Alex, meanwhile, was going the other way. Over holes 11 to 16 in the same round he collected 21 points, built on pars at the 11th, 13th and 14th and bogeys at the 12th, 15th and 16th, none of them costing him more than a single point against his handicap allowance.

The finish reflected the split. Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson — the seventh-best total in the event's history and the second-best of his own career. Jon Baker finished the week on 126 points and collected the Wooden Spoon.

## Camiral's catalogue of catastrophe: the double-figure holes and the record-worst round

**Camiral's catalogue of catastrophe**

The scorecards at PGA Catalunya carried an unusual number of double-figure entries, and one of them ended up in the record books.

Alex Baker signed for 106 gross in the second round on the Tour course, a new course-worst. The previous high mark was 105, set across 22 prior visits. Two holes did most of the damage: a quintuple-bogey 8 at the par-three 16th, and a sextuple-bogey 11 at the par-five 18th. Both were the worst scores Baker had recorded on those pars. He added a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-four 14th on the Stadium course in round four.

The opening round had already produced its share. John Patterson bled 22 shots across the eight holes from the 3rd to the 10th, a run that began with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the par-four 3rd and continued through triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th and double bogeys at the 5th, 6th and 10th. He took no more than two Stableford points from the entire stretch.

Elsewhere in round one, Gregg Williams ran up a 9 at the par-five 7th, and David Mullin posted an 11 at the par-five 15th — a sextuple bogey and his own worst return on a par five.

The Stadium course contributed one more in round three, when Jon Baker made a quintuple-bogey 9 at the par-four 4th.

## Gregg Williams builds the Green Jacket out of a ruined opening round

Gregg Williams's tournament began badly. On the Tour course at PGA Catalunya, his opening round unravelled early: a bogey at the 2nd, back-to-back triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th, a double at the 5th, and then a nine at the par-5 7th, a quadruple bogey worth no points. Bogeys followed at the 8th, 9th, 10th, 11th, 12th, 13th and 14th before a birdie at the par-5 15th finally ended the run of dropped shots. There was still time for another quadruple bogey, an eight at the par-4 17th, and the round closed on 31 points.

The response was immediate. Williams returned 42 points in the second round, an improvement of 11, and at the 7th hole he took the Green Jacket (Gross) lead from Jon Baker, who had held it for 20 holes since the 5th of round one.

He consolidated on the Stadium course. A run of four straight pars from the 2nd to the 5th in round three kept him from dropping a gross shot across that stretch, and he moved back into the outright gross lead at the 2nd. In the final round he collected nine points across holes 8 to 10, playing them par, bogey, par.

Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin. It ranks as the sixth-best gross total recorded, and his own best.

## Jon Baker, defending champion, takes the Wooden Spoon by 23

**Baker's week ends with the Wooden Spoon**

Jon Baker finished the tournament on 126 Stableford points, enough to collect the Wooden Spoon.

It had not started that way. Baker led the Green Jacket (Gross) competition from the 5th hole of round one and held the position for 20 holes, across into the second round at PGA Catalunya's Tour course. The run ended at the 7th, a par 5, where he took six for a bogey and two points, and Gregg Williams took over at the top.

Baker's best golf came at the end of that same round. Over the closing five holes he collected 16 points and did not drop a gross shot: a birdie two at the par-3 14th, followed by pars at the 15th, 16th, 17th and 18th. It was the most sustained stretch of scoring he produced all week.

The damage arrived in round three at the Stadium course. At the 4th, a par 4 of stroke index 9, Baker ran up a nine — a quintuple bogey worth no points at all. Five shots gone on a single hole is difficult to recover from in a Stableford format, and the blank return there stood in stark contrast to the steady threes he had been posting a round earlier.

The 126-point total was where the week settled, and the Wooden Spoon followed.
