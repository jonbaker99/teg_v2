# An Eleven, a Ten, and the Trophy: Alex Baker Wins in Catalonia

## Alex Baker's Trophy: 169 points, two field-best rounds, and a course-worst gross that cost him nothing

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It stands as the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of Baker's own career. He led the Stableford count after each of the final three rounds, and was never headed once he got to the front after R2.

The scoring came in bursts. On the Tour Course in R1 he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run built on bogeys at stroke-index holes rather than anything spectacular, then added 13 more across 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-five 15th. His biggest single stretch came in R3 on the Stadium Course: 21 points from holes 11 to 16, six holes without a blank.

The gross figures tell a different story. In R2 he made an 8 at the par-three 16th and an 11 at the par-five 18th, both personal worsts against par, and signed for 106 — a new PGA Catalunya Tour course-worst, beating the previous mark of 105 set across 22 prior visits. R4 brought a 10 at the 14th and an 8 at the 17th. His best gross of the week was 98 on the Tour and 100 on the Stadium, against prior bests of 92 and 93. It cost him the Green Jacket, where he slid from second after R1 to fifth. On Stableford, it cost him nothing.

## David Mullin: an 11 at the 15th, then the lowest gross round ever played at the Stadium

**David Mullin**

Mullin's week ran to both extremes, and the first of them arrived at the 15th on the Tour Course in Round 1. The par five took him eleven strokes — a sextuple bogey, no Stableford points, and the worst score he has ever recorded on a par five. The damage was not confined to the scorecard: the hole dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, the bottom of that particular race.

Two days later, on the Stadium Course, he produced something the tournament had never seen. His 84 gross in Round 3 is a new course record at the Stadium, beating the previous mark of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was also comfortably his own best there: his previous best gross on the course was 91 across five earlier visits, and the round was eight strokes better than his last time out on it.

Round 4 offered a reminder that the record had not rewritten him entirely. Through the closing stretch he went four holes without a net par — a double bogey at the 14th, a triple at the 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. Nine shots went in four holes, and five Stableford points he might reasonably have expected went with them. A tournament, then, of one number nobody will forget and several he would rather.

## John Patterson's second place, and the eight holes on the opening morning

John Patterson's tournament was defined, and very nearly derailed, in a single stretch of the opening morning on the Tour Course. From the 3rd he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, and did not recover: holes 3 through 10 cost him 22 shots to par, including triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th. Across the seven holes from the 3rd to the 9th he failed to make a single net par. By the 4th he had slipped to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, dropping from fourth to fifth.

What followed was the reason he was still in the argument at the end. Later in the same round he took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, three pars and a bogey at the par-five 15th. In round two he made another eight at the 4th and leaked 11 shots between the 6th and the 10th, then answered with 16 points across the 11th to the 15th. That round produced a 94, his best gross on the Tour Course, bettering his previous mark of 96.

The pattern held to the last day. After a triple at the 14th and a double at the 15th on the Stadium, he stopped the run with a bogey at the 16th and a birdie at the 17th. Second place, built on the recoveries rather than the collapses.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: reigning Wooden Spoon holder to +66 and a 14-shot win / Jon Baker's Wooden Spoon: the defending Trophy champion finishes 23 points adrift of the next man

**Round 1, Tour Course.** Gregg Williams began the week the way a Wooden Spoon holder is expected to: a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 7th, another quadruple bogey — an 8 — at the 17th, and a run of dropped shots that stretched from the 2nd all the way to the 14th, including triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th. A birdie at the par-5 15th finally stopped it. He signed for 31 points. Jon Baker, meanwhile, was in front in the Green Jacket gross race from the 5th hole of that same round.

**Round 2, Tour Course.** Baker's tenure at the top lasted 20 holes. A bogey at the 7th on Sunday handed the gross lead to Williams. Baker answered late: a birdie at the 14th began a run of five holes without dropping a gross shot, worth 16 points to the 18th, and by the end of the round he was the Jacket leader again, with Alex Baker heading the Trophy. Williams had his own surge over the same closing stretch — 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, four pars in a row and not a gross shot dropped — and posted 42 points, an 11-point improvement on his opening round.

**Round 3, Stadium Course.** The Jacket changed hands for good early. Williams parred the 2nd and took the outright gross lead. Baker's round came apart at the 4th, where a 9 — quintuple bogey on a par 4 — went down as a blank. By nightfall the boards read Alex Baker in the Trophy, Williams in the Jacket.

**Round 4, Stadium Course.** Williams birdied the par-3 5th, parred the 6th, and gathered nine points across the 8th, 9th and 10th, closing out an 84 that was his best gross score in seven visits to the Stadium — eight shots better than his last time round it.

The two ledgers closed in opposite corners. Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin: the sixth-best gross total in TEG history and his own best. Jon Baker, the defending Trophy champion, finished on 126 Stableford points and collected the Wooden Spoon.
