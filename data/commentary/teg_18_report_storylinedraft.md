# The Baker Reversal: Alex Takes the Trophy, Jon Takes the Spoon

## How Alex Baker, owner of three prior Wooden Spoons, led the Trophy from the fifth hole of round one to the last and won by eight

Alex Baker won the Trophy at TEG 18 in Catalonia with 169 Stableford points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It is the seventh-highest total on record and the second best of his own career.

The points came in bursts. On the Tour Course in round one he took 17 from holes 8 to 12, three-point bogeys either side of pars at the 10th and 11th, then 13 more over holes 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-5 15th. His 98 that day was his best gross of the week on that course, though six shots off his previous best there of 92.

Round two was the opposite. He ran up an 11 at the par-5 18th and an eight at the par-3 16th, both blank holes, and signed for 106 gross. That is a new course-worst at PGA Catalunya Tour, one stroke worse than the 105 that had stood across 22 prior visits. He still led the Trophy at the end of the day.

He led after round three too, helped by 21 points across holes 11 to 16 on the Stadium Course, and he still led after round four, despite a 10 at the 14th and an eight at the 17th.

## David Mullin's week of extremes: an 11 in R1, the lowest gross round the Stadium has ever surrendered in R3, and nine shots gone in four holes in R4

David Mullin's week in Catalonia swung between the best and the worst golf of the trip.

It began badly. On the 15th at the Tour Course in round one — a par 5, stroke index 5 — he ran up an eleven, a sextuple bogey worth no Stableford points and the worst hole against par of his TEG career. It cost him ground in the Wooden Spoon race too: second in that competition before the hole, fifth after it.

Round three was the reverse. At the Stadium Course he shot 84 gross, the lowest round the course had ever surrendered, beating the previous record of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was a personal best there as well. His best in five previous Stadium rounds was 91, and the 84 was eight shots better than his last visit to the course.

Round four then unravelled over four holes. From the 14th to the 17th he went double bogey, triple bogey, double bogey, double bogey — 6, 8, 5, 6 — nine shots dropped in four holes and four holes without a net par, a single Stableford point on three of them and none at the 15th.

He finished runner-up in the Green Jacket, 14 shots behind Gregg Williams, who won at +66.

## John Patterson is bottom of the Wooden Spoon race after four holes of the tournament and finishes as Trophy runner-up, eight points back

John Patterson's tournament was decided at both ends, and the first end came fast. Four holes into round one on the Tour course he was already bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, dropping from fourth to fifth after a quadruple-bogey eight at the 3rd and a triple-bogey seven at the 4th. The bleeding did not stop there. He lost 22 shots to par across holes 3 to 10, and went seven straight holes, the 3rd to the 9th, without a net par.

Then he turned it around. Holes 13 to 16 brought him 13 points, and he finished round one 14 points stronger on the back nine than the front, for 34 points in all. Round two repeated the shape: another quadruple-bogey eight, this time at the 4th, 11 shots dropped from the 6th to the 10th, then 16 points across holes 11 to 15.

The recovery held. In round four he took nine points from holes 4 to 6, and ended a run of bogeys or worse with a birdie at the 17th. Both courses yielded personal bests: 94 on the Tour, beating his previous 96, and 93 on the Stadium, beating his previous 95.

It was not enough. Alex Baker took the Trophy on 169 points, eight clear, with Patterson runner-up.

## Gregg Williams, reigning Wooden Spoon holder, improves in every round and turns a wretched R1 into the Green Jacket by fourteen

Gregg Williams started badly. On the Tour course he ran up a nine at the par-five 7th and an eight at the par-four 17th, both quadruple bogeys, and failed to make a par from the 2nd through the 14th before a birdie at the par-five 15th finally stopped the run. The round came to 31 points.

Round two was a different story: 42 points, eleven better. He took nine points from holes 3 to 5 and thirteen from 13 to 16, four consecutive pars without a gross shot dropped. The par at the 13th drew him level at the head of the Green Jacket, and during that round Jon Baker's 20-hole spell in front ended with Williams overtaking him.

On the Stadium course a par at the 2nd put him outright in the lead. Four pars from the 2nd to the 5th were worth twelve points before a double bogey at the 6th ended the sequence. In round four he birdied the par-three 5th and gathered nine points from the 8th to the 10th, closing to nine off the Trophy lead having at one stage trailed by fifteen. His 84 that day was his best on the Stadium course, eight strokes better than his last visit there.

He finished on +66, fourteen clear of David Mullin: his own best gross total, and the sixth best recorded.

## Jon Baker arrives as defending Trophy and Jacket champion and leaves 23 points adrift at the bottom, last in three of the four rounds

Jon Baker's week in Catalonia ended with the Wooden Spoon, his 126 Stableford points the total nobody wants.

It did not begin that way. He took the Green Jacket lead on the fifth hole of round one and held it for 20 holes, across into round two, before a bogey six at the par-five seventh on the Tour course handed the lead to Gregg Williams.

He answered on the same afternoon. Over the closing five holes he birdied the par-three 14th and parred 15, 16, 17 and 18, banking 16 points and covering the stretch one under par without dropping a gross shot anywhere. It put him back in front: after round two he led the Jacket, with Alex Baker top of the Trophy.

Round three, on the Stadium course, undid the good work. He ran up a nine at the par-four fourth, a quintuple bogey worth no points at all.

The wider numbers tell the same story. This was his seventh visit to the Stadium course, and his best gross round there this week, 97, was eight strokes worse than the 89 he had managed on a previous trip, and eight worse than his last visit. On the Tour course, 87 against a previous best of 84.
