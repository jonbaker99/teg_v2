# Same Surname, Opposite Ends: Alex Baker's 169, Jon Baker's Spoon

## Alex Baker leads from the 5th hole of R1 to the last and wins the Trophy on 169, by 8

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It is the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of Baker's own career.

The foundation was laid on the Tour Course on the Saturday. Baker took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run of three bogeys and two pars that, off his handicap, produced three or four points every time. He added 13 more across holes 14 to 16, the birdie at the par-five 15th worth five on its own.

What followed was less orderly. His second round on the same course came in at 106 gross — a new course worst at PGA Catalunya - Tour, one stroke beyond the previous mark of 105 set across 22 prior visits. An eight at the par-three 16th and an eleven at the par-five 18th, both personal worsts at those pars, accounted for much of it. Yet he still led the Trophy after R2, with Jon Baker heading the Jacket race.

Round three on the Stadium Course steadied things: 21 points between holes 11 and 16, four pars among them. He led after R3 and again after R4, despite a ten at the 14th and an eight at the 17th in the final round.

Neither course yielded a personal best — 98 on the Tour against a prior best of 92, 100 on the Stadium against 93 — but the points column did not care.

## David Mullin's 84: the lowest round ever played at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, and what it bought him

David Mullin's week at Camiral moved between the two extremes the venue allows.

It began badly. On the Tour Course in round one he ran up an 11 at the 15th, a par five, a sextuple bogey worth nothing on the Stableford card. The damage was immediate in the side competitions: that single hole dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race, the bottom of the field.

Two days later, on the Stadium Course, he produced the best round anyone has played there. His 84 gross in round three beat the previous course record of 89, a mark that had survived 27 prior visits. It was also comfortably his own best at the venue: across five previous visits his lowest score had been 91, and the 84 represented an improvement of eight strokes on his last time round.

What it bought him was one day. Back on the Stadium Course for the final round, Mullin lost his way over the closing stretch. From the 14th through the 17th he made double bogey, triple bogey, double bogey and double bogey — nine shots dropped in four holes, and four consecutive holes without a net par, a shortfall of five points against his allowance.

The record stands regardless. Across two visits to the Stadium Course this week, the gap between his best and his finish was the story.

## John Patterson: 22 shots underwater by the 10th hole of the tournament, runner-up by the end of it

**John Patterson**

Patterson's tournament began in the worst possible way. At the 3rd on the Tour Course he ran up an 8, a quadruple bogey, and it did not stop there: the 4th went triple, the 5th and 6th double, then three more triples at the 7th, 8th and 9th before a double at the 10th. Eight holes, 22 shots dropped. Seven of them — the 3rd through the 9th — passed without a single net par, and the triple at the 4th was enough to drop him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, from fourth to fifth.

The rest of the week was a steady climb back. His back nine that afternoon was 14 points better than his front, including 13 points across holes 13 to 16, and he signed for 34 points off a gross round of 30 over par. Round two followed the same pattern in miniature: another quadruple bogey at the 4th, 11 shots lost between the 6th and the 10th, then 16 points in five holes from the 11th. That 94 was his best gross on the Tour Course in five visits, beating his previous mark of 96.

He went lower again on the Stadium, a 93 in round three, four shots better than his last visit there. On the final day he took nine points from holes 4 to 6, and after a triple and a double at the 14th and 15th, he answered with a birdie at the 17th.

## The closing stretch as the tournament's crime scene: holes 14-18, four rounds, both courses

**Holes 14 to 18: where the tournament did its damage**

Whatever happened over the opening thirteen holes at Camiral, the closing stretch usually rewrote it.

It started on the Saturday. David Mullin took eleven at the par-five 15th on the Tour Course, a sextuple bogey and the worst he has made on a par five. Gregg Williams, already thirteen holes into a run without a par, finally stopped the bleeding with a birdie at the same hole — then handed it straight back with an eight at the 17th. Alex Baker arrived at the 17th on the back of a par, a birdie and a par, and left it with a double bogey.

Sunday split the field in two. Jon Baker birdied the 14th and parred in from there, five holes without dropping a gross shot and sixteen Stableford points over the stretch; Williams took thirteen points from 13 through 16. Alex Baker, meanwhile, made eight at the par-three 16th and eleven at the 18th — both his worst returns on those pars.

The Stadium Course on Tuesday finished the job. Alex Baker ran up a ten at the 14th and an eight at the 17th. Mullin went four holes from the 14th without a net par, dropping nine shots. John Patterson went triple, double, bogey — and then, at the 17th, holed out for a birdie, five points and the only clean escape of the afternoon.

## Gregg Williams, the reigning Wooden Spoon holder, wins the Green Jacket at +66 by 14

**Williams takes the Jacket by 14**

Gregg Williams won the Green Jacket with a gross total of +66, finishing 14 clear of runner-up David Mullin — the sixth-best gross aggregate on record, and the best of Williams's own career.

It did not begin that way. His first round on the Tour Course was a struggle: a nine at the par-five 7th, a quadruple-bogey eight at the 17th, and a run of bogeys or worse that stretched from the 2nd to the 14th before a birdie at the 15th finally stopped it. He signed for 31 points.

Round two was a different game. Williams improved by 11 points to 42, gathering nine points across holes 3 to 5 and then 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, four holes played without dropping a gross shot. That surge took the gross lead off Jon Baker at the 7th, ending a run of 20 consecutive holes in front for Baker, and Williams was level at the top by the 13th.

He moved outright ahead at the 2nd hole of round three on the Stadium Course, backing it with 12 points and four flawless gross holes from the 2nd to the 5th. He led the Jacket after both the third and fourth rounds, with Alex Baker ahead in the Trophy. His closing 84 on the Stadium was a course best, eight strokes better than his previous visit and well inside his prior best of 92 there.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 126 — 23 adrift of the next worst

**Jon Baker**

The week did not begin the way it ended. Baker took the Green Jacket lead at the fifth hole of round one and held it for 20 holes, across two rounds, before a bogey at the par-five seventh on the Tour course in round two handed the gross lead to Gregg Williams. He got it back by the close of that round — after 36 holes he led the Jacket, while Alex Baker headed the Trophy — and the recovery was built on a strong finish: 16 Stableford points across holes 14 to 18, opening with a birdie at the short 14th and adding four straight pars, five holes without dropping a gross shot. His 87 on the Tour course was his best of the week there, a stroke better than his last visit, though still three shy of the 84 he has managed at the venue before.

Round three on the Stadium course undid it. A nine at the par-four 4th — quintuple bogey, no points — was the clearest marker of the slide, and by the end of the day Williams held the Jacket lead, which he did not surrender. Baker's best Stadium round of the week was a 97, eight strokes worse than his last visit there and well off his 89 from a previous trip.

He finished the tournament on 126 points, and with that came the Wooden Spoon.
