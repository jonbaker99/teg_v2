# The Great Reversal at Camiral

## Alex Baker, three-time Spoon collector, leads the TEG 18 Trophy from the 5th hole of R1 to the end despite a course-worst 106 and two double-figure holes
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

Alex Baker won the TEG 18 Trophy with 169 Stableford points, eight clear of John Patterson. It stands as the second-highest total of his own career.

The platform was laid in the first round on the PGA Catalunya Tour course, where he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12 — two pars and three bogeys, none worse — and then 13 more across 14 to 16, the birdie at the par-five 15th the pick of them. A double bogey at the 17th ended that run of par or better. His 98 was his best score of the week on the Tour course, though some way off the 92 he posted there in TEG 11.

Round two ran the other way. Alex Baker signed for 106 gross, the highest round ever recorded on the Tour course, beating the previous worst of 105 set across 22 prior visits. A quintuple-bogey eight at the par-three 16th and an eleven at the par-five 18th were his worst returns on those pars. He still led the Trophy at the end of the day, with Jon Baker ahead in the TEG 18 Green Jacket.

He held the lead after round three, built on 21 points from holes 11 to 16 on the Stadium course, and again after round four, despite a ten at the 14th and an eight at the 17th. Gregg Williams led the Jacket over the closing two rounds.

## David Mullin takes 11 at the 15th in R1, then breaks the Stadium course record with 84 in R3, then bleeds it away over the closing stretch of R4
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Mullin's week of extremes at Camiral**

David Mullin's TEG 18 ran from one end of the scale to the other. It began badly on the Tour Course in Round 1, where he ran up an 11 at the par-five 15th, a sextuple bogey that returned no Stableford points and stands as the worst score relative to par of his career. The hole also moved him in the standings nobody wants: he dropped from second to the bottom of the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon race.

Round 3 on the Stadium Course produced the opposite. Mullin went round in 84 gross, a new course record, beating the previous mark of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was also comfortably his own best there — seven shots better than his previous personal best of 91 on the course, and eight fewer than his last visit.

Round 4, back on the Stadium, gave much of it away over the closing stretch. From the 14th he went four holes without a net par: double bogey at the 14th, triple at the par-five 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. That run cost him nine shots against par and yielded three Stableford points from a possible eight. The record stood, but the finish meant his second Stadium round bore little resemblance to his first.

## John Patterson loses the TEG 18 Trophy on the front nine of R1, bleeding 22 shots in eight holes, then spends three rounds proving he was the better player
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Eight holes in Girona**

John Patterson's TEG 18 Trophy was settled before the first round was half done. Starting the third at PGA Catalunya's Tour Course, he ran up an 8 — a quadruple bogey — and did not steady himself for another seven holes. From the 3rd to the 10th he bled 22 shots: triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th, doubles at the 5th, 6th and 10th. Across the 3rd to the 9th he failed to make a single net par. The damage was quick enough to register on the board at once; the triple at the 4th dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon race.

What followed made the loss harder to explain away. Patterson took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th and finished the round 14 points better on the back nine than the front, for 34 points in all. In round two he took 16 points in five holes from the 11th to the 15th, and his 94 there was his best gross score on the Tour Course, beating the 96 he posted in TEG 12. Round three brought a 93 on the Stadium Course, also a personal best there and four strokes better than his last visit. In round four he collected nine points across the 4th, 5th and 6th, and after a triple and a double at the 14th and 15th, stopped the slide with a birdie at the 17th.

## A catalogue of the TEG 18 double-figure and blow-up holes: two elevens, a ten, two nines and a new course-worst gross at the Tour
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

**The wreckage column: TEG 18's double-figure holes**

TEG 18 produced three holes of ten or more strokes, and the PGA Catalunya Tour course accounted for two of them on consecutive days.

David Mullin opened the week with an 11 at the par-five 15th in round one, a sextuple bogey and the worst hole against par of his TEG career. The same round brought a nine from Gregg Williams at the seventh and an eight at the 17th, plus an eight at the third from John Patterson, who added another at the 4th on Sunday.

Round two belonged to Alex Baker. He took eight at the par-three 16th, five over par, and then closed with an 11 at the last — a sextuple bogey and the heaviest single hole of his TEG record. The round totalled 106 gross, a new worst score ever recorded on the Tour course, beating the previous mark of 105 set across 22 prior visits. For scale, Alex Baker's best gross on the course before this week was 92.

The Stadium course was kinder but not clean. Jon Baker ran up a nine at the fourth in round three, and in the final round Alex Baker added a ten at the 14th and an eight at the 17th. Eleven holes in all cost their players a blank Stableford score.

## Gregg Williams, reigning Wooden Spoon holder, recovers from a ruinous R1 to take the TEG 18 Green Jacket by 14 from nine-time winner David Mullin
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Williams turns a wrecked opening round into a 14-shot Green Jacket win**

Gregg Williams won the TEG 18 Green Jacket at +66 gross, 14 clear of David Mullin, and he did it after an opening round that looked like it had ruled him out. On the Tour Course at PGA Catalunya he ran up a nine at the par-five 7th and an eight at the par-four 17th, both quadruple bogeys, and did not make a hole better than bogey until a birdie at the 15th finally broke the run. The round came in at 31 points.

The second round was 42, an eleven-point swing. Williams took nine points from holes 3 to 5, then played 13 to 16 in level par for 13 points. Jon Baker, who had led the gross competition since the 5th hole of round one, lost it at the 7th in round two after 20 holes in front; Williams drew level at the 13th and went outright ahead at the 2nd hole of round three, on the Stadium Course.

From there he mostly held on. Four pars to open round three were followed by a double bogey at the 6th, but he birdied the 5th in the final round and picked up nine points across holes 8 to 10. His 84 in round four was his best score on the Stadium Course, eight better than his previous best there, and +66 is the lowest gross total of his TEG career.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, leads the gross for 20 holes and then finishes the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon 23 points adrift of anyone else
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**Jon Baker: 20 holes in front, then the Spoon**

Jon Baker arrived in Catalonia as the defending Trophy champion and left with the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon, his 126 Stableford points the lowest of the week.

For a while it looked like a different tournament. Jon Baker took the lead in the TEG 18 Green Jacket, the gross competition, at the fifth hole of the opening round on the Tour Course and held it for 20 holes, into the second round. It ended at the par-five seventh on Sunday, where a six handed the lead to Gregg Williams. He answered late: birdie at the 14th and pars at the 15th, 16th, 17th and 18th gave him 16 points in five holes without dropping a gross shot, and his 87 on the Tour Course was one stroke better than his previous visit there. By the close of round two, with Alex Baker leading the TEG 18 Trophy, Jon Baker was back in front in the Jacket.

The Stadium Course undid it. At the fourth in round three he ran up a nine, a quintuple bogey worth no points, and his best gross round on the Stadium all week was 97 — eight strokes more than his last visit to the course. The Jacket lead did not come back, and neither did the Trophy defence.
