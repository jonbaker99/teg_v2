# One Point After Eighteen Holes

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best of the five cards handed in, and it was really two quite different rounds stapled together. He had already held the 2026 TEG Trophy lead once, taken outright with a par at the 4th, before Alex Baker relieved him of it a hole later. Then came the trees. Double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th, a triple at the 10th, which is the hardest hole on the property, and seven shots gone in three holes without one net par among them. One looks away and says nothing.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he gathered himself at once. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th, nine points taken from three holes, with the 12th restoring his outright lead: a brief period of golden rule in which nothing whatever went wrong. Alex Baker nosed back in front at the 17th, but Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good and amounted to a coronation. He finished one clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, with a gross 90 for second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria went to extremes in both directions. The first trouble arrived in three holes from the 5th: a triple bogey at the short hole, a double bogey at the 6th, and then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone, and no defence available for any of them.

Then the reverse, and it was glorious. From the 8th to the 11th he put together a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, the bogey at the 10th alone bringing in three of them on the hardest hole on the course. Four holes in which the man did nothing wrong at all, and the rest of the field could only stand and admire.

The second collapse was the worse of the two. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake, was followed by a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, with a single point salvaged. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th is the hole that explains the course's reputation: 422 metres, stroke index 1, out of bounds the whole way down the left and a lake in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there deserves a standing ovation. In one pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over and not a Stableford point to show for it. David Mullin took eight, likewise for nothing, and it moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven, which was merely the middle of a broader slide, doubles at the 8th and the 9th sitting either side of it and seven shots dropped across three holes.

Between them the three gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. On a course that asks more of this field than most, nobody was greatly astonished.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Saturday round at Quinta da Ria, a course that parts with strokes grudgingly, had Alex Baker holding and then losing the 2026 TEG Trophy inside three holes. He took the lead outright with a par at the 5th. He then mislaid it during a wretched spell on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots in three holes, and the lead passed to Jon Baker. He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th, went back ahead with a par at the 17th, as cool a piece of work as anyone managed all afternoon, and lost it for the last time at the 18th. Jon Baker closed on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87, which is about as close to a formality as this game ever permits. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31, the pair sharing the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
