# One Point After Eighteen Holes

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in the field of five. It arrived in two halves of quite different character. He had already led the 2026 TEG Trophy once, taking it outright with a par at the 4th, whereupon Alex Baker reclaimed it a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in. Double bogeys at the 8th and 9th, and a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots gone in three holes, with not a net par among them. Three holes of poor golf, recorded without further comment.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he steadied at once. Pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th brought him nine points, and the 12th restored his outright lead. That is golf of real quality after such a start. Alex Baker edged back in front at the 17th. Then Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good. He finished one clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, with a gross 90 for second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria went in both directions, and with some force. The first trouble came in three holes from the 5th. A triple bogey at the short hole, then a double bogey at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone. The 7th was not a success.

Matters then improved considerably. From the 8th to the 11th he posted a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points. The bogey at the 10th was worth three of them on the hardest hole on the course. Four holes of thoroughly sound golf, and entirely to his credit.

The second collapse was the worse of the two. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake, was followed by a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, with one point salvaged. There is no kind word available for that stretch. The 14th also took him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He finished with 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. His 31 points left him level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th explains a good deal about the course. It measures 422 metres, carries stroke index 1, has out of bounds the whole way down the left, and sets a lake in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there is worth celebrating. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over, and no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and the damage moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven. That merely capped a wider slide, doubles at the 8th and 9th sitting either side of it, seven shots dropped across three holes.

Between them the trio gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. None of it was especially surprising on a course that asks more of this field than most.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

Quinta da Ria gives up strokes grudgingly. The Saturday round there left Alex Baker holding the 2026 TEG Trophy and then losing it inside three holes. He took the lead outright with a par at the 5th. He then lost it in a wretched spell on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th, and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots gone in three holes, and the lead passed to Jon Baker. It was poor golf at an inconvenient moment. He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th, went back ahead with a par at the 17th, and lost it for the last time at the 18th. Jon Baker closed on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards, finishing on 87. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31. The pair share the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
