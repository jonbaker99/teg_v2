# One Point After Eighteen Holes

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in a field of five, and it arrived in two halves that barely belong to the same round. He had already tasted the 2026 TEG Trophy lead, taking it outright with a par at the 4th before Alex Baker took it straight back a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in around him. Double bogeys at the 8th and 9th, a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, and seven shots handed over in three holes without a single net par to show for it. Bad golf, plainly and unarguably, from the man who would end the day on top.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he steadied at once. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th, nine points in three holes, and the 12th putting his outright lead back where he felt it belonged. Three consecutive pars after that sort of morning is a small golden age, and he played it with the face of a man who had never dropped a shot in his life.

Alex Baker nudged ahead again at the 17th. Jon Baker answered with a par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, and that was the lead taken back for good. A gross 90 gave him second in the Green Jacket behind Gregg Williams, and he finished a point clear of Alex Baker and Williams in the Trophy.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round went in both directions with real enthusiasm. The first damage came in three holes from the 5th: a triple bogey at the short hole, a double at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone, and nothing to be said in anyone's defence.

Then he turned round and played beautifully. From the 8th to the 11th he made a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, three of them arriving at the 10th alone. Four holes of complete composure from a man who, twenty minutes earlier, had been posting a 9.

The second collapse was the worse of the two. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 that doglegs around a lake, then a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, one solitary point salvaged, and an onlooker still trying to make sense of it. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, with 31 points.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 462-yard par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

The 10th explains a good deal about why this course hurts: 462 yards, stroke index 1, out of bounds the whole way down the left and a lake sitting in front of the green from 110 yards out. A par there would be worth talking about for weeks. In one pass, three of the five players could not get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over, nothing for it. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and that was enough to shift him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker got away with a seven, which only looks like an escape when you set it beside what he had already done at the 8th and 9th.

Between them the three gave twelve shots back to par on a single hole. Nobody who had seen the tee shot was surprised.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker spent the day finding the 2026 TEG Trophy lead and then mislaying it. He took it outright with a par at the 5th, a cool piece of work, and held on until the back nine undid him in three holes: that 9 at the 10th, a double bogey at the 11th and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots gone, the lead with them, and Jon Baker in front.

He refused to go quietly. A bogey at the 14th drew him level, a par at the 17th put him back ahead, and then the 18th took it off him for the last time. Jon Baker signed for 39, Alex Baker for 38, Gregg Williams also for 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87, which is the sort of afternoon the rest of them can only envy. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31, the two of them sharing the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight and neither keen to claim it.
