# One Point After Eighteen Holes

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in a field of five, and it arrived in two halves of quite different character. He had already held the 2026 TEG Trophy lead once, taken outright with a par at the 4th, before Alex Baker reclaimed it a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in. Double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th, and a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots in three holes, and not a net par among them. He was not, at that stage, on terms with the course.

From the 11th, where the ground opens out towards the sea, he steadied at once. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th brought nine points, and the 12th restored his outright lead. To go par, par, par directly after such a passage is a considerable feat of composure. Alex Baker edged back in front at the 17th. Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good. He finished one clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, with a gross 90 for second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria went to both extremes. The first trouble arrived across three holes from the 5th. A triple bogey at the short hole, a double bogey at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone.

Then the reverse, and it was genuinely good golf. From the 8th to the 11th he posted a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points. The bogey at the 10th earned three of them on the hardest hole on the course. Four holes of real discipline from a man who had every excuse to abandon the project.

The second collapse was the heavier of the two. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake, was followed by a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, with one point salvaged. That was poor golf and there is no polite way to record it otherwise. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th runs to 422 metres at stroke index 1, with out of bounds the whole way down the left and a lake in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there is worth celebrating. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over and no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and it moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven. That merely capped a wider slide, with doubles at the 8th and the 9th on either side of it, seven shots dropped across the three.

Between them the trio gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. On a course that asks more of this field than most, none of it was especially surprising.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Saturday round at Quinta da Ria, a course that gives up strokes grudgingly, saw Alex Baker both take and lose the 2026 TEG Trophy lead inside three holes. He went ahead outright with a par at the 5th. He then lost it during a wretched passage on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots in three holes, and the lead passed to Jon Baker. He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th and went back in front with a par at the 17th. At the 18th it left him for the last time. Jon Baker closed on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87. At the other end, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31, the pair sharing the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
