# Bom Sucesso Buries The Record Book

## Henry Meller's -15 breaks the TEG round record he had set only one round earlier
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller's round of -15 at Bom Sucesso stands as the best round in TEG history — remarkable in itself, but more so because it beat the record he had set only one round earlier. His gross score of +10 was the second-best gross round recorded in the competition to that point, behind only his own mark from Round 1.

The round was built on a run through holes 3 to 9, where Meller gained nine shots to par, including a birdie on the par-5 7th and pars either side of bogeys at the 4th and the 6th, the hardest hole on the course. He followed that with three holes, 7 through 9, without dropping a gross shot, and later added another push over holes 15-17, picking up three more shots to par.

The damage was felt across both races. Henry Meller had already led the TEG 2 Trophy overnight; his gap stretched from 3 to 11. In the Green Jacket, he seized the lead from Jon Baker, opening a 3-shot advantage.

## David Mullin and Stuart Neumann both produce career- and TEG-best rounds in Meller's shadow
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Round two at Bom Sucesso produced the two best gross rounds in TEG history to that point, and neither belonged to the man leading the race. David Mullin shot a gross 9-over, the best Gross round recorded, built on a stretch of five shots gained to par across holes 6-9 and another four across the front holes 1-4. It also stood as his personal-best round, at -10.

Stuart Neumann went one better in gross terms with a +10, the second-best Gross round in TEG history, and his net -13 ranked second-best of any round in TEG history outright. He piled up six shots to par through holes 8-12, including a birdie at the short 10th, before a double bogey at the 13th snapped his run of pars. He recovered to gain four more coming in, holes 15-17. It was a dramatic reversal from a poor round one score of -3.

Yet for all that, Henry Meller's lead over the field only grew, up to 11 in the TEG Trophy race.

## Alex Baker and Gregg Williams each blow up twice on the way to propping up the leaderboard
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Round 2 at Bom Sucesso brought little relief for Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, both of whom littered their cards with big numbers. Williams was the chief offender, running up a quintuple bogey 9 at the 15th, one of the hardest holes on the course, before adding a quadruple bogey 9 at the 2nd. Baker matched him blow for blow, opening with a quadruple bogey 8 at the 1st and then bleeding eight shots over a three-hole stretch from the 14th to the 16th, with triple bogeys either side of a double bogey.

Neither round did anything to ease their position at the foot of the field. Baker remained the TEG 16 Wooden Spoon's laggard, though the gap to safety had at least closed slightly to three shots after Bom Sucesso. With Henry Meller pulling clear at the top of the TEG 16 Trophy standings, the damage down at the bottom mattered little to the wider race, but for Baker and Williams it was a day to forget.

## The Green Jacket lead changes hands three times before Jon Baker's overnight advantage disappears
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Bom Sucesso the TEG 7 Green Jacket lead shifted three times through the closing stretch. Jon Baker had carried an overnight advantage into round two, but by the 12th hole Henry Meller had drawn level, parring the sixth-hardest hole on the card to match him at the top. Three holes later, at the 15th, David Mullin also parred his way level, pulling into a share of the lead despite the hole ranking as one of the toughest on the course, its second-hardest. The picture shifted again at the 14th, where Baker himself drew back level, leaving all three men tied for the Jacket lead with the round unresolved.

By the finish it was Meller who had pulled clear, taking over the Jacket outright and extending to a three-shot gap, with Baker's overnight lead gone entirely. Meller also held his Trophy lead through the round, though the gap there stretched out to eleven. Baker's level-par 14th proved to be his last say in the Jacket battle.
