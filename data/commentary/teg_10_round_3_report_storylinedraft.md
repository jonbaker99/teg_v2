# The Thirty-Hole Reign Ends at Palmares

## Alex Baker's Stableford personal best and Gregg Williams's near-mirror gross career-best share round-of-the-day honours at Palmares
<!-- storyline: round -->

Round three at Palmares belonged to Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, whose fortunes tracked each other almost hole for hole. Baker carded 47 points for a personal Stableford best, backed by a gross career-best +22, while Williams matched him for entertainment even if not for the record books.

Baker's round swung wildly. He piled up ten points across holes 2-4 to draw level for the TEG 3 Trophy lead, then took it outright at the 6th. A blow-up followed: a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 13th, one of the harder holes on the course, threatened to derail things, but he answered with nine points across holes 9-11 and levelled the lead again at the 15th.

Williams shadowed him throughout, drawing level at the 4th, then seizing the lead himself at the 13th on the back of his own nine-point burst through holes 11-13. A rough patch of bogeys from the 13th to the 16th threatened to undo that work, but a birdie at the 17th arrested the slide, capping a round that pushed Baker's lead in the Trophy from five strokes down to level with David Mullin.

## John Patterson's horror stretch through holes 11-15 buries his TEG 10 Wooden Spoon hopes deeper still
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's round began promisingly enough, with three points apiece at the 1st, 2nd and 3rd taking him to 9 points through as many holes. It did not last. A quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 8th was an early warning, and the real damage came from the 11th to the 15th, where he shed 19 shots without registering a single stableford point across the five holes.

The stretch included a triple bogey at the 11th, one of the course's harder holes, before a quintuple bogey 8 at the 12th — a hole ranked as the easiest on the layout. A triple bogey at the 13th and a quadruple bogey at the 14th followed, and the sequence closed with a quadruple-bogey 9 at the 15th.

The collapse pushed John Patterson deeper into last place in the TEG 10 Wooden Spoon race, his gap to the next-worst player stretching from 5 shots before the round to 14 afterwards, with his position unchanged as the field's clear laggard.

## David Mullin's 30-hole grip on the TEG 10 Trophy lead ends on the 6th, and the race resets to a share at the top
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin had led the TEG 10 Trophy since the 12th hole of Round 1, a run of 30 holes that carried him through to the 6th hole of Round 3 at Palmares. There, with a routine par, his advantage simply ran out: Alex Baker had closed in behind him, and the two were level.

Mullin responded immediately, drawing back level for the lead at the 7th, but the round had already turned against the run of form that built his cushion. After a strong Round 2, he slipped to a poor Round 3, dropping 11 points on the previous round's tally. There was still a flourish in it — birdies at the 15th and 17th either side of a par at the 16th brought him 11 points in three holes without a dropped shot — but a quadruple bogey at the 18th undid the good work at the death.

By the end of the round the Trophy race was tied at the top between Mullin and Baker, with Mullin still leading the Green Jacket.
