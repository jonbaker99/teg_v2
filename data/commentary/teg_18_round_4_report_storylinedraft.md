# Held On, Hauled In

## Alex Baker wins the TEG 18 Trophy by eight from John Patterson while signing for 36 over par, a ten and an eight, and finishing last of five in the Green Jacket
<!-- storyline: race -->

**Alex Baker keeps the Trophy lead while posting the day's worst gross**

Alex Baker came off PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course on Tuesday still at the top of the TEG 18 Trophy, eight points clear of John Patterson, having signed for 36 over par — the worst gross round in a field of five.

The damage was spread across the back nine and the turn. Alex Baker bled seven shots in three holes from the 9th, with a triple bogey on the hardest hole on the course followed by doubles at the 10th and 11th. Worse came at the 14th, where a sextuple bogey 10 produced no Stableford points, and again at the 17th, where a quadruple bogey 8 did the same.

Patterson, meanwhile, put together the round's most productive sequence, taking nine points from holes 4 to 6 with two bogeys and a par. That was enough to cut the Trophy gap from twelve points to eight, but not enough to change the order at the top.

The gross scoring told a different story. Alex Baker began the week second in the TEG 18 Green Jacket after the opening round; he ends this one fifth and last. Gregg Williams remains the Jacket leader, and his advantage has grown from eight shots to fourteen.

## Jon Baker's 29-point closing round — five consecutive double bogeys from the 7th to the 11th — completes a Wooden Spoon taken by 23 shots
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Jon Baker's closing 29 seals the 2025 Wooden Spoon by 23**

Jon Baker finished the week at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course with 29 points, the lowest score in the five-man field on Tuesday, and it confirmed a Wooden Spoon he had already led going into the round. His margin over the next man back grew from 14 to 23 across the day.

The damage came in one unbroken sequence. Having gone double bogey, bogey, double bogey, bogey, bogey through holes 2 to 6, he then made five double bogeys in a row from the 7th to the 11th — ten shots dropped in five holes, one Stableford point apiece. The stretch took in the 9th, the hardest hole on the course, but also the 7th and the 11th, two of the easiest, which is the part that stings. A bogey at the 12th kept the run going: eleven consecutive holes without a par.

The birdie at the 13th ended it, his only hole under par in that span and worth four points on its own.

There was a modest consolation buried in the card. His 97 was his best gross of the week on the Stadium Course and three shots better than his last visit here, though still eight adrift of the 89 he posted on a previous trip.

## The Stadium's closing stretch, the 14th to the 17th, as the round's real protagonist — David Mullin nine shots gone in four holes, John Patterson a triple then a double then a rescuing birdie
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**The Stadium bit back over the last four**

The closing stretch at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course did the damage in Round 4. David Mullin arrived at the 14th and did not make another net par all day. A double bogey at the 14th, one of the hardest holes on the course, was followed by an 8 at the par-5 15th — the easiest hole on the card by stroke index, and the only one of the four where he failed to register a Stableford point. Doubles at the 16th and 17th completed the set: nine shots dropped in four holes, three points banked where a par run would have brought eight.

John Patterson's version of the same stretch started worse and ended better. He took 7 at the 14th and 7 again at the 15th, a triple and a double, and by then had been making bogey or worse for some time. A bogey at the 16th steadied things without stopping the slide. Then he holed out for a birdie 3 at the 17th — five points, the streak broken, and the one genuinely good number either man produced over the run-in.

Neither player left Catalunya with a course best: Mullin's 84 and Patterson's 93 here both came in Round 3, matching rather than beating what they had managed on previous visits.

## Gregg Williams's 44-point, 84-gross closing round at the Stadium — the best round of the day by two points and the card that sealed the TEG 18 Green Jacket by 14
<!-- storyline: round -->

**Williams's 84 is a Stadium best, and the Jacket lead doubles**

Gregg Williams closed TEG 18 with 44 points and 84 gross at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course, the best round anyone in the five-man field produced on the day. The 84 was also his lowest gross score in eight visits to the Stadium, one better than the 85 he had carried as his course best.

The round was built on steadiness rather than fireworks. A birdie at the par-3 5th and a par at the 6th took him through two holes without dropping a gross shot, and the middle of the card was better still: nine points from the 8th, 9th and 10th, three apiece, with the bogey at the 9th — the hardest hole on the course — worth as much as the pars either side of it.

The effect on the two races ran in different directions. Williams had led the TEG 18 Green Jacket going into the round and left with the margin widened from eight strokes to 14. In the TEG 18 Trophy, where Alex Baker held the lead before the round and still held it afterwards, Williams's 44 points cut a deficit that had reached 15 points at its widest down to nine — progress that came too late to matter, but progress all the same.
