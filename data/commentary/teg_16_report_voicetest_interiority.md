# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total recorded and the second-best of his own career. Neither of those numbers appeared to detain him. He had the thing itself.

The margin was built early and then, more importantly, not handed back. He led after round two at Troia. He led after round three at Penha Longa. He was still leading when Estoril had finished with him. Williams led the Jacket standings at each of those same checkpoints, one man counting handicap-adjusted points and the other counting raw strokes, and for three days neither conceded a yard in his own competition.

The final round was not flawless. At Estoril's 8th, a par four and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann took nine. A quintuple bogey. It returned no points at all, which is to say the hole was played, walked, recorded and rendered entirely notional. With the cushion he had assembled it cost him nothing beyond tidiness. A man that far clear is permitted one act of magnificent indifference to his own scorecard, and he elected to take it at the hardest hole on the course, in the round that decided the tournament.

Estoril otherwise gave him his best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and he saved it for the day the Trophy was settled. Somewhere along that back nine the prospect of losing quietly ceased to be available to him.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin's tournament ended a very long way from where it began. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he ran up a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. Fifth, in that particular contest, is the bottom of the pile.

Round three at Penha Longa was worse in places. A quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, and then pars at 17 and 18 delivered with the composure of a man tidying a room he has just set fire to. The round came to 30 points. His 98 was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was a different matter entirely. He birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through bogey, bogey, double at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th stopped the slide. The back nine simply held. Nine points from the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to close.

The total was 45 points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. Fourteen. The man who had been last in the Spoon race on the opening afternoon spent the closing round rewriting the course's books, and did it without any apparent sense of occasion.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to develop. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th, and signed for 113 gross, a new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits. At Troia the following day the damage was more concentrated: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples followed by two doubles, a collapse conducted with real administrative efficiency.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers found the same piece of ground. Jon Baker made a double-bogey six at the 11th, then ran up a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth nothing, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, and three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the trip in which he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his prior best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes doing something similar, though on a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, then a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots across four holes, and, like his brother, a three-hole run without a net par from the 12th onward. Whatever the 12th at Penha Longa was asking of the family, neither of them had it.

The final round at Estoril separated them again. Jon's one significant lapse came at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card, where he made an eight. Alex's round was constructed almost entirely from such lapses: nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled over the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every single one a double or worse. Then came the 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst score he has ever recorded on a par four in his life. The round totalled 107 gross, a second course-worst of the trip, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. He finished on 127 points and took the Wooden Spoon. Two all-time course records, both of them the wrong way round, and a personal worst on a par four. Few men leave a place having altered its history quite so thoroughly.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never seriously in question, and the final margin, 16 shots clear of David Mullin, states the case adequately. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

What it was not was tidy. Troia, a course none of the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his least dignified passages: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six gross shots surrendered without ceremony.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered immediately with a birdie at the short 5th, which was a fine piece of work, and which he then dismantled with a bogey, a triple and a double through the 6th, 7th and 8th. He steadied on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and collecting nine points across the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit here.

Estoril produced both his best round and his loudest mistake. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit, and it came despite an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that ended a run of three straight pars. He won the Jacket by 16 shots. He did it with an eight on the card in his finest round of the four days, which, in this company, is roughly what winning looks like.
