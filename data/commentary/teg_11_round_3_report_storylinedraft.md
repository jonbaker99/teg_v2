# Leads Borrowed and Lost at El Prat

## John Patterson's 39 points at El Prat, the best round of the day and the third-best of his eleven TEG rounds, a nine at the sixteenth included
<!-- storyline: round -->

John Patterson produced the best round of the day at El Prat - Azul, a 39-point Trophy score that stands as the third-best of his eleven TEG rounds. It came despite a horror show at the 16th, one of the tougher holes on the course by stroke index, where he ran up a quintuple bogey nine for no points at all.

The rest of the round told a different story. Patterson strung together three bogeys in a row from the 8th, 9th and 10th, including one on the hardest hole on the course at the 8th, and still banked nine points across that stretch — proof of how generous the Stableford format could be to steady bogey golf.

The round arrived too late to lift him off the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, where he had started the day as the outright laggard; by day's end David Mullin had taken over that unwanted distinction. It did nothing to disturb the Trophy race either, where Alex Baker moved ahead of Jon Baker at the top.

## David Mullin holds the TEG 11 Green Jacket lead from the fifth green to the sixth tee, then falls to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin's brief spell atop the TEG 11 Green Jacket (Gross) lasted barely one hole. He drew level with Jon Baker at the par-4 3rd, taking outright control soon after, only to hand it straight back when Baker bogeyed the par-3 6th and still came out ahead — a swap that ended Baker's 37-hole hold on the lead stretching back to the first round.

Mullin's own round unravelled from there. He had gone through the 2nd and 3rd without dropping a shot, but a quadruple bogey 7 at the par-3 6th started a slide that turned into a five-hole stretch from the 11th to the 15th without a single net par, including triple bogeys at both the 11th and the 13th. A bogey at the 12th, one of the easiest holes on the course, dropped him from fourth to bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, taking over from John Patterson as the round's new laggard. What began as a genuine tilt at the Jacket ended as the day's worst turnaround.

## Jon Baker loses a 45-hole TEG 11 Trophy lead to Alex Baker at the thirteenth, the two-point margin surviving intact with only the names reversed
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker had held the lead in the TEG 11 Trophy since round one, hole four — 45 holes of unbroken possession — but it went at the 13th on Saturday at El Prat's Azul course, and it went to his brother. Alex Baker had already clawed level for the lead at the very first hole of the round, only for Jon to restore his advantage. Then, between the 11th and 13th, the round turned. Jon dropped seven shots in three holes, a double bogey, another double, and a triple on the 13th, without a single net par to show for it. Alex did the opposite over almost the same stretch, three pars in a row from the 12th through the 14th for nine clean Stableford points, and took outright possession of the lead on that 13th green. The margin at the top of the Trophy did not move — it stayed at two points — but the two names had swapped places, Alex now in front and Jon the man chasing.
