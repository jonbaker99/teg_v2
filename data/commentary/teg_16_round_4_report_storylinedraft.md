# Records Fall Both Ways at Estoril

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy, sealed under pressure
<!-- storyline: race -->

Stuart Neumann closed out the TEG 17 Trophy at Estoril Golf Club, his first, having led throughout the final round and extended his overall advantage from 12 to 13 as he crossed the line. Gregg Williams took the round with the Green Jacket lead, but the Trophy was Neumann's alone to defend.

It was not comfortable. Between the 7th and 9th he bled nine shots, the low point a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 8th — the hardest hole on the course — sandwiched between two double bogeys. Anywhere else in the round that stretch might have cost him the title.

It didn't, because he had built a cushion earlier. Holes 4 to 6 brought nine Stableford points, bogey-par-bogey, enough padding to absorb the damage that followed.

The round itself was a personal landmark at Estoril: his best score there this TEG, 92, a full 14 strokes better than the 106 he had shot on his previous visit — the platform from which the Trophy was won.

## Gregg Williams retains and extends the Green Jacket
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams retained his lead in the 2023 Green Jacket race and stretched it further, extending his advantage from 14 shots to 16 with a round at Estoril that carried both his best moments of the week and his worst. He shot a course personal best of 80, six strokes better than his previous visit to the club.

The round built through two hot patches, picking up nine stableford points across holes 2-4 and again across holes 6-8, the latter stretch played without dropping a gross shot on holes that included the course's hardest hole, the 8th. That steadiness carried him to the turn before a double bogey at the 9th broke the run. The back nine brought the round's low point, a quadruple bogey 8 at the 14th, before he settled again with pars at 15 and 16 to close out the day. The Green Jacket lead was never troubled by the fluctuations, with Williams remaining out front throughout.

## Alex Baker's Estoril meltdown confirms a third Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Alex Baker's round four at Estoril Golf Club was a collapse from start to finish. It began with a quintuple bogey nine at the 1st and never settled, building through quadruple bogeys at the 3rd and 6th before the round's low point at the 12th, where a septuple bogey 11 stood as his worst hole against par of the day. In between, a six-hole stretch from the 5th to the 10th bled 14 shots, three of them without a single net par.

The gross total of 107 was a new Estoril course record for the worst score recorded at the venue, beating the previous mark of 106 set across six prior visits, and eight shots worse than Baker's own effort there last time out.

The damage confirmed what had long looked inevitable: a third Wooden Spoon for Baker, whose gap to safety, already three shots before the round, stretched further to four.

## David Mullin's course-record 80 at Estoril
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin set a new course record at Estoril Golf Club in the 2023 TEG, carding a gross 80 in round 4 to beat the previous mark of 86, which had stood across the group's six prior visits there. It was only Mullin's second time at Estoril, and the round improved his own prior best on the course, 94, by 14 shots.

The round did not start smoothly: bogeys at the 4th and 5th were followed by a double bogey at the par-5 6th, one of the harder holes on the course, before Mullin halted the slide with a birdie at the 7th. From there the round steadily gathered points, with a nine-stableford-point burst across holes 11 to 13, and stretches without a dropped gross shot at 12-13, 16-17, and 2-3, the last including a birdie. It amounted to a sharp turnaround from his previous round: a swing of 15 stableford points, up from a poor 30 in round 3 to 45 in round 4.
