## David Mullin and Stuart Neumann both card 82 gross to break the Boavista course record, with Neumann's net -9 standing as the best round in TEG history to date
<!-- storyline: round -->

The course record at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort was 83. It had survived 40 prior visits. On the second round it was broken twice in the same afternoon, by David Mullin and Stuart Neumann, both of whom signed for 82 gross. Records tend to fall to one man. This one was dismantled by committee.

Neumann's round was worth -9 net. That is the best round in TEG history to date, a sentence he will be repeating for years and nobody can stop him. His +11 gross matched Mullin's +11, jointly the best gross round ever recorded here. Let us be clear about what that means. The two finest rounds of golf in the history of this competition were both eleven over par.

For Mullin it was a personal best, his -4 net eclipsing anything he had managed before. Both men also set course personal bests. Mullin had never beaten 83 at Boavista across eight prior visits. Neumann's previous best there was 89, making this an improvement of 15 strokes on his last appearance. He had clearly been saving himself.

Mullin stayed top of both the 2012 Trophy and the 2012 Green Jacket standings, extending his leads to three and fourteen shots respectively. At the foot of the table, Henry Meller relieved Neumann of last place in the 2012 Wooden Spoon race. He now trails by 15.

## Henry Meller's three-hole collapse from the 14th to the 16th hands him the Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Henry Meller's round 2 at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort was undone in one stretch of three holes. Across the 14th, 15th and 16th he bled 12 shots. Not one net par among them.

It opened with a quadruple-bogey 7 on the par-3 14th. A par 3 is a hole designed to be over quickly. He continued with a triple bogey on the par-4 15th, stroke index 1, the hardest hole on the course and therefore the least surprising item on the charge sheet. Then came the quintuple-bogey 10 on the par-5 16th, his worst hole of the day. At the 18th he added a further quadruple bogey. Nobody had asked him to.

The curious detail is that his back nine still came in 12 shots better than his front. The outward holes, one concludes, had been grim in their own right. His gross 30 over par was the worst score anyone in the field posted that day. It lifted Stuart Neumann clean out of the Wooden Spoon cellar and installed Meller there instead, last by 15 shots. The collapse was sudden. It was also complete.

## Gregg Williams' career-best round and Stuart Neumann's leap from last both vault past a level-par Jon Baker in the Trophy chase, while Henry Meller's collapse flips the Wooden Spoon race entirely
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Boavista in TEG 9's second round, Gregg Williams posted the best round of his career, finishing six-under-par net. It was enough to leapfrog Jon Baker. Baker had gone round level par, which is a perfectly respectable thing to do and, on this particular day, entirely beside the point.

Neumann matched the move stroke for stroke. A poor first-round 6-over became a nine-under second round, a swing of 15 shots. It also carried him clear of the Wooden Spoon cellar he had occupied overnight. He vacated the premises without leaving a note.

The tenancy passed to Henry Meller. A double bogey on the par-5 3rd sent him from fourth to last in the TEG 9 Wooden Spoon standings, flipping the race entirely. David Mullin, meanwhile, retained his TEG 9 Trophy lead. The gap to the chasing pack widened from one shot to three. He did it with a card that was eleven over par. Round here, that is what command looks like.
