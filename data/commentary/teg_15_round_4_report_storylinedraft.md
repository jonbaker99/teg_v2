# Gregg Williams Sweeps Trophy and Jacket

## Gregg Williams closes out TEG 15 with victories in both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, completing the tournament's sixth double.
<!-- storyline: race -->

Gregg Williams closed out TEG 15 by winning both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, sealing the tournament's sixth double — a feat previously achieved only at TEG 2, TEG 4, TEG 11, TEG 13 and TEG 14.

The final round at Oitavos Dunes did the job without needing to be spectacular. Williams led both races going in and led both coming out, stretching the Trophy gap from 18 to 21 and the Jacket gap from 17 to 29. The best of his day came in a three-hole burst from the 13th to the 15th, where three pars — the last on the easiest hole on the course — brought him 9 points without a dropped gross shot. His round score of 91 also stood as his best at Oitavos Dunes in two visits, two shots inside the mark he had set there at TEG 8.

The finish came despite a marked fall-off in form: having carded 44 stableford points in round three, Williams managed only 34 in round four, a ten-point swing. It mattered not at all — the cushion built earlier in the week was more than enough to see out both titles.

## David Mullin's final round produces his worst Stableford and worst gross scores in the same afternoon, capped by a quadruple bogey at the last.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin's final round at Oitavos Dunes produced his worst Stableford score and his worst gross score of the TEG in the same 18 holes. He returned 22 points, well down on the 40 he had posted the previous round, and carded a gross 33 over par.

The damage came in two spells. He opened with a quadruple bogey 8 at the 1st, followed by a triple bogey at the 2nd and a double bogey at the 3rd, dropping nine shots in the first three holes without registering a single net par. He steadied through the middle of the round before falling apart again at the finish, going bogey, double bogey, double bogey and then another quadruple bogey 8 at the 18th — a hole ranked the second-hardest on the course — to close out four holes without a net par and eight shots dropped from the 16th home. It was, by some distance, the worst round of his TEG.

## John Patterson opens with a sextuple bogey at the 1st, bleeds ten shots across the first three holes, and never finds a rhythm.
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's fourth round at Oitavos Dunes unravelled before it began. He opened with a sextuple bogey 10 on the par-4 1st, one of the harder holes on the course, then added a double bogey at the 2nd and another at the par-3 3rd — ten shots gone across the first three holes without a single net par among them.

There was no recovery. Between the 9th and 13th he dropped a further twelve shots, including a quadruple bogey 7 on the par-3 12th. Two holes later, at the par-4 17th, he ran up another quadruple bogey, this one for an 8. The only stretch of calm came at the 15th and 16th, where back-to-back pars briefly stopped the bleeding, but by then the damage was done. It was Patterson's first visit to Oitavos Dunes, and this round left him with a gross of 105 to show for it.

## Stuart Neumann posts the best Stableford round of the day, then contrives a quintuple bogey at the final hole to spoil it.
<!-- storyline: round -->

Stuart Neumann put together the best Stableford card of the day at Oitavos Dunes, matching the field's top score of 36 points — better than anyone else in the group of six could manage on Tuesday's links round. It was the kind of card that should have stood as the highlight of round four.

Instead, he found a way to undercut it at the very last hole. The 18th, a par four ranked the second-hardest on the course, turned into a disaster: Neumann needed nine shots to get home, a quintuple bogey that returned zero points and dragged his card back down to earth just as he was closing it out.

The damage told in the bigger picture too. Neumann's best gross score at Oitavos Dunes this TEG, 99, finished four strokes worse than the 95 he carded on his only previous visit to the course — a course-specific step backwards capped, fittingly, by the collapse at the last.
