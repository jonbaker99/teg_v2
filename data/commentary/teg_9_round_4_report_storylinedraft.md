# Praia D'El Rey Settles All Three Prizes

## Alex Baker's brief level lead for the TEG 9 Trophy, undone by a back-nine collapse
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker's round at Praia D'El Rey in the TEG 9 Trophy swung from disaster to reward and back again. Early trouble on the front nine brought a triple bogey at the 4th and a quadruple bogey at the 6th, sandwiched by bogeys at the 5th and 7th, before a birdie at the 8th stopped the bleeding. The recovery held long enough for a double bogey at the 14th to draw him level for the outright Trophy lead. It did not last. Baker immediately came apart, dropping 11 shots across the closing stretch from the 14th to the 17th, including a second quadruple bogey at the 15th, one of the hardest holes on the course, and a double bogey at the brutal, index-one 17th. Even so, the round produced personal bests of 44 Stableford points and a gross 27-over par. It was not enough to hold the lead: John Patterson finished the day out front for the Trophy, with the gap widening to six, while David Mullin remained ahead for the Green Jacket.

## David Mullin's wire-to-wire TEG 9 Green Jacket, sealed with the best gross round of the day
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin's TEG 9 Green Jacket was a wire-to-wire triumph, and he sealed it in style with the best gross round of the day at Praia D'El Rey. The final round did not start smoothly: a quadruple bogey 8 at the 1st undid any hope of a clean card. But Mullin steadied himself, running off three straight pars from the 2nd through the 4th before a double bogey at the 5th — one of the hardest holes on the course — snapped that run. He responded with another bogey-free stretch from the 7th through the 9th, picking up nine stableford points in the process. That recovery was enough to produce the strongest gross score in the field for the round. It capped a Jacket race he had led throughout, and he used the day to stretch his advantage from seven strokes to ten. John Patterson, meanwhile, held his own lead in the TEG 9 Trophy, though his cushion had shrunk to nothing by the finish.

## Stuart Neumann's rollercoaster final round confirms the TEG 9 Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Stuart Neumann's final round at Praia D'El Rey summed up his TEG 9: flashes of real quality wrapped around the collapses that confirmed him the Wooden Spoon. He opened with a rough stretch, bleeding nine shots over holes 2-5, including a triple bogey on the 18th-stroke-index 3rd, and going three straight holes without a net par. The middle of his round told a different story entirely: birdie at the 13th sparked a run of 16 points from holes 11-15, backed by four holes without dropping a gross shot from 11-14, his best form of the week. But it didn't last. A quadruple bogey at the 16th, one of the harder holes on the course, tipped him into another slide, and he bled eight shots over the closing three holes to finish holes 16-18 in double bogey, double bogey, quadruple bogey fashion. The Wooden Spoon gap to the next player widened to 16, with Neumann never threatening to escape last place all week.

## John Patterson's closing 47 stableford points, the best round of the final day
<!-- storyline: round -->

John Patterson closed the week with 47 stableford points at Praia D'El Rey, the best round of the final day and a huge swing from his third-round 34, an improvement of 13 points. It came despite a nightmare at the 5th, one of the hardest holes on the course, where Patterson ran up a quintuple-bogey 9. He recovered to string together two stretches without dropping a gross shot, at the 6th and 7th and again at the 12th and 13th, the latter including a birdie on the par-5 12th.

The round extended his overall lead in the TEG Trophy, which he had held before and after the round, with his advantage growing from 3 to 6. Elsewhere in the same round Gregg Williams took a sextuple-bogey 11 at the 7th, his worst hole of the tournament, a reminder of how difficult the course could bite even as Patterson prospered on it.
