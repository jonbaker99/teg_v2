# The Stadium Rearranges Everything

## John Patterson's 45 points at the Stadium Course, the third-best round of his TEG career and a personal course best, built around two double-figure disasters
<!-- storyline: round -->

At the Stadium Course in Girona, John Patterson carded 45 points in round three of the 2019 TEG Trophy, the best score of the day and a personal best on the course, improving his previous best gross there of 99 down to 95. The round was built around two big blow-ups: a quintuple-bogey 10 on the par-5 3rd, and a quadruple-bogey 9 on the par-5 7th, one of the easiest holes on the course. Despite those disasters, Patterson drew level for the Trophy lead with a bogey at the 1st and took it outright with another bogey at the 4th, then piled up 10 points across holes 4 to 6. After further bogeys at the 13th and 14th, he stopped the bleeding with a birdie on the par-5 15th, part of a stretch that yielded 19 points across holes 12 to 17. The round carried him from one point behind Gregg Williams overnight to an eight-point lead at the top of the Trophy.

## Alex Baker's day at the bottom of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race, wrecked twice over at either end of the round yet still five shots better than his last visit here
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's third round at PGA Catalunya - Stadium was wrecked at both ends. He opened with a quadruple bogey 8 at the 2nd, one of the easier holes at stroke index 5, and never recovered from it, bleeding nine shots over the next three holes without a single net par across the 2nd, 3rd and 4th. He then closed the same way, dropping nine shots over the last four holes, a double bogey at the 15th, another at the 16th, a third at the 17th, and a triple bogey at the 18th, one of the hardest holes on the course, without a net par across any of them.

The damage cost him at both the 11th and the 17th, each dropping him to the bottom of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race, though he stayed there regardless, with the gap to the next-worst player narrowing from four shots to two. There was a crumb of comfort: his 101 gross was five shots better than his last visit here, even if it fell short of his 100 best on this course.

## Stuart Neumann and David Mullin both surrender their rounds on the inward half while Patterson is collecting points on the same holes
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

On the inward stretch at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, Stuart Neumann and David Mullin both lost control of their rounds just as John Patterson was pulling away at the top of the 2019 TEG Trophy.

Neumann's collapse ran from the 14th to the 17th, where he shed 10 shots in four holes: a double bogey at the 14th, a quadruple bogey 9 at the par-5 15th — the easiest hole on the course — and further doubles at the 16th and 17th.

Mullin's day fell apart after a strong second round of 40 points; his third-round score dropped to 30, a ten-point swing. A double bogey at the 9th and a bogey at the 14th, both hurting his Wooden Spoon standing, left him last of the six players in the field.

Meanwhile Patterson moved into the Trophy lead, turning a one-shot deficit into an eight-shot advantage over Gregg Williams, who had led going into the round.

## Gregg Williams leads the TEG 12 Trophy and shares the TEG 12 Green Jacket lead at the start of the day and finishes holding neither, undone by twelve gross shots between the 11th and the 13th
<!-- storyline: race -->

Gregg Williams began the third round of the 2019 TEG 12 at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course sharing the lead in both the TEG 12 Trophy and the TEG 12 Green Jacket, and briefly tightened his grip further — a par at the 3rd took him outright ahead in the Trophy, while a bogey at the 2nd had already pulled him level for the Jacket lead. He drew level again at the 9th to share the Trophy top spot, this time with a bogey on the 1st-stroke-index hole.

Then the round turned. Between the 11th and the 13th he shed twelve gross shots without a net par, capped by an 11 at the par-4 13th, his worst hole against par of the tournament. He steadied with pars at the 14th and 15th, but the damage was done. Jon Baker had already reclaimed the Jacket lead at the 4th, and by day's end John Patterson led the Trophy by eight while Baker held the Jacket by five. Williams left the day holding neither.
