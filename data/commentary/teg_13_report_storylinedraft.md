# Thirty-Four Pars and a Hatful of Nines

## Jon Baker leads the TEG 13 Trophy after every round, loses the lead only once at Prince's and takes it straight back, winning by six
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

Jon Baker took the lead in the TEG 13 Trophy at the second hole of the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports and, apart from one brief interruption, never gave it up. He finished on 158 points, six clear of Gregg Williams.

The interruption came late on the second day. Baker had held the Stableford lead for 32 holes across two rounds when a par at the 16th at Prince's Dunes/Himalayas was not enough to keep it, and Gregg Williams moved in front. Baker had it back almost immediately.

Round three at Littlestone, the third of four links courses in Kent, was where he settled the matter. He went from the 8th to the 11th without dropping a gross shot — par, birdie at the short 9th, par at the stroke-index-one 10th, par — and collected 13 points over the four holes. His gross round of 79, eight over par, is the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to that point. Gregg Williams answered with 15 points from the 6th to the 10th and his own best gross of the week, 86, and the pair finished the day level at the top of the Trophy, with Baker also leading the TEG 13 Green Jacket.

Across the four rounds Baker recorded 34 pars or better, an all-time TEG record.

## John Patterson, defending Trophy champion, produces the third-best round in TEG history at Littlestone and surrounds it with eights and nines
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's TEG 13 was a study in extremes across four Kent links, all four of them new to him.

It began badly. At Royal Cinque Ports he opened with an 8 at the 1st, added a 9 at the 7th, another 8 at the 10th and a 9 at the par-5 16th, the last of which dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 108. Friday at Prince's Dunes/Himalayas was no better: 11 shots bled over the first four holes, another eight lost between the 13th and 15th, 8s at the 1st and the 17th, 105 on the card and 28 points.

Then Littlestone. Patterson went round in 83, a personal-best gross round at +12, and took 49 Stableford points — the third-best round in TEG history to date. He gathered 13 points in four holes from the 8th, including a net eagle at the stroke-index-one 10th, and still had room for a 7 at the par-3 17th. The 21-point jump from Saturday's round to Friday's was the swing of the week.

The final round at Prince's Shore/Dunes settled at 36 points, 13 fewer. It contained both his best and worst single holes of the tournament: an eagle three at the par-5 2nd — the only eagle recorded by anyone in TEG 13, and the first eagle in TEG history — and a 9 at the par-5 15th.

## Alex Baker bottoms out in the Spoon race at Littlestone and answers with a personal-best gross round at Prince's on the final day
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Alex Baker's week in Kent turned on its final two days. Littlestone, on the Saturday, was where it came apart. He opened the round with a double bogey, then a quadruple-bogey eight at the 2nd and another double at the 3rd — eight gross shots gone in three holes. The bleeding resumed at the turn: a triple bogey at the 8th, a double at the par-three 9th and another double at the 10th cost seven more. The five at the 9th was the decisive one; it dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon race, last of the five, having started the hole a place clear. He signed for 32 points and a gross 100.

It was not an isolated collapse. He had run up a quadruple-bogey eight at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports in round one, going three holes from the 10th without a net par, and at Prince's on the Friday he closed with five straight double bogeys, ten shots dropped over the last five holes.

The answer came on the Sunday at Prince's Shore/Dunes. Baker went out in pars at the 1st and 2nd, paired pars again at the 9th and 10th, and came home in +17 gross — a personal best for a single round. His 44 points were a 12-point improvement on Littlestone.

## Jon Baker wins the TEG 13 Green Jacket at +50 by eighteen shots, leading gross from R1 to R4 with no lead change at all
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Baker's Kent procession**

Jon Baker won the TEG 13 Green Jacket with a gross total of +50, finishing eighteen shots clear of Gregg Williams. It is the lowest gross aggregate recorded in TEG history, and comfortably the best of Baker's own career.

The margin was built on consistency across four links courses, all of them new to the field on this first visit to Kent. Baker opened with 85 at Royal Cinque Ports, added 88 over Prince's Dunes/Himalayas, and closed with 85 on the Shore/Dunes combination. The round that separated him came on the Saturday at Littlestone, where he went round in 79 — eight over par, and the second-best gross round anyone has produced in a TEG. By the end of that third day he led the Jacket outright, and he also headed the TEG 13 Trophy on Stableford, level at the top with nothing between him and the chasing player.

The underlying number is the one that explains the eighteen shots. Baker made 34 pars or better across the four rounds, more than any player has managed in a single TEG. That is an average of better than eight clean holes a round on unfamiliar links, and it left his rivals with no route back into the gross contest at any stage of the week.

## David Mullin, eight-time Jacket winner and reigning Spoon holder, takes a third consecutive TEG 13 Wooden Spoon by being unwaveringly mediocre
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**David Mullin takes the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon**

David Mullin finished the week in Kent with 135 Stableford points, the lowest total in the field, and collected the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon.

There was nothing sudden about it. Across four links courses, none of which he had played before in TEG competition, his gross scores were 94 at Royal Cinque Ports, 89 over Prince's Dunes/Himalayas, 92 at Littlestone and 92 on the Shore/Dunes combination that closed the tournament. That is a spread of five strokes from best round to worst — a level of consistency that would be an asset in most contexts, but which here simply kept him within reach of the bottom place from Thursday onwards.

The moment it became settled came on the 10th at Prince's in the final round. Mullin took nine shots on the par 4, a quintuple bogey and no Stableford points on a stroke index 3 hole. It is the worst score he has recorded on a par 4 in TEG competition. He had entered the hole fourth from bottom in the Spoon race; he left it in last place, and stayed there.

The Spoon is decided on Stableford points, and 135 across four rounds was not enough to escape it. Mullin's week was defined less by disaster than by the absence of any round good enough to lift him clear.
