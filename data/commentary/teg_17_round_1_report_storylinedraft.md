# Baker's 114 Rewrites the Record Book as Patterson Surges

## John Patterson's round survives two early quadruple bogeys to become the best score of the day
<!-- storyline: round -->

John Patterson opened round one of TEG 9 at Praia D'El Rey in the worst possible fashion, running up quadruple bogeys at both the 1st and the 6th to put an 8 on his card twice inside the first six holes. Two disasters that early would sink most rounds, but Patterson steadied himself, and by the turn he was building something worthwhile.

The recovery began at the 12th, where a birdie broke a run of bogeys after a shaky 10th and 11th. From there he barely put a foot wrong, stringing together pars and staying gross-shot clean from the 12th through the 16th, a stretch that included two separate two-hole spells without dropping a shot. A par at the 16th was enough to take him to the outright lead in the Trophy standings.

The finished round of 39 Stableford points stood up as the best score of the day, comfortably clear of the field's worst return of 32, a remarkable turnaround from two early blow-up holes that could easily have written off his day.

## Alex Baker shatters the Praia D'El Rey course record with a 114
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker set a new course-worst at Praia D'El Rey in the opening round of TEG 16, carding a gross 114. That eclipsed the previous worst of 110, a mark that had stood across 24 prior visits to the course, and it also arrived on Baker's own fifth visit — his previous best there had been 100, meaning he found 14 strokes more than the last time he played it.

The damage was done in a run from the 3rd to the 5th, where he dropped 13 shots and failed to record a single net par. The 5th, one of the hardest holes on the course at stroke index 2, produced a sextuple-bogey 10, while the 4th added a quadruple-bogey 8. Baker also closed with a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 18th. Three holes alone accounted for 27 shots, and the round stands as the highest gross score ever recorded at Praia D'El Rey.

## David Mullin's round splits neatly into a horror front nine and a big finish
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

David Mullin's opening round at Praia D'El Rey split into two distinct halves. The front nine turned into a disaster stretch from the 4th to the 8th, four straight double bogeys followed by a triple on the par-3 8th, bleeding 11 shots in five holes and including three holes in a row, from the 6th to the 8th, without a single net par.

The back nine told the opposite story. From the 9th he found pars on two holes without dropping a shot, then ran off three holes from the 12th to the 14th without a blemish, capped by a birdie on the 13th. That form carried through to the 16th, with the stretch from the 12th yielding 16 stableford points. The result was a round worth 33 points overall, but split unevenly: 13 points stronger on the back nine than the front. It left him well short of his best gross score at the course, eight shots worse than his last visit here.

## The early state of all three races after one round at Praia D'El Rey
<!-- storyline: race -->

Round one at Praia D'El Rey settled into shape gradually across all three competitions. Jon Baker was first to move, taking the outright TEG Trophy (Stableford) lead with a par at the 3rd, but it was John Patterson who claimed it for keeps, going ahead at the 16th and holding it through to the close of play. Patterson's round leaves him level with the field on Stableford at the top, with his best gross score at the course now standing at 96.

In the TEG Green Jacket (Gross), Gregg Williams drew level for the lead with a par at the notoriously difficult par-5 17th, the hardest hole on the course, and finished the day out in front, backing up a 93 that matches his best gross score around this course.

At the other end of the field, Alex Baker's day fell apart at the last, a quintuple bogey on the 18th dropping him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings after one round.
