# Eighty-Two at Penha Longa: Gregg Williams Takes the Lot

## How Gregg Williams led the TEG 15 Trophy from the fourth hole of Round 1 to the last, winning three rounds outright and finishing 157 points, 21 clear
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**Williams takes the TEG 15 Trophy by 21**

Gregg Williams won the TEG 15 Trophy on 157 points, finishing 21 clear of runner-up Stuart Neumann across the four rounds on the Lisbon Coast.

The week's defining round came on the Monday at Penha Longa, the Atlantic course at Sintra, where Williams signed for 82 gross. That is a Penha Longa course record: the previous mark of 86 had stood through six prior visits. It was also a personal best gross round for Williams at +10, and it took 14 strokes off the 96 he had posted on his only previous visit to the course.

The rest of his week was steady rather than spectacular by that standard. He opened with 86 at Estoril, a course new to the field, and closed with 91 at Oitavos Dunes — two strokes better than the 93 he managed there in TEG 8, and a course best for him.

The one clear blemish came in Round 2 at West Cliffs, where Williams ran up a 10 at the par-five 15th. The quintuple bogey returned no Stableford points and left him with 88 for the round. Given the margin at the finish, it cost him nothing but the scorecard. By the time he reached Oitavos Dunes on the Tuesday, the Trophy was not seriously in question.

## David Mullin arrives as defending Trophy champion and nine-time Jacket winner, makes the only eagle of TEG 15, and leaves with the worst round of his TEG career
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Mullin's week of extremes**

David Mullin produced the single most eye-catching swing of TEG 15 and also its most punishing collapse.

The high point came at West Cliffs in round two, where he holed out for a three at the par-5 15th. It was the only eagle struck by anyone in the tournament. The same round contained a 10 at the par-5 6th, a quintuple bogey worth nothing, and by the 11th — a triple bogey at another blank hole — Mullin had slipped from fourth to last in the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon race.

Round three at Penha Longa opened as well as any stretch of his week: par, birdie, par for 10 points through three holes, before a triple bogey at the 4th, the hardest hole on the card, ended the run. He still signed for 85 gross there, one stroke better than his previous best at the course.

Oitavos Dunes undid all of it. Mullin began with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st and finished with another 8 at the 18th, closing with four holes in a row without a net par. The round came in at 22 points and +33 gross — both the worst figures of his TEG career to date. His 104 at Oitavos was 16 strokes worse than the 88 he had managed on his only previous visit.

## Stuart Neumann posts 115 gross at West Cliffs, spends Round 2 collecting quintuple bogeys, and still finishes runner-up in the TEG 15 Trophy
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Stuart Neumann's week of big numbers**

Stuart Neumann's TEG 15 was built on holes that got away from him. It started at Estoril, where a 9 at the par-four 3rd was followed by a 10 at the par-four 15th — six over on a single hole, and the worst he has recorded on a par four. He signed for 106 on a course none of the field had played before.

Round 2 at West Cliffs, another first visit, was worse. Stuart made an 8 at the 4th, a 10 at the par-five 6th, an 11 at the par-five 13th, a 9 at the 14th and a 9 at the 17th — five holes accounting for 26 shots over par, and none of them returning a point. The 9 at the 14th, the hardest hole on the card, dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon race. His gross total was 115.

Penha Longa turned it round. Stuart shot 92, seven strokes better than his 99 there in TEG 8 and a course best, with three straight pars-or-better against his handicap at the 14th, 15th and 16th worth nine points.

The pattern had not gone away, though. At Oitavos Dunes he closed with a 9 at the 18th, part of a 99 that left him four shots off the 95 he managed there in TEG 8.

## John Patterson plays the best six holes of anyone's tournament at Penha Longa, then opens the final round at Oitavos Dunes with a sextuple-bogey 10
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

**John Patterson: six holes of gold, then the wheels come off**

For six holes on the Monday at Penha Longa, John Patterson played better than anyone else managed all week. From the 3rd to the 8th of round three he collected 19 Stableford points — pars at the 3rd, 5th, 6th and 7th, the last of those a four-pointer on the stroke index 4 short hole, and bogeys at the 4th and 8th that still banked three apiece.

It did not last. The 9th, 10th and 11th cost him eight shots against par, including a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 10th, and the stroke index 1 16th took a quintuple-bogey 9. He signed for a 99, his best gross of the week on a course he was seeing for the first time.

Tuesday at Oitavos Dunes began worse. Patterson took 10 on the opening par 4, a sextuple bogey, and followed it with double bogeys at the 2nd and 3rd — ten shots gone in three holes, and no net par among them. The middle of the round repeated the pattern: doubles at the 9th, 10th, 11th and 13th around a quadruple-bogey 7 at the par-3 12th, twelve shots dropped across five holes. A quadruple-bogey 8 at the 17th finished the job. His 105 was his highest round of the tournament, and his four blow-up holes there cost 16 shots on their own.

## Gregg Williams, with no previous Green Jacket to his name, wins every single round of the gross and takes the TEG 15 Jacket at +63, by 29 from Jon Baker
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

Gregg Williams had never won a Green Jacket before. He now has one, and he won it in a manner that leaves little room for argument: a gross total of +63 to take the TEG 15 Green Jacket by 29 strokes from Jon Baker. It is the best gross total of Williams's career.

The tournament turned on the third round at Penha Longa. Williams went round in 82 gross — +10 — which set a new course record, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and stood as the best gross round of his career. It was also a fourteen-stroke improvement on his own previous best at the mountain parkland course, the 96 he posted on his first visit there. He closed with 91 at Oitavos Dunes, two better than his previous best there.

Baker was the nearest challenger and had his own bright spots, including an 85 at Penha Longa, six strokes better than his previous best on the course, and a 94 at Oitavos Dunes. But the damage had been done a day earlier at West Cliffs, where he ran up an 11 at the par-four 17th — a septuple bogey, and the worst score he has recorded on a par four. Against a leader in the form Williams found in Portugal, that kind of hole is unrecoverable.

## Alex Baker takes the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon on 114 points, 11 adrift, handing over nothing to nobody — and taking it directly from his brother, the holder
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**Alex Baker takes the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon**

Alex Baker finished the week on 114 points and collected the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon. The damage was concentrated rather than spread: two rounds, and within those rounds two short stretches, accounted for most of it.

The first came at West Cliffs in round two, on a links course none of the field had played before. At the 6th, a par 5, Alex Baker signed for an 11 — a sextuple bogey and the worst he has recorded on a par 5. He followed it with a 9 at the 7th and a 7 at the 8th, dropping 13 shots in three holes and going the whole stretch without a net par. Later the same afternoon he took 10 at the 17th. His round of 110 gross was the outcome.

Penha Longa the next day produced a similar shape. Nine at the 12th, six at the 13th and 10 at the 14th cost him 12 shots across three holes, again with no net par among them. He still signed for 106, matching the gross he made here on his previous visit in TEG 8.

The closing round offered something better. His 103 at Oitavos Dunes was a shot inside the 104 he posted there in TEG 8 — a course best, arriving a round too late to alter the standings.
