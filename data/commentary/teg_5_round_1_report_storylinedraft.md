# Mullin's Record 83 Sets The Tone

## David Mullin's 83 gross at Boavista — a new course record and the best gross round in TEG history to date, arriving on his eighth visit to the venue.
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin opened with an 83 gross at Boavista, a new course record and the best gross round recorded in TEG history to date. It came on his eighth visit to the venue, and it demolished the previous course mark of 85 — a record that had stood across 35 prior visits by the field — while also beating his own best gross there by eight strokes.

The round started shakily, with bogeys at the 1st and 2nd, but Mullin halted the slide with a birdie at the par-5 3rd, a hole he would return to at the top of his card. That birdie also lifted him to the outright lead in both the Green Jacket (Gross) and the Trophy (Net VP) after just three holes. He added a three-hole run without dropping a gross shot from the 7th to the 9th, and despite a late bogey at the 18th, he still finished the day on top of the Trophy leaderboard as Round 1 closed.

## Gregg Williams's round unravels twice over — nine shots lost in his first four holes and ten more in his last three, including a quintuple bogey at the 17th.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams's opening round at Boavista never found any rhythm. He dropped nine shots inside the first four holes, opening with a triple bogey on the 1st before compounding it with double bogeys at the 2nd, 3rd and 4th. That early bleeding pushed him to the bottom of the TEG 1 Wooden Spoon race after just two holes.

Any hope of recovery vanished at the other end of the round. A double bogey at the 16th was followed by a quintuple bogey at the 17th — a 9 on a hole that ranked as one of the easiest on the course by stroke index — and then a triple bogey at the 18th. That closing stretch alone cost him ten shots across three holes, mirroring the nine lost in his opening four.

Between those two collapses there was little to build on, and the round finished well outside his best figures at the course. Eight visits to Boavista have not yet produced anything close to repeating the pace he needed here.

## The Wooden Spoon race changes hands three times inside round one before settling on Stuart Neumann, three points clear of trouble at the foot of the table.
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

At Boavista in round one of the 2012 TEG, the battle for the bottom of the Wooden Spoon table changed hands three times. Henry Meller was first to drop into last place, carding a quadruple bogey on the par-5 13th. Stuart Neumann took over soon after, following a triple bogey on the 18th with another on the 16th that kept him anchored at the foot of the field. A third triple bogey, on the 5th, dropped him from third all the way to last, and a double bogey on the 7th kept him there. Meller briefly swapped back into the bottom spot with a double bogey on the 9th, but a bogey on the 10th sent Neumann back down to stay. By the close of the round he had settled into last place, three points clear of the trouble above him.

## Jon Baker's tussle with David Mullin for the TEG 5 Trophy lead, and the much wider gap that opened in the Green Jacket race off the same round.
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Boavista in the opening round of TEG 5, David Mullin and Jon Baker traded the Trophy lead throughout the day. Mullin struck first, a birdie on the par-5 3rd handing him the outright lead in both the TEG 5 Trophy (Net VP) and the TEG 5 Green Jacket (Gross). Baker drew level with a bogey at the 4th, then did so again with a birdie at the 16th, but Mullin closed it out at the last, a bogey at the 18th still enough to retake the Trophy lead outright. By the end of the round Mullin held it, though only by a gap of zero on net-versus-par, as tight as a lead can be.

The Jacket race told a very different story. Where the Trophy contenders were separated by a span running from -3 to 6, the field's gross scores that day ranged from 12 to 35, a far wider spread that left Mullin's Jacket lead considerably more secure than his Trophy one. Both men produced their best rounds of the week, Mullin's 83 a course personal best.
