## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time, a Jack Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve with water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes. Of the 28 courses TEG has rated, only two play harder. Jon Baker looked at all that and parred the opening three holes for 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes on the card.

The 2026 TEG Trophy lead then spent the afternoon shuttling between him and Gregg Williams. Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon took it straight back at the 12th with one of his own, Williams reclaimed it at the 13th while making a bogey, and Jon drew level again at the 15th. That one deserves its own paragraph and will have to settle for a sentence: a mid-length par 4 that runs alongside a lake from tee to green, the hardest hole on the course, and he made a par on it for four points. At the 16th he moved clear.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a single gross shot on the way in. He signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round, and his overnight lead went from one point to five. Four closing pars on a course of this temperament is the sort of finish the group will be bringing up for years, and Jon will not be stopping them.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Jon's 42 was the outlier. Everywhere else, the course's TEG debut was conducted in nines, eights and sevens, and all five players made a contribution.

Alex Baker got the worst of it before most people had settled: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st and another 9 at the 2nd, which is a dreadful way to begin anything, followed much later by a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. Jon Baker was not exempt, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's trouble arrived in clusters. A 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, and a second 9 turned up at the 16th.

Mullin did find an ending, though. Having gone a long stretch without beating a bogey, he bogeyed the 17th and then birdied the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. After the afternoon he had been having, a birdie was close to bad manners.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes: those two nines, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 out there, he signed for a 10, the worst he has made on a par 3 anywhere. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. Then, out of nowhere, bogey-bogey-par from the 12th for nine points in three holes, a spell of proper golf that deserved a far better home than this scorecard. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, ended the recovery. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th got him going, a triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him down there on his own. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th, which is a bleak way to walk off any course. No leader changed all day. The gaps did: Jon Baker's Trophy lead stretched to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin went from nothing to nine.
