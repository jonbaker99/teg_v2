## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time. It is a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve, and it concedes nothing. Only two rated courses play harder for golfers of this standard. Jon Baker opened with three pars worth 10 Stableford points. The third was a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead changed hands repeatedly. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with another. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th with a bogey, which tells you something about the territory being fought over. Jon drew level at the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the course. The par there was worth four points. He moved clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes. Fourteen points, not a gross shot dropped, 42 on the card. It was the best Trophy score of the round. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round was Monte Rei's first appearance in TEG. The Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve has water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes. It introduced itself as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated. The introductions were thorough.

Alex Baker got the worst of it early. A quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, another 9 at the 2nd, and later a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. Jon Baker was not spared either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's damage arrived in clusters. A 9 at the par-5 6th began a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par. A second 9 arrived at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a long stretch without beating a bogey. He followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th, then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. It was the only genuinely good news the course handed out here.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks as the third-hardest course in the TEG's rated set, and it showed at the bottom of the field. Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes. A 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. He had not yet reached the fifth tee. At the 7th, the course's longest and meanest par 3, he signed for a 10. It is the worst he has made on a par 3, a record that took genuine commitment to establish. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. A run of bogey-bogey-par from the 12th brought nine points in three holes and a flicker of dignity. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, closed the door on it. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th started it. A triple at the 8th took him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him there alone. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots. He closed with another 10 at the 18th, a man finishing exactly as he began. No leader changed, but the gaps did. Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one to five. Patterson's Spoon margin grew from nothing to nine. Some things are earned.
