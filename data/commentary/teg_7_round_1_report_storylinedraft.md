# Meller And Jon Baker Rewrite TEG History

## Henry Meller's -13 at Praia D'El Rey, the best round recorded in TEG history to date
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller's opening round at Praia D'El Rey produced a net score of -13, the best round recorded in TEG history to date. It also stood as a personal best, with a gross score of +12.

The round was not flawless. Meller opened with a par, birdie and par across the 1st to 3rd, then saw that steady start broken by a double bogey at the 4th. Worse followed at the 8th, the hardest hole on the course, where a quadruple bogey 9 went into the card. Neither blip derailed him. Over the closing nine, from the 10th through the 18th, Meller gained ten shots to par, playing the stretch in pars and only two bogeys, including one at the 14th, the second-hardest hole on the course.

It was a round built on recovery as much as brilliance, with two costly blowups more than offset by sustained par golf either side of them, enough to set the highest bar the format has yet seen.

## Alex Baker's horror show around Praia D'El Rey, three quadruple bogeys and a quintuple leaving him rooted to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's opening round at Praia D'El Rey started with a stretch of bogeys, three straight from the 2nd to the 4th, but it unravelled from there. On the par-3 5th, one of the easier holes on the card at stroke index 17, he ran up a quintuple bogey 8, his worst hole of the day against par. A triple bogey followed at the tough, stroke-index-3 6th, and by the turn he had already dropped to the bottom of the TEG 16 Wooden Spoon race.

The damage deepened on the back nine. Holes 8 through 11 bled 11 shots in total, headlined by a quadruple bogey at the 10th, and a second quadruple bogey arrived at the 14th, one of the hardest holes on the course at stroke index 2. That hole confirmed his place last in the Spoon standings, a position he could not shake for the rest of the round. His gross 110 stands, for now, as his only mark at the course.

## Praia D'El Rey's teeth on its TEG debut, with a big number found for every player in the field
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Praia D'El Rey wasted no time asserting itself on its first appearance in TEG history, with Round 1 leaving marks on much of the field. Jon Baker took a quadruple bogey 7 at the short 2nd, one of the course's easier holes on paper at stroke index 13. Gregg Williams found the same grade of trouble at the 6th, a quadruple bogey 8 on a hole ranked the third-hardest on the course. David Mullin closed his round with an identical quadruple bogey 8 at the 18th, a stroke-index-4 finishing hole that gave nothing back.

Stuart Neumann's day unravelled in more sustained fashion, with triple bogeys at both the 3rd and the 7th. Between them they were enough to send him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, a position he dropped into during this round. With four players in a six-man field each producing a blow-up hole, Praia D'El Rey's TEG debut made its intentions clear from the very first round.

## A day-one Trophy and Green Jacket picture split by wafer-thin margins between Henry Meller and Jon Baker
<!-- storyline: race -->

The opening round at Praia D'El Rey produced a Trophy and Green Jacket picture too close to separate cleanly, despite one of the players involved briefly wrecking his card early on. Jon Baker took a quadruple bogey 7 at the short 2nd, one of the course's harder holes at stroke index 13, but answered it with the best round TEG history had yet seen: -10 net, the third-best round recorded to that point, built on a gross +11 that stands as the best gross round on record. Two hot stretches, three pars from 12-14 and another three from 16-18, did the damage.

The lead in both competitions changed hands repeatedly. Henry Meller took the Green Jacket (Gross) lead at the 2nd, only for David Mullin to draw level at the 5th and Meller to level again at the 14th, one of the toughest holes on the course, with Baker also squaring it at the 12th. The Trophy lead shifted just as often, passing through Mullin, Gregg Williams, Meller and Baker, all level at various points around the 8th and 10th. By day's end Meller held the Trophy lead by the barest of margins, with Baker in front for the Jacket.
