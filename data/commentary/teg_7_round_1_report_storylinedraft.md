# Two Records in an Afternoon on the Lisbon Coast

## Henry Meller's -13 at Praia D'El Rey, the best round in TEG history to date, built on a back nine of almost total serenity after an early wobble and a nine at the 8th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller opened TEG 1 at Praia D'El Rey with a round of -13, still the best round in TEG history to date, built gross at +12, itself a personal best. The front nine was not flawless: three pars to open, at the 1st, 2nd and 3rd, gave way to a double bogey at the 4th, and worse followed at the 8th, the hardest hole on the course, where a quadruple bogey nine went down. That should have wrecked the card. Instead the back nine was almost untouched by trouble. From the 10th to the 18th, Meller gained ten shots against par, running off five holes in a row, from the 10th to the 14th, without dropping a single gross shot, and closing with only two bogeys, at the 15th and 18th, either side of largely untroubled pars. The nine at the 8th stands as the round's only real blemish, an anomaly swallowed whole by the serenity either side of it.

## Alex Baker's first round of TEG golf: three eights, a quintuple bogey at the 5th, and last place in the TEG 7 Wooden Spoon race from the sixth hole onwards
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's opening round of TEG golf, on debut at Praia D'El Rey, offered a taste of the trouble to come. He opened steadily enough, shipping three bogeys through holes 2 to 4, but the round unravelled at the par-3 5th, where a quintuple bogey 8 stood out even against everything that followed.

Worse was still to come. Holes 8 through 11 bled 11 shots, including a quadruple bogey at the 10th, and by the time a second quadruple bogey arrived at the 14th — one of the harder holes on the course — Baker had dropped to the bottom of the TEG 7 Wooden Spoon race, a place he would not leave for the rest of the round. The pain continued through 14 to 16, three more holes that cost him eight shots combined. Three separate eights on the card, on a course he was seeing for the first time, made for a debut round to forget.

## Praia D'El Rey itself: a course new to every player in the field that handed out a quadruple bogey to four of the six and still let two records fall
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Praia D'El Rey, making its first appearance in TEG history on Thursday 2 October 2014, gave the six-man field a taste of both its links teeth and its parkland forgiveness in the same round. David Mullin ran up a quadruple bogey 8 at the 18th, one of the course's harder holes, while Gregg Williams matched him with a quadruple at the 6th before steadying himself with two four-hole stretches of nothing worse than bogey, at 7-10 and 13-16, that carried him level with Mullin for the Trophy lead by the 10th. Stuart Neumann had the rougher time of it, triple-bogeying the 3rd and 7th to sink to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race. With nobody in the field having ever played the course before, the round's extremes were unavoidable records: the best score of the day finished 11 over par, the worst 37 over, and both stood as the first course marks Praia D'El Rey had on the books.

## Jon Baker's +11 gross, the best gross round in TEG history to date, which delivered the TEG 7 Green Jacket lead by a single stroke while leaving him three adrift of Henry Meller in the TEG 7 Trophy
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker opened TEG 7 at Praia D'El Rey with a gross round of +11, the best gross round in TEG history to date. It was not flawless — a quadruple bogey seven at the short 2nd, one of the easier holes on the card at stroke index 13, threatened to derail things early — but Baker recovered to card a net -10, the third-best round in TEG history, and closed with three straight pars from the 16th, a stretch that lost him nothing gross or net.

That finish proved decisive. On the 10th he had drawn level for the Trophy lead, and by the 12th he had done the same for the Green Jacket, only for Henry Meller to reclaim the outright Jacket lead moments later at the 2nd. By day's end Baker held the Green Jacket lead by a single stroke, but it was Meller who topped the Trophy standings, three ahead of Baker, setting up the first genuine split at the top of TEG 7.
