# Four From Four at Boavista

## The final-day verdicts at Boavista seen from the men who were not winning the Trophy: David Mullin's second Green Jacket, Stuart Neumann's near-miss with the Spoon, and Henry Meller's second Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

On the final day at Boavista, the Trophy contest went to Jon Baker, but three other stories closed out around him. David Mullin secured his second Green Jacket, extending his lead from nine shots to twelve. He built the round around a tidy stretch from the 2nd to the 4th, three holes without dropping a gross shot and three shots gained to net par, before a triple bogey at the 5th ended the run and a quadruple bogey at the 11th briefly threatened to unravel things. Neither dent cost him the Jacket.

Stuart Neumann kept himself off the bottom with a small run of his own, two holes without a dropped shot around the 6th and 7th, enough to survive a near-miss with the Wooden Spoon.

Henry Meller could not escape it. A four-hole stretch from the 12th to the 15th, gaining shots to par each time, narrowed the gap to fourth from five to four, but it was not enough, and Meller finished with his second Wooden Spoon.

## The 17th at Boavista, stroke index 17 and the most destructive hole of TEG 3, which took a 12 from Henry Meller, a 9 from David Mullin and finally a 10 from Stuart Neumann
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The 17th at Boavista is a stroke index 17 hole — officially the easiest test on the course — but in round four of TEG 3 it played as the round's wrecking ball. Three players took turns being ruined by it. Henry Meller ran up a 12, David Mullin carded a 9, and Stuart Neumann added a 10, a sextuple bogey against the hole's par of 4.

Neumann's collapse was the most costly relative to expectation, adding six shots to par on a hole with almost no business allowing that. It stood as his worst score against par of the round, though the wider damage of the day was already done by the time he reached it. None of the trio's blowups changed the shape of the race — Jon Baker stayed clear at the top of the Trophy, Mullin still led the Green Jacket, and Meller remained anchored to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon — but the 17th's ability to punish three separate players in one afternoon made it the defining scene of the round.

## Gregg Williams's 35-over gross round at Boavista, assembled out of three quadruple bogeys and a closing four holes that cost him nine shots, which was nonetheless his third-best net round in seven
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Gregg Williams closed out round four at Boavista with a gross 35-over — the worst gross score recorded in the five-man field that day — yet it stood as his third-best net round in seven, testament to how much of the pain was gross rather than net.

The round was built on three separate quadruple bogeys: a 9 at the par-5 3rd, an 8 at the par-4 7th, and another 8 at the par-4 12th. Between the first and second of those, holes 4 to 6 actually worked in his favour — three straight bogeys that, with strokes received, netted him three shots against par.

There was no such relief at the finish. Williams bled nine shots over the closing four holes, with double bogeys at the 15th, 16th and 18th sandwiching a triple at the 17th — the 15th coming on Boavista's hardest hole, the 18th on one of its toughest, a grim finish to match the round's blowups.

## Jon Baker's level-net 0 at Boavista, the best score in the field for the fourth round running, completing the sweep that delivers his first TEG Trophy
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's final round at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort completed a clean sweep of TEG 16: his level-net 0 was the best score in the field for the fourth round running, sealing his first TEG Trophy.

The round turned through holes 7 to 9, where Baker picked up four shots against par, the key blow a net birdie on the par-5 8th, one of the hardest holes on the course, despite carding only a gross par there. He followed it by going through holes 8 and 9 without dropping a single gross shot, closing out the round with total control.

It left Jon Baker as the Trophy leader after the round, with David Mullin leading the separate Green Jacket race. Baker's overall Trophy lead grew to 18 shots by the finish, unchanged in name from the start of the day but stretched further by four straight rounds as the field's best. It was his seventh visit to Boavista, and while not a course best for him this TEG, it was enough to finish the job.
