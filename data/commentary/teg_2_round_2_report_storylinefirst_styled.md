## Jon Baker's near-record round undone by a ragged finish
<!-- storyline: round -->

For most of the afternoon, Jon Baker's second round at Boavista was historic. He finished +1 for the round. That is the second-best round in TEG history. It was also a course personal best, a gross 100, three shots better than his previous visit here.

He built it from the front. Across holes 6 to 8 he gained four shots to par. He took the outright lead in the TEG 2 Trophy at the 4th, before a bogey at the 6th let David Mullin draw level again. Baker surged back ahead. By the close he had taken the Trophy race lead from Mullin and stood three clear.

Then came the closing holes. A triple bogey at the brutal par-4 15th. A double at the 16th. Then quadruple bogeys at the 17th and the 18th, back to back. Thirteen shots surrendered over the closing stretch. A man does not often get to dismantle his own monument with the tools still warm in his hands. He held the lead regardless.

Stuart Neumann's round of gross 27 over par was also a course best for him, and ranks as the second-best gross round in TEG history. It was not the story of the day. Such is the timing of things.

## Boavista's 18th hole punishes three cards in one afternoon
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The 18th at Boavista is a par 4 and, at stroke index 5, one of the harder holes on the course. During round 2 of TEG 2 it took payment from three cards. Jon Baker, Gregg Williams and David Mullin all closed with quadruple-bogey 8s.

For Baker it was the more costly of the three. It landed on a day that still delivered a course personal best and the lead in the 2009 TEG Trophy race, three clear of Mullin, who had led before the round. Williams also set a course best for himself despite the finishing blow. Mullin's 8 arrived with no such consolation attached. He slipped from the Trophy lead he had held overnight, though he stays on top of the Green Jacket race, his advantage narrowing slightly to eight strokes.

Three quadruple bogeys on one hole in one afternoon. The 18th was the day's clear villain, and it undid good work from two of the men it struck.

## Henry Meller's quiet transformation at Boavista
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Henry Meller's second round at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort, in TEG 2, contained two rounds. He had finished Round 1 there at 24 over net par. He came back 11 strokes better, at 13 over. That is a genuine turnaround in form. It also produced a course personal best: a gross 102, beating his previous Boavista mark by 11 strokes, on only his second visit to the venue.

The round was not smooth throughout. Across holes 13 to 16 he went four straight without a net par and shipped 11 gross shots. A double bogey at the 13th, then triple bogeys at the 14th, the 16th and the par-4 15th. The 15th is the hardest hole on the course. The stretch cost him seven strokes net.

None of it moved him. Meller remains anchored to the bottom of the TEG 2 Wooden Spoon table, now 16 shots adrift of the next player up. He had trailed by 12 before the round began. He played the best golf of his Boavista life and lost four shots of ground doing it.

## David Mullin's Trophy lead slips away despite a career round
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin produced one of the finest rounds in TEG history at Boavista. A gross +28, the third-best gross round the event has ever seen. For most of the day it looked set to extend his Trophy lead and his Green Jacket advantage together. On the 5th, a bogey was enough to put him back in outright control of the Trophy on net scoring. It seemed a round destined to be remembered as simple domination.

The finish undid it. From the 16th through the 18th he went three straight holes without a net par, bleeding 10 gross shots, including a triple bogey at the 17th and the quadruple-bogey 8 at the last. Baker had already traded the net lead with him twice earlier in the round, at the 4th and the 6th. Baker's own finish was no work of art either. It simply cost him less. By the close Baker led the Trophy outright, three clear on net scoring.

Mullin still leads the Green Jacket. He also has the round of his life, and a Trophy lead somewhere behind him on the 18th tee.
