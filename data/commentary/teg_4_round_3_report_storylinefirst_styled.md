## Jon Baker follows his worst round of the TEG 4 Trophy so far with the best net score of the day at Crowborough, built around a four-hole purple patch either side of two closing quadruple bogeys.
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker arrived at Crowborough carrying the wreckage of Round 2, which had left him nine over par net. He left it three under net. That is a swing of twelve shots, and it was the best net score of the day at the TEG 4 Trophy. The round-of-the-day figure came in at 13, so he was not far off that mark either.

The recovery was assembled in the middle of the round. From the 11th to the 14th he made four consecutive pars and gained five shots on net par. One of them came at the 12th, the second hardest hole on the course. Nobody could accuse him of picking on the weak.

Then came the 15th, and a quadruple bogey, arriving without notice or apology. He steadied himself over the next two holes. At the 18th a second quadruple bogey removed any suggestion that the steadying had taken. Four flawless holes, then two blow-ups. The round contained his worst golf of the tournament and his best, and made no attempt to separate them.

## Gregg Williams's outward nine at Crowborough contains four separate quadruple bogeys inside six holes, producing the third-worst gross round recorded in TEG history to date and his own second-worst round on both scales.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams's third round at Crowborough Beacon took six holes to complete its essential work. A double bogey at the 1st. Quadruple bogeys at the 2nd, the 4th and the 5th. Double bogeys at the 3rd and the 6th, filling in the gaps between them with real diligence. Eighteen shots dropped in six holes, and a five-hole stretch without a single net par.

The quadruple at the 2nd came at the hardest hole on the course. At least he was selective.

A fourth quadruple bogey followed at the 15th. He then played the 16th and the 17th to gross par, a passage of entirely competent golf that arrived several hours after it was needed. Any repair work had long since been rendered decorative.

The round also cost him in the Wooden Spoon race. The damage at the 4th dropped him from third to fifth in a field of five. There is no sixth.

It stands as the third-worst gross score recorded in TEG history to date, and Williams's own second-worst on both the gross and net scales. A man can only do so much in one afternoon.

## David Mullin's 17-gross round, the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to date, blows the TEG 4 Green Jacket race wide open even as the TEG 4 Trophy tightens to a flat tie, while the TEG 4 Wooden Spoon changes hands from Stuart Neumann to Henry Meller.
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin went round Crowborough Beacon in a gross 17. It is the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to date. It also managed to disturb all three competitions at once.

On the Trophy, the lead changed hands on net score four times. Mullin took it at the 10th. Jon Baker drew level at the 11th, then drew level again at the 2nd. Mullin pulled back level at the 15th. They finished tied outright, gap zero. Mullin had started the day two clear, so the second-best gross round in TEG history earned him precisely nothing where it counted.

The Green Jacket tells the other story. Mullin still leads, and the margin has stretched from seven to 16, blown open by the sheer scale of the gross number. Same round, same man, two different scoring systems doing their jobs.

The Wooden Spoon moved too. Stuart Neumann held the bottom spot, and a double bogey at the 2nd, the stroke index 1, pushed him further into it. He was then rescued from below. Henry Meller, coming off a strong second round, made a triple bogey at the 14th and slid to last himself. The Spoon is now his, by four shots.
