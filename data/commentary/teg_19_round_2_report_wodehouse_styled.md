## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time, a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve with water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes and a rating that makes it the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has measured. Jon Baker answered all that by parring the first three holes for 10 points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes on the card.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead went back and forth all afternoon. Gregg Williams moved outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon took it back at the 12th with one of his own, and Williams reclaimed it at the 13th while making a bogey, which takes a certain cheek. At the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole out there, Jon made a par worth four points to draw level, then went clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes. Fourteen points, not a single gross shot dropped, and a 42 that was the best Trophy score of the round. His overnight lead went from one point to five. People who weren't even in his group will be describing that finish to each other for years.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The other four spent the day being introduced.

Alex Baker got the worst of it early: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, another 9 at the 2nd, and a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th later on. Nine shots gone in two holes before anyone had settled, which is bad golf in anybody's accounting. Jon Baker was not exempt either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's trouble came in clusters: a 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, and a second 9 arrived at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a long stretch without beating a bogey, then followed that 16th with a bogey at the 17th and a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. One birdie, extracted under protest.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes: those two nines, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 on the course, he signed for a 10, the worst he has ever made on a par 3. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. Then, from the 12th, bogey, bogey, par for nine points in three holes, and for twenty glorious minutes the man was unplayable. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, ended all that. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th started it. A triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him down there on his own. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th. Two tens in one round takes real commitment. No leader changed all day, but the gaps did: Patterson's Spoon margin went from nothing to nine.
