## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time, a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve where only two rated courses in the whole set play harder. Jon Baker received this information and promptly made three pars, worth 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there. A man may play golf for a decade and never open a round like that on ground of this temper.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead passed back and forth all afternoon in the most civil fashion. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par, which on this course counts as a small act of defiance, and Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with one of his own. Williams then reclaimed the lead at the 13th while making a bogey, a trick worth studying. Jon drew level at the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that follows a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the property, with a par worth four points. He moved clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a single gross shot among them, and signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round. Four closing pars at Monte Rei is about as close to rule by divine right as this competition allows. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Monte Rei made its TEG debut on the Sunday, and the Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve, with water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes, turned out to be the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker got the worst of it, and got it early: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, another 9 at the 2nd, and a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th to follow. There is no polite construction that improves this, so one simply records that it was bad golf. Jon Baker was not excused either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin suffered in clusters, a 9 at the par-5 6th beginning a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, with a second 9 arriving at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. Having spent a long stretch unable to better a bogey, he followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. A birdie, here, at that hour of the afternoon, deserved a standing ovation from everyone in the vicinity. It was the only genuinely good news the course gave up all day in this stretch.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks third-hardest in TEG's rated set, and the bottom of the field felt every yard of it. Alex Baker stood 14 over par after four holes, by way of a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 on the course, he signed for a 10, the worst he has made on a par 3 anywhere. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots, and the decent thing is to state that plainly and move on. Then came bogey, bogey, par from the 12th, nine points in three holes and a brief period of open rebellion against the whole afternoon. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, put a stop to it. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th began it, a triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him down there without company. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th, which is a cruel hole on which to be made an example. No leader changed, but the gaps did: Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one point to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
