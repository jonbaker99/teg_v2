## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time. It is a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve, and it concedes nothing. Only two rated courses play harder for golfers of this standard. Jon Baker opened with three pars worth 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead passed back and forth all afternoon. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with another. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th despite a bogey. Jon then drew level at the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the course. His par there was worth four points. At the 16th he moved clear.

He parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a gross shot. The card read 42, the best Trophy score of the round. To close out a course of this severity with four pars in hand is handsome golf, and it was done under pressure from a man who had led as recently as the 13th. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round was Monte Rei's first appearance in TEG. The Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve has water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes, and it entered the records as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker got the worst of it early. A quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, then a second 9 at the 2nd, and later a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. There is no defending any of it. Jon Baker was not spared either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th. David Mullin's troubles arrived in clusters. A 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par, which was poor golf on any reading, and a second 9 followed at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a good stretch without beating a bogey. He followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. It was the only good news the course gave up all afternoon.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks as the third-hardest course in TEG's rated set, and the bottom of the field felt it. Alex Baker stood 14 over par after four holes: a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the course's longest and meanest par 3, he signed for a 10. It is the worst he has made on a par 3. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots, and that stretch cannot be dressed up. A run of bogey, bogey, par from the 12th brought him nine points in three holes, which was proper recovery work. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, ended the matter. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th began it. A triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him there on his own. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, poor golf sustained over some distance, and he closed with another 10 at the 18th. Every leader stayed where he was, but the gaps widened. Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one point to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
