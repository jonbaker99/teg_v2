## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time, a Jack Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve with water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes and a rating that makes it the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has measured. Jon Baker's answer to all this was to par the first three holes for 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there. Men have been introduced to easier golf courses and gone home early.

From there the 2026 TEG Trophy lead refused to settle anywhere. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon took it straight back at the 12th with one of his own, and Williams then reclaimed it at the 13th while making a bogey, which is a neat piece of work if you can manage it. At the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the course, Jon made a par worth four points and drew level. He went clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points without dropping a gross shot, which on this course borders on showing off. Forty-two points, the best Trophy score of the round, and his overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

All five players were invited to introduce themselves, and most of them did it with very large numbers.

Alex Baker got the worst of it before anyone had warmed up: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, another 9 at the 2nd, and then, once he had every reason to believe the horrors were behind him, a quadruple-bogey 7 at the 11th. That is bad golf, calmly and repeatedly delivered. Even Jon Baker, in the middle of 42 points, contrived a 7 at the par-3 7th.

David Mullin preferred to suffer in batches. His 9 at the par-5 6th opened a three-hole run that cost him eight shots to par, and a second 9 arrived at the 16th for good measure. Two nines in one round is more than anybody needs.

Mullin did find an ending worth keeping. Having gone a long stretch without beating a bogey, he made a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. On the evidence of the preceding three hours, nobody expected it of him.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker reached the 5th tee 14 over par, those two nines having been joined by a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 on the course, he signed for a 10, the worst score he has ever made on a par 3. Three holes from the 9th cost him nine shots to par. Then came bogey, bogey, par from the 12th for nine points in three holes, a passage of such sudden competence that he must have wondered where it had been hiding. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, shut the recovery down, and he finished with 29 points.

John Patterson's 25 was the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th set the tone. A triple at the 8th drew him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him down there on his own. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, and he closed with a second 10, this one at the 18th, which takes real commitment on a short par 5 that somebody else had just birdied.

No leader changed anywhere. The gaps did: Jon's Trophy cushion stretched to five, and Patterson's hold on the Spoon went from a shared nothing to nine points of daylight.
