# The Two Faces of Costa Brava

## Henry Meller's career-best round, arriving a day after his worst finish of the tournament
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller arrived at Costa Brava for round two sitting last in the 2013 TEG Wooden Spoon race, the legacy of a poor opening round of +7 net vs par. The trouble continued early: a double bogey on the third, a triple bogey on the par-5 fourth, and another double bogey on the fifth bled seven shots in three holes and dropped him further still in the Spoon standings. From there, though, Meller turned his round around completely. Holes 9 to 12 brought five shots gained against net par without a single gross shot dropped, capped by a net eagle on the 12th, one of the hardest holes on the course. He closed with a round of -5 net vs par and +17 gross, both personal bests - a 12-shot swing from his opening effort. It was, in fact, the best gross round recorded by the field that day, even though his net score also proved the weakest of the round. The turnaround was enough to lift him off the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, with Stuart Neumann inheriting last place instead.

## Gregg Williams's front-runner stumble at Costa Brava
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams arrived at Club de Golf Costa Brava on 22 September 2013 leading the TEG Trophy after a strong opening round of net -12, and Round 2 put that lead to the test. The front nine brought a run of bogeys interrupted by a quadruple bogey at the 7th, the hardest hole on the course, and by the turn Williams was leaking shots everywhere. Oddly, the stretch from the 9th to the 12th told a different story on paper: four more bogeys, but with handicap strokes attached they banked him four shots against the field. Any relief was brief. From the 13th to the 16th he shed 11 gross shots without a single net par, the round's low point. A birdie at the 17th, the easiest hole, finally broke the streak of bogey-or-worse. The swing from Round 1 to Round 2 was 12 shots, yet Williams's overnight cushion was big enough to survive it — he still led the Trophy race afterwards, the gap unchanged at nine.

## David Mullin stretches his Green Jacket lead while Stuart Neumann inherits the Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin extended his grip on the Green Jacket at Costa Brava, closing his round with three straight pars from 15 to 17 - a stretch that cost him nothing in gross terms and gained three shots to par on his handicap line. It was a tidy finish to round two, and it pushed his Jacket lead out from one shot to five, even as Gregg Williams held firm atop the Trophy standings with the gap there unchanged at nine.

At the other end of the table, the Wooden Spoon changed hands. Stuart Neumann's bogey on the par-4 12th, one of the harder holes on the course, dropped him to the bottom of that race, displacing Henry Meller. The margin is thin - just one shot back to the next player - so there is little separating Neumann from climbing straight back out of the cellar in round three. For Mullin, though, the direction of travel looks rather more settled.
