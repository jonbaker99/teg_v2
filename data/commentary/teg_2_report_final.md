# Two Records, One Wobble, and the 14th Hole for Everybody Else

The third hole at Boavista is a par 5, and on the Monday morning David Mullin took six shots to negotiate it. Nobody watching would have called it distinguished golf. With his stroke it was a net par, and it was enough to move him from second to first in the Trophy, outright, for the first time since the previous afternoon.

He never gave the lead back.

Jon Baker played the same hole in eight. He had begun the day three clear, holding an advantage he had constructed over eighteen careful holes, and he would surrender it in under an hour. By then the Green Jacket had been settled for two days and the Wooden Spoon for rather longer than that — Henry Meller went outright bottom at the third hole of the entire tournament and stayed there like a man who had found his seat and did not intend to move.

To understand how a three-shot advantage evaporated so cheaply, you have to go back to the Saturday, and to the fact that Mullin had already twice rewritten what a good round in TEG looked like.

## Round 1 — Mullin's +0: The Best Round TEG Had Ever Seen (So Far)

Saturday 16 May, TEG's first landing in the Algarve, and Boavista's Howard Swan parkland presented itself to five men who had never seen a yard of it. Atlantic views, two distinctly different nines, and no form book for anybody. Everything set here would become the founding benchmark.

The opening exchanges were the usual bunched shuffling. Gregg Williams drew level at the top on the 2nd, Baker went outright ahead at the 4th, Mullin and Neumann both drew level at the 5th, and Baker levelled again at the 8th. Routine, all of it. What was not routine was that after the 5th hole of the tournament, the Green Jacket lead never changed hands again.

Mullin signed for a net score of level par. It was the finest round in TEG's history to that point. The gross behind it was +20, likewise the best gross round TEG had recorded. Nine clear in the Jacket, four clear in the Trophy, and the afternoon was still young.

Behind him, two men played beautifully and got nothing for it. Baker's +4 net was the second-best round TEG had seen, off a gross +32, itself the third-best. Stuart Neumann's +11 net was third-best all time, off a gross +29 that was second-best all time. On any other day either would have been the story.

Then the back nine began eating.

Meller played the 11th to the 15th in twenty over par. A triple at the short 11th, a quadruple 8 at the 12th, a 9 at the par-5 13th, a quintuple 8 at the par-3 14th, and an 8 at the 15th, which happens to be the hardest hole on the course. He came in at +24 net and 42 over gross, and had been last since the 3rd.

Williams did something similar with more staying power, losing twenty-two shots across the 12th to the 18th. The centrepiece was a 9 at the 14th — a sextuple bogey at a par 3, which takes a certain commitment — followed later by another 9 at the 17th, one of the easiest holes on the property. He signed for +12 net, 40 gross.

Neumann's worst hole of the whole trip also arrived at the 14th: a quintuple 8. Meller's 8, Neumann's 8, Williams's 9, all at the same one-shotter, all before dinner on day one.

The 14th had opened its collection.

## Round 2 — Ten Shots in Three Holes, and Mullin Gift-Wraps the Lead

Sunday 17 May, same course, and this time the field had at least met it.

The Trophy lead moved three times inside six holes. Baker took it outright at the par-3 4th with a par worth two under net. Mullin took it straight back at the 5th with a bogey worth a net par. Baker drew level again at the 6th, and then produced the best sustained stretch of golf anyone managed all trip: the 6th, 7th and 8th played in four net shots under par, the last of them a bogey at the par-5 8th, the second-hardest hole on the card.

Mullin, meanwhile, was busy putting together another entry for the record books. His gross round of +28 was the third-best TEG had recorded, and it kept him eight clear in the Jacket without any difficulty at all.

His net round was +8, and the reason was the last three holes. A triple 8 at the par-5 16th. A triple 7 at the 17th. A quadruple 8 at the 18th. Ten shots to par in twenty minutes of golf, delivered with the calm of a man filing a report.

Baker took the handover graciously. His +1 net was the second-best round in TEG history, the second time in two days he had posted that mark, and his gross 100 remains his best figure at Boavista. Three shots clear overnight, and thoroughly deserved.

It is only in the margins that you notice the flaw in the masonry. Baker played the 15th to the 18th in thirteen over: a triple, a double, and then quadruple bogey 8s at both the 17th and the 18th. Two of TEG's finest net rounds had now been assembled around a recurring habit of taking eight shots at par 4s. Nobody thought much of it at the time.

Neumann posted a gross +27, second-best all time, his second such round in two days. He also returned to the 14th and made another quintuple 8 there, which by any standard is devotion. Meller lost eleven shots across the 13th to the 16th, including a triple at the 14th, and improved his net round to +13.

## Round 3 — Baker Leads by Three, Trails by the Third

Monday 18 May, same course for the third time. The scores tightened everywhere, because familiarity does that. Mullin simply got better, which is a different thing.

Baker's final round began with a double bogey at the 1st, a double at the short 2nd, a triple 8 at the par-5 3rd, a triple at the 4th and a double at the 5th, the second-easiest hole on the course. Twelve shots to par in five holes. The quadruple bogeys that had lurked politely in the corners of his previous two cards now arrived all at once and refused to leave.

By the 3rd, Mullin was in front outright. By the 5th, the argument was over.

What followed was the round of Mullin's life and, at that point, of TEG's. Two under net, the finest round TEG had ever recorded, beating the mark he had set himself forty-eight hours earlier. The gross was +18 — 89 shots, the best gross round in TEG history, again beating his own. On a course he had first seen on the Saturday, he had now set four separate all-time marks in three days.

A three-shot deficit became a seven-shot victory. The Jacket margin stretched to eleven.

Elsewhere there was fine golf that nobody will remember. Neumann bogeyed the 8th, doubled the 9th — the hardest hole out there — bogeyed the 10th, and then holed out for a birdie 2 at the par-3 11th. He signed for 92, his best at Boavista.

And Meller, at last, played the golf he had come for: 91 shots, +20 gross, then the second-best gross round in TEG history, and a net +2. He had improved in every single round of the tournament, from +24 to +13 to +2, the only man in the field to do so.

It made no difference whatsoever. The Spoon had been decided at the 3rd hole on Saturday morning and had spent the intervening 51 holes waiting for him to catch up. Williams shot 105, his best of the three, which felt like the appropriate note on which to close.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy (net vs par).** Mullin +6, Baker +13, margin seven. Mullin led by four after R1 on the back of his level-par net round, lost the lead in R2 by dropping ten shots across the 16th, 17th and 18th, and reclaimed it outright at the 3rd hole of R3 with a bogey for a net par. Baker was, at that precise moment, five holes into a twelve-over start. Nine lead changes in all, five of them inside the first eight holes of the tournament.
- **The Green Jacket (gross).** Mullin +66, Neumann +77, margin eleven. Effectively over by tea on day one: the +20 opened a nine-shot cushion and the Jacket lead never changed hands after the 5th hole of R1. Mullin's three rounds included two all-time-best gross rounds and a third-best. Neumann's +29 and +27 were each, when posted, the second-best gross round in TEG history, and they bought him nothing but second place.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Meller, +39 net vs par. Bottom from the 3rd hole of R1 and bottom after all three rounds; there was exactly one bottom change in the entire tournament, and it was that one. The bill was the 11th to the 15th on day one, twenty shots in five holes, including the quintuple at the 14th.

## Player-by-player summary

- **David Mullin** — Rounds of level, +8 and −2 net; +20, +28 and +18 gross. Set the finest net round in TEG history twice and the best gross round twice, on a course he had never previously seen, and won both the Trophy by seven and the Jacket by eleven. His only lapse cost him ten shots over three holes and the lead for precisely one night.
- **Jon Baker** — Posted TEG's second-best net round on consecutive days, +4 then +1, and led by three going into the final day. Then took 12 shots to par off the first five holes of the closing round. Runner-up by seven, with the quadruple bogeys at the 17th and 18th in R2 reading, in hindsight, like a warning nobody troubled to open.
- **Stuart Neumann** — The most reliable striker in the field after the winner, with second-best-ever gross rounds in both R1 and R2 and a closing 92. Jacket runner-up by eleven. Also made quintuple bogey 8s at the par-3 14th on two consecutive days, which is the sort of consistency you do not advertise.
- **Henry Meller** — Last after every round and the Spoon settled by the 3rd hole of the tournament, yet the only man to improve every day: +24, +13, +2. Closed with 91 shots, then the second-best gross round TEG had recorded. It arrived roughly two days too late to matter.
- **Gregg Williams** — Level at the top of the leaderboard on the 2nd hole of the tournament, which was as good as it got. The back nine of R1 relieved him of 22 shots across seven holes, including a 9 at the 14th and another at the far gentler 17th. His 105 in the final round was the best of his three.
