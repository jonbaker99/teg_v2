# Sixth, Fifth, Third, Second — and Now First: Neumann Tops the Ladder

At Estoril, the shortest and gentlest course of the four, and the oldest club in Portugal, two cards came in on the same afternoon. David Mullin signed for 80, a new course record, beating the 86 that had stood across six prior visits. Alex Baker signed for 107, a new course-worst, exceeding the 106 that had stood across the same six. Twenty-seven shots apart, on the same pile, on a Mackenzie Ross parkland that nobody had ever accused of cruelty.

Neither number changed anything.

By the time those cards were handed in, Stuart Neumann had led the Trophy at the end of every round since the first evening, Gregg Williams had held the gross lead without interruption since Round 1, and Alex Baker had been bottom of the pile since the opening hole of the tournament. TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast was two processions marching in opposite directions, and the only thing Estoril did was print the extremes in large type.

## The Ladder

Neumann arrived with a career that read like a man patiently working his way up a ladder in the dark: sixth at TEG 9, fifth at TEG 10, third at TEG 12, second at TEG 15. Two Wooden Spoons behind him. No Trophy, no Green Jacket, nothing on the sideboard at all. One rung left.

He took it on the first afternoon. His 39 points at Oitavos Dunes, Arthur Hills's Atlantic links and TEG's third visit there, opened a five-point cushion. The lead did change hands twice that morning, at the 4th and the 5th, when Williams and then Mullin drew level. Neither took it outright. With the field still bunched and eighteen holes played, this was the ordinary jostling of men who have not yet worked out how bad the trip is going to be. It never happened again.

At Troia, Robert Trent Jones Sr.'s peninsula and a course entirely new to TEG, Neumann assembled the best three holes anyone played all tournament. A birdie at the par-4 10th, a par at the 11th — the easiest hole on the card — and then a four-point par at the 12th: eleven points in three holes. He then made a triple bogey at the 13th and posted 35. The lead went to seven.

Penha Longa, up in the Sintra hills and the hardest layout of the four, was where the arithmetic stopped being interesting. Neumann went round for 39 points while the rest of the field walked backwards down the mountain. Seven became twelve. Everything after that was ceremony.

Which is not to say it was tidy. At Estoril he lost nine shots to par in three holes from the 7th, the centrepiece being a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 8th, the hardest hole on the course. He signed for 43 points, his best round of the tournament, and a 92 gross — fourteen shots better than the 106 he managed on the same course at TEG 15.

A total of 156, and a winning margin of 13. The ladder had one rung left, and he stood on it for four days.

## The Inventory

Alex Baker came to Portugal as the holder of the Wooden Spoon, with two others already in the cabinet, and set about the defence with an efficiency that bordered on the professional. He was bottom of the Trophy standings after the 1st hole at Oitavos Dunes. He was still bottom at the last putt at Estoril, ninety-five holes later.

The Oitavos card is best read as a collection rather than a round. A 9 at the 1st. A 9 at the 7th. A 9 at the 11th. A 10 at the 17th, sextuple bogey, on a par 4. The closing three holes alone cost him eleven shots to par: a double at the 16th, the 10, and a triple at the last. The total was 113, ten worse than his previous visit here and a new course-worst across thirteen TEG rounds at the venue, beating the 111 that had survived the previous twelve.

Bottom place did wobble in that opening round — five changes, alternating between Alex Baker and Mullin, the last of them at the 15th. This was noise. The signal came the following day at Troia, at the 13th, where a triple bogey dropped him to outright last and left him there for the remaining 42 holes. Nobody remarked on it at the time. It was the single most decisive shot of the tournament that no one watched.

Troia was two long bleeds either side of one good hole. Holes 4 to 9 cost thirteen shots to par, a triple at the short 4th followed by five consecutive doubles. Holes 13 to 17 cost another thirteen. In between, a birdie at the par-4 10th brought in five points and ended a long run of bogey-or-worse. Thirty-two points.

Penha Longa was more of the same in mountain form: eleven shots gone across the 2nd to the 6th, twelve more across the 11th to the 14th, including a 9 at the par-5 12th. Thirty-three points.

And then Estoril, where the collection was completed. A 9 at the 1st, an 8 at the 3rd, a 9 at the 6th, and, at the par-4 12th, an 11. That is a septuple bogey, and it is the worst hole against par he has recorded in TEG. Holes 5 to 10 cost fourteen shots between them. The 107 was eight worse than his last visit and a second course-worst of the trip.

Thirty-three points again, 127 in total, last at the end of all four rounds. Back-to-back Spoons, with the receipts filed in triplicate.

## The Mountain

Gregg Williams arrived as the defending Trophy champion. He never got within a postcode of defending it, finishing second on Stableford, 13 adrift. He seemed entirely untroubled by this, on account of being busy elsewhere.

His gross round of 89 at Oitavos Dunes was a personal best at the course, improving his previous 91, and gave him a three-shot lead. Mullin drew level at the 6th that day; Jon Baker drew level at the 11th and again at the 14th at Troia. Nobody ever went past him. A 94 at Troia held the margin at four, and at that stage the Green Jacket looked like a competition.

Penha Longa ended it. Williams shot 87. Jon Baker shot 97, twelve worse than his previous round there, undone by three holes that cost eleven shots to par: a double at the 11th, a quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 12th — the easiest hole on the card, stroke index 17 — and an 8 at the 13th. Mullin shot 98, thirteen worse than his TEG 15 visit, with a quadruple bogey at the 16th, which is the hardest hole on the course and at least had the decency to be difficult.

Four shots became fourteen in an afternoon.

Mullin's record 80 at Estoril was therefore two rounds too late, a magnificent piece of work delivered to an address that had already been vacated. Williams matched it shot for shot, a personal best of his own at the course, and did so despite a quadruple bogey at the 14th. Jacket to Williams, +66, by 16. His second.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy.** Neumann led at the end of every round — 39 points at Oitavos Dunes, 35 at Troia, 39 at Penha Longa, 43 at Estoril — for 156 against Williams's 143. The only lead changes came at the 4th and 5th holes of the opening round, both level rather than outright. The 39 at Penha Longa, on the hardest of the four courses, stretched the lead from seven to twelve and settled it. A first Trophy, at the fifth attempt of the last five.
- **The Green Jacket.** Williams from Round 1 to the finish, never headed. The third round did it: his 87 at Penha Longa against Jon Baker's 97 and Mullin's 98 moved the margin from four to fourteen. Mullin's course-record 80 at Estoril took nothing back, because Williams shot 80 as well. Final margin 16, on +66.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Alex Baker, 127 points, bottom at the end of every round. Last place changed five times in Round 1 between him and Mullin; the last word came at Troia's 13th, where a triple bogey put him outright last for the final 42 holes. Two course-worsts, at Oitavos Dunes and Estoril, on the way.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Stuart Neumann** — Sixth, fifth, third, second, and now first. Led from the first evening to the last, closed with a tournament-best 43 points at Estoril in spite of a 9 at the hardest hole on the course, and won by 13. Two Wooden Spoons in the past; the sideboard now has better company.
- **Gregg Williams** — Surrendered the Trophy without much of a struggle and took a second Green Jacket instead, by 16. Personal bests at Oitavos Dunes (89) and Estoril (80), with the 87 at Penha Longa the round that actually won it.
- **Alex Baker** — Bottom from the 1st hole of the tournament to the final putt. Course-worsts at Oitavos Dunes (113) and Estoril (107), four nines in the opening round, and an 11 at Estoril's 12th, his worst hole against par on record. A second consecutive Spoon on 127.
- **David Mullin** — Nine Green Jackets already, and three rounds here spent nowhere, including a 98 at Penha Longa, thirteen worse than his TEG 15 self. Then the 80 at Estoril: a course record, fourteen better than his last visit, and worth precisely nothing to the margin.
- **Jon Baker** — Personal bests at Oitavos Dunes (92) and Estoril (93), the latter containing a quadruple bogey at the 11th, one of the hardest holes on the card. Between them, the 97 at Penha Longa, where a 10 at the easiest hole handed Williams the room he needed.
