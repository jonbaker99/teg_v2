# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third-best of his own career. He did not avoid the wreckage. He simply arranged for his to cost less than everyone else's.

The foundation was laid on the first two links. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit. The engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars, 13 points banked. The sequence ended at the 13th with a double bogey. At Littlestone he shot 85, seven better again, and another course best.

Prince's declined to co-operate. In Round 3 he took nine at the par-five 8th, a quadruple bogey worth precisely nothing, before recovering with 13 points over the closing four holes: three pars and a birdie at the last. Round 4 offered a companion piece. An eight at the 10th, another quadruple bogey. He was leading the tournament comfortably at the time. One can only assume he had decided the margin was becoming impolite.

It opened the door for Alex Baker, who had at one point trailed by nine. He answered with 10 points across the first three holes on Sunday and 11 more from the 13th to the 15th, and the deficit was two. Then came the 16th, and a ten. Baker's undoing had in truth begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5. A man can survive one such passage. Two begins to look like a method.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Two of the four rounds in Kent ended with Alex Baker writing a 10 on his card, and on both occasions the hole was numbered 16. Different courses, different holes, no relation beyond the digit. The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports, a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's on the Sunday, was worse in relative terms. A 10 at a par four. A sextuple bogey. Stroke index 17, the second-easiest hole on the card.

The Prince's ten hurt more for what preceded it. Baker had played the 13th, 14th and 15th in par, par, par. Three holes without dropping a gross shot, eleven Stableford points, a man visibly assembling a case for himself. The 16th withdrew the lot in a single sitting.

He was not alone in losing his footing there. Jon Baker arrived at that same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th, and made a double bogey for no points.

These were the headline numbers, not the complete set. Baker also had an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in round three. Five further holes returning nothing whatsoever. For all that, his opening 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was still four strokes better than his previous visit, which says a good deal about the previous visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. It was built on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than his previous mark there of 94, and 86 at Prince's in round three, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

It was not tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing on the card. Littlestone was worse in the wider sense: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he managed there on his previous visit, and 27 Stableford points.

Round three was the correction. He gained twelve points on the day, reaching 39, and the middle of it was genuinely solid. Pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points, three holes without dropping a gross shot, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. Between them sat an 8 at the par-five 12th. It ended the run.

Sunday brought more of the same steadiness in patches, gross pars at the 5th and 6th and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at one stage, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered. Eight still owing.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket at TEG 14 with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth-best of Mullin's own career at the metric.

The number was built on consistency across Kent's four links rounds. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his previous visit in TEG 13, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. None of those is spectacular in isolation. Together they were enough to keep him out of reach, which is the entire job description of a gross total.

Behind him, the chase was real but late. Jon Baker at one point trailed by 15 and closed to seven, the bulk of that work coming in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record. The previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits, and was Baker's own. Gregg Williams made up even more ground, trailing by 18 at his worst and finishing eight back. Williams's +76 is a personal-best Green Jacket gross total, and his 86 at Prince's in round three was a course best for him too.

The gap never closed far enough. Mullin's margin of seven at the finish flattered the pursuit. The lead was larger for most of the four days.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker finished last in the Stableford count and took the Wooden Spoon with 125 points across the four rounds. Twelve adrift of the next worst is not a margin. It is a separate competition.

The tournament's early links did him no favours, though he offered them plenty of assistance. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven strokes worse than the 85 he had managed on his previous visit. At Littlestone he posted 92 against a prior best of 79. That is thirteen strokes, mislaid in open country. His third round at Prince's brought 28 points, a total held down in part by a run of pars from the 14th that ended with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

The final round was a different matter, and it deserves saying plainly. Baker returned 39 points, an improvement of 11 on the previous day, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the mark of 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best on the layout, two strokes better than his last time there. The scoring came in a burst: five holes without dropping a gross shot from the 11th to the 15th, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th, a two-hole birdie streak that equals the all-time TEG record, and 14 points collected across holes 12 to 15.

The close undid some of it. A double bogey at the 16th ended the run, and he ran up an eight at the 18th. The course record stands. So does the Spoon.
