# First Blood at Boavista: Baker Runs Away, Mullin Rewrites the Record

Three cards were handed in at the Boavista scoring hut on the opening afternoon, and each of them altered the record book. Jon Baker signed for 90 gross, −9 net: the best round anyone had played in TEG on the Trophy metric. David Mullin signed for 88, +17: the best gross round TEG had recorded. Henry Meller signed for a 12 at the 17th, the worst hole relative to par in TEG history to that point, and went to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race.

The 17th is stroke index 17. On a card of eighteen holes, it is the second-easiest.

All four rounds were played at Howard Swan's parkland above Lagos, TEG's fourth to seventh visits to the venue and only its second trip to the Algarve, on a course of two halves that do not much resemble one another. By the Friday evening every man in the field had posted a personal best there, and the course record that had survived fifteen previous TEG visits had been taken apart. The tournament, in other words, was a festival of improvement.

It was also over after one round.

## Round 1 — Baker Plunders, Mullin Purrs, Meller Finds the 17th

The Tuesday round, the fourth TEG had played at Boavista, began with five men of settled reputation and ended with the Trophy essentially allocated.

Baker's 90 was the third-best gross round in TEG history at the time, which was not remotely the point. The point was that his previous best at Boavista was 100, set in TEG 2, and that his last visit here had produced a number 17 shots worse than the one he now handed in. A man does not find 17 shots on a golf course through refinement. He finds them because the handicap has not yet caught up, and Baker spent the afternoon calmly emptying it.

Mullin, meanwhile, purred round in 88 for +17, at that moment the finest gross round TEG had seen, and −1 net, itself third-best all-time on the Trophy metric. He drew level on gross at the 4th, as did Meller; neither took the lead outright. At the 6th Mullin drew level with Baker on the Trophy. That was the only lead change the Trophy would witness in four days, and with the field bunched after six holes it was nothing more than opening traffic.

Baker was gone by the turn and never came back.

At the bottom, the afternoon was busier. The Spoon passed between Stuart Neumann and Meller five times — Neumann at the 3rd, Meller at the 6th, Neumann again at the 13th and the 15th — before the 17th settled the matter. Meller took twelve shots there, seven over his net allowance, and fell from third to last. No hole in TEG had ever cost anyone more against par.

Gregg Williams then closed with a flourish of his own: triples at the 15th, the 16th and the 17th, and a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 18th. Thirteen shots dropped in four holes. The 15th is stroke index 1 and therefore had an excuse; the other three did not.

Baker led the Trophy by 8. Mullin led the Jacket by 2. Meller was last, and had every reason to think he had already seen the worst of it.

## Round 2 — Meller's Finest Hour, Baker's Quiet Larceny

He had not, but the Wednesday gave him a magnificent reprieve.

Meller went round in 89 for +18 and −2 net, at the time the second-best round in TEG history on both the gross and the Trophy measures, and a personal best at Boavista by some distance. It lifted him from last to third. For roughly eighteen hours, the reigning Wooden Spoon holder was a contender.

Baker also returned −2, and this is where the tournament's quiet larceny lives. His card read 97 gross, and it contained five consecutive double bogeys — the 10th, 11th, 12th, 13th and 14th, ten shots surrendered in a single unbroken sulk. He gained five on the field anyway. His lead went from 8 to 13.

Mullin's contribution to the day was a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 17th, which cost him four net and, in the wider scheme, nothing at all. His Jacket lead grew to 5. The 17th had now taken two of the field's leading men in two rounds, and was showing no signs of embarrassment about it.

Williams filed a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 10th. Neumann dropped to the bottom of the Spoon race at the 8th and stayed there to the close, an arrangement he was not consulted about and did not appear to enjoy.

## Round 3 — Eighty-Five: Mullin Takes Boavista Apart

The Thursday produced the single best piece of golf of the four days and, roughly ninety minutes earlier, the fastest collapse.

Mullin shot 85. It was a new Boavista course record, beating the 89 that had stood across fifteen prior TEG visits, and four better than his own previous best there. At +14 it was the best gross round in TEG history, superseding the mark he had set himself in the opening round. At −4 net it was joint third-best all-time on the Trophy metric. He gained four on Baker and moved his Jacket lead to 9, at which point the Green Jacket stopped being a competition and became an administrative process.

Behind him the standard was startling. Neumann posted 89 for +18, third-best gross round in TEG history. Williams shot 97 for −4, matching Mullin's net figure and equalling the third-best round TEG had recorded on the Trophy metric; the 97 was eight shots better than his previous Boavista best of 105, from TEG 2.

Baker, characteristically, made a mess of it and still won the day handsomely. His 92 for −7 was the second-best round in TEG history on the Trophy metric, and it included a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 5th and a quintuple-bogey 9 at the last. He gained three. His lead stood at 16 with a round to play, which is not a lead so much as a postcode.

The Spoon, by contrast, changed owner three times in three holes. Meller opened with a triple bogey at the 1st and fell from third to last. Williams responded at the 2nd with a 7 on a par 3 ranked stroke index 16, and took the bottom place off him. Then at the 3rd Meller made 10, and returned to last, where he remained for the rest of the tournament.

Three holes. Everything the Wednesday had built, dismantled and swept up.

## Round 4 — The 17th Claims One Last Victim

The Friday had nothing left to decide and a good deal left to survive.

Baker played it in level net par, which was ample. The Trophy closed at −18, an 18-shot margin, and a first TEG title for a man whose entire honours board previously consisted of finishing second in TEG 2. Mullin's +20 took his gross total to +72 and the Green Jacket by 12 from Neumann.

Williams spent the round adding to the archive: quadruple bogeys at the 3rd, the 7th and the 12th, taking his tournament total to seven. It is a body of work with a consistent house style. Meller matched him with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 7th, signed for +4, and confirmed a second consecutive Wooden Spoon at +16.

Which left the 17th, and one last piece of business. Neumann arrived at the second-easiest hole on the card and took 10, six over. It was the third large number the hole had extracted in four days, after Meller's 12 and Mullin's 9, and it completed a tidy set: a 12, a 9 and a 10, from three different players, on the hole the course rates as barely worth defending.

Howard Swan presumably designed it as a breather.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy (net vs par).** Baker won at −18, by 18 from Mullin, the ninth-best Trophy score in TEG history and his first title. It was settled on the opening afternoon: his −9 gave him an 8-shot lead after one round, and across the remaining 66 holes he was never headed. The lead grew to 13, then 16, then 18. The competition's only lead change came at the 6th hole of Round 1, when Mullin drew level — not outright, and routine with the field still bunched.
- **The Green Jacket (gross).** Mullin led wire to wire and won at +72, 12 clear of Neumann, whose +84 made him comfortably the second-best gross player of the four days. Mullin's rounds ran +17, +21, +14, +20. The decisive act was the course-record 85 in Round 3, which turned a 5-shot cushion into 9 and left the final round ceremonial. The two lead changes both came at the 4th hole of Round 1; neither was outright.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Meller took it at +16, his second in a row. His 12 at the 17th in Round 1 put him last for the first time; his 89 in Round 2 lifted him to third. Round 3 undid it inside three holes — the triple at the 1st, Williams's quadruple at the 2nd briefly taking it off him, and Meller's 10 at the 3rd handing it back for good. There were nine changes at the bottom in all, five of them in the opening round.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Jon Baker** — Won the Trophy at −18 with the best round in TEG history on day one and never trailed thereafter. His 90 in Round 1 was 17 shots better than his last visit to Boavista, and 10 better than anything he had managed here before. Won by 18 while making five consecutive doubles in Round 2 and a 9 at the last in Round 3, which tells you everything about how the front-running was done.
- **David Mullin** — The defending Trophy champion played the best golf in the field by every raw measure available: two TEG-record gross rounds, a course record of 85 that ended a mark standing since fifteen visits previously, and the Green Jacket by 12. He finished second on the Trophy by 18. Different competition, different metric, no consolation.
- **Henry Meller** — Arrived holding the Wooden Spoon and left holding it again. In between he produced 89 for −2 in Round 2, a personal Boavista best and one of the finest rounds TEG had then seen, which lifted him to third. The 12 at the 17th booked the Spoon in Round 1; the 10 at the 3rd in Round 3 confirmed the reservation.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Second in the Jacket at +84, including an 89 in Round 3 that was among the best gross rounds in TEG history at the time. Spent the back end of Round 2 at the bottom of the Spoon race, then closed the tournament's running theme with a 10 at the 17th.
- **Gregg Williams** — Improved his Boavista best by eight shots with a 97 in Round 3, worth −4 net and a share of the third-best Trophy round on record. He also compiled seven quadruple bogeys across the four days, held the bottom place for two holes of Round 3, and dropped 13 shots in the last four holes of Round 1. A full and varied ledger.
