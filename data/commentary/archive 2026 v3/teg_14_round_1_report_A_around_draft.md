# The Spoon Holder's Opening Statement

Royal Cinque Ports Golf Club, Deal, Kent. Thursday. The 2nd TEG round at this venue.

David Mullin scored 40 Stableford points. Best in the field, on one of the Open's most demanding championship links, on a course that has made considerably more distinguished golfers look fairly ordinary over the years.

Mullin arrived in Kent as the reigning Wooden Spoon holder. Three consecutive last-place finishes — a streak maintained with a persistence that his Stableford scoring had conspicuously failed to match until now. He scored 40. There will be a reasonable explanation for this and it will almost certainly not involve any of his previous TEGs.

---

**David Mullin — 40 Stableford points**

The round's decisive passage ran from the 9th to the 12th: par at the par-4 9th (3 points), birdie at the par-4 10th (4 points), par at the par-4 11th — one of the hardest on the course (3 points), par at the par-4 12th (3 points). Thirteen Stableford points across four consecutive holes. He drew level in the Trophy at the 11th, and took the outright lead at the 16th — with a par at the par-5 — at precisely the moment Alex Baker was completing a 10 on the same hole.

There was a double bogey at the par-4 13th along the way. It did not prevent him from leading both competitions at close of play.

**Alex Baker — 37 Stableford points**

The front nine provided early evidence of a theme. Holes 3, 4, and 5: double bogey at the par-5 3rd, double bogey at the par-3 4th (the easiest hole on the course, stroke index 18), double bogey at the par-5 5th — one of the hardest, stroke index 2. Six shots over par across three consecutive holes. He then drew level with Mullin for the Green Jacket lead at the par-4 6th, which suggested a certain resilience, or at least a certain capacity for switching channels.

The back nine paid the outstanding bill in full. At the par-4 11th — one of the hardest on the course, stroke index 3 — Baker returned an 8: quadruple bogey, zero Stableford points. At the par-5 16th, a 10: quintuple bogey, zero Stableford points. Eight points surrendered across two holes. Mullin's Trophy lead ceased at that moment to be a provisional arrangement.

Baker finished on 37 — three behind Mullin, and with a front-nine pattern that invited closer monitoring.

**Jon Baker — 29 Stableford points**

The defending Trophy champion moved to last place in the Wooden Spoon standings at the par-3 4th. A bogey. Stroke index 18 — the easiest hole on the course. He remained in last place for the remaining 14 holes.

His back nine contained three consecutive pars at H11, H12, and H13 — 9 Stableford points in three holes — which represented the round's most composed stretch by a player who needed more of it and found it too late. He closed on 29 points, four behind Williams in the Spoon standings and eleven behind Mullin.

**Gregg Williams — 33 Stableford points**

Williams absorbed a 9 at the par-5 3rd — quadruple bogey, zero points — and recovered steadily to post 33. Third in the Trophy, 4 clear of Jon Baker in the Spoon race. On a day when the field was characterised chiefly by its capacity for large numbers on individual holes, Williams was the one who kept his within reasonable range after the 3rd. This is not, perhaps, the kind of statement that fills the back pages. It is, however, the kind of statement that keeps you out of the Spoon.

---

Mullin drew level in the Trophy at H11 and took the outright lead at H16 — the same hole where Baker was completing his quintuple. The Jacket saw routine opening exchanges (Baker level at H6, Mullin back at H9) before Mullin held it from H9 through the close. Jon Baker arrived at the foot of the Wooden Spoon standings at the par-3 4th, on the course's most forgiving hole, and stayed there for the afternoon.
