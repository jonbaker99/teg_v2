# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total recorded and the second-best of his own career.

The margin was built early and then simply never given back. Neumann held the Trophy lead after round two at Troia. He still held it after round three at Penha Longa. He carried it through the closing round at Estoril without ever appearing to consider the alternative. Williams, meanwhile, led the Jacket standings at each of those same checkpoints. Two competitions ran side by side for three days and neither man conceded an inch of his own.

The final round was not flawless. At Estoril's 8th, a par 4 and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann ran up a nine. A quintuple bogey, worth no points at all. He had spent three days assembling a cushion for precisely this sort of occasion. The cushion absorbed it without a word of complaint.

Estoril otherwise gave him his best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and it arrived on the day the Trophy was settled. Timing of that order cannot be taught.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin's tournament ended a very long way from where it began. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he ran up a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. Fifth is the bottom of the pile. There is nowhere below it to go.

Round three at Penha Longa was worse in places. A quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, before he gathered himself for pars at 17 and 18. The round came to 30 points, and his 98 there was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was a different matter entirely. Mullin birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through a bogey-bogey-double stretch at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th stopped the slide. The back nine held. Nine points from the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to close.

The total was 45 points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. A man last seen at the foot of the Spoon standings had gone out and set the course record. Nobody had asked him for one.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to change course. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th, and signed for 113 gross. It was a new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits. At Troia the following day the damage was more concentrated: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples followed by two doubles.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers found the same piece of ground. Jon Baker made a double bogey six at the 11th, then ran up a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth no points, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, and three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the four where he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his prior best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes, doing something similar but at a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, then a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, then a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots dropped across four holes, and, like his brother, a three-hole run without a net par from the 12th onward. The brothers arrived at that stretch separately and left it in much the same condition.

The final round at Estoril split them again. Jon's one significant lapse came at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card, where he made an eight. Alex's round was a sequence of them: nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled over the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every single one a double or worse. Then came the 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst score he has ever made on a par four. The round totalled 107 gross, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. That is two all-time course-worst records in one tournament. Most men pass through a golf course without leaving a trace. Alex left two. His tournament finished on 127 points, and with it the Wooden Spoon.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never really in doubt, and the final margin of 16 shots over David Mullin says as much. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

What it was not was tidy. Troia, a course none of the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his worst passages of the tournament: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six gross shots gone in as many holes.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered immediately with a birdie at the short 5th, which felt at the time like order being restored. A bogey-triple-double run through the 6th, 7th and 8th then undid the repair in full. He steadied on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and collecting nine points across the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit here.

Estoril produced both his best round and his loudest mistake. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit, and it came despite an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that ended a run of three straight pars. Sixteen shots is a margin that forgives a great deal. It was asked to.
