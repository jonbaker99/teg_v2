# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points. Gregg Williams finished 13 behind, which is the sort of gap that stops being a contest and becomes a commute. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total recorded and the second-best of Neumann's own career.

The margin was built early and never surrendered. He led after round two at Troia. He led after round three at Penha Longa. He led through the closing round at Estoril. Williams, at each of those same checkpoints, led the Jacket standings. For three days the two men ran adjacent campaigns and declined, with great courtesy, to interfere with one another.

The final round was not flawless. Estoril's 8th is a par four and stroke index 1, the hardest hole on the card, and Neumann greeted it with a nine. A quintuple bogey. No points whatsoever. He had spent three days assembling a cushion for precisely this eventuality, and he sat down on all of it at once. It cost him nothing beyond tidiness, and tidiness has never been legal tender here.

Estoril otherwise gave him his best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and he chose the day the Trophy was settled to do it.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin ended a very long way from where he began. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he took a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. Fifth is the bottom of the pile. One hole, three places, and a new view of the world. Round three at Penha Longa was worse in places: a quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, though he collected himself with pars at 17 and 18. The round came to 30 points, and his 98 there was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was a different matter entirely. Mullin birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through a bogey-bogey-double stretch at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th stopped the rot. The back nine simply held. Nine points from the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to close.

The total was 45 points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. Six visits had left the record standing. The man who had been last in the Spoon race on day one took it down.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to develop. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th, and signed for 113 gross. That is a new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits. Twelve visits had probed for the floor without finding it. Alex went straight through it before lunch. At Troia the following day the damage was more concentrated: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples followed by two doubles.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers found the same piece of ground. Jon Baker made a double bogey six at the 11th, then ran up a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth nothing, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, and three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the four days in which he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his prior best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes, doing much the same thing at a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, then a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots across four holes, and, like his brother, a three-hole run without a net par from the 12th onward. Whatever it was, it ran in the family.

The final round at Estoril separated them. Jon's one significant lapse was an eight at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card. Alex's round was constructed almost entirely out of lapses: nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled over the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every single one a double or worse. Then came Estoril's 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst score he has ever made on a par four. This was Estoril's 12th, not Penha Longa's; the hole number is coincidence, the golf was not. The round totalled 107 gross, a second course-worst of the tournament, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. He finished on 127 points and took the Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, for the third time.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never in doubt, and the final margin of 16 shots over David Mullin makes the point without needing help. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

It was not, however, tidy. Troia, a course none of the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his worst passages of the trip: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six gross shots gone.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered at once with a birdie at the short 5th, at which point a bogey-triple-double run through the 6th, 7th and 8th dismantled the repair work. He settled on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and collecting nine points across the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit here.

Estoril produced both his best round and his loudest error. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit, and it was not even the only 80 posted at Estoril that afternoon. It also survived an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that ended a run of three straight pars. He won the Jacket by 16. He did it while occasionally forgetting how to play golf, which is the local standard for dominance.
