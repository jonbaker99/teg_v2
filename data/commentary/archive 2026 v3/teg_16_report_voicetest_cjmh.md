# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total ever recorded and the second-best of his own career. He earned it, and at no point did he look like handing it back.

The margin was built early and thereafter defended with the grim patience of a man guarding a bridge he has no intention of explaining. Lead after round two at Troia. Lead after round three at Penha Longa. Lead intact through the closing round at Estoril. Williams led the Jacket standings at each of those same checkpoints, so for three days the two competitions ran alongside one another like duelling processions, neither man ceding an inch in his own.

The final round was not flawless. At Estoril's 8th, a par 4 and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann took nine. A quintuple bogey, worth no points whatsoever. It was an act of magnificent indifference to his own scorecard, committed at the precise moment the tournament was his. With the cushion he had assembled it cost him nothing but tidiness, which is broadly what a 13-point lead is for.

Estoril otherwise gave him the best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and he elected to do it on the day the Trophy was settled.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin ended a considerable distance from where he began. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he ran up a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points shunted him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. Fifth being, in a field of five, the bottom of the pile. Round three at Penha Longa was in places worse: a quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, though he composed himself sufficiently to par 17 and 18. The round came to 30 points, and his 98 was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was another proposition entirely. Mullin birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through bogey, bogey, double at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th halted the drift. The back nine simply held: nine points from the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to finish.

Forty-five points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. A man who had spent the opening round at the very bottom of the Spoon standings left holding the course record. Golf does not usually apologise this handsomely.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to improve. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th, and signed for 113 gross. That is a new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits, which is to say twelve years of collective incompetence surpassed in a single afternoon. At Troia the following day the destruction was more concentrated: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples followed by two doubles.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers located the same stretch of turf. Jon Baker made a double bogey six at the 11th, then a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth no points, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the tournament in which he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his prior best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes, doing the same thing at a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, then a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots dropped across four holes and, like his brother, a three-hole run without a net par from the 12th onward. Whatever the family were attempting there, they were attempting it in unison.

The final round at Estoril separated them once more. Jon's single lapse came at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card, where he made an eight. Alex's round was constructed almost entirely out of lapses: nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled over the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every one of them a double or worse. Then the 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst he has ever scored on a par four in his life. The round came to 107 gross, a second course-worst of the trip, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. He finished on 127 points and took the Wooden Spoon, his third, and the second in succession. Two all-time course records, both of them in the wrong direction. Few men leave a mark on this tournament at all.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never seriously threatened, and the final margin of 16 shots over David Mullin makes the point without needing help. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

Tidy it was not. Troia, a course none of the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his most distressing passages: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six gross shots gone in as many holes.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered at once with a birdie at the short 5th, whereupon a bogey, triple and double through the 6th, 7th and 8th dismantled the repair entirely. He recovered on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and gathering nine points across the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit here.

Estoril supplied both his best round and his loudest error. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit, and it arrived despite an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that terminated a run of three straight pars. He did not avoid the wreckage. He merely arranged for his to cost the least.
