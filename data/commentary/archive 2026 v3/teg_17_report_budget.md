# The Coronation at Óbidos

There is a photograph nobody took. Jon Baker on the 16th green at Royal Óbidos, putter still in hand. Behind him, six holes — the 11th through the 16th — worth twenty Stableford points. Birdie at the 11th. Birdie at the short 13th, the easiest hole on the card. Pars at 14, 15 and 16. The Trophy lead stood at eleven, the Jacket lead was widening by the hole, and the 86 he would sign for was the lowest gross round ever recorded on Seve Ballesteros's final design, a course TEG had visited eighteen times before.

Consider who he had been. Fourth on the Trophy. Then fourth. Then fourth again. Three tournaments spent in the particular purgatory of being nobody's problem — too good to laugh at, too far back to fear, a man whose name turned up in reports inside the phrase "and behind them". He arrived on the Lisbon Coast, for its sixth TEG visit, as the perennial almost. He left with both of the things worth having.

Somebody had to be the counterweight. Gregg Williams came in as the reigning Green Jacket holder, on back-to-back Jacket wins, a four-time Trophy winner with precisely one Wooden Spoon in nine previous TEGs. He led the Green Jacket after eighteen holes. He finished the four days at the bottom of everything.

## Round 1 — A Single Point Clear at Praia

The Saturday began at Praia D'El Rey, Cabell Robinson's links-meets-parkland hybrid, on its fifth TEG outing. John Patterson led the Trophy at the close by a single point. This requires qualification. He had a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st and another 8 at the 6th, and led anyway. His back nine was rescued at the 12th, where a birdie halted a run of bogey-or-worse and reminded the field that Patterson, on his day, can play.

The Trophy lead changed hands twice, in the ordinary way a bunched field jockeys on day one: Jon Baker outright at the 3rd, Patterson reclaiming it at the 16th. No pulse anywhere was affected.

Williams took the Green Jacket with a 93, seven shots better than his last visit here. He drew level at the 17th and finished three clear. Given what follows, hold that in mind like a receipt. David Mullin, meanwhile, was quietly loading the springs: sixteen points across holes 12 to 16, a birdie at the 13th, pars at 12, 14 and 16, the finish of a man who has found something and intends to use it.

At the other end, Alex Baker arrived carrying back-to-back Wooden Spoons and went round in 114. In twenty-four previous visits to Praia D'El Rey, no TEG player had scored worse than 110. The engine room: a quadruple 8 at the 4th, then a sextuple-bogey 10 at the 5th, the second-hardest hole on the course, and a quintuple 9 to close at the 18th. He was bottom overnight, comfortably.

## Round 2 — Two Course Records at the Cliffs, In Both Directions

Cynthia Dye's West Cliffs, on only its second TEG visit, gave up its best and worst rounds on the same Sunday afternoon. Coincidence seems the wrong word.

Mullin's 82 was the round of the trip and the new course record. It beat the previous mark, set across six prior visits, by six shots. Fourteen points came in four holes: bogey, par, birdie, birdie at the 7th through the 10th. By the 18th he had drawn level on the Green Jacket, and he carried a three-shot lead into the third round. Twelve shots better than his last appearance here. None of it was luck.

The other record went the other way. Patterson signed for 116, one worse than anyone had managed here before. It opened with a quadruple 8 at the 3rd, the hardest hole on the course. Then, between the 8th and the 15th, he shed twenty-seven shots: double, triple, triple, triple, double, a quintuple-bogey 10 at the 13th, triple, and a sextuple-bogey 11 at the 15th. He had arrived at West Cliffs leading the tournament. By the 10th he was bottom of the Spoon race.

In the middle of all this, without ceremony, the tournament was decided. Jon Baker took the outright Trophy lead at the 1st. He held it for the remaining fifty-three holes and was never headed. The mechanism was mundane: fourteen points across the 2nd to the 5th — par, par at the brutal 3rd, birdie, par. His 86 was fifteen shots better than his last visit to West Cliffs and a personal best on the course, the first of two he would set in four days.

Alex Baker went round in 105, his own venue best by five, with sixteen points between the 4th and the 8th and a birdie at the short 12th, the easiest hole out there, immediately after quadruple 8s at the 10th and 11th. In absolute terms it was not a good round. It got him off the floor, and the floor is where the cutlery is decided.

## Round 3 — Jon Baker Rewrites Seve's Course

Royal Óbidos, Monday, fourth TEG visit, and the pivot in every sense.

The Green Jacket was still live at the first tee. Jon Baker drew level at the 2nd. Mullin went outright ahead at the 6th and held the lead of the tournament's premier gross competition for exactly one hole, roughly the time it takes to walk to the next tee. It was the high point of his campaign. Baker was level again at the 7th, and clear thereafter.

Then came the 86: a new course record, one better than the mark standing after eighteen previous visits, and eleven shots better than his own 89 from TEG 9. Twenty points across the 11th to the 16th. Eight shots clear on the Jacket at the close, eleven points ahead on the Trophy. Patterson posted a personal best here too, a 94, six better than TEG 9. In another tournament that would have been the headline. Here it registered as weather.

Williams's afternoon had a different character. A birdie at the 11th stopped a run of bogey-or-worse, and the round looked survivable. Then, from the 12th to the 18th, he made seven consecutive double bogeys: the 12th, the second-hardest hole; the 13th, the easiest; and on through 14, 15, 16, 17 and 18 without deviation. Fourteen shots, seven holes, no variation at all. Twenty-six points for the round, worst in the field. He dropped outright to the bottom of the Spoon race at the 14th. He stayed there for the remaining twenty-two holes.

Alex Baker was busy too: fifteen shots gone between the 12th and the 16th, a sextuple-bogey 10 at the 14th among them, plus a quadruple 9 at the last. It changed nothing. The reigning Spoon holder had handed it on. The recipient was the reigning Green Jacket champion.

## Round 4 — Procession at Praia

Back to Praia D'El Rey on the Tuesday for its sixth TEG round. Eleven points and eight shots in the bank, nothing left to determine. Jon Baker made the point by playing rather badly and winning anyway. A quadruple-bogey 9 at the 10th, another quadruple 9 at the 17th, the hardest hole on the course; earlier he had stopped a bogey-strewn opening with a birdie at the 4th. Two nines in a closing round, and the margins still finished at eighteen points and thirteen shots.

Patterson's farewell was more emphatic. A sextuple-bogey 9 at the short 14th, then a sextuple 10 at the 15th, one of the hardest holes here, with quadruple 8s at the 1st and 18th as bookends.

Alex Baker dropped twenty-five shots between the 5th and the 13th: double, double, triple, triple, double, double, triple, then a quadruple 9 at the 12th and a quadruple 8 at the 13th. He was in no danger whatsoever. The Spoon had been settled a day earlier by a superior competitor. Williams opened with a quintuple 9 at the 1st. By then it read less as a score than as a statement of position.

Final figures. Jon Baker, 166 points, the ninth-best Trophy total ever recorded, eighteen clear of Mullin's 148. Jon Baker, +67 gross for the Green Jacket, his third, thirteen clear of Mullin's +80. Gregg Williams, 123 points, and the cutlery.

The Trophy turned on one hole, the 1st at West Cliffs, fifty-six holes from the end. The Jacket turned on the 7th at Royal Óbidos. The Spoon turned on the 14th of that same Óbidos card, a different hole on the same afternoon, and after those three moments the outcome never wavered by so much as a putt.

## The men, in brief

- **Jon Baker** — Fourth, fourth and fourth on the Trophy going in. Out the other end: 166 points and +67 gross, both by margins that made the closing round decorative. Course record at Royal Óbidos, personal best at West Cliffs, third Green Jacket. The nearly-man filed his paperwork and left.
- **David Mullin** — Runner-up in both competitions, and author of the finest round anyone played: an 82 that lowered the West Cliffs record by six. He led the Green Jacket outright for one hole of the third round. That was the whole of his time at the front.
- **Gregg Williams** — Reigning Jacket holder, back-to-back winner, four-time Trophy champion, three shots clear after eighteen holes. Then seven consecutive doubles to finish at Royal Óbidos, a 26-point round, and his second Wooden Spoon in ten TEGs. A complete reversal, conducted without visible resistance.
- **John Patterson** — Led the tournament overnight after the opening round, with two quadruple 8s on his card and no evident embarrassment. Lost the lead at the first hole of the second round, shed twenty-seven shots in eight holes, and signed for a 116, the worst round West Cliffs has seen. His personal best at Royal Óbidos passed without comment.
- **Alex Baker** — Set a Praia D'El Rey course-worst 114 on day one, at a venue that had hosted twenty-four previous rounds without anyone going near it. Then a personal-best 105 at West Cliffs, which was enough. Two Spoons in, none out.
