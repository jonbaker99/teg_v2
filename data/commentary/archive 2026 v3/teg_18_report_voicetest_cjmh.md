# An Eleven, a Ten, and the Trophy: Alex Baker Wins in Catalonia

## Alex Baker's Trophy: 169 points, two field-best rounds, and a course-worst gross that cost him nothing

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It is the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of his own career. He led the Stableford count after each of the final three rounds, and from the moment he got to the front after R2 nobody laid a glove on him.

The scoring came in bursts, as though rationed by an unseen authority. On the Tour Course in R1 he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run built on bogeys at stroke-index holes rather than on anything a spectator would remember, then added 13 more across 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-five 15th. His largest single seizure came in R3 on the Stadium Course: 21 points from holes 11 to 16. Six consecutive holes without a blank. In this company that is close to statesmanship.

The gross figures record a separate and less flattering history. In R2 he made an 8 at the par-three 16th. He then went to the par-five 18th and made an 11. Both are personal worsts against par, achieved within three holes of one another, which suggests a certain thoroughness. He signed for 106, a new PGA Catalunya Tour course-worst, beating a mark of 105 that had stood across 22 prior visits. Twenty-two visits of accumulated human incompetence, dismantled in a single afternoon. R4 brought a 10 at the 14th and an 8 at the 17th, by which point they were merely furnishings. His best gross of the four days was 98 on the Tour and 100 on the Stadium, against prior bests of 92 and 93. It cost him the Green Jacket, where he slid from second after R1 to fifth. On Stableford, it cost him nothing whatsoever.

## David Mullin: an 11 at the 15th, then the lowest gross round ever played at the Stadium

**David Mullin**

Mullin visited both extremes available to a golfer, and the first arrived at the 15th on the Tour Course in Round 1. The par five took him eleven strokes. A sextuple bogey, no Stableford points, and the worst score he has ever recorded on a par five in his life. The consequences were administrative as well as spiritual: the hole moved him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, which is to say the bottom of it.

Two days later, on the Stadium Course, he did something the tournament had never witnessed. His 84 gross in Round 3 is a new course record at the Stadium, beating the previous mark of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was also a comprehensive repudiation of his own past: his previous best there was 91 across five earlier visits, and the round was eight strokes better than his last time out on it. For one afternoon he was the finest golfer ever to walk the place.

Round 4 arrived to clarify that the record had not altered his character. Through the closing stretch he went four holes without a net par: a double bogey at the 14th, a triple at the 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. Nine shots went in four holes, and with them five Stableford points he was entitled to consider his own. A tournament, then, of one number nobody will forget and several he would prefer nobody mentioned again.

## John Patterson's second place, and the eight holes on the opening morning

John Patterson's tournament was defined, and very nearly ended, in one stretch of the opening morning on the Tour Course. At the 3rd he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, and then declined to stop. Holes 3 through 10 cost him 22 shots to par, including triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th. Across the seven holes from the 3rd to the 9th he did not make a single net par. By the 4th he was bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, having dropped from fourth to fifth in the space of one hole.

What followed explains why he was still in the argument at the close. Later in the same round he took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, three pars and a bogey at the par-five 15th. In round two he made another eight at the 4th and leaked 11 shots between the 6th and the 10th, then replied with 16 points across the 11th to the 15th. That round produced a 94, his best gross on the Tour Course, bettering his previous mark of 96. The man was losing and winning the same tournament simultaneously.

The pattern held to the final day. After a triple at the 14th and a double at the 15th on the Stadium, he halted proceedings with a bogey at the 16th and a birdie at the 17th. Second place, constructed entirely out of the recoveries, with the collapses left in as load-bearing walls.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: reigning Wooden Spoon holder to +66 and a 14-shot win / Jon Baker's Wooden Spoon: the defending Trophy champion finishes 23 points adrift of the next man

**Round 1, Tour Course.** Gregg Williams opened in the manner the holder of a Wooden Spoon is contractually obliged to: a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 7th, a further quadruple bogey, an 8, at the 17th, and a haemorrhage of dropped shots running from the 2nd to the 14th, taking in triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th. A birdie at the par-5 15th eventually applied a tourniquet. He signed for 31 points. Jon Baker, by contrast, had led the Green Jacket gross race since the 5th hole of that same round.

**Round 2, Tour Course.** Baker's tenure at the summit lasted 20 holes. A bogey at the 7th on the Sunday handed the gross lead to Williams. Baker responded in the evening: a birdie at the 14th began a run of five holes without dropping a gross shot, worth 16 points to the 18th, and by the close he was Jacket leader again, with Alex Baker in front in the Trophy. Williams staged his own insurrection over the identical closing stretch, 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, four pars in succession and not a gross shot conceded, and posted 42 points. An 11-point improvement on his opening round, which was not a high bar to clear, but he cleared it by a distance.

**Round 3, Stadium Course.** The Jacket changed hands for the last time, and early. Williams parred the 2nd and took the outright gross lead. Baker's round disintegrated at the 4th, where a 9, a quintuple bogey on a par 4, was entered in the ledger as a blank. By nightfall the boards read Alex Baker in the Trophy and Williams in the Jacket, and they were not going to read anything else again.

**Round 4, Stadium Course.** Williams birdied the par-3 5th, parred the 6th, and gathered nine points across the 8th, 9th and 10th, closing out an 84 that stands as his best gross score in seven visits to the Stadium, eight shots better than his last time round it.

The two ledgers closed in opposite corners of the room. Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin: the sixth-best gross total in TEG history and the best of his own career, achieved by a man who had come to Catalonia holding the Spoon. Jon Baker, the defending Trophy champion, finished on 126 Stableford points, 23 adrift of the next man, and collected it from him.
