# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third-best of his own career, and he earned every point of it, including the ones he later attempted to give away.

The foundation was laid on the first two links. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit there. The engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars, 13 points banked without fuss. He then made a double bogey at the 13th. Momentum, in this company, has a half-life measured in holes. At Littlestone he shot 85, seven better again, and another course best. For two days he looked like a man who had forgotten he was bad at this.

Prince's declined to indulge him. In Round 3 he stood on the par-five 8th with the tournament comfortably in hand and took nine. A quadruple bogey. No points at all. It was an act of magnificent indifference to his own position, and he followed it by playing the closing four holes for 13 points, three pars and a birdie at the last, as though nothing had occurred. Round 4 brought another quadruple bogey, an eight at the 10th. He was, by then, treating the surrender of a stroke cushion as a form of hospitality.

The door duly opened for Baker, who had at one stage trailed by nine. He took 10 points from the first three holes on Sunday and 11 more from the 13th to the 15th, and the gap came down to two. Then he arrived at the 16th and ran up a ten. His unravelling had in truth begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5 and never quite got them back.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Twice in four rounds in Kent, Alex Baker wrote a 10 on his card. Both times the number arrived at a hole called the 16th. Different courses, different holes, the same digit sitting there like an accusation.

The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports, a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's on the Sunday, was the worse offence. A 10 at a par four. A sextuple bogey. Stroke index 17, the second-easiest hole on the card.

What makes the Prince's 10 genuinely tragic is the approach to it. Baker had played the 13th, 14th and 15th in par, par, par, three holes without dropping a gross shot, eleven Stableford points, a man visibly reassembling a golf tournament. The 16th removed all of it in a single hole. He was not the only one to lose his footing on that green. Jon Baker reached the same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th, and made a double bogey for nothing.

These were merely the headline numbers. Baker also had an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in the third round. Five more holes returning precisely nothing. His opening 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was, for what it is worth, four strokes better than his last visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. It rested on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than the 94 he had managed there before, and 86 at Prince's in the third round, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

None of it was tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing. Littlestone was the greater indignity: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he shot there last time, and 27 Stableford points for the day.

Round three was the correction, and it was emphatic. He gained twelve points on the day to reach 39. The middle of the round was genuinely good golf: pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points and three holes without conceding a gross shot, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. In between he took an 8 at the par-five 12th, which is where the run stopped and where, one suspects, the enjoyment did too.

Sunday brought the same steadiness in patches, gross pars at the 5th and 6th and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at his lowest point, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered. Still eight short. The finest gross golf of his TEG career, and it bought him nothing at all.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker. His ninth. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth-best of his own career at the metric.

It was built on consistency, which in Kent counts as an exotic skill. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his TEG 13 visit, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. Taken individually, none of these numbers would trouble a scorer. Taken together, they were untouchable.

The chase behind him was real, and late. Baker trailed by 15 at his worst and closed to seven, most of that work done in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record. The previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits, and it had been Baker's own. Williams made up more ground still, 18 behind at his lowest and eight back at the finish, his +76 a personal-best Green Jacket total and his 86 at Prince's in round three a course best.

Neither got close enough. A margin of seven at the finish rather flatters the field, given how much larger it had been for most of the four days.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker arrived in Kent as defending Trophy champion and left with the Wooden Spoon, 125 points, twelve behind the next worst man in the field. Not a narrow defeat. A category of its own.

The early links were merciless. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven worse than the 85 he had posted on his previous visit. At Littlestone he also managed 92, against a prior best there of 79. Thirteen shots. The third round at Prince's yielded 28 points, a total dragged down by a run of pars from the 14th that concluded with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

And then the final round happened, and it was the best golf of his life on the layout. Baker returned 39 points, eleven better than the day before, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best there, two strokes better than his last time round. The scoring arrived in a burst: five holes from the 11th to the 15th without dropping a gross shot, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th, which equals the all-time TEG record for a birdie streak, and 14 points across holes 12 to 15. For roughly an hour, the Wooden Spoon was somebody else's problem.

Then a double bogey at the 16th ended the run, and an eight at the 18th ended the argument. The Spoon went home with him.
