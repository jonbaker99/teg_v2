# The Coronation at Óbidos

## The 16th green at Royal Óbidos, late afternoon

Consider Jon Baker, walking off the 16th at Royal Óbidos on the third afternoon with twenty Stableford points in his pocket from the previous six holes — birdie, bogey, birdie, par, par, par — gathered across a stretch of Seve Ballesteros's final routing which had, until this Monday, been inspected by eighteen prior TEG rounds and seen off every one of them. He is on his way to an 86. It is the course record. It is also his second new personal best at a Lisbon Coast course in two days. The Trophy lead, which was two overnight, is about to become eleven. The Jacket advantage stands at eight, and rising.

To appreciate the scale of the thing you need to know who Jon Baker had been until the opening morning. Three TEGs, three fourth places on the Trophy: a man so dependably nearly-there that fourth had stopped being a finishing position and become a personality trait. The perennial bridesmaid of Stableford, slightly hunched, impeccably good-natured about it.

What follows is the story of how, across four consecutive days on the Lisbon Coast — the sixth such visit, Praia D'El Rey bookending West Cliffs and Royal Óbidos in the middle — that stopped being true. And how, at the other end of the leaderboard, the reigning Green Jacket holder discovered that defending a title is not something that happens while you stand still.

---

## Act I — A Single Point Clear at Praia

The Saturday round at Praia D'El Rey, Cabell Robinson's links-meets-parkland routing on its fifth TEG outing, opened the way the venue tends to open: everybody arriving at the first tee in good order, several of them then playing their second, third, fourth and fifth shots in noticeably worse. John Patterson, by close of play, was a single Stableford point clear of the field, which on the evidence of the round itself was slightly miraculous. He had run up a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st, repeated the trick at the 6th, and steadied himself only on the back nine, with a birdie at the par-5 12th that interrupted what had been a bogey-or-worse run.

Gregg Williams, meanwhile, sat top of the Green Jacket on a gross 93, three clear and, by his own recent standards, unremarkable. The reigning Jacket holder, back-to-back winner of the thing, four-time Trophy champion in his time, Williams arrived in Lisbon with the bearing of a man who knew exactly where the cutlery drawer was and had no intention of opening it. His 93 contained a quadruple at the par-4 5th, one of the harder holes on the course, but he drew level on the Jacket at the 17th and edged ahead by the close. David Mullin did what Mullin does, which is wait: sixteen points across holes 12 to 16, a birdie at the 13th, pars at 12, 14 and 16, the sort of stretch that whispers rather than shouts.

And at the very bottom, Alex Baker, who arrived with back-to-back Wooden Spoons already in his luggage and set about consolidating the portfolio with 114 gross — a new course-worst at a venue that had hosted twenty-four prior TEG rounds without anyone locating a route to a number that high. The damage centred on a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 5th, with supporting work from a quadruple at the 4th and a quintuple 9 at the 18th. After eighteen holes the Spoon was his.

The leaderboard at this stage looked, in hindsight, like a magician's setup: Patterson nominally ahead, Williams in charge of the Jacket, Alex at the bottom, Jon Baker fourth-ish and saying nothing. Everybody precisely where you'd have put them. Nothing yet seen.

## Act II — Two Course Records at the Cliffs, in Both Directions

Sunday at West Cliffs, Cynthia Dye's clifftop design, on only its second TEG outing — and in a single round it produced both the best and the worst gross scores anybody has posted there.

The best belonged to David Mullin. An 82, six shots inside the previous course record, anchored by a fourteen-point burst across holes 7 to 10 — bogey, par, birdie, birdie — and otherwise unblemished. It was the round of the tournament. It was also, as things worked out, very nearly the only stretch of golf in which Mullin led the Jacket: he drew level at the 18th to carry it into the next morning, then held it outright for exactly six holes the following afternoon before it was removed from him. A Jacket lead with a half-life measured in single holes.

The worst belonged to John Patterson, who twenty-four hours after sitting a point clear of the Trophy signed for a 116. The previous course-worst was 115; he beat it by one, a margin suggesting not misfortune so much as commitment. The route began with a quadruple at the par-4 3rd, the hardest hole out there, and continued across holes 8 to 15, over which Patterson bled twenty-seven shots: double, triple, triple, triple, double, a quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 13th, triple, and a sextuple-bogey 11 at the par-5 15th. He dropped to the bottom of the Spoon race at the 10th and remained there for the rest of the afternoon.

Between Mullin's ascension and Patterson's descent, Jon Baker did the thing that settled the Trophy. At the 1st hole of the round he took the outright lead from Patterson, and he held it for the remaining fifty-six holes of the tournament. Fifty-six holes; never headed again. The taking of it was undramatic — fourteen points across holes 2 to 5, a par on the SI 1 third, a birdie at the 4th — the sort of stretch a man assembles while the wheels come off everybody else's wagon in the background and he, alone, has thought to pack a wrench.

Alex Baker, for his part, posted a personal-best 105 at West Cliffs, five better than his previous visit, built on a sixteen-point surge across holes 4 to 8 and a recovery birdie at the par-3 12th, the easiest hole on the course, taken directly after consecutive quadruple-bogey 8s at the 10th and 11th. The PB did not vault him up the field, but it lifted him off the basement floor. The Spoon was now formally available. Patterson was holding it.

Two rounds in: Jon Baker leading the Trophy by two, Mullin level on the Jacket, Patterson at the bottom, Alex Baker free at last. The lid was off.

## Act II, continued — Jon Baker Rewrites Seve's Course

Monday at Royal Óbidos, Seve Ballesteros's last design, fourth TEG visit. This is the pivot; this is where it was decided.

Jon Baker shot 86. The previous course record, across eighteen prior TEG rounds at Royal Óbidos, was 87 — his own 89 from TEG 9 among many others left considerably behind. The Jacket arithmetic went like this: level with Mullin at the 2nd; Mullin outright at the 6th and holding it for one hole; Jon level again at the 7th, then clear, then clear for good, so that by the time he walked off 18 he was eight gross shots ahead of the field. The six holes from 11 to 16 — birdie at the par-5 11th, bogey at the par-4 12th, one of the hardest, birdie at the par-3 13th, the easiest, then pars at 14, 15 and 16 — yielded twenty Stableford points, and the Trophy cushion went from two to eleven on the strength of them. Mullin's Jacket reign, born on a course record at West Cliffs the previous afternoon, had lasted six holes.

At the other end, the inversion was unfolding in real time. Gregg Williams — reigning Green Jacket holder, back-to-back winner, four-time Trophy champion, a man for whom the Wooden Spoon was something other people occasionally got stuck holding — assembled a round of 26 Stableford points. The deterioration was concentrated in seven consecutive holes from the 12th to the 18th: double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey. Fourteen shots to par across the run. At the 14th, a par-4, Williams dropped outright to the bottom of the Spoon race for the first time in the tournament, and there he stayed for the remaining twenty-two holes. Earlier in the round he had produced a recovery birdie at the par-5 11th, breaking a bogey-or-worse run; it now reads like the barometer falling.

Elsewhere in the afternoon's wreckage, Alex Baker bled fifteen shots across holes 12 to 16, including a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 14th, and added a quadruple 9 at the par-5 18th, and was nevertheless — by virtue of Williams's collapse — no longer the man holding the Spoon. Patterson posted a new personal-best 94 at Royal Óbidos, six better than his TEG 9 visit, the sort of achievement that in any other tournament would have been the story, and here went under the surface without a ripple.

By the close of R3: Jon Baker eleven clear on the Trophy, eight clear on the Jacket, Williams alone at the bottom. The tournament was, in every sense that mattered, finished.

## Act III — Procession at Praia

The Tuesday round at Praia D'El Rey, the venue's sixth TEG outing, was a closing ceremony with light golf attached. The eleven-point Trophy lead and eight-shot Jacket advantage were never threatened. Jon Baker ran up a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 10th, then another quadruple 9 at the par-5 17th — the SI 1, the hardest hole on the course — and still won both prizes pulling up; he had earlier stopped a bogey-or-worse opening with a birdie at the par-4 4th. Final margins: 18 on the Trophy, 13 on the Jacket. The Trophy total of 166 is the ninth-best in TEG history.

Patterson, who had spent the first eighteen holes of the tournament leading the Trophy and the last fifty-six watching it recede, signed off with a sextuple-bogey 9 at the par-3 14th and a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 15th, one of the harder holes, plus quadruple 8s at the 1st and the 18th, for symmetry. Alex Baker dropped twenty-five shots across nine holes from the 5th to the 13th, and it made not the slightest difference; the Spoon was settled. Williams opened with a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, and the procession continued.

## How it was decided

**Trophy.** Patterson led after R1 by one. Jon Baker took the outright lead at the 1st hole of R2 at West Cliffs and was never headed across the following fifty-six holes. The cushion: two after R2, eleven after R3, eighteen at the close. Final: Jon Baker 166, Mullin 148.

**Green Jacket.** Five lead changes in the first forty-two holes. Williams led after R1; Mullin's course-record 82 took it at the close of R2; Mullin held it outright for one hole of R3, the 6th; Jon Baker drew level at the 7th and pulled clear with a course record of his own. Final: Jon Baker +67, Mullin +80.

**Wooden Spoon.** Alex bottom after R1, Patterson bottom from R2 H10, Williams outright bottom from R3 H14 and there for the remaining twenty-two holes. Williams 123. The reigning Jacket holder, second Spoon of his career.

## The men, in brief

- **Jon Baker** — Three TEGs of fourth on the Trophy resolved, in one Lisbon trip, into the double. Course records at Royal Óbidos (86, beating his own 89 from TEG 9) and at West Cliffs (86, fifteen better than his TEG 15 visit). The 166 is the ninth-best Trophy total ever posted. Third Green Jacket. The nearly-man arrives.
- **David Mullin** — Runner-up in both competitions and author of the round of the tournament, the 82 at West Cliffs that took six off the course record. His Jacket lead ran from the 18th green of R2 to the 6th green of R3, roughly an hour of competitive golf the next morning. Beaten, in the end, by a man having a better four days.
- **Gregg Williams** — Arrived as reigning Green Jacket, departed with the Wooden Spoon. Led the Jacket after R1, then 26 points at Royal Óbidos, seven consecutive double bogeys from the 12th to the 18th, fourteen shots to par across the closing seven holes, and the basement to himself from the 14th onwards. Second Spoon of nine prior TEGs. The inversion of the trip.
- **John Patterson** — Eighteen-hole Trophy leader, then fifty-six holes of absence. The West Cliffs 116 — a new course-worst, twenty-seven shots bled across holes 8 to 15 — was the round he never came back from. A new personal-best 94 at Royal Óbidos passed unmarked in the circumstances.
- **Alex Baker** — Carried two Spoons in, set a course-worst 114 at Praia D'El Rey on day one, then posted a personal-best 105 at West Cliffs and quietly passed the cutlery along. The streak ends at two. Williams took it from there.
