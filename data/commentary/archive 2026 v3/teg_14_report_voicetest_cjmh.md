# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third-best of his own career, which is to say it was very good and never once felt safe.

The foundation was poured on the first two links, while nobody was watching. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit, and the engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars, 13 points banked. The sequence ended at the 13th with a double bogey, as though somebody had been alerted. The damage was limited. At Littlestone he shot 85, again seven better than last time out, and another course best.

Prince's declined to cooperate. In Round 3 he stood on the par-five 8th and took nine. A quadruple bogey, worth precisely nothing, committed by the man leading the tournament and apparently keen to establish that he could afford it. He could. He answered with 13 points over the closing four holes, three pars and a birdie at the last, as if the 8th had been somebody else's afternoon entirely. Round 4 brought a second helping: an eight at the 10th, another quadruple bogey, another shrug.

That opened the door for Baker, who had at one point trailed by nine. He took 10 points from the first three holes on Sunday and 11 more from the 13th to the 15th, and suddenly the gap was two, with three to play. He then made a ten at the 16th. Baker's undoing had in fact begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Two of the four rounds in Kent ended with Alex Baker writing a 10 on his card, and on both occasions it was the 16th that took the dictation. Different courses, different holes, identical figure.

The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports: a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's on the Sunday, was worse in every meaningful respect. A 10 at a par four. A sextuple bogey. On stroke index 17, the second-easiest hole on the card.

The Prince's ten was the crueller because of what preceded it. Baker had played the 13th, 14th and 15th in par, par, par. Three holes without dropping a gross shot, eleven Stableford points, a man visibly reassembling a tournament. The 16th removed the lot in one sitting. He was not the only casualty there. Jon Baker arrived at the same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th, and made a double bogey for no points.

These were the headline numbers, not the complete set. Baker also had an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in round three: five further holes returning nothing whatsoever. His opening 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was nonetheless four strokes better than his previous visit, which tells you a good deal about the previous visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. It was built on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than his previous mark there of 94, and 86 at Prince's in round three, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

It was not tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing, and the tone was set for a man who would spend the next three days walking back towards respectability. Littlestone was worse in the wider sense: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he managed there on his previous visit, and only 27 Stableford points.

Round three was the correction. He gained twelve points on the day, reaching 39, and the middle of it was genuinely solid. Pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points, three holes without dropping a gross shot, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. Between them sat an 8 at the par-five 12th, which ended the run and restored the natural order of things.

Sunday brought more steadiness in patches, gross pars at the 5th and 6th and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at one stage, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered. Eight short. The best gross golf of his life, and the cabinet stays empty.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket at TEG 14 with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker, and a ninth to his name. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth-best of Mullin's own career at the metric.

It was built on consistency, the least glamorous virtue available, applied across four links. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his previous visit in TEG 13, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. None of those numbers would frighten anybody in isolation. Assembled, they were enough to keep him out of reach.

Behind him the chase was real, and it was late. Baker at one point trailed by 15 and closed to seven, most of that work done in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record; the previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits, and was Baker's own. Gregg Williams made up more ground still, trailing by 18 at his worst and finishing eight back. Williams's +76 is a personal-best Green Jacket gross total, and his 86 at Prince's in round three was a course best for him too.

The gap never closed far enough. A margin of seven at the finish is a polite summary of a lead that had, for most of the four days, been rather ruder than that.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker finished last in the Stableford count and took the Wooden Spoon with 125 points across the four rounds. The nearest man was 12 points away, and never in any danger.

The early links were unkind, though he did meet them halfway. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven strokes worse than the 85 he had managed on his previous visit. At Littlestone he posted 92 against a prior best of 79, a thirteen-stroke regression that cannot have happened by accident. His third round at Prince's brought 28 points, held down in part by a run of pars from the 14th that ended with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

Then, with the Spoon effectively engraved, he produced the round of the tournament. Baker returned 39 points, an improvement of 11 on the previous day, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the mark of 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best on the layout, two strokes better than his last time there. The scoring came in a burst: five holes from the 11th to the 15th without dropping a gross shot, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th, a two-hole birdie streak that equals the all-time TEG record, and 14 points across holes 12 to 15.

He then set about undoing it. A double bogey at the 16th ended the run, and at the 18th he ran up an eight. The record stands. So does the Spoon.
