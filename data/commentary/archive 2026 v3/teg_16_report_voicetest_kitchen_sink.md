# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total ever recorded and the second-best of his own career. He has been better once. He has never been better at a more convenient moment.

The margin was built early and never surrendered. Neumann held the Trophy lead after round two at Troia, held it still after round three at Penha Longa, and carried it intact through the closing round at Estoril. Williams, for his part, led the Jacket standings at each of those same checkpoints. For three days the two competitions ran alongside one another like rival delegations, neither man conceding so much as a yard in his own.

The final round was not flawless. At Estoril's 8th, a par 4 and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann took nine. A quintuple bogey. No points at all. He arrived at the hole in possession of a golf tournament and left it having contributed precisely nothing to the cause. With the cushion he had assembled, it cost him nothing beyond tidiness, which is the sort of thing a man can afford only once he has stopped needing to care.

Estoril otherwise gave him his best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and he chose the day the Trophy was settled to do it.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin's tournament ended a very long way from where it started. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he ran up a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. There is no fifth-and-a-half. That is the bottom of the pile. Round three at Penha Longa was worse in places: a quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, though he gathered himself for pars at 17 and 18. The round came to 30 points, and his 98 was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was a different matter entirely. Mullin birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through a bogey-bogey-double stretch at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th stopped the slide. The back nine simply held. Nine points from the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to finish. Somewhere on that back nine he stopped being a man salvaging a round and became a man who had forgotten he was capable of anything else.

The total was 45 points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. Unquestionably the finest round played at Estoril in the history of the tournament.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to develop. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th. Three holes, twenty-eight strokes. He signed for 113 gross, a new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits. At Troia the following day the damage was more efficiently organised: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples followed by two doubles.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers found the same stretch of ground and set about it in their own styles. Jon Baker made a double bogey six at the 11th, then ran up a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth nothing, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, and three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the four where he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his prior best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes, doing something broadly similar at a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, then a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots dropped across four holes and, like his brother, a three-hole run without a net par from the 12th onward. Fraternal solidarity has taken stranger forms, but rarely more expensive ones.

The final round at Estoril separated them again. Jon's one significant lapse came at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card, where he made an eight. Alex's round was assembled entirely out of such moments: nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled over the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every single one a double or worse. Then the 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst score he has ever made on a par four in his golfing history. The round came to 107 gross, a second course-worst of the trip, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. He finished on 127 points and collected the Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, his third. At this point the trophy may as well be engraved in advance.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never seriously in doubt, and the final margin, 16 shots clear of David Mullin, states the case without needing help. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

What it was not was tidy. Troia, a course none of the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his worst passages of the trip: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six gross shots gone in as many holes.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered at once with a birdie at the short 5th, only for a bogey-triple-double run through the 6th, 7th and 8th to demolish the repair job within the hour. He steadied on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and collecting nine points across the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit here.

Estoril produced both his best round and his loudest mistake. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit, and it arrived despite an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that terminated a run of three straight pars. He won the Jacket by sixteen shots. He also took eight on a par four while doing it. Both facts belong on the same card, and the card says champion.
