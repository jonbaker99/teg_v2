# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third-best of Mullin's own career, and he carried it home the way a man carries a full bowl of soup down a staircase in the dark.

The foundation was laid on the first two links, and it was proper work. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit, and the engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars for 13 points. The sequence ended abruptly with a double bogey at the 13th. Nobody said anything. At Littlestone he shot 85, again seven better than last time out, and another course best.

Prince's was less hospitable. In Round 3 he arrived at the par-five 8th with the tournament comfortably in his possession and took nine. A quadruple bogey, worth nothing, entered into the record without comment. It was an act of magnificent indifference to his own interests, and he answered it by taking 13 points from the closing four holes, three pars and a birdie at the last. In Round 4 he did much the same thing again, an eight at the 10th, another quadruple bogey. The soup, by this stage, was mostly on the stairs.

That opened the door for Baker, who had at one point trailed by nine and now had a genuine invitation. He took 10 points from the first three holes of the final round and 11 more from the 13th to the 15th, closing to within two. Then he reached the 16th and made a ten. His unravelling had in fact begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5, a stretch of golf that took less time to ruin his tournament than it takes to describe.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Two of Alex Baker's four rounds in Kent contained a 10, and on both occasions the number was written at a hole numbered 16. They were different holes on different courses and share nothing but the digit, which is the only mercy available here. The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports, a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's in the final round, was worse in relative terms: a 10 at a par four, a sextuple bogey, at stroke index 17. That is the second-easiest hole on the card. It is the hole they give you.

The Prince's 10 was the more painful for what came immediately before it. Baker had played the 13th, 14th and 15th in par, par, par, three holes without dropping a gross shot, eleven Stableford points banked and a Trophy back within reach. The 16th removed all of it in one hole. He was not the only man to mislay himself there. Jon Baker arrived at the same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th, and made a double bogey for nothing.

These were the headline numbers, not the complete set. Baker also had an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in the third round. Five further holes returning nothing at all. His round-one 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was still four strokes better than his previous visit, which tells you something about the previous visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. It was built on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than his previous mark there of 94, and 86 at Prince's in the third round, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

None of it was tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing. Littlestone was a broader disaster: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he managed there before, and 27 Stableford points, which is the sort of return that makes a man study the yardage book for answers it does not contain.

Round three was the correction, and it was a good one. He gained twelve points on the day, reaching 39, and the middle of the round was genuinely solid: pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points, three holes without a gross shot dropped, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. Between them sat an 8 at the par-five 12th, arriving like a phone call in the night.

The final round brought more steadiness in patches, gross pars at the 5th and 6th and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at one stage, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered, and still eight short.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket at TEG 14 with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth-best of Mullin's own career at the metric.

The number was built on consistency across Kent's four links rounds, and the consistency was real. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his previous visit in TEG 13, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. None of those numbers would frighten anybody in isolation. Collectively they were unreachable, which is the only test that matters.

Behind him the chase was real, and late. Baker at one point trailed by 15 and closed to seven, most of that work done in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record. The previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits and was Baker's own. Williams made up more ground still, trailing by 18 at his worst and finishing eight back, with a personal-best Green Jacket gross of +76 and a course best of 86 at Prince's in the third round.

The gap never closed far enough. Mullin's seven-shot margin at the finish was the smallest it had been for most of the four days, which is one way of saying the soup arrived.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker finished last in the Stableford count and took the Wooden Spoon with 125 points. The nearest man above him was 12 points away, a margin that removes any suggestion of bad luck.

The early links treated him as an intruder. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven strokes worse than the 85 he had managed there previously. At Littlestone he posted 92 against a prior best of 79, a regression of thirteen shots on a course he had already solved once. His third round at Prince's yielded 28 points, held down by a run of pars from the 14th that ended with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

The final round was something else entirely, and it deserves saying plainly. Baker returned 39 points, eleven better than the day before, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best on the layout by two strokes. The scoring came in a burst: five holes without dropping a gross shot from the 11th to the 15th, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th that equal the all-time TEG record for consecutive birdies, and 14 points across holes 12 to 15.

Then he made a double bogey at the 16th, which ended the run, and an eight at the 18th, which ended the rest. He holds a course record and the Wooden Spoon. Both were earned.
