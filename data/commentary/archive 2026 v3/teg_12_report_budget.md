# The 12 That Wasn't a Eulogy

The card says 12. Par 4, stroke index 2, the fourth hole of the whole tournament. Eight strokes over par, in a single box. John Patterson signed for it. No player in TEG history had posted a worse hole relative to par. Anywhere. Ever.

He won the Trophy by ten.

That is the story of TEG's third pilgrimage to Catalonia, and it needs unpicking. Nothing about it obeys the usual arithmetic of doom. The shape was simple: two days on the Tour Course, the gentler of the two Coles-Gallardo siblings, then two days on the Stadium, Spain's grandest examination and a venue that had spent fifteen TEG visits declining to yield a round in the eighties. Gregg Williams led for two days. He led on the Tour. Both pots were decided on the Stadium, and the hinge was one hole on the third afternoon.

## Round 1 — A 12, a 105, and a Leader Nobody Mentions

The Thursday round on the Tour Course produced two numbers everybody discussed and one nobody did.

Williams played the best gross round of his life: 84, twelve over, a personal best at the course by eight shots on his last visit here. It put him five clear in the Green Jacket race and one clear on the Trophy. In bar-conversation terms, it was the fourth or fifth most interesting thing that happened.

Patterson had his 12 at the 4th, the second-hardest hole on the property. Then he did something more remarkable than the 12: he carried on. Par, par, par, par from the 8th through the 11th, the sort of quiet, humourless competence that follows a man who has decided not to discuss it, until a double at the 12th ended the sequence. He signed for 45 points, one behind Williams. Reduce that 12 to a bogey and he almost certainly goes to bed in front. He will have run the counterfactual several hundred times that evening. It cost him nothing at all.

Stuart Neumann's afternoon was less recoverable. His 105 was a new worst gross round at the Tour Course across ten prior TEG visits. It took the record from 103. It was built on solid foundations: a sextuple bogey 10 at the 13th, which is the hardest hole out there, and a 12 at the par-5 15th. Alex Baker ran up a quintuple bogey 10 at the 10th. By the 14th he was bottom of the Spoon table. He stayed there for the best part of three days.

## Round 2 — Williams Holds, Patterson Hunts

Friday, same course, same margins, minimal movement. Williams held both leads by a single shot, and did so despite a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th, a hole that appeared to be collecting a toll from everyone who passed. Patterson kept pace and posted a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 15th. It was the only thing between him and parity. Neumann opened with an 8 at the 1st, getting the formalities out of the way early.

David Mullin quietly returned an 84, a Tour Course personal best by one stroke. Two rounds gone, and the leaderboard had barely twitched. The Tour Course had finished with the field; the Stadium hadn't started.

## Round 3 — The Stadium Reshuffles Everything

Saturday. Everything that mattered happened inside four holes, and most of it at one.

Patterson opened with a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 3rd, his third double-figure hole of the tournament, filed with the same lack of ceremony as the first two. Williams, playing steadily, drew level on Stableford with a par at that 3rd. Level, note: not ahead. It was the last moment in which he could see the summit.

Then the 4th. Patterson made bogey, took three points and the outright Trophy lead, and never gave it back. At the same hole Jon Baker also made bogey. From fourth place on gross, he took the outright lead in the Green Jacket. Two competitions changed hands at one hole, decided by two bogeys, which is either an indictment of the field or a fair summary of the Stadium. Williams made 9 there.

He was not done. A septuple bogey 11 at the 13th — twelve strokes over par across those two holes alone — removed him from the argument he had been leading since Round 1.

And Patterson, having done his damage in single, spectacular instalments, chose the closing stretch for the opposite. Nineteen points from the 12th to the 17th: par, bogey, bogey, then a birdie at the 15th, then bogey, bogey — no heroics, no eagle, just six holes of a man refusing to give anything back.

He went to bed eight clear.

The Spoon race, unmoved by any of this, spent the day passing itself back and forth. Mullin dropped to the bottom with a double at the 9th, the hardest hole on the course; Alex Baker reclaimed the position at the 11th; Mullin dropped again at the 14th; Alex Baker took it back at the 17th with a double. Three days holding the floor, one round left to lose it.

## Round 4 — Mullin's Sunday Morning, Baker's Sunday Afternoon

Sunday resolved everything, in order.

Mullin's 9 at the par-5 3rd dropped him to the bottom. It was the first time he had been there since the 14th hole of Round 3. His quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th confirmed the arrangement. He did not climb out again, and finished on 139 points with a Wooden Spoon he has now held for a second consecutive TEG, having spent three rounds watching another man look after it. Two holes. That was the whole handover.

Then Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, went about a different business. His 89 was the first sub-90 round in fifteen TEG visits to the Stadium, breaking a course record of 90 that belonged to him, from TEG 11, by a single stroke. It sealed the Green Jacket at +63, eight clear of Mullin. He had been the third-most-discussed man on the property for two days. He never once appeared to mind.

Neumann, whose tournament began with the worst round the Tour Course has ever seen, eagled the par-5 15th — the easiest hole on the Stadium, and the only eagle anybody managed all trip.

Patterson signed for 40 and won by ten. Along the way he set personal bests at both courses, 96 at the Tour and 95 at the Stadium, which is the least dramatic sentence available about a man who made three double-figure holes.

Williams, for the record, finished runner-up in both competitions with a personal-best gross total of +76. Individual bests at both venues, too: 84 at the Tour, 92 at the Stadium. He kept absolutely none of the silverware.

## The men, in brief

- **John Patterson** — Second Trophy, by ten, after sixth and fourth in his last two outings. Made 12, 10 and 10 in consecutive rounds and won more comfortably than anyone in living memory. The 12 was an anecdote, not an epitaph.
- **Gregg Williams** — Led both competitions for two rounds on the back of the best gross round and gross total of his career, and lost them both on one Stadium afternoon. Runner-up in the Trophy for the third time. Two course records of his own and no pots. Also a 9 and an 11 he will be explaining for years.
- **Jon Baker** — Took the gross lead with a bogey at the 4th in Round 3 and never gave it back. Then broke his own Stadium record with the venue's first 89. The Green Jacket by eight, won while everyone else argued about Stableford.
- **David Mullin** — Retains the Spoon on 139, having spent three rounds being handed a reprieve and roughly twenty minutes of the final morning handing it back where it belonged. Jacket runner-up, and an 84 at the Tour that stands as his best there. A curious ledger, but an honest one.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Opened with 105, the worst round the Tour Course has recorded in ten TEG visits, and closed with the tournament's only eagle at the 15th. For the first time in two TEGs, the Spoon went elsewhere.
- **Alex Baker** — Held the bottom of the table from the 14th hole of Round 1 to the 17th of Round 3, then contrived to escape it on the final day. A Stadium personal best of 93, thirteen shots better than his last visit. And the strange dignity of having earned the Spoon without receiving it.
