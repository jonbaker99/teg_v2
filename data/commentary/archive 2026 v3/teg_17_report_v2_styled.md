Jon Baker had led the Green Jacket for thirty-five holes. He surrendered it on the first green at Royal Óbidos, to a double bogey at the par-4 opener, and David Mullin inherited the lead without appearing to have asked for it.

Baker drew level again at the 2nd. Mullin went outright ahead at the par-3 6th with a par of no particular drama. That lasted one hole.

Baker was level again at the 7th, in front from there, and by the 11th had begun the stretch that settled the tournament: birdie, bogey, birdie, par, par, par — twenty points in six holes. He signed for 86, the lowest gross Royal Óbidos has ever given up, beating a mark of 87 that had stood across eighteen previous visits. It was also his own personal best there, eight shots better than his last attempt, and it improved on the 89 he had posted at TEG 9.

Both trophies were effectively decided on that third afternoon. At the other end of the same fairways, Gregg Williams was midway through seven consecutive double bogeys and dropping to the bottom of the field for the first time.

Four rounds on the Lisbon Coast, TEG's sixth visit to the region, and four course records — two of them worth framing, two of them worth burning. The book was rewritten in both directions, frequently on the same day.

## Round 1 — Twenty-Four Visits, One New Worst {.round1 .round}

The Saturday round at Praia D'El Rey was TEG's fifth at the venue, which is to say the field knew exactly what it was doing there. Alex Baker, the reigning Spoon holder and holder of the two before that, signed for 114. Across twenty-four prior TEG visits to Praia D'El Rey nobody had managed worse; the previous mark was 110. He was also eleven shots worse than his own last outing to the place.

The damage was efficiently concentrated. A quadruple-bogey 8 at the par-4 4th, a sextuple-bogey 10 at the 5th — SI 2, one of the hardest holes on the course — and a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 18th. Fifteen over par across three holes.

He had company at the 5th, where Gregg Williams took an 8 of his own. John Patterson opened his tournament with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st, which he would later come to regard as a warm-up.

Mullin's day was a study in patience: 33 points, with a thirteen-point split between the nines in favour of the back, and sixteen of them harvested from the 12th to the 16th — par, birdie at the par-4 13th, par, bogey, par. Jon Baker scored 38.

The Trophy lead was exchanged in the manner of a committee passing a document down the table: Baker ahead at the 3rd, Patterson taking it back at the 16th, Patterson closing the day one point clear. In the gross, Baker led from the 2nd hole until Williams drew level at the 17th.

Williams, it should be recorded, arrived here as the form man. TEG 15 Trophy winner, back-to-back Green Jackets, one Wooden Spoon in an entire career. Third after the opening round.

## Round 2 — Mullin's 82, Patterson's 116 {.round2 .round}

Cynthia Dye's 2017 links at West Cliffs had hosted TEG precisely once before. On the Sunday it gave up both of its extremes inside a single round.

Mullin went round in 82, lowering the course record from 88 and beating his own previous visit there by twelve shots. Jon Baker took fifteen shots off his 2015 mark on the same links. Patterson shot 116, the highest gross West Cliffs has recorded, past a prior worst of 115 and nineteen shots adrift of what he managed there at TEG 15. At +44 it was the worst round of his TEG life.

The wheels came off over eight holes from the 8th, in which Patterson dropped twenty-seven shots: double, triple, triple, triple, double, a quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 13th, triple, then an 11 at the par-5 15th. That hole is SI 16, one of the easiest on the course. The 11 was the worst score relative to par he has ever signed for. He had already taken an 8 at the 3rd, SI 1, so the round had form.

Baker, meanwhile, did the quietest decisive thing of the whole tournament. He bogeyed the 1st — SI 17, and no bogey has ever mattered more — and took the outright Trophy lead. He held it for the remaining seventy-one holes.

He then made fourteen points in four holes from the 2nd, including a par at the 3rd and a birdie at the 4th, added nine more from the 14th to the 16th, and posted 44. Mullin's own purple patch ran from the 7th to the 10th: bogey, par, birdie, birdie, for fourteen points.

Alex Baker set a personal best at West Cliffs, built on sixteen points from the 4th to the 8th, which reads oddly next to the quadruple-bogey 8s he took at the 10th and the 11th. Both things happened.

Baker led the Trophy by two. Mullin drew level with him in the gross at the 18th.

## Round 3 — Lost at the 1st, Won by the 16th {.round3 .round}

Monday brought Seve's last design, on TEG's fourth visit, and the afternoon already described: Baker's double at the 1st, Mullin's single hole in front at the 6th, Baker level at the 7th and gone. The 44 points put him eleven clear on the Trophy and eight clear in the gross, and the 86 put his name on the course.

Behind him, Royal Óbidos performed its other function. Williams double-bogeyed seven holes in succession from the 12th to the 18th, fourteen shots dropped without deviation or complaint. The one at the 14th moved him to the bottom of the field for the first time. He had earlier taken a 9 at the par-5 5th. His round was 26 points.

Alex Baker assembled four separate blow-ups of 8 or more: an 8 at the 4th, SI 1; a 9 at the par-5 5th; a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 14th; and a 9 at the par-5 18th. From the 12th to the 16th he shed fifteen shots.

Patterson took a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 11th and then, on the same nine, produced thirteen points from the 12th to the 15th — par, par, bogey, par. He signed for 94, a personal best at Royal Óbidos. Two days after the 116, on a different course entirely, the man had rebuilt himself.

## Round 4 — Nine, Nine, and Still Eighteen Clear {.round4 .round}

The Tuesday return to Praia D'El Rey was, in the phrase, a formality, though nobody told the coastal dunes on the back nine.

Baker opened with a double at the 1st, bogeys at the 2nd and 3rd, then a birdie at the 4th. He took a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 10th and another 9 at the par-5 17th, SI 1. He also took nine points from the 14th to the 16th. The round came to 40 points and +19, and he finished it leading both competitions.

Alex Baker's nine holes from the 5th cost him twenty-five shots: double, double, triple, triple, double, double, triple, then a 9 at the par-5 12th and an 8 at the 13th. He escaped a third consecutive Spoon regardless, because Williams was falling faster.

Williams opened with a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, scored 29, and finished on 123 points.

Patterson closed with an 8 at the 1st, a sextuple-bogey 9 at the par-3 14th — SI 17, one of the easiest holes out there, and his worst score relative to par — a 10 at the 15th, and an 8 at the 18th.

Baker finished on 166 points, a personal best and the ninth best total in TEG history, from a man who had come fourth on the Trophy in each of the previous three TEGs.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy.** Jon Baker took the outright lead with a bogey at the 1st hole of Round 2 at West Cliffs and never gave it back over the remaining seventy-one holes. Back-to-back 44s at West Cliffs and Royal Óbidos turned a two-point lead into eleven; the 40 in the final round made it eighteen. Final: 166 points, from Mullin's 148. It is Baker's personal best and the ninth-highest total TEG has recorded.
- **The Green Jacket.** Baker led the gross from the 2nd hole of Round 1. Williams drew level at the 17th that day, Mullin at the 18th of Round 2, and Mullin took it outright at the 6th at Royal Óbidos after Baker's double at the 1st. Baker was level again at the 7th and never behind thereafter. The course-record 86 built an eight-shot cushion; two quadruple-bogey 9s in the final round barely dented it. Final: +67, thirteen clear of Mullin.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Williams was third after Round 1 on 33 points and fourth after Round 2 on 35. Seven consecutive doubles from the 12th at Royal Óbidos dropped him to last at the 14th, on a round of 26, and he never climbed out. The 9 at the 1st in Round 4 confirmed it. Final: 123 points, and only the second Spoon of his career — one TEG after he won the Trophy.

## The men, in brief

- **Jon Baker.** Three straight fourth places on the Trophy, answered with the lot: 166 points, the Jacket at +67, the Royal Óbidos course record and fifteen shots taken off his last West Cliffs visit. He also took two quadruple-bogey 9s in the final round and still won by eighteen and thirteen, which tells you how far ahead he was.
- **David Mullin.** Runner-up in both, and the only man who genuinely inconvenienced Baker. His 82 lowered the West Cliffs course record and beat his own previous visit there by twelve. He held the outright gross lead for exactly one hole. For a man with nine Jackets already, it was a handsome way to lose.
- **Gregg Williams.** Arrived as the reigning Jacket holder and TEG 15 Trophy winner; left with the Spoon. The seven doubles from the Royal Óbidos 12th were less a collapse than an administrative process, executed in order and completed on schedule.
- **John Patterson.** Led the Trophy after the opening round, then posted the highest gross West Cliffs has ever seen and the worst round of his career at +44. Two days later he set a personal best of 94 at Royal Óbidos. The 9 at the par-3 14th in the final round was a new personal worst relative to par.
- **Alex Baker.** The worst gross in twenty-four TEG visits to Praia D'El Rey, four blow-ups of 8 or more at Royal Óbidos, twenty-five shots in nine holes in the last round, and a personal best at West Cliffs. He avoided a third consecutive Spoon by finishing above a man who fell further.
