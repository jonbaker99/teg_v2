# An Eleven, a Ten, and the Trophy: Alex Baker Wins in Catalonia

## Alex Baker's Trophy: 169 points, two field-best rounds, and a course-worst gross that cost him nothing

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It stands as the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of his own career. He led the Stableford count after each of the final three rounds, and was never headed once he got to the front after R2. There is no asterisk. There is, however, a scorecard.

The scoring came in bursts. On the Tour Course in R1 he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run assembled almost entirely from bogeys at generously indexed holes rather than anything a spectator would have remembered. He then added 13 more across 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-five 15th. His largest single stretch came in R3 on the Stadium Course: 21 points from holes 11 to 16, six holes without a blank, the most sustained competence anyone managed all tournament.

The gross figures are a separate document altogether. In R2 he made an 8 at the par-three 16th. Then he made an 11 at the par-five 18th. Both were personal worsts against par, achieved consecutively, which suggests a certain thoroughness. He signed for 106, a new PGA Catalunya Tour course-worst, beating the previous mark of 105 set across 22 prior visits. Twenty-two visits of patient, accumulated inadequacy, overturned in an afternoon. R4 brought a 10 at the 14th and an 8 at the 17th, lest anyone think the point had not been made. His best gross across the four days was 98 on the Tour and 100 on the Stadium, against prior bests of 92 and 93. It cost him the Green Jacket, where he slid from second after R1 to fifth. On Stableford, it cost him nothing whatsoever.

## David Mullin: an 11 at the 15th, then the lowest gross round ever played at the Stadium

**David Mullin**

Mullin visited both extremes available to a golfer, and the first arrived at the 15th on the Tour Course in Round 1. The par five took eleven strokes. It returned no Stableford points, and it is the worst score he has ever recorded on a par five. The damage was not confined to the card: the hole dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, which is to say the bottom of it.

Two days later, on the Stadium Course, he produced something the tournament had never seen. His 84 gross in Round 3 is a new course record at the Stadium, beating the previous mark of 89 set across 27 prior visits. It was comfortably his own best there too: his previous best on the course was 91 across five earlier visits, and the round was eight strokes better than his last time out on it. For eighteen holes he played like a man who had temporarily forgotten his own history.

Round 4 restored the balance with some efficiency. Through the closing stretch he went four holes without a net par: a double bogey at the 14th, a triple at the 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. Nine shots went in four holes, and five Stableford points went with them. A tournament, then, of one number nobody will forget and several he would rather.

## John Patterson's second place, and the eight holes on the opening morning

John Patterson's tournament was defined, and very nearly ended, in one stretch of the opening morning on the Tour Course. From the 3rd he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, and then declined to stop: holes 3 through 10 cost him 22 shots to par, including triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th. Across the seven holes from the 3rd to the 9th he failed to make a single net par. By the 4th he had already arrived at the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, fourth to fifth, which is a remarkable turn of speed in any discipline.

What followed is the reason he was still in the argument at the end. Later in the same round he took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, three pars and a bogey at the par-five 15th. In round two he made another eight at the 4th and leaked 11 shots between the 6th and the 10th, then answered with 16 points across the 11th to the 15th. That round produced a 94, his best gross on the Tour Course, bettering his previous mark of 96. He is, evidently, a man who requires a crisis before he will engage.

The pattern held to the last day. After a triple at the 14th and a double at the 15th on the Stadium, he stopped the bleeding with a bogey at the 16th and a birdie at the 17th. Second place, built entirely on the recoveries.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: reigning Wooden Spoon holder to +66 and a 14-shot win / Jon Baker's Wooden Spoon: the defending Trophy champion finishes 23 points adrift of the next man

**Round 1, Tour Course.** Gregg Williams opened exactly as a Wooden Spoon holder is expected to open: a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 7th, another quadruple bogey, an 8, at the 17th, and an unbroken sequence of dropped shots running from the 2nd to the 14th, taking in triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th. A birdie at the par-5 15th finally halted it. He signed for 31 points. Jon Baker, meanwhile, had led the Green Jacket gross race since the 5th hole of that same round.

**Round 2, Tour Course.** The Sunday round examined Baker's tenure at the top and found it to be twenty holes long. A bogey at the 7th handed the gross lead to Williams. Baker answered late: a birdie at the 14th began a run of five holes without dropping a gross shot, worth 16 points to the 18th, and by the close he was the Jacket leader again, with Alex Baker heading the Trophy. Williams surged over the same closing stretch, taking 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, four pars in a row and not a gross shot dropped, and posted 42 points, an 11-point improvement on his opening round.

**Round 3, Stadium Course.** The Jacket changed hands for good, and early. Williams parred the 2nd and took the outright gross lead. Baker's round dismantled itself at the 4th, where a 9, a quintuple bogey on a par 4, went down as a blank. By nightfall the boards read Alex Baker in the Trophy and Williams in the Jacket, and they did not read anything else again.

**Round 4, Stadium Course.** Williams birdied the par-3 5th, parred the 6th, and gathered nine points across the 8th, 9th and 10th, closing out an 84 that was his best gross in seven visits to the Stadium, eight shots better than his last time round it.

The two ledgers closed in opposite corners. Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin: the sixth-best gross total in TEG history and his own personal best, delivered by the man who had arrived holding the Spoon. Jon Baker, the defending Trophy champion, finished on 126 Stableford points and 23 adrift of the next man up. A title defence is supposed to end in disappointment. His ended in a different postcode.
