# The Coronation at Óbidos

## The 16th green at Royal Óbidos, late afternoon

A man walks off a green. That's it. That's the image. Jon Baker, third afternoon, stepping off the 16th at Royal Óbidos with the mild expression of someone who has just remembered where he parked.

Behind him: twenty Stableford points in six holes. Birdie, bogey, birdie, par, par, par. Delivered across a stretch of Seve Ballesteros's final routing that had, until this Monday, absorbed eighteen prior TEG rounds and shrugged all of them off like a horse twitching flies. He is en route to an 86. That is the course record. It is also his second new personal best at a Lisbon Coast course in as many days. The Trophy lead, two overnight, is about to swell to eleven. The Jacket advantage sits at eight and climbing.

To understand the size of this you need to know what Jon Baker was before R1. Three TEGs. Three fourth places on the Trophy. Fourth so consistently that it had stopped being a result and become a diagnosis. The perennial bridesmaid of Stableford. Pleasant about it. Slightly hunched. Forever handing someone else the good champagne.

What follows is four consecutive days on the Lisbon Coast — the sixth such visit, Praia D'El Rey bookending West Cliffs and Royal Óbidos — during which that ended. And during which, down in the sediment at the bottom of the leaderboard, the reigning Green Jacket holder learned that a title does not defend itself while you stand there admiring it.

---

## Act I — A Single Point Clear at Praia

The Saturday round at Praia D'El Rey, Cabell Robinson's links-meets-parkland routing on its fifth TEG outing, began exactly as this place tends to begin: everybody arrives at the first tee in a state of dignity, and then hits their second, third, fourth and fifth shots.

By the close, John Patterson was one Stableford point clear. Looking at the actual round, this is roughly as plausible as being crowned Employee of the Month while asleep under the photocopier. He carved out a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st, reproduced it faithfully at the 6th, and only stopped the bleeding with a birdie at the par-5 12th that interrupted a bogey-or-worse run.

Gregg Williams led the Green Jacket on a gross 93. Three clear. Business as usual for him. Reigning Jacket holder, back-to-back winner, four-time Trophy champion — Williams turned up in Lisbon with the calm of a man who knows exactly which drawer the good knives live in and sees no reason to open it. His 93 contained a quadruple at the par-4 5th, one of the sterner holes out there, but he drew level on the Jacket at the 17th and nudged ahead by the finish.

David Mullin did the Mullin thing, which is to lurk. A back-nine surge produced sixteen points across holes 12 to 16 — birdie at the 13th, pars at 12, 14 and 16. A round that whispers, from behind you, unnervingly.

And then Alex Baker, arriving with back-to-back Wooden Spoons already rattling around in the boot, set about extending the collection. 114 gross. A new course-worst at a venue that had hosted twenty-four prior TEG rounds without anyone locating that particular basement. The centrepiece was a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 5th, buttressed by a quadruple at the 4th and a quintuple 9 at the 18th. Eighteen holes gone; the Spoon was his.

In hindsight the leaderboard looks like a magician arranging props. Patterson nominally ahead. Williams holding the Jacket. Alex in the cellar. Jon Baker fourth-ish and silent. Everyone precisely where you'd file them. Nothing visible yet.

## Act II — Two Course Records at the Cliffs, in Both Directions

Sunday at West Cliffs, Cynthia Dye's clifftop layout, on only its second TEG outing. In one afternoon it yielded the best gross score ever posted there and the worst. Both. Same day. Like a hospital simultaneously curing and inventing a disease.

The best was Mullin's. An 82, six shots inside the previous course record, built on a fourteen-point burst across holes 7 to 10 — bogey, par, birdie, birdie — and otherwise close to spotless. The round of the tournament. It was also, as things transpired, nearly the entirety of Mullin's tenure at the top of the Jacket: he drew level at the 18th to carry it overnight, then held it outright for six holes the following afternoon before it was removed from him. A Jacket lead with a half-life measured in individual greens.

The worst was Patterson's. A 116, twenty-four hours after sitting one point clear of the Trophy. The previous course-worst was 115. He beat it by one, which stops being bad luck and starts looking like ambition. It opened with a quadruple at the par-4 3rd, the hardest hole on the course, and then, across holes 8 to 15, he shed twenty-seven shots: double, triple, triple, triple, double, a quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 13th, triple, and a sextuple-bogey 11 at the par-5 15th. Eight holes of a man being dismantled by his own hands. He hit the bottom of the Spoon race at the 10th and stayed welded there.

Somewhere between the ascension and the disembowelling, Jon Baker won the Trophy. At the 1st hole of the round he took the outright lead from Patterson, and then held it for the remaining fifty-six holes of the tournament. Fifty-six. Never headed again. The taking of it was undramatic — fourteen points across holes 2 to 5, par on the SI 1 third, birdie at the 4th — the golf of a man who has noticed that everyone else's wheels are rolling into a ditch and has, alone, brought a wrench.

Alex Baker posted a personal-best 105 at West Cliffs, five better than his previous visit, with a sixteen-point surge across holes 4 to 8 and a recovery birdie at the par-3 12th, the easiest hole out there, arriving directly after consecutive quadruple-bogey 8s at the 10th and 11th. The PB didn't hoist him up the field. It did hoist him off the floor. The Spoon was now, technically, unclaimed. Patterson had it.

Two rounds in: Jon Baker two clear on the Trophy, Mullin level on the Jacket, Patterson underneath everything, Alex Baker unshackled. The lid was off.

## Act II, continued — Jon Baker Rewrites Seve's Course

Monday at Royal Óbidos, Seve Ballesteros's last design, fourth TEG visit. This is the hinge. This is where the thing stopped being a competition.

Jon Baker shot 86. The previous course record, across eighteen prior TEG rounds here, was 87 — and his own 89 from TEG 9, among plenty of others, was simply vaporised. The Jacket sequence: level with Mullin at the 2nd; Mullin outright at the 6th and holding it for exactly one hole; Jon level again at the 7th, then clear, then permanently clear. By the 18th green he was eight gross shots ahead of everybody. The six holes from 11 to 16 — birdie at the par-5 11th, bogey at the par-4 12th (one of the toughest), birdie at the par-3 13th (the kindest), pars at 14, 15 and 16 — returned twenty Stableford points. The Trophy cushion went from two to eleven on that alone. Mullin's Jacket reign, conceived on a course record the previous afternoon, expired six holes into the next round.

Meanwhile, at the other end, an inversion was taking place in real time. Gregg Williams — reigning Green Jacket holder, back-to-back winner, four-time Trophy champion, a man for whom the Wooden Spoon was something that happened to other, sadder people — returned 26 Stableford points. The collapse arrived in seven consecutive holes from the 12th to the 18th: double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey, double bogey. Fourteen shots to par in one unbroken sequence, like watching a filing cabinet fall down a stairwell one drawer at a time. At the 14th, a par-4, he dropped outright to the bottom of the Spoon race for the first time in the tournament, and remained there for the remaining twenty-two holes. Earlier he'd found a recovery birdie at the par-5 11th, snapping a bogey-or-worse run. It now reads as the barometric hush before actual weather.

Elsewhere in the debris, Alex Baker bled fifteen shots across holes 12 to 16, including a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 14th, and appended a quadruple 9 at the par-5 18th — and, thanks entirely to Williams, was no longer the man holding the cutlery. Patterson recorded a new personal-best 94 at Royal Óbidos, six better than his TEG 9 visit, an achievement that in any other tournament would have earned a paragraph and a handshake. Here it was buried under the rubble of everyone else.

By Monday evening: Jon Baker eleven clear on the Trophy, eight clear on the Jacket, Williams alone in the cellar. It was, in every meaningful sense, finished.

## Act III — Procession at Praia

The Tuesday round at Praia D'El Rey, the venue's sixth TEG outing, was a closing ceremony with golf loosely attached. The eleven-point Trophy lead and eight-shot Jacket cushion were never so much as prodded. Jon Baker himself ran up a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 10th and another quadruple 9 at the par-5 17th — the SI 1, the hardest hole on the course — and still won both prizes with his feet up. He'd earlier halted a bogey-or-worse opening with a birdie at the par-4 4th. Final margins: 18 on the Trophy, 13 on the Jacket. The Trophy total of 166 is the ninth-best in TEG history.

Patterson, who led the Trophy for eighteen holes and then spent fifty-six watching it evaporate, signed off with a sextuple-bogey 9 at the par-3 14th and a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 15th, one of the harder ones, plus quadruple 8s at the 1st and 18th, for tidiness. Alex Baker dropped twenty-five shots across nine holes from the 5th to the 13th and it changed nothing; the Spoon was already engraved. Williams opened with a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st. The procession moved on.

## How it was decided

**Trophy.** Patterson led after R1 by one. Jon Baker took the outright lead at the 1st hole of R2 at West Cliffs and was never headed across the next fifty-six holes. Two after R2, eleven after R3, eighteen at the close. Final: Jon Baker 166, Mullin 148.

**Green Jacket.** Five lead changes in the first forty-two holes. Williams after R1; Mullin's course-record 82 at the close of R2; Mullin outright for one hole of R3, the 6th; Jon Baker level at the 7th and gone with his own course record. Final: Jon Baker +67, Mullin +80.

**Wooden Spoon.** Alex bottom after R1, Patterson bottom from R2 H10, Williams outright bottom from R3 H14 — and there for the remaining twenty-two holes. Williams 123. The reigning Jacket holder. Second Spoon of his career.

## The men, in brief

- **Jon Baker** — Three TEGs of fourth resolved, in one Lisbon trip, into the double. Course records at Royal Óbidos (86, past his own 89 from TEG 9) and West Cliffs (86, fifteen better than his TEG 15 visit). The 166 is the ninth-best Trophy total ever. Third Green Jacket. The nearly-man arrives, unhurried.
- **David Mullin** — Runner-up in both, and author of the round of the tournament: the 82 at West Cliffs, six shots off the course record. His Jacket lead ran from the 18th green of R2 to the 6th green of R3, roughly an hour of competitive golf. Beaten by a man simply having a better four days.
- **Gregg Williams** — Arrived as reigning Green Jacket, left with the Wooden Spoon. Led the Jacket after R1, then 26 points at Royal Óbidos, seven consecutive double bogeys from the 12th to the 18th, fourteen shots to par over the closing stretch, and the basement to himself from the 14th onward. Second Spoon of nine prior TEGs.
- **John Patterson** — Eighteen-hole Trophy leader, then a fifty-six-hole absence. The West Cliffs 116 — new course-worst, twenty-seven shots surrendered across holes 8 to 15 — was the wound that never closed. A new personal-best 94 at Royal Óbidos went entirely uncelebrated.
- **Alex Baker** — Two Spoons in the boot, a course-worst 114 at Praia D'El Rey on day one, then a personal-best 105 at West Cliffs and a quiet handover of the cutlery. The streak ends at two. Williams took it from there.
