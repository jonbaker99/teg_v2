# Baker Leads Both, Loses Both

## Jon Baker's two-competition collapse, and the survivors either side of him
<!-- storyline: race -->

At PGA Catalunya's Stadium course, Jon Baker briefly ended Gregg Williams's grip on the TEG 4 Trophy (Net VP) lead, taking it outright at the 7th hole after Williams had led every hole since the 2nd of Round 1 — a run of 59 holes. It lasted two holes. At the 9th, the hardest hole on the course, Baker ran up a 10, a sextuple bogey, and Williams's own triple bogey there was enough to snatch the lead straight back.

Baker's day carried a second reversal. Having taken a one-stroke lead into the round for the TEG 4 Green Jacket (Gross), he watched David Mullin seize that lead at the very same 9th hole, drew level again at the 16th, but could hold no further — Mullin finished the round four clear.

Williams's own round was no smoother: 11 shots bled over the first four holes, four straight holes without a net par from the 6th, then five shots gained from the 10th before another 14 bled over the closing five. Even so, his overall Trophy lead narrowed only from eight strokes to five. Further back, Henry Meller added two big blow-ups of his own and stayed rooted in last for the Wooden Spoon, the gap behind him swelling from eight to seventeen.

## Stuart Neumann's front-nine disaster and late recovery
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Stuart Neumann's final round at PGA Catalunya's Stadium course began to unravel at the par-5 3rd, where a quintuple bogey 10 opened a horror stretch. He followed it with a double bogey at the 4th, another double at the par-3 5th, and a triple bogey at the 6th, bleeding 12 shots over those four holes without a single net par along the way.

The damage left Neumann trailing the Trophy lead by as much as 16 shots during the round. But he steadied himself over the closing holes, clawing his way back to finish just 9 off Gregg Williams's lead — a recovery of 7 shots from his lowest ebb. It was not enough to trouble the podium, with Williams's advantage over the field cut to 5 by the day's end, but the fightback turned what looked like a write-off round into a respectable finish, salvaged almost entirely on the back nine after the front had threatened to ruin the day completely.

## David Mullin's rollercoaster card at Camiral
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin's round at the Stadium course at Camiral was a rollercoaster from the start. He opened with a bogey, a double bogey and another bogey through the first three holes before halting the slide with a birdie at the 4th. A quadruple bogey seven followed at the 5th. He then settled, playing holes 8 to 10 without dropping a gross shot and gaining three shots to net par across the stretch. The last of those, the 9th — the hardest hole on the course — carried him into outright top spot in the 2013 Green Jacket (Gross) standings, a lead he had taken from Jon Baker.

The round then fell apart again from 14 to 16: a double bogey, a triple bogey (the triple coming at the 15th, the easiest hole on the course) and another double bogey bled seven gross shots to par. Baker drew level with Mullin for the Green Jacket lead at the 16th, but Mullin still finished the round out in front, the gap standing at four.
