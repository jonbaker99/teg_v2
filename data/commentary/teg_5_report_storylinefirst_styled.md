## How Gregg Williams, the reigning Wooden Spoon holder, took his first Trophy by two after David Mullin's five-shot lead evaporated at Boavista in Round 3
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

Gregg Williams won the TEG 5 Trophy at Lagos, six under net par and two clear of Jon Baker. He arrived holding the Wooden Spoon. He left holding the Trophy. The distance between those two objects is shorter than anyone in this field would care to admit. It ranks third among Williams's own Trophy returns, a detail he is under no obligation to raise again.

For much of the tournament it belonged to someone else. David Mullin took the net lead late on the opening day at Boavista and held it from the 18th hole of Round 1 across 21 holes, which is precisely long enough to become attached to something. Williams passed him at the 3rd in Round 3: a par on the par five, worth two under net. No thunderclap. A five, and the leaderboard quietly rearranged itself.

The damage had been done the day before. Williams's Round 2 at Boavista, 27 over gross for six under net, was the best round of his career by that measure, and it came on his ninth visit to the course, eight strokes better than his previous appearance there. It also erased a Round 1 in which he had contrived a nine at the 17th.

Baker's own chance went at Boavista in Round 3, where he made two quintuple-bogey nines, at the 7th and again at the closing hole. One is misfortune. Two is a theme.

That left Williams with room to survive Palmares, a first visit for the group, and he used every inch of it. He dropped ten shots across holes 10 to 13, then added another nine at the 16th, the whole sequence conducted with the calm of a man who had checked the arithmetic and found he could afford it. It was enough. Mullin took the TEG 5 Green Jacket on gross.

## Stuart Neumann posts the best round in TEG history at Boavista, then hands the whole of it back on the same closing holes a day later
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Stuart Neumann**

On the Monday at Boavista, Stuart Neumann played the best round in TEG history. His -9 net was a record, and his +11 gross was a record too: an 82 on a course he had already visited seven times, where his previous best was 89. Twelve strokes better than his last visit there, and a course personal best by a distance. For one afternoon he was, incontestably, the finest golfer the tournament had ever produced.

The Tuesday at the same course took it all back. He opened proceedings with a 7 at the par-three 2nd, a quadruple bogey, which turned out to be merely the overture. The round was lost over the closing stretch: a double bogey 7 at the 16th, a triple bogey 7 at the 17th and a quintuple bogey 9 at the 18th. Ten gross shots in three holes, with no net par among them.

Wednesday's move to Palmares, the first TEG visit to the course, offered a clean slate, and he declined it. Triple bogey, triple bogey, double bogey through the 2nd, 3rd and 4th: eight shots gone in three holes. He at least finished tidily, playing the 16th, 17th and 18th in level par gross, worth three net shots to par, for a round of 96 on a course nobody in the field had seen before. The man who set the record and the man who signed for the 96 were, technically, the same man.

## Henry Meller, holder of two Wooden Spoons and bottom of the race after Rounds 2 and 3, escapes a third with the best round of his TEG career
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Henry Meller's rounds at Boavista gave little warning of how it would end. His second round produced three of the tournament's biggest holes: a 10 at the par-five 16th, the worst score against par he has recorded anywhere; an 8 at the par-four 18th; and a 7 at the par-three 14th. The round finished 30 over gross, with a twelve-shot split between his two nines, one of which was plainly having a different day from the other.

Round three was steadier without ever threatening much. Pars at the 3rd, 4th and 5th, then a double bogey at the short 6th. His 91 was the best of his three visits to Boavista in this tournament, four strokes better than his last round there, though still short of the 89 he shot on the course in TEG 3.

Palmares, played for the first time in round four, changed the picture. The damage arrived early and got itself over with: seven shots dropped across holes 9 to 11, a triple bogey at the 9th followed by doubles at the 10th and 11th. Everything else held. Meller signed for 89, three under his handicap allowance and a personal best both on net and on gross, where +17 bettered anything he had managed in four TEGs. A man with two Wooden Spoons, bottom of the race after Rounds 2 and 3, had chosen the final available day to play the best golf of his life.

It was enough to close the gap in the TEG 5 Green Jacket from a peak of 30 shots to 18 by the finish.

## Jon Baker loses the TEG 5 Trophy by two while producing a record-tying birdie streak and a personal-best gross round on the final day
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

**Jon Baker: the best round of his life, and still short**

Jon Baker's TEG 5 ended with the finest golf of his career and no trophy to show for it. What came before was littered with wreckage: a quintuple-bogey 8 at Boavista's par-three 6th in round one, then two nines in round three, at the par-four 7th and again at the par-four 18th to close the day. Between them came a passage of genuine competence, holes 9 to 12, worth four shots to par on net.

The final round at Palmares, a course none of the field had played before, produced more of both. Jon opened with five net shots gained across the first four holes, including a birdie at the short 3rd, then put an 8 on the par-three 5th, which is a substantial quantity of golf for a hole of that size. What followed was the moment of the tournament for him: birdies at the 14th and 15th, back to back, tying the all-time TEG record for consecutive birdies.

The round added up to a gross 90, +18, the best gross round Jon Baker has recorded. It hauled him from 19 behind in the TEG 5 Green Jacket race to 12 at the finish. It did not overhaul the TEG 5 Trophy leader. His Boavista scoring had already been eight strokes better than his last visit there; the arithmetic still declined to help.

## David Mullin's fourth Green Jacket in succession, effectively settled inside two rounds at Boavista and including a course record that had stood 35 visits
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

David Mullin won the TEG 5 Green Jacket at +72 gross, 12 clear of Jon Baker, and the contest was effectively decided over the first two days at Boavista.

He opened with a gross round of +12, the lowest gross round recorded in TEG to that point. He then bettered it the following day with +11, a gross 82 that set a new Boavista course record and swept aside the 85 that had stood across 35 prior visits to the Howard Swan parkland at Lagos. The 85 had been Mullin's own mark, set at TEG 3 on one of his seven previous visits. He therefore spent the afternoon dismantling himself, nine strokes better than his last time round the course. The same round was a personal best against the Trophy's net-versus-par measure at −4.

The third day was the opposite extreme: +12 net of par, +27 gross, the worst round of Mullin's career to date. It made no difference whatsoever. Henry Meller, who at one stage trailed by 30, pulled 12 shots back over the closing stretch and still finished 18 adrift. Baker's recovery was smaller in scale: 19 behind at his lowest point, closed to 12 by the end of the final round at Palmares, the first visit to the Robert Trent Jones Jr. course. Neither got close enough to make the last day matter.

## The defending Trophy champion leads by five at the 1st in Round 3 and finishes last: David Mullin's descent from the top of everything to the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

David Mullin led the TEG 5 Trophy on net-versus-par from the 18th hole of the opening round, and held it for 21 holes across three rounds. He set a course best at Boavista along the way: his 82 in Round 2 beat the 85 that had stood as his best there through seven previous visits. He was leading the Trophy, walking towards the Green Jacket and holding a course record. There was nowhere left to go but down, and he went with commitment.

The lead went at the third hole of Round 3, a par five he played in five for a net two. Not a mistake. Simply not enough, as Gregg Williams moved ahead. Round 3 then became Mullin's worst round to date, +12 net and +27 gross, including a nine at the 13th, a par five, for a quadruple bogey.

At Palmares, a course none of the field had played before, he took another nine at the par-five 6th. That one hole dropped him from fourth to last in the TEG 5 Wooden Spoon race, and he stayed there, unbothered and unassisted. He finished the Trophy fifth, having been first after Round 1, and collected the Spoon at +12 net.

The oddity is that the same four rounds produced his best scoring, too. Back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th in Round 4 tied the all-time TEG record for consecutive birdies, and his six birdies or better across TEG 5 is an all-time TEG record for a single tournament. The Spoon and the record belong to the same four rounds, and largely to the same afternoon at Palmares.
