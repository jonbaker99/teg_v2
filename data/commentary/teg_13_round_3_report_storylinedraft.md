# The Marsh Gives It Up

## John Patterson's 49 points at Littlestone, the third-best round in TEG history to date and comfortably the best of his own 19 rounds, arriving straight after two days spent at the bottom of every table
<!-- storyline: round -->

John Patterson's 49 points at Littlestone was the third-best round in TEG history to date, and comfortably the best round of his career. It came straight after two rounds in which he had sat at the bottom of the 2020 Wooden Spoon race, including a modest 28 points in round two — a swing of 21 points between the two days.

The round itself was not flawless. A bogey at the par-5 5th, one of the easier holes at Littlestone, briefly dropped him back into last place in the Spoon standings mid-round. He answered immediately, taking 13 points across holes 8 to 11, the highlight a par at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Steady gross golf either side of that run also delivered a personal-best gross score of +12, and a late quadruple bogey at the 17th did little to dent the total. The 49 points flipped the Wooden Spoon race outright, leaving Alex Baker rather than Patterson anchoring the bottom of the standings.

## Alex Baker's 100 at Littlestone, eight shots dropped in the first three holes and seven more from the 8th to the 10th, which delivers him the bottom of the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon race for the first time
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

A round 100 at Littlestone did the damage. Alex Baker opened with a double bogey at the 1st, then ran up an 8 at the par-4 2nd, one of the harder holes on the course, for a quadruple bogey, before a second double bogey at the 3rd — eight shots bled across the first three holes. There was barely time to settle before another slide: a triple bogey at the 8th, a double at the 9th and another double at the 10th, seven more shots gone in three holes, the 10th coming on the hardest hole on the course.

The double bogey at the 9th also tipped the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon race, sending Baker from fourth to last place, three shots adrift of the next-worst finisher, and displacing John Patterson at the bottom for the first time this TEG. It was Baker's first visit to Littlestone, and the round stands as his benchmark there — not one he will want to chase.

## Gregg Williams's 40 points and 86 gross at Littlestone, a round of sustained competence that gained him nothing at all
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Gregg Williams's third round at Littlestone was a study in quiet, unspectacular consistency. From the 6th he ran off four straight pars, missing no gross shots across the stretch, and the run stretched further still — five holes from the 6th brought him 15 stableford points before a bogey at the 10th, Littlestone's hardest hole, finally interrupted it. He found the same rhythm again from the 12th, three more pars worth nine points, only for the streak to end at the 15th with a double bogey on one of the course's easiest holes, the stroke index 17.

None of it moved him anywhere. Williams closed with 40 points and 86 gross, respectable enough but well short of the 49 that topped the field of five, and with Jon Baker's lead over the field growing rather than shrinking through the round, the steady par-after-par golf left Williams exactly where he started: in the pack, gaining nothing.

## Jon Baker's +8 gross round, the second-best gross round in TEG history to date, which turned a one-point Trophy lead into five and a seven-shot Green Jacket lead into fourteen while Gregg Williams's 40 points went unrewarded
<!-- storyline: race -->

On Littlestone's Championship Links, Jon Baker's round threatened at first to be shared spoils. Gregg Williams opened with a par on the short 1st, one of the easiest holes on the course, to draw level with Baker atop the leaderboard for the 2020 TEG Trophy. It would be the high point of his day.

Baker simply took over. A birdie on the 9th launched a run from the 8th to the 11th in which he gained 13 Stableford points and went four holes without dropping a gross shot, including a par on the brutal 10th, the hardest hole on the course. He finished the round at +8 gross, the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to date, worth 79 strokes.

The damage to the field was severe. Baker's Trophy lead swelled from one point to five, and his 2020 Green Jacket lead ballooned from seven shots to fourteen. Williams's own strong contribution counted for nothing against it, his early share of the lead erased within a round he never controlled again.
