# One Point After Eighteen Holes

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in the field of five. It arrived in two halves that barely acknowledged one another. He had already led the 2026 TEG Trophy once, taking it outright with a par at the 4th. Alex Baker reclaimed it a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in. Double bogeys at the 8th and 9th, then a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots gone in three holes. Not a net par among them.

From the 11th the course opens out towards the sea, and so, abruptly, did he. Pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th for nine points, the 12th restoring his outright lead. Nine points from three holes, having surrendered seven shots on the previous three. The same man, allegedly. Alex Baker edged back in front at the 17th, but Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good. One clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, and a gross 90 for second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria swung violently in both directions. The first damage came in a three-hole stretch from the 5th. A triple bogey at the short hole, a double bogey at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone.

Then the reverse. From the 8th to the 11th he posted a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, the bogey at the 10th earning three of them on the hardest hole on the course. For four holes he looked like a man in full command of his own affairs.

The second collapse was worse. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake. A triple at the 15th followed. Then another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes. One point salvaged, and it was the only thing on the card with a pulse. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He finished with 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 462-yard par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th is the sort of hole that explains why the course bites. It runs 462 yards, stroke index 1, with out of bounds the whole way down the left and a lake in front of the green from 110 yards out. A par there is worth celebrating. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over and no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and the damage moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven, which only capped a wider slide. Doubles at the 8th and 9th sat either side of it, seven shots dropped across three holes.

Between them the trio gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. The hole did not have to do anything clever. It simply stood there. None of it was especially surprising on a course that asks more of this field than most.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

Quinta da Ria gives up strokes grudgingly, and it spent the day taking the 2026 TEG Trophy in and out of Alex Baker's hands. He took the lead outright with a par at the 5th. He lost it in a wretched stretch on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, then a double bogey at the 11th and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots gone in three holes, and the lead gone with them to Jon Baker. He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th. He went back ahead with a par at the 17th. He arrived at the 18th tee leading and left it not leading. Jon Baker closed on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards, finishing on 87. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31. The pair share the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight, which is a sentence neither man will care to read twice.
