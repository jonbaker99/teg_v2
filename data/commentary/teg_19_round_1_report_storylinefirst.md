# One Point in It at Quinta da Ria

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best score of the opening day, salvaged from a three-hole wobble at the 8th, 9th and 10th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker signed for 39 points at Quinta da Ria, the best score of the opening day and three clear of the 31 that propped up the field. It did not arrive tidily. He took the top of the 2026 TEG Trophy as early as the 4th on the strength of a par, which is about as understated as a lead change gets, and Alex Baker had taken it back off him one hole later.

Then the middle of the round came apart. Doubles at the 8th and the 9th, then a triple at the 10th: seven shots gone in three holes, one solitary point from each, on a course that gives up very little to golfers of this standard. That was bad golf, and it was bad golf in a run, which is the version nobody enjoys.

The repair was immediate and frankly heroic. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th, where the holes open out towards the sea and the temptation to look at the view instead of the ball must be considerable, brought him nine points and the lead back at the 12th. Three pars in a row after the morning he'd had is the work of a man who had decided the previous three holes were someone else's problem. Alex Baker edged in front again at the 17th, and then Jon played the 18th in par for four points and took it 39 to 38.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 462-yard par four with water and out of bounds, took a nine, an eight and a seven from the field
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The 10th is the signature hole and, by stroke index, the hardest on the card: 462 yards of par four, out of bounds the whole way down the left, and a lake sitting in front of the green from 110 yards out. A par there would have been talked about all evening. Nobody got within two of one.

Alex Baker took nine, a quintuple bogey, which is worth precisely no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and it shifted him from second from bottom to last in the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race, bottom of the five. Jon Baker's seven was the best of the three, and calling it the best of the three is doing a lot of work.

Between them they handed back 12 shots to par on one hole.

## John Patterson's 105, built from two nines and a ruinous finish, with the best four-hole scoring run of the day in the middle of it
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson came in with 105, 33 over par and the highest score in the field. It all happened in two sittings. From the 5th he went triple bogey, double bogey, quintuple bogey, the last of those a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes out there. Ten shots gone in three holes, and the card was already in serious trouble.

What followed was the best scoring run anyone produced all day. Par, bogey, bogey, bogey from the 8th through to the 11th, three Stableford points on every one of them, 12 points in four holes, and one of those holes was the 10th. Having just been dismantled by the front nine, he turned round and out-scored the entire field over four holes. There are players who go whole rounds without a stretch of that quality.

The finish took all of it back. A 9 at the 14th, the par 4 that doglegs around a lake, then a triple at the 15th, then another at the par-5 16th between the big lakes: 11 shots in three holes, which is a lot of golf to lose in the final stretch. The 14th also dropped him alone to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He finished on 31 points, level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG Trophy, and last in the Green Jacket.

## Alex Baker led the Trophy standing on the 18th tee and finished a point short, while Gregg Williams's 87 opened a three-shot Green Jacket lead
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker's round hinged on two short stretches, one glorious and one not. Three bogeys and a par across the 3rd to the 6th gave him 13 Stableford points and the outright lead in the 2026 TEG Trophy at the 5th, four holes of steady, sensible, unflappable golf. Then came the turn: the quintuple bogey 9 at the 10th, a double, and a triple at the 12th. Ten gross shots in three holes, and the lead posted straight back to Jon Baker. To his enormous credit he drew level again at the 14th and went ahead outright at the 17th, standing on the last tee with the Trophy lead in his pocket. He finished on 38 to Jon Baker's 39.

Gregg Williams took the lead in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket with a par at the 3rd, was clear for good from the 7th, and then refused to let anybody near it, holding firm with pars at the 12th and the 13th while others were posting nines around him. His 87 is three clear of Jon Baker's 90, and his 38 points tied Alex Baker for second in the Trophy. A fine day's work from a man who simply kept making fours.
