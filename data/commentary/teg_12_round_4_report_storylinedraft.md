# Records Fall, Nothing Changes

## John Patterson is crowned TEG 12 Trophy champion by ten from Gregg Williams, whose best ever Stadium round cost him two further points
<!-- storyline: race -->

John Patterson closed out TEG 12 as Trophy champion, beating Gregg Williams by ten points after the final round at PGA Catalunya's Stadium course, where Patterson had also carried the outright lead into the day. He made his move early, picking up 13 points across holes 3-6 despite two bogeys, the strokes he carries on the card keeping the net returns strong. He then dropped six shots in three holes from the 15th, three straight double bogeys, but the overnight cushion meant it barely dented his position. Williams, playing for the runner-up spot, opened his round in trouble: a bogey and two doubles through the 6th before a birdie at the 7th, one of the easier holes on the course, stopped the slide. The round that followed was his best ever on the Stadium course, eight shots better than his last visit there, yet the Trophy gap still widened from eight to ten, leaving him two points further back at the finish. Jon Baker led the Green Jacket race throughout the same round.

## David Mullin takes the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon after quadruple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th, despite producing the best four-hole stretch of the day
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin took the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon on the final round at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course, dropping to the bottom of that standing after a wretched start undid a brilliant finish.

The damage came at the 3rd and 4th, back-to-back quadruple bogeys — a 9 on the par-5 3rd and an 8 on the par-4 4th — followed by a double bogey at the 5th. Ten shots bled away across those three holes, and Mullin went the entire stretch without a single net par, enough to flip him past Alex Baker and to the foot of the Spoon race, four points adrift of the next man up.

Yet the same round contained the best four-hole stretch of his day. From the 11th to the 14th he played par, birdie, par, birdie without dropping a gross shot, picking up 14 stableford points. It was not enough to undo the early collapse: the Spoon was decided by three ruinous holes, not the four good ones that followed them.

## Stuart Neumann's eagle at the par-5 15th, the rescue act inside a personal best at the Stadium
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Stuart Neumann's fourth round at PGA Catalunya's Stadium course was a rescue job. He bled seven shots across holes 3 to 5, running double bogey, triple bogey, double bogey on the par-5 3rd, the par-4 4th and the par-3 5th. A second slide followed late, a double bogey at the 13th and a bogey at the 14th threatening to undo any recovery. But at the par-5 15th, one of the easiest holes on the course, Neumann holed out for an eagle, stopping the bleeding and turning a run of bogeys-or-worse into a five-point stableford swing.

The eagle proved decisive to the shape of his round. Neumann closed with a gross 96, his personal best at the Stadium course and four shots inside the previous mark of 100 he had carried into this, his fourth visit to the venue. Two rough patches threatened to derail the round, but the eagle at the 15th turned it into his best score yet at Camiral.

## Jon Baker's 89 at PGA Catalunya - Stadium: a new course record, a personal best at the venue, and the round that closed out the TEG 12 Green Jacket
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker closed out the TEG 12 Green Jacket in style on the final day at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, carding a gross 89 that set a new course record, beating the prior mark of 90 across 21 previous visits to the venue. It was also a personal best there, improving on his own previous low of 90 at the course. He had already held the Green Jacket lead going into the round, and by the close his advantage had grown from five strokes to eight, with the race unchanged at the top.

The round itself was not without turbulence. A double bogey at the par-5 7th, one of the easiest holes on the course, followed two bogeys in a row, before a birdie at the 8th halted the slide. Baker then settled, playing 14 through 16 without dropping a shot, only for a double bogey at the 17th to end that run just as the record was taking shape. It was enough regardless: the 89 stood as the best round ever posted on the course.
