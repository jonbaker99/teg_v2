# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third best of Mullin's own career, which is to say he has been better twice and rarely at a more convenient moment.

The foundation was laid on the first two links. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit, and the engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars for 13 points. For those four holes he looked like a man who had forgotten he was capable of anything else. The sequence ended at the 13th with a double bogey. The damage was limited. At Littlestone he shot 85, again seven better than last time out, and another course best.

Prince's declined to co-operate. In Round 3 he stood on the par-five 8th and took nine. A quadruple bogey, worth nothing, no explanation offered to anybody. He then recovered with 13 points over the closing four holes, three pars and a birdie at the last, as though the 8th had happened to someone else. Round 4 produced a second quadruple bogey, an eight at the 10th. Two holes cost him eight shots against par and returned not a single point, and he won regardless. That is roughly what winning looks like round here.

The door thus ajar, Baker went through it. He had trailed by as many as nine. Ten points across the first three holes on Sunday, eleven more from the 13th to the 15th, and the margin was two. Then the 16th, where he ran up a ten. His unravelling had in fact begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5, a stretch no amount of late resistance was ever going to refund.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Two of the four rounds in Kent ended with Alex Baker writing a 10 on his card, and on both occasions the 16th was the culprit. Different courses, different holes, identical digit.

The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports: a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's on the Sunday, was worse in relative terms. A 10 at a par four. A sextuple bogey. On stroke index 17, the second-easiest hole on the card, a hole the handicapping system had examined and concluded he required no assistance with whatsoever.

The Prince's 10 was the more painful for what preceded it. Par, par, par at the 13th, 14th and 15th. Three holes without dropping a gross shot, worth eleven Stableford points. The 16th removed the lot in a single visit. He was not alone in losing his footing there. Jon Baker arrived at the same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th, and made a double bogey for nothing.

These were merely the headline numbers. Baker also had an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in round three. Five further holes returning nothing at all. His round-one 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was, against all available evidence, four strokes better than his previous visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. The finest gross golf of his TEG career, and worth precisely no silverware. It was built on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than his previous mark there of 94, and 86 at Prince's in round three, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

It was not tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing on the card. Littlestone was the broader indignity: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he managed there on his previous visit, and only 27 Stableford points. A long afternoon by any measure.

Round three was the correction. He gained twelve points on the day, reaching 39, and the middle of it was genuinely solid. Pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points and three holes without dropping a gross shot, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. Between them came an 8 at the par-five 12th, which ended the run. There is always an 8.

Sunday offered more of the same steadiness in patches, gross pars at the 5th and 6th and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at one stage, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered. Eight short.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket at TEG 14 with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth best of Mullin's own career at the metric.

The number was built on consistency across Kent's four links rounds. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his previous visit in TEG 13, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. None of those is a spectacular score in isolation. Together they were enough to keep him out of reach.

Behind him the chase was real but late. Baker at one point trailed by 15 and closed to seven, the bulk of that work coming in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record. The previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits, and was Baker's own. Gregg Williams made up even more ground, trailing by 18 at his worst and finishing eight back. Williams's +76 is a personal-best Green Jacket gross total, and his 86 at Prince's in round three was a course best for him too.

The gap never closed far enough. Mullin's margin of seven at the finish flatters the field, since the lead was larger for most of the tournament.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker finished last in the Stableford count and took the Wooden Spoon with 125 points across the four rounds, twelve adrift of the next worst. This was not a near miss. It was a vacancy filled with conviction.

The tournament's early links did him no favours. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven strokes worse than the 85 he had managed on his previous visit. At Littlestone he posted 92 against a prior best of 79, a comparison it is difficult to look at directly. His third round at Prince's brought 28 points, held down in part by a run of pars from the 14th that ended with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

The final round belonged to a different man entirely. Baker returned 39 points, an improvement of 11 on the previous day, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the previous mark of 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best on the layout, two strokes better than his last time there. The scoring came in a burst. Five holes without dropping a gross shot from the 11th to the 15th, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th, a two-hole birdie streak that equals the all-time TEG record, and 14 points across holes 12 to 15. For four holes he played like a man who had never heard of the Wooden Spoon.

Then the 16th, a double bogey, and the run was over. Then an eight at the 18th. The Spoon, which had briefly wondered whether it had chosen wrongly, settled back into his hands.
