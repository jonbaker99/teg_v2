# Ninety at the Stadium

## Jon Baker's 90 gross at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, a new course record and 35 Stableford points to lead the TEG 11 Trophy after Round 1
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker began TEG 11 in Girona with the best round anyone has posted at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, a gross 90 that broke the course record of 94, a mark set across five prior visits to the venue. It was only Baker's second time on the course, and it improved his own previous best there, 99, by nine strokes.

The round was built on steady par golf rather than fireworks. Baker went bogey-free through the 3rd and 4th, and again through the 10th and 11th, holding his score together at a course that had never yielded anything close to this. A par on the 3rd also drew him level for the lead in both the Green Jacket (Gross) and the TEG 11 Trophy, having sat third beforehand.

He carried that form to the clubhouse with 35 Stableford points, the best score of the day in a five-man field where the worst was 26. After Round 1, Baker shares the lead in both the Trophy and the Jacket.

## Gregg Williams's 101 at the Stadium Course — a dreadful opening three holes and an 8 at the par-three 5th, and still twelve shots better than his last visit here
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams opened his round at the Stadium Course in the worst possible fashion, dropping eight shots across the first three holes: triple bogeys at the 1st and 2nd, then a double bogey at the par-five 3rd, without managing a single net par along the way. Things got no better at the par-three 5th, one of the easier holes on the card by stroke index, where he ran up a quintuple bogey 8. Between the opening stretch and that hole alone, the damage was already done for the day.

Yet the round represented a substantial improvement for Williams at this course. His gross 101 was twelve shots better than the 113 he carded on his only previous visit here, making it his best score yet at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course through two visits. Given how the round started, and the further blow at the 5th, salvaging a course-best score from such wreckage was no small thing.

## The state of the TEG 11 Trophy, Green Jacket and Wooden Spoon races after Round 1, told from the bottom of the card where John Patterson's four blow-up holes cost him the ground
<!-- storyline: race -->

John Patterson's Round 1 in Catalonia was a story of two entirely different rounds stitched together. Early on he was sharing the TEG 11 Trophy lead, drawing level on the par-5 7th despite a bogey there. But the round did not hold. A quintuple-bogey 9 at the 4th, part of a stretch that bled nine shots across holes 2-4, was the first sign of trouble, and worse followed: a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 10th, then a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 13th as part of an 11-shot bleed across holes 12-14, and a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 17th. That triple bogey on the 14th, one of the harder holes on the course, was enough to drop Patterson to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race. He finished the round having gone from a share of the lead to last place, with a gross score of 112. At the top, Jon Baker emerged after the round leading both the Trophy and the TEG 11 Green Jacket.
