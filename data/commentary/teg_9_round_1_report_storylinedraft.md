# A New Best, A New Worst At Royal Óbidos

## David Mullin's course-record 87 gross at Royal Óbidos in the TEG 9 first round
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin set a new course record at Royal Óbidos in the opening round of TEG 9, shooting 87 gross to beat the previous mark of 90 he had set on his only prior visit, at TEG 7. The improvement of three strokes came together with a steady middle stretch, three holes without dropping a gross shot from the 13th through the 15th, including a par on the 18th-hardest hole on the course. That run ended at the 16th, one of the easiest holes at Royal Óbidos, where a double bogey broke the streak. It did little to derail the round, though: Mullin finished with a par at the par-5 18th, a hole ranked among the harder ones on the course, to move into outright second in the TEG 9 Green Jacket (Gross) standings. With a field-best gross score of 15 recorded elsewhere on the day, Mullin's 87 stood as the new benchmark for the course, one no player had matched across two TEG visits there.

## Alex Baker's 115 gross, a new Royal Óbidos course-worst and his own poorest round on record
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's opening round at Royal Óbidos on Friday produced a 115 gross, worse than any score recorded at the course before it — beating the previous worst of 108, set across six prior visits by the field, by seven strokes. It was also comfortably the worst gross round of Baker's own career.

The damage was built hole by hole. At the 2nd, one of the harder holes on the course, he ran up a quadruple bogey. Worse followed at the 12th, another tough hole, where a quintuple bogey 9 went down. The low point came at the 14th, where Baker took an 11 — a septuple bogey, and his own worst score against par recorded anywhere. The round finished at +43, the worst gross score of the day by some distance, with the field's best sitting at 15 over.

The round also cost him ground in the Wooden Spoon race, dropping him to last place after the 2nd hole — a position that, on this evidence, was no fluke.

## John Patterson's TEG debut at Royal Óbidos, a brief Trophy lead undone almost as soon as it began by a sextuple bogey
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's first appearance in the TEG record book, at Royal Óbidos, began badly and never fully settled. At the 4th, the hardest hole on the course, he ran up a 10, a sextuple bogey that yielded nothing on the Stableford card. Yet the damage did not stop him climbing: a par at the par-5 5th, worth four points, lifted him from third into the outright lead of the 2016 TEG Trophy, an early high point in a debut round that had already gone wrong once.

It did not last. At the short 15th he carded a quadruple bogey 7, the kind of hole that undoes a Stableford card built on recovery rather than consistency. Patterson did find a spell of control late on, going two holes without dropping a gross shot at the 16th and 17th, closing with a par and a birdie. But the two big numbers had already done the damage to his debut.

## Jon Baker's one-point Trophy lead and Stuart Neumann's early anchor in the Wooden Spoon after round one of TEG 9
<!-- storyline: race -->

Royal Óbidos hosted the opening round of TEG 9, and the Trophy lead changed hands twice inside the first five holes. Gregg Williams drew level at the 4th despite a double bogey on the hardest hole on the course, before John Patterson moved outright ahead at the par-5 5th. By the end of the round, though, it was Jon Baker sitting atop the TEG 9 Trophy standings, level at the top after going nine strokes better than his previous visit here for a course personal best. David Mullin, also posting his own course PB, took the early lead in the TEG 9 Green Jacket.

Stuart Neumann had a rougher time of it. A triple bogey at the 14th opened a five-hole stretch that bled him 16 shots, capped by a quintuple bogey at the 15th. That collapse left him bottom of the TEG 9 Wooden Spoon race after round one, a hole he will be looking to climb out of over the remaining rounds.
