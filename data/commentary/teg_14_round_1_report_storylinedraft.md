# Deal Rewrites Its Own Numbers

## David Mullin's 40-point round at Royal Cinque Ports, built on a four-hole surge through the turn and delivering an 87 that is his personal best on the course by seven shots
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin's opening round at Royal Cinque Ports was built around a four-hole surge through the turn. From the 9th to the 12th he picked up 13 points, holing a birdie at the 10th and playing the other three in par without dropping a gross shot, a run that took him level for the Trophy lead through the 11th. The double bogey that followed at the 13th, one of the harder holes on the course, ended that steady stretch, but Mullin regrouped, adding two more untroubled pars at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course, and the 16th, the latter enough to put him outright in front for the Trophy lead. He finished with 40 points, the best score of the round, and a gross 87 that beat his previous best on the course, set on his only prior visit, by seven shots.

## Alex Baker posts a 10 at the 16th and an 8 at the 11th, bleeds six shots across the 3rd, 4th and 5th, and still walks off with 37 points and a personal best at Royal Cinque Ports
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

At Royal Cinque Ports Golf Club, Alex Baker's round swung between fine golf and outright calamity. Three straight doubles at the 3rd, 4th and 5th bled six shots off his card early on, but he steadied to draw level for the lead in the 2021 Green Jacket (Gross) with a par at the 6th. The recovery did not hold: an 8 at the 11th, one of the course's hardest holes, and a quintuple-bogey 10 at the 16th wiped out the good work. Even so, Baker closed with 37 Stableford points and a gross 98 — four shots better than his previous best at the course and a personal best there. It was a round of extremes: a promising start, a costly stretch, two blow-up holes, and still enough in the bank to leave Deal with his best score yet at Royal Cinque Ports.

## Jon Baker, who owns the lowest round this field has ever played at Royal Cinque Ports, loses seven shots on that mark and ends day one last in the TEG 14 Trophy and bottom of the Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker arrived at Royal Cinque Ports, on his second visit to the course, holding the best round this field has ever managed there — an 85 shot in the TEG 13 Trophy. Round one of the TEG 14 Trophy went the other way entirely: a 92 that gave back seven shots on that mark. The damage came early. A double bogey on the 2nd was followed by a triple bogey on the par-5 3rd, a bogey on the short 4th — one of the easiest holes on the course — and further triple and single bogeys on the 5th and 6th, five holes without a net par that dropped him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race. A tidy run of three straight pars from the 11th, worth nine stableford points, offered brief respite but came too late to matter. Baker finished last in the Trophy standings after round one, and bottom of the Spoon race too, while David Mullin's 87 took the lead in both the Trophy and the Green Jacket.
