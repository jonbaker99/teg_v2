# Four Tens and One Untroubled Leader: Gregg Williams Takes the TEG 8 Trophy by Twelve

## How Gregg Williams won the TEG 8 Trophy by 12: three best-in-field rounds either side of one ruinous R3, quintuple bogeys included
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

Gregg Williams won the TEG 8 Trophy in Portugal with 149 Stableford points, finishing 12 clear of Alex Baker. He led the Trophy standings at the end of rounds two, three and four, and never gave the lead back.

The route there was not tidy. His best round came at Penha Longa on the Thursday, 41 points, and even that included a quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-five 12th, a hole ranked stroke index 17. He signed for a 96 there, and led the Trophy at the halfway stage, with David Mullin heading the TEG 8 Green Jacket race on gross.

Friday's return to Quinta da Marinha was the damage. Williams went triple bogey, double bogey, quadruple bogey, quintuple bogey across the 8th, 9th, 10th and 11th — a seven at the 8th, a five at the short 9th, a nine at the 10th and another 10 at the par-five 11th. Fourteen shots went in four holes, and three of them returned no points at all. The round came in at 29 points, a drop of 12 on the previous day, and by then Jon Baker had taken over at the top of the Jacket.

The lead survived it. Williams closed at Oitavos Dunes with a 93, his best gross of the week, still in front on both counts that mattered to him.

## Alex Baker, reigning Wooden Spoon holder, finishes runner-up in the TEG 8 Trophy while carding a 10 and three 9s along the way
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker opened TEG 8 at Quinta da Marinha with five straight bogeys across holes 1-5, three points on each and 15 in all, and turned with a front nine ten points better than the back. The round came in at 30 points and 41 over par, with the damage compressed into the closing stretch: a double bogey at the 15th, a triple at the short 16th, a nine at the 17th and an eight at the last — 14 shots dropped in four holes, and not a single net par from the 16th onwards.

The pattern recurred. At Penha Longa he ran up a nine at the par-five 18th. Back at Quinta da Marinha in round three, he played the 7th to the 10th in 13 over: a double, a ten at the 8th, the stroke index one hole, another double, then a triple at the par-five 10th. An eight at the 12th followed. From the 3rd to the 16th he made nothing better than a bogey — 14 consecutive holes — until a birdie at the 17th ended the run. That round's 111 was his better of two visits to the course.

At Oitavos Dunes he finished with a seven at the par-three 15th and a nine at the 18th. His 104 there was the lowest gross score he posted all week, on a links he was seeing for the first time.

## The defending Trophy champion goes backwards: Henry Meller's title defence ends with 22 shots lost over the first six holes at Oitavos Dunes
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Henry Meller's week on the Lisbon Coast turned on holes he could not stop bleeding. It began in the opening round at Quinta da Marinha, where he ran up a 10 at the par-five 3rd — a quintuple bogey, and the worst score against par he has recorded on any hole. That single hole moved him to the bottom of the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon standings, from fifth to sixth. The pattern repeated at Penha Longa in round two, where he took nine at the 16th, the stroke index one hole.

Round three offered a reprieve: 34 points at Quinta da Marinha, off a gross 95, his best round of the week. It made what followed at Oitavos Dunes harder to explain. Meller played the first six holes in 22 shots over par — double bogey, quadruple, triple, then a 10 at the par-four 4th and a nine at the 5th, before a second double at the 6th. Six holes, no net par, two Stableford points, and a hole in his card that could not be repaired over the closing twelve.

He signed for 21 points, a fall of 13 from the previous day, and a gross 111 on his first visit to the links. The blowups that had punctuated the first three rounds arrived together in the fourth.

## Quinta da Marinha's return in R3 takes an 8 or worse from four of the six and rearranges both leaderboards in a single afternoon
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

**Quinta da Marinha bites back**

The second visit to Quinta da Marinha, on the Friday, undid most of what the field had built at Penha Longa. Four of the six players ran up an 8 or worse: Stuart Neumann took 11 at the par-five 10th, Alex Baker 10 at the par-four 8th, Gregg Williams 10 at the par-five 11th, and David Mullin 8 at the 12th.

Neumann's damage came in bulk — 17 shots dropped over the first five holes, including a 10 at the 1st, then 13 more across the 9th, 10th and 11th. Alex Baker bled 13 shots from the 7th to the 10th and added a quadruple bogey at the 12th. Williams gave back 14 shots between the 8th and the 11th.

The scoring told the story. Mullin signed for 26 points, the worst round of his career, ten fewer than the 36 he posted in round two. Williams fell from 41 points to 29. Mullin also lost the TEG 8 Green Jacket lead at the 13th hole, having held it for 36 holes since the 13th in round one; Jon Baker took it over, his 89 the lowest gross return of the day's field on the course.

After three rounds Williams led the TEG 8 Trophy on Stableford, level with the player behind him, while Jon Baker held the Jacket.

## David Mullin's sixth Green Jacket: 36 holes in front, dethroned by Jon Baker in R3, and the lead taken back on the second hole of R4
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

David Mullin won the TEG 8 Green Jacket, finishing at +77 to beat Jon Baker by six strokes. The route there was not straightforward.

Mullin took the lead in the gross competition at the 13th hole of the opening round at Quinta da Marinha and held it for 36 holes, through Penha Longa and into the third round. It ended back at Quinta da Marinha's 13th, where a par was enough to see him overtaken by Jon Baker. The damage had been done a hole earlier: a quadruple-bogey eight at the 12th, the course's second-hardest hole, contributed to a round of 26 points — the worst round of Mullin's career to that point, and ten points down on the 36 he had posted at Penha Longa the day before. He left Quinta da Marinha with a best gross there of the 91 he shot in round one. Baker led the Jacket overnight, with Gregg Williams top of the TEG 8 Trophy standings.

Round four at Oitavos Dunes brought more of the same in patches. Mullin ran up another eight at the 11th, then bogeyed the 14th and 15th before a birdie at the par-five 16th halted the slide. His 88 was his best gross of the week on the links. By the close he had the Jacket lead back, while Williams still headed the Trophy.

## Stuart Neumann's Wooden Spoon: bottom of the field from the fourth hole of R1 onwards, and 11 adrift of Henry Meller at the end
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

Stuart Neumann took the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon with 100 points, and the shape of it was set on the opening day.

At Quinta da Marinha in round one he signed for 17 points and a gross of +43 — the worst round of his TEG career on both counts. The damage was concentrated in a nine-hole stretch from the 7th to the 15th, where he bled 29 shots to par and failed to make a single net par: back-to-back nines at the par-five 10th and 11th, a nine at the 12th, and an eight at the par-three 14th.

Penha Longa offered a partial reprieve — his best gross of the week to that point, 99 — though the 16th still cost him a nine. The return to Quinta da Marinha in round three was worse in patches. He opened with a ten at the 1st and dropped 17 shots across the first five holes, then ran up an eight at the par-three 9th and an eleven at the 10th, a sextuple bogey. That round produced 20 points.

The closing day at Oitavos Dunes at least gave him something to take home. Neumann posted 32 points, a 12-point improvement on round three and comfortably his strongest golf of the week, with a gross 95 on a links course he had never seen before. It was not enough to move him off the bottom.
