# An Eleven, a Ten, and the Trophy: Alex Baker Wins in Catalonia

## Alex Baker's Trophy: 169 points, two field-best rounds, and a course-worst gross that cost him nothing

Alex Baker won the Trophy with 169 points, eight clear of runner-up John Patterson. It is the seventh-highest Trophy total on record and the second-best of his own career. He led the Stableford count after each of the final three rounds and, once he reached the front after R2, was never headed again. That is a proper win, and nothing that follows takes it away from him.

The scoring came in bursts. On the Tour Course in R1 he took 17 points from holes 8 to 12, a run built not on brilliance but on bogeys arriving at precisely the holes where a bogey pays. He then added 13 more across 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-five 15th. His largest single stretch came in R3 on the Stadium Course: 21 points from holes 11 to 16, six holes without a blank. For roughly ninety minutes he looked like a man who had solved golf.

The gross figures record a different man entirely. In R2 he made an 8 at the par-three 16th and, at the par-five 18th, an 11. Both were personal worsts against par. He signed for 106, a new PGA Catalunya Tour course-worst, beating the 105 that had stood across 22 prior visits. Twenty-two visits had failed to find the bottom. He found it in an afternoon. R4 then produced a 10 at the 14th and an 8 at the 17th, offered without apparent embarrassment. His best gross of the trip was 98 on the Tour and 100 on the Stadium, against prior bests of 92 and 93. It cost him the Green Jacket, where he slid from second after R1 to fifth. On Stableford, it cost him nothing whatsoever. He did not appear to lose sleep over the distinction.

## David Mullin: an 11 at the 15th, then the lowest gross round ever played at the Stadium

**David Mullin**

Mullin visited both extremes, and the first arrived at the 15th on the Tour Course in Round 1. The par five took him eleven strokes. A sextuple bogey, no Stableford points, and the worst score he has ever recorded on a par five. The consequences reached beyond the card: the hole dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, which is to say the bottom of it.

Two rounds later, on the Stadium Course, he produced something the tournament had never seen. His 84 gross in Round 3 is a new course record at the Stadium, beating the 89 that had survived 27 prior visits. It was also emphatically his own best there, his previous mark being 91 across five earlier visits, and it was eight strokes better than his last time round the place. For one afternoon he was the finest golfer ever to walk it.

Round 4 arrived to correct the record. Through the closing stretch he went four holes without a net par: a double bogey at the 14th, a triple at the 15th, then doubles at the 16th and 17th. Nine shots in four holes, and five Stableford points he could reasonably have banked went with them. A tournament, then, of one number nobody will forget and several he would prefer they did.

## John Patterson's second place, and the eight holes on the opening morning

John Patterson's tournament was defined, and very nearly buried, in a single stretch of the opening morning on the Tour Course. At the 3rd he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, and then declined to stop. Holes 3 through 10 cost him 22 shots to par, with triple bogeys at the 4th, 7th, 8th and 9th. Across the seven holes from the 3rd to the 9th he did not manage a single net par. By the 4th he had dropped from fourth to fifth in the Wooden Spoon standings, which is the bottom, and it was still breakfast time.

What followed explains why he was in the argument at the end. Later in that same round he took 13 points from the 13th to the 16th: three pars and a bogey at the par-five 15th. In round two he made another eight at the 4th and leaked 11 shots between the 6th and the 10th, then answered with 16 points across the 11th to the 15th. That round produced a 94, his best gross on the Tour Course, bettering his previous 96. The disaster and the personal best shared a scorecard without either acknowledging the other.

The pattern held to the last day. After a triple at the 14th and a double at the 15th on the Stadium, he halted the slide with a bogey at the 16th and a birdie at the 17th. Second place, built entirely on the recoveries, and made necessary entirely by the collapses.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: reigning Wooden Spoon holder to +66 and a 14-shot win / Jon Baker's Wooden Spoon: the defending Trophy champion finishes 23 points adrift of the next man

**Round 1, Tour Course.** Gregg Williams opened in the manner the holder of a Wooden Spoon is expected to. A quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 7th. Another quadruple bogey, an 8, at the 17th. In between, a sequence of dropped shots running from the 2nd to the 14th, taking in triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th. A birdie at the par-5 15th eventually brought it to a halt. He signed for 31 points. Jon Baker, meanwhile, had led the Green Jacket gross race since the 5th hole of that same round and looked entirely comfortable there.

**Round 2, Tour Course.** The Sunday round ended Baker's tenure at the top after 20 holes. A bogey at the 7th handed the gross lead to Williams. Baker responded with some conviction: a birdie at the 14th began a run of five holes without dropping a gross shot, worth 16 points to the 18th, and by the close he was the Jacket leader again, with Alex Baker in front in the Trophy. Williams had staged his own surge over the same finish, 13 points from the 13th to the 16th, four consecutive pars and not a gross shot dropped, and posted 42 points, an 11-point improvement on his opening round.

**Round 3, Stadium Course.** The Jacket then changed hands for good, and early. Williams parred the 2nd and took the outright gross lead. Baker's round dismantled itself at the 4th, where a 9 on a par 4, a quintuple bogey, was recorded as a blank. By nightfall the boards read Alex Baker in the Trophy and Williams in the Jacket, and neither line moved again.

**Round 4, Stadium Course.** Williams birdied the par-3 5th, parred the 6th, and collected nine points across the 8th, 9th and 10th. It closed out an 84, his best gross in seven visits to the Stadium and eight shots better than his last time round it. That is the second 84 in this report, and the second eight-shot improvement. The Stadium had evidently decided who it liked.

The two ledgers closed in opposite corners. Williams took the Green Jacket at +66, winning by 14 from David Mullin, the sixth-best gross total in TEG history and comfortably his own best. Jon Baker, the defending Trophy champion, led the Jacket from the fifth hole of the tournament, finished on 126 Stableford points, 23 adrift of the next man, and collected the Wooden Spoon. He has the whole year to consider how he managed both.
