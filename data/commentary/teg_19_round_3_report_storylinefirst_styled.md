## Alex Baker's 50 points, the best round of the day and the third-best of his 51 TEG rounds, assembled out of two brilliant stretches and four consecutive double bogeys
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker signed for 50 points at Monte Rei. It was the best score in a field of five. The worst was 35, a number that will matter later. It was also the third-best round of his 51 in TEG. His 89 gross was a personal best at the course, one stroke better than the 90 from his only previous visit.

It was built out of extremes. He took 14 points from the opening four holes, a bogey followed by three pars, and then handed a double bogey to the 5th, one of the easiest holes on the course. A par at the 6th and a birdie two at the 7th restarted him. From the 11th to the 14th he made four consecutive double bogeys and bled eight shots. Four holes, one idea, executed without deviation. Then he took 11 points from the 15th to the 17th, including a par at the hardest hole Monte Rei has.

The gross ledger was less generous. Alex Baker has slipped from second after Round 1 to fifth in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. He still leads the Trophy by 14. He is being measured by two instruments and only one of them likes him.

## John Patterson's 49 points, the second-best round of his 35, built on 21 points across seven holes from the 4th to the 10th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's 49 points at Monte Rei was the second-best round of his 35 in TEG. Almost all of it came from simply refusing to make a mess. From the 4th to the 10th he collected 21 points across seven holes: five pars and two bogeys, three points every single time. The dropped shots came at the par-5 6th and the 10th, two of the harder holes on the Nicklaus North Course. Holes 7 to 9 went by without a gross shot lost. So did his opening two, where a par at the par-5 2nd was worth four points.

It was not quite enough to take the round. The best score of the day was 50. His 84 gross was also two strokes more than the 82 he posted here on the first visit, so no course best either. He did the diligent thing for seven straight holes and was beaten by one point. It lifted him to second in the 2026 TEG Trophy on 147, 14 behind Alex Baker, and third in the Green Jacket at +36.

## Gregg Williams loses the TEG 19 Green Jacket lead and falls level at the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race on the day he sets a Monte Rei course worst
<!-- storyline: race -->

Gregg Williams arrived at Monte Rei on the Monday holding the TEG 19 Green Jacket lead. A par at the 3rd, one of the hardest holes on the course, kept him there outright. It did not last. Bogeys at the 4th and 5th and a double at the 6th cost him four shots. Birdies at the 7th and 8th steadied things, briefly. Jon Baker drew level with a par at the 11th, then went ahead outright with a birdie at the 12th. By the end of the round the gap was six.

The back nine then took Gregg Williams somewhere worse. A triple-bogey 7 at the 15th, the hardest hole at Monte Rei, put him level with David Mullin at the bottom of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon race. A triple-bogey 8 at the 16th left him there alone. Then David Mullin's double bogey at the last squared it again, an act of solidarity nobody had requested. His 91 is a new Monte Rei course worst, beating the previous 90 by a single stroke. His 35 Stableford points were 15 fewer than his R2 total. He was, at least, consistent about the direction of travel.
