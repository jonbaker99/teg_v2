# Nine Clear on the Romney Marsh

## David Mullin's 41-point, 85-gross round at Littlestone, a new personal best at the course by seven shots, built out of a scruffy front nine and a birdie finish
<!-- storyline: round -->

**David Mullin's 41 at Littlestone**

David Mullin's second round at Littlestone was the best score of the day — 41 points, from a gross 85 — and it did not look like much for the first six holes. He took a triple bogey seven at the 4th, one of the hardest holes on the course, followed it with a double bogey at the 5th and a bogey at the 3rd-hole-length par-3 6th, and had gone a run of holes without making anything better than a bogey before the 7th arrived. There he made a birdie, and added a par at the 8th.

The middle of the round was steady rather than spectacular: pars at the 10th — stroke index 1, the hardest hole on the course — and the 11th, then pars again at the 13th and 14th. The finish wobbled, with bogeys at the 15th and 16th and a double bogey five at the 17th, before another birdie at the last closed it out.

The gross total is a Littlestone personal best. Mullin's only previous visit to the course had produced a best of 92, so this was an improvement of seven shots. It also stretched both of his leads: his advantage in the 2021 TEG Trophy grew from three points to nine, and his lead in the 2021 Green Jacket went from five shots to twelve.

## Littlestone's revenge on the field — three of four players worse than their TEG 13 visit, and the day's modest definition of a good stretch
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Littlestone takes its due**

The Championship Links had only been seen once before, in TEG 13, and it collected on the debt. Jon Baker went round in 92, thirteen strokes worse than the 79 he posted here on the first visit. Alex Baker matched his TEG 13 figure exactly: 100, again.

Alex Baker's card was undone by two holes. A quadruple bogey 9 at the par-5 5th — one of the easiest holes on the course — was followed three holes later by an 8 at the 8th, also a quadruple bogey, and neither returned a Stableford point.

The day's best run of golf, on the gross figures, tells you most of what you need to know about conditions and form. Jon Baker's strongest passage was two holes long: a par at the 14th and a par at the 15th, the second-easiest hole on the course, for five points and not a shot dropped to par across the pair.

The consequence was a widening at both ends. David Mullin's lead in the 2021 Trophy grew from three to nine, and his Green Jacket margin from five to twelve. Jon Baker stayed bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, though his cushion to the player above shrank from four shots to two.

## Alex Baker's six-point slide behind Mullin in the TEG 14 Trophy, and the narrowing of the Wooden Spoon gap between Jon Baker and Gregg Williams
<!-- storyline: race -->

**Alex Baker's four-hole collapse hands Mullin daylight**

The damage was done inside twenty minutes. Alex Baker played holes 2 to 5 at Littlestone in twelve shots over par: a triple bogey at the 2nd, a double at the 3rd, another triple at the 4th — one of the hardest holes on the course — and then a quadruple bogey 9 at the 5th, which is one of the easiest. Four holes, three single points and a blank, five points short of net par over the stretch.

That was enough to turn a close race into a comfortable one. David Mullin, who led the TEG 14 Trophy after round one, saw his advantage widen from three points to nine, and his lead in the TEG 14 Green Jacket grow from five to twelve. Mullin shot 85 at Littlestone, a course best and seven strokes better than his previous visit here; Alex Baker matched his 100 from that same first visit exactly.

At the other end, the TEG 14 Wooden Spoon is getting tighter rather than looser. Jon Baker still holds last place, as he did before the round, but the cushion between him and the player immediately above him has halved, from four points to two. Two rounds remain at a venue only being played for the second time, and neither end of the table is settled.
