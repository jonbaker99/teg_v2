## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker went round Quinta da Ria on the Saturday in 39 points, the best anyone in the field of five could manage, and he did it in two halves that barely seem to belong to the same man. He had already held the 2026 TEG Trophy lead once, taken outright with a par at the 4th, only for Alex Baker to help himself to it again a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine got hold of him: double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th, followed by a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots gone in three holes, not a net par among them, and no way of dressing it up as anything but bad golf.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he steadied himself at once. Pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th, nine points in three holes, and the 12th handed him his outright lead back. Three consecutive pars on this course counts as a golden age, and the field will be hearing about it for some time. Alex Baker nosed ahead again at the 17th, which only set up the finish: Jon Baker's par at the 18th, one of the hardest holes here, worth four points, and the lead was his for good. One clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, with a gross 90 for second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria lurched about in both directions, and rarely gently. The first damage came in three holes from the 5th: a triple bogey at the short hole, a double bogey at the 6th, and then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone, and still a long way to go.

Then he turned the whole thing round. From the 8th to the 11th he produced a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, the bogey at the 10th bringing three of them on the hardest hole on the course. For four holes he was the best player on the property.

The second collapse was worse than the first. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake, then a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes and one point salvaged from the lot. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th explains most of what you need to know about the place: 422 metres, stroke index 1, out of bounds the whole way down the left and a lake sitting in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there is worth telling your family about. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over, no Stableford points whatsoever. David Mullin took eight, equally fruitless, and it shifted him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker got away with seven, though that was merely the middle of a wider slide, with doubles at the 8th and the 9th on either side of it and seven shots dropped across the three.

Between them the three of them gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. Nobody who has played this course was especially surprised.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Saturday round at Quinta da Ria, which parts with strokes reluctantly, saw Alex Baker take the 2026 TEG Trophy lead and then lose it inside three holes. He went outright in front with a par at the 5th. Then came a thoroughly miserable spell on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th and a triple at the 12th. Ten shots gone in three holes, and the lead gone with them to Jon Baker. Credit where it is due, he kept coming back, drawing level with a bogey at the 14th and going ahead again with a par at the 17th, before losing it for the last time at the 18th. Jon Baker finished on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and never gave anyone the faintest encouragement, finishing on 87. At the other end, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31, the two of them sharing the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
