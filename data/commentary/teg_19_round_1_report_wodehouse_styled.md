## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best round anyone in the field of five managed, and it came in two halves that barely look like the work of the same man. He had already held the 2026 TEG Trophy lead once, taking it outright with a par at the 4th, only for Alex Baker to take it straight back a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in on him. Double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th, a triple at the 10th, seven shots gone in three holes and not a net par among them. Genuinely poor golf, delivered at the worst possible moment.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he simply stopped losing shots. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th, nine points in three holes, and the 12th put him back in the outright lead. Three pars on the bounce, taken straight off the back of the worst stretch of his round, is the sort of recovery most players only manage in the retelling.

Alex Baker nosed in front again at the 17th. Jon Baker answered with a par at the 18th, four points on one of the hardest holes out there, and that was the lead back for good. He finished one point clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams, with a gross 90 for second place in the Green Jacket behind Williams.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round swung hard in both directions, and rarely halfway. The first wreck began at the 5th: a triple bogey at the short hole, a double at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots in three holes. There is no kind way to describe that.

Then he turned the whole thing round. From the 8th to the 11th he put together a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, the bogey at the 10th worth three of them on its own. Four holes of calm, competent golf from a man who had just handed back ten shots, and for a while he looked unstoppable.

The second collapse was worse. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 that doglegs round a lake, followed by a triple at the 15th and another at the 16th, the par 5 threading between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, with a single point salvaged from the wreckage. The 14th also carried him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He signed for 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

The 10th explains most of what went wrong on this course. It runs 422 metres, it is stroke index 1, there is out of bounds down the whole left side and a lake sitting in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there should be announced to the room. In one pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over and no Stableford points at all. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and it shifted him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker got away with seven, which only counts as getting away with anything next to those two. Twelve shots given back to par by three players on a single hole, and not one of them looked remotely surprised.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker walked to the 18th tee with the 2026 TEG Trophy lead and walked off without it. He had taken that lead outright with a par at the 5th, then lost it on the back nine in a stretch that does not bear close inspection: the nine at the 10th, a double bogey at the 11th, a triple at the 12th. Ten shots in three holes, and the lead passed to Jon Baker. He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th, edged back in front with a par at the 17th, and lost it for the last time at the last. Jon Baker closed on 39 to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams on the same 38.

Williams, meanwhile, had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87. Sixteen holes in front of a field that never got near him, which is as serene a day's work as this course allows. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31, the two of them sharing the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
