# Sixteen Clear and Two to Spare

## Alex Baker takes the TEG 19 Trophy by 16, Jon Baker holds the TEG 19 Green Jacket by two, and Gregg Williams collects the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker finished the job at Monte Rei with 52 points, the third-best round in TEG history to date, and the TEG 19 Trophy by 16 — 213 to John Patterson's 197. He had it effectively settled by the fourth green: 15 points from the first four holes, a birdie at the 1st, a par at the 2nd, then three points even after a bogey at the 3rd, one of the hardest holes on the course. He closed with a par and a birdie at the last for an 87, two better than his previous best at Monte Rei.

His gross figures went the other way. Alex Baker's +15 was the worst of the five on the day, and he slid from second after Round 1 to fifth in the TEG 19 Green Jacket at +55. That left Jon Baker holding it at +43, two clear of David Mullin and Gregg Williams on +45 — Mullin having trailed by 11 at one stage, Williams by eight. Williams also inherited the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon from Mullin, ending 168 and seven adrift.

## The Wooden Spoon changed hands three times in the first three holes at Monte Rei and then never moved again
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams and David Mullin arrived at Monte Rei on the Tuesday level at the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon, and the first three holes of the final round decided which of them stayed there. Williams bogeyed the first to sit alone at the bottom. Mullin parred the par-5 second to draw back level — a tie, not a change of occupancy. Then Williams took a double bogey at the third, one of the hardest holes on the course, and was on his own again.

That was the end of the movement. Neither man troubled the other over the remaining fifteen holes, and Williams finished the tournament on 168 Stableford points to Mullin's 175, a gap of seven where there had been none at the start of the day. The margin is the more awkward for the fact that Mullin's closing 78 was his best gross round at Monte Rei in three visits, beating his previous best there by seven strokes.

## Four triple bogeys around Monte Rei, two of them on the 9th, the shortest hole and the easiest on the card
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Monte Rei's final round produced four triple bogeys, and two of them arrived at the same hole. The 9th is a par 3 carrying stroke index 18 — the easiest on the card — and it still took six from both David Mullin and John Patterson, for no points either time.

Mullin's was the more painful interruption. He had played the first eight holes in two under, six pars and birdies at the 6th and 8th, before the 6 at the 9th wiped out the outward nine's momentum. John Patterson reached the same hole off a par, two birdies and another par, and walked off it the same way.

Alex Baker's pair came back to back and earlier: a 6 at the short 5th and an 8 at the 6th, one point between the two holes, before he steadied himself immediately with a birdie 2 at the 7th for five points. Jon Baker waited longer — four straight pars from the 9th, then a 7 at the 13th.

## David Mullin's career-best 49 points and 78 gross at Monte Rei, the best round of the day on both counts
<!-- storyline: round -->

**Mullin saves his best for last**

The Tuesday round at Monte Rei gave David Mullin the best golf of his four days: 49 Stableford points and a gross 78, six over par. The points figure is a personal best, and the 78 is his best score at Monte Rei on three visits, seven strokes better than the last time he played it and well inside his previous course-best 85. It did not win him the day — 52 points was the best Stableford return in the field — but nobody bettered his six over on gross.

The round was built on the front nine. Mullin played the first eight holes without dropping a gross shot, five pars, birdies at the 6th and 8th, and 26 points banked. He added nine more in pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th, and closed with three straight pars for another nine.

It left him tied second in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket on 45 over, two behind Jon Baker, and lifted him clear of the Wooden Spoon he had held going into the round.
