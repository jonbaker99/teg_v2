# A Record 83 at Monte Rei, and the Trophy by Four

## Jon Baker's final round at Monte Rei, where a ten-point Trophy lead shrank to four and still held, with the Jacket and Spoon settled behind him
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Tuesday round at Monte Rei, the third-hardest course these players have rated, asked Jon Baker to defend a ten-point cushion in the 2026 TEG Trophy, and he spent most of the day giving it away. Three double bogeys in a row at the 7th, 8th and 9th cost him a net par on each. Worse came at the 17th, one of the hardest holes on the course, where a quadruple bogey 8 returned nothing at all. Gregg Williams, who had trailed by as many as ten, closed to within four — but no further. Baker finished on 159 Stableford points to Williams's 155.

Williams took the 2026 Green Jacket in the other direction, his 83 at Monte Rei a course best and his winning gross margin stretching from two to twelve. The Wooden Spoon was settled by John Patterson's back nine: a 9 at the 16th inside a stretch of three holes that leaked nine shots, leaving him seven adrift of the player above.

## Monte Rei's 7th, the long par three that took a ten off John Patterson and sixes off two others in one afternoon
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Monte Rei's 7th is the hardest of the course's par threes: better than 230 yards from the back tee, with only a narrow channel onto the putting surface and deep hollows waiting either side. On Tuesday afternoon it collected scores from four of the five players in the field.

John Patterson took the worst of it, walking off with a 10 — seven over on a single par three, and the worst hole relative to par he has recorded. Alex Baker and David Mullin each made six there. For Alex Baker it came in the middle of a stretch from the 4th to the 8th that cost 13 shots; for Mullin it was the central hole of a three-hole run from the 6th that cost eight. Jon Baker escaped comparatively lightly with a five, though that began his own sequence of three straight double bogeys through the 9th.

On a course that already ranks among the three hardest the group plays, the 7th did most of the damage in one go.

## Alex Baker's final round of 33 points at Monte Rei, ten fewer than his 43 on Monday, and a 106 gross
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Alex Baker's Monday 43 at Monte Rei had been the best golf of his trip; the Tuesday round that followed was ten points worse, a 33 that left him with a gross 106 on a course that ranks among the three hardest the group has rated.

The damage came in blocks. He went four holes without a net par from the 4th — a triple-bogey 7, a double at the short 5th, an 8 at the 6th and a triple at the 7th — and then ran up a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 12th, the horseshoe par 4 bending around water where anyone not driving the green has to lay up and wedge in. Back-to-back pars at the 13th and 14th were the only holes all day where he gave nothing away, and three straight doubles from the 15th, including the hardest hole on the course, cost six shots to the close.

He finished the TEG 2026 Trophy third on 143 points.

## Gregg Williams's 83 gross at Monte Rei, a new course record and 42 Stableford points on the final day
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Tuesday round at Monte Rei gave Gregg Williams the best score anyone has posted there. His 83 — eleven over on a course that ranks among the three hardest the group has played, and where no one had previously broken 87 in ten visits — is a new course record, and six shots better than his own effort on his last trip round it.

The scorecard was built on patience rather than fireworks. Pars at the 4th, 5th and 6th brought nine Stableford points in three holes without a gross shot dropped, and he closed with a par at the 17th, the third hardest on the card, and a birdie at the lake-bending par-5 18th. The 42 points were the best in the field, eleven clear of the worst.

It stretched his lead in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket from two shots to twelve, and cut Jon Baker's advantage in the TEG Trophy from ten points to four.
