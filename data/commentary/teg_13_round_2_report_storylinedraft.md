# Six Becomes One at Prince's

## Gregg Williams's 41-point, 86-gross round at Prince's, the best of the day by four points and the second-lowest gross round of his 45 TEG rounds
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams produced the round of the day at Prince's Golf Club on the Friday of TEG 2020, scoring 41 points off a gross 86 to finish four clear of the rest of the field, whose best was 37. It stands as the second-lowest gross round of his 45 TEG rounds.

The round turned on a stretch through the Dunes and Himalayas holes 11 to 13, where three straight pars, including one on the par-4 13th, the third-hardest hole on the course, brought nine points in a row. Williams kept the momentum through 15 and 16, adding two more clean holes without dropping a gross shot, a par on the par-5 15th, the hardest hole at Prince's, followed by another on the par-3 16th.

The round did little to disturb the wider picture. Jon Baker remained the leader of both the 2020 TEG Trophy and the TEG Green Jacket, though Williams's score cut the Trophy gap to the top from six shots to just one.

## John Patterson's 28 points and 105 gross at Prince's, bookended by quadruple-bogey 8s and interrupted by three of the best holes of his week
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's second round at Prince's was a study in extremes, opening and closing with quadruple-bogey 8s while still finding enough in between to card 28 points and a gross 105.

The trouble started immediately. A quadruple bogey at the 1st set the tone for a wretched start, and by the time he reached the 4th he had bled 11 shots in four holes without managing a single net par across the first three. The 5th and 6th steadied him, and holes 7 and 8 brought his best spell of the round, two pars in a row without dropping a shot. A three-hole run from the 10th added 9 points, built on pars at 10 and 12 either side of a bogey at the par-5 11th.

But the collapse returned in the closing stretch. Holes 13 to 15 cost him eight shots, including a triple bogey at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course. Then, at the 17th, a second quadruple-bogey 8 of the day arrived to bookend the round. He remains anchored at the foot of the 2020 Wooden Spoon table.

## Alex Baker's finish at Prince's: five consecutive double bogeys from the 14th to the 18th, ten gross shots dropped
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

At Prince's Golf Club's Dunes/Himalayas course on Friday, Alex Baker's second round swung wildly before falling apart at the finish. After parring the 3rd, he bogeyed the 4th and 5th to pick up 9 stableford points across that stretch, then bled 7 shots over the 10th to 12th with a triple bogey and two doubles. The real damage came at the end: five straight double bogeys from the 14th through the 18th, dropping ten gross shots in as many holes. The stretch began with a double on the par-3 14th, one of the easier holes on the course, and included another double on the par-5 15th, Prince's hardest hole by stroke index. Two more doubles at the 17th and 18th closed out the round. It left Alex Baker's gross score for the day at 99, the only figure recorded for him at the course so far this TEG.

## Jon Baker's afternoon at the top of the TEG 13 Trophy: 32 holes in front, dispossessed at the 16th, restored by a birdie at the 18th with a one-point lead
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker had held the lead in the 2020 TEG 13 Trophy since round one, hole two, a run stretching 32 holes across two rounds at Prince's - Dunes / Himalayas. It ended on the 16th of round two, a par at the hardest hole on the course, when Gregg Williams drew level and then moved in front. Williams had already squared the race at the 15th before nudging ahead.

Baker did not stay behind for long. He closed with two holes without dropping a gross shot, capped by a birdie at the 18th, one of the easier finishing holes, to retake the lead outright. It left him a single point clear heading into the rest of the week, having earlier steadied himself with a birdie at the 6th to halt a bogey-double bogey slide.

Elsewhere on the course, David Mullin ran up a quadruple bogey at the 9th. But it was Baker's day: after round two he led both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, his overnight cushion in the latter having grown to seven.
