## Jon Baker's completion of the TEG 13 Trophy and TEG 13 Green Jacket double at Prince's, and Gregg Williams's four-day failure to lay a glove on him
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker won at Prince's, and in doing so completed the TEG 13 double. The Green Jacket joined a Trophy already secured. It is the fourth such double in TEG history, after those of TEGs 2, 4 and 11. He began the final round leading both races. He ended it leading both races. In between, a certain amount of golf occurred.

The decisive stretch was holes 12 to 14. Three pars, nine Stableford points, three holes without a gross shot dropped. The par at the 13th, the hardest hole on the course, was the one that mattered. Then the 15th and 16th, where he posted a bogey and a double bogey and left the door pointedly ajar. Nobody walked through it. He shut it himself with a birdie at the 17th, which is roughly how winning looks round here.

Gregg Williams matched him hole for hole through the same stretches. He parred 12 and 13, then 15 and 16, without dropping a shot anywhere. It was immaculate. It was also, in every measurable sense, futile.

## David Mullin taking the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon after a nine at the 10th on the final afternoon
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

On the final afternoon at Prince's Shore/Dunes, David Mullin was playing with genuine discipline. Bogey-free through the 1st and 2nd. Bogey-free again through the 4th, 5th and 6th. A man, you would have said, in full command of himself.

Then came the 10th, stroke index 3, and a nine. A quintuple bogey, his worst hole of the round, and the point at which the composure of the previous nine holes was revealed as a loan rather than a gift.

It settled the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon. Before the 10th, Alex Baker held the bottom spot with a three-shot gap to the man above him. Afterwards, Mullin held it, with six shots of clear air behind him. At the top, the Trophy and Green Jacket races went on with Jon Baker in front, entirely undisturbed by any of this. Down at the bottom, the arithmetic was final. Mullin had the Spoon.

## John Patterson's final round of extremes — an eagle three at the par-5 2nd and a nine at the 15th — following his 49-point Saturday
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's final round at Prince's Shore/Dunes, the course's first visit of this TEG, contained both halves of the game and very little in between. At the par-5 2nd, stroke index 12, he holed out for an eagle three. It was one of the highlights of the day and he had every right to feel that something had changed.

Nothing had changed. Thirteen holes later, at the par-5 15th, he made a quadruple bogey nine and four Stableford points vanished at a stroke.

The broader picture is a descent. Patterson had posted 49 points in the third round on Saturday, among his best returns of the four days. He followed it with 36. A swing of 13 points, and evidence that a links course keeps its own ledger. His 97 gross stands as his best score on the course. He has only played it once.

## Alex Baker's 44-point Sunday at Prince's, a personal-best gross round of 89 (+17) that dragged him off the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race at the last opportunity
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker closed at Prince's Shore/Dunes with the round of his life. Forty-four points, and a personal-best gross of 89, seventeen over. It was the best Stableford total in the field on the final Sunday. It came directly off a 32-point third round, a swing of 12 that arrived with the timing of a man hearing the fire alarm and finally locating the door. He had started the day on the bottom of the 2020 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. He finished it six points clear of David Mullin, who had taken his place.

The round was built on control, not fireworks. Two holes to open without a dropped shot, pars at the 1st and 2nd. The same again at the 9th and 10th, the latter one of the hardest holes on the course. It was his first visit to this routing at Prince's, so the 89 is a personal best by the narrowest of definitions. It was also, on the day, exactly enough.
