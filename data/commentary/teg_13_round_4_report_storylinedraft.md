# A Double at Prince's

## Jon Baker's completion of the TEG 13 Trophy and TEG 13 Green Jacket double at Prince's, and Gregg Williams's four-day failure to lay a glove on him
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker's victory at Prince's completed the TEG 13 double, adding the Green Jacket to a Trophy already won — the fourth such double in TEG history, following those achieved in TEGs 2, 4 and 11. He came into the final round leading both races and left it exactly where he started, closing out the week with Jon Baker still out front of the Trophy and the Jacket after the last round at Prince's Shore/Dunes.

The decisive stretch came at holes 12 to 14, where Baker reeled off three pars, gaining nine Stableford points and going three holes without dropping a gross shot — including a par at the 13th, the hardest hole on the course. A bogey and a double bogey at 15 and 16 threatened to open the door, but he steadied himself with a birdie at the 17th.

Gregg Williams matched him hole for hole through the same stretches, parring 12-13 and 15-16 without ever dropping a shot, but it was never enough to close the gap.

## David Mullin taking the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon after a nine at the 10th on the final afternoon
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

On the final afternoon at Prince's Shore/Dunes, David Mullin had been playing steadily, going bogey-free through the 1st and 2nd and again through the 4th, 5th and 6th. That composure vanished at the 10th, one of the harder holes on the course at stroke index 3, where he ran up a nine, a quintuple bogey and his worst hole of the round.

The blow-up proved decisive for the TEG 13 Wooden Spoon. Before it, Alex Baker held the unwanted bottom spot with a three-shot gap to the next player up. The nine at the 10th flipped that: Mullin dropped to last place himself, and the gap behind him stretched to six shots. Elsewhere the Trophy and Green Jacket races stayed with Jon Baker, unaffected by events at the bottom of the field, but for Mullin the damage was done, and it was enough to hand him the Spoon.

## John Patterson's final round of extremes — an eagle three at the par-5 2nd and a nine at the 15th — following his 49-point Saturday
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's final round at Prince's Shore/Dunes, on the course's first visit for this TEG, swung between the two extremes of the game. At the par-5 2nd, a stroke index 12 hole, he holed out for an eagle three, one of the highlights of the day. Thirteen holes later, at the par-5 15th, the round turned the other way: a quadruple bogey nine wiped out four stableford points in one blow.

The round as a whole marked a heavy comeback down to earth. Patterson had posted 49 points in Saturday's third round, one of his best returns of the week, but followed it with just 36 on Sunday — a swing of 13 points, and a reminder of how sharply form can turn on a links course from one day to the next. The 97 gross he carded stood as his best score on this course simply because it was his only one.

## Alex Baker's 44-point Sunday at Prince's, a personal-best gross round of 89 (+17) that dragged him off the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race at the last opportunity
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker closed out the week at Prince's Shore/Dunes with the round of his life, carding 44 points for a personal-best gross score of 89 (+17). That stableford total was the best in the field on the final Sunday, and it came off the back of a poor 32-point Round 3 — a 12-shot swing that arrived at exactly the right moment. Alex Baker had gone into the round on the bottom of the 2020 TEG Wooden Spoon standings; by the close it was David Mullin who had slipped into last place, with the gap back out to six points.

The round was built on control rather than fireworks. Alex Baker opened with two holes without dropping a shot, pars at the 1st and 2nd, and found the same form again through the 9th and 10th, the latter one of the hardest holes on the course. It was his first visit to this routing at Prince's, and the 89 stood as a new personal best there — arriving just in time to save his Spoon fight.
