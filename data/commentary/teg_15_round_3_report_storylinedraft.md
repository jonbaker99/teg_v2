# The Mountain Finally Yields

## Gregg Williams sets a new Penha Longa course record with a gross 82
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams shot a gross 82 in the third round at Penha Longa, breaking the course record of 86 that had stood across six previous visits to the venue. It also stands as Williams's own personal-best gross round in the TEG, +10 to par. His previous best at Penha Longa, a 96 set at TEG 8, was his only other visit here — meaning he cut fourteen strokes from his own course mark on the second attempt.

The round was built on consistency rather than a single purple patch: two separate two-hole stretches, 7-8 and 10-11, each yielded a birdie and a par without a dropped shot, including a birdie at the par-5 8th.

Williams already led both the TEG 16 Trophy and the TEG 16 Green Jacket heading into the round, and stayed there afterwards, with his Trophy lead growing from 9 to 18 and his Jacket lead from 14 to 17.

## John Patterson's wild first look at Penha Longa
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's third round at Penha Longa was a study in extremes, played out on his first-ever look at the Atlantic Course. He opened with a stretch of genuine control, running holes 3 to 7 without dropping a gross shot and turning that patience into 19 Stableford points across holes 3-8.

Then the round turned. A double bogey at the 9th was followed by a quadruple bogey 8 at the 10th, one of the harder holes on the course, and another double bogey at the 11th — eight shots bled across three holes. The damage continued late, with a quintuple bogey 9 at the 16th, Penha Longa's stroke index 1, wiping out any points on offer there.

The round finished with a gross 99, the best he would manage at the course this TEG given it was his only visit. Elsewhere, Gregg Williams retained his lead in both the TEG 3 Trophy and Green Jacket races, while the Wooden Spoon picture shifted, with Alex Baker moving into last place.

## The Wooden Spoon changes hands as Alex Baker collapses and Stuart Neumann recovers
<!-- storyline: race -->

Round three at Penha Longa saw the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon change hands, as Alex Baker collapsed while Stuart Neumann recovered.

Baker started badly, dropping to double bogey at the 1st before running through a stretch of four holes without better than a bogey. A birdie at the par-3 5th finally halted the run, but the reprieve was temporary. Baker's round unravelled again from the 12th, bleeding 12 shots over three holes: a quadruple bogey at the 12th, a double bogey at the 13th, and a 10 at the par-4 14th, one of the harder holes on the course. Three holes without a single point to show for it dropped Baker to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings.

Neumann's round moved the opposite way. Having also dropped shots around the turn, going seven over across holes 11 to 13, he answered immediately with 9 points from three holes to close, including a birdie-free but efficient bogey-par-bogey stretch. It capped a big swing after a poor second round, and left Neumann's Wooden Spoon lead over Baker at 14 shots.
