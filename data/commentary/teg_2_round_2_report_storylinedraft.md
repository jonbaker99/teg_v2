# Baker Takes the Lead as Boavista Draws Blood

## Jon Baker's near-record round undone by a ragged finish
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's second round at Boavista produced the best net score TEG had ever seen at the halfway point of his own effort, finishing +1 for the round — the second-best round in TEG history. It was also a course personal best for him, a gross 100 improving three shots on his prior visit here.

Baker built the round from the front, gaining four shots to par across holes 6 to 8, and took the outright lead in the TEG 2 Trophy at the 4th before a bogey on the 6th let David Mullin draw level again. He surged back ahead, and the round finished with Baker taking over the Trophy race lead from Mullin, now three shots clear.

But the closing holes turned ragged: a triple bogey on the brutal par-4 15th, a double at the 16th, then back-to-back quadruple bogeys at the 17th and 18th, bleeding 13 shots over the closing stretch.

Stuart Neumann's own round of gross 27 over par, also a course best for him, ranks as the second-best gross round in TEG history.

## Boavista's 18th hole punishes three cards in one afternoon
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The 18th at Boavista, a par 4 and one of the harder holes on the course at stroke index 5, took its toll on three cards during round 2 of TEG 2. Jon Baker, Gregg Williams and David Mullin all closed with quadruple-bogey 8s.

For Baker it was the more costly of the three: it came on a day that still saw him post a course personal best and take over the lead in the 2009 TEG Trophy race, moving three points clear of Mullin, who had led before the round. Williams also set a course best for himself despite the finishing blow. Mullin's 8 arrived without any such compensation, and he slipped from the Trophy lead he had held overnight, though he remained on top of the Green Jacket race, his advantage narrowing slightly to eight strokes.

Three quadruple bogeys on the same hole in one afternoon made the 18th the day's clear villain, undoing good work from two of the men it struck.

## Henry Meller's quiet transformation at Boavista
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Henry Meller's second round at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort, in TEG 2, told two very different stories in one. Having finished Round 1 there at 24 over net par, he came back 11 strokes better in Round 2, at 13 over — a swing sharp enough to count as a genuine turnaround in form. It also delivered a course personal best: his gross 102 beat his previous Boavista best by 11 strokes, on only his second visit to the venue.

The round was not smooth throughout. Across holes 13 to 16 Meller went four straight without a net par, shipping 11 gross shots. A double bogey on the 13th was followed by triple bogeys on the 14th, the 16th, and the par-4 15th — the hardest hole on the course — for a net shortfall of seven strokes in the stretch.

Even with the improved round, Meller stayed anchored to the bottom of the TEG 2 Wooden Spoon table, now 16 shots adrift of the next player up, having trailed by 12 before the round began.

## David Mullin's Trophy lead slips away despite a career round
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin produced one of the finest rounds in TEG history at Boavista, a gross +28 that ranks as the third-best gross round the event has ever seen. For much of the day it looked set to extend his Trophy lead as well as his Green Jacket advantage. On the 5th, a bogey was enough to put him back in outright control of the Trophy on net scoring, and it seemed the round would be remembered simply as dominant.

Instead, the finish undid it. From the 16th through the 18th, Mullin went three straight holes without a net par, bleeding 10 gross shots over that stretch, including a triple bogey on the 17th and a quadruple bogey 8 at the last. That collapse handed the initiative to Jon Baker, who had already traded the net lead with Mullin twice earlier in the round, on the 4th and 6th. Baker's own finish held firm, and by the close he had taken the Trophy lead outright, three clear on net scoring. Mullin still leads the Green Jacket, but the Trophy has slipped from his grasp despite the round of his life.
