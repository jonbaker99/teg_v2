# Williams Sets the Bar in Catalonia

## Gregg Williams's -12 becomes the best round recorded in TEG history so far
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams's first round at PGA Catalunya - Tour produced a net score of -12, the best round recorded in TEG history so far — a mark set on the very first weekend of competition, but a record nonetheless. His gross tally of +20 stood as a personal best in its own right.

The round was no procession. Williams shed seven shots over holes 3 to 5, running up a triple bogey at the 3rd, a double at the 4th, and another double at the par-5 5th. The bleeding continued into the turn before a birdie at the 11th arrested it, part of a stretch across holes 9-11 in which he gained five shots to net par. A second birdie, at the 14th, sparked an even better run: seven shots gained over holes 13-16, including back-to-back pars either side of the birdie. That purple patch ended abruptly with a double bogey at the 17th, but the damage to his card was already done — in the best possible sense.

## Henry Meller's two quadruple-bogey 9s bury him at the foot of the Wooden Spoon race, with Stuart Neumann's own meltdown a reminder the course spared almost nobody
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Round one at PGA Catalunya's Tour Course found the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race a crowded place. Henry Meller was the worst affected, running up quadruple-bogey 9s twice on the same day — first at the par-5 7th, then again at the par-5 15th. Either blow-up would sting on its own; taking both in one round left him rooted at the foot of the Spoon table, with a double bogey at the 11th offering no relief.

He was not alone. Jon Baker slipped to the bottom of the same race three times over the round, undone by a triple bogey at the 4th and two further double bogeys, while Stuart Neumann's day featured a triple at the 3rd and then a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 9th — a hole he managed to butcher despite it ranking only mid-difficulty on the card. Between them, the trio's misadventures painted a round where the course punished nearly everyone chasing the wrong end of the leaderboard, not just one unlucky victim.

## David Mullin's tidy round keeps the Green Jacket race tight even as the Trophy race runs away
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin put together the steadiest round of the day at PGA Catalunya's Tour course. He drew level for the Trophy (Net VP) lead at the 3rd, slipped back to level again with a bogey at the par-5 5th, and by the same hole had also taken outright control of the Green Jacket (Gross) lead. Two separate two-hole stretches without a dropped gross shot, at the 6th-7th and again at the 10th-11th, underlined the consistency behind the numbers.

By the close, Mullin's tidiness had him leading the Jacket outright, while Gregg Williams headed the Trophy with no gap at all on net scoring, keeping that race level after round one. The bigger story of the day belonged elsewhere, with Jon Baker carding a personal-best gross round of +18, but Mullin's round was the one that mattered most to the shape of the week ahead, setting up a Jacket race as tight as the Trophy race had suddenly become loose.
