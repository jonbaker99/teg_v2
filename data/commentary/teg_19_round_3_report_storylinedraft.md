# Records and Ruin at Monte Rei

## Alex Baker's 43 points, the best score of the day at Monte Rei, assembled around a ten at the 2nd and a nine at the 12th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker's 43 points was the best score of the round at Monte Rei, three clear of anyone else in the five-man field and 13 better than the day's worst return — and he built it around two holes that should have wrecked it. A quintuple-bogey 10 at the 2nd produced nothing; a 9 at the 12th, the horseshoe par 4 bending around water, produced nothing either. Two holes, no points, on a course where water is in play on eleven of the eighteen and the scoring is as unforgiving as anywhere the group plays.

Everything in between paid. He took 13 points from holes 6 to 9 alone, with pars at the 6th and 9th and bogeys either side. The 43 was 14 points better than the 29 he managed in Round 2, and his gross 98 beat the 114 he signed for on his only previous visit here — a course best by 16 strokes. He sits third in the 2026 TEG Trophy on 110.

## Jon Baker's 87 at Monte Rei, equalling the course record and built on five consecutive pars from the 11th to the 15th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Monday round at Monte Rei produced the best gross score the course has given up in TEG: Jon Baker's 87 equalled the record of 87 set across the five previous rounds played there, and bettered his own 88 from his first visit by a shot. On a Nicklaus parkland that ranks as the third-hardest course in the TEG books, that is a serious number.

It did not look like one early. Jon Baker went triple bogey, double, double from the 2nd to the 4th, seven shots gone in three holes, with the 6 at the 3rd coming on one of the hardest holes on the card.

The round turned on a stretch of five straight pars from the 11th to the 15th, worth 16 Stableford points — including a par at the 13th, where even a short approach rarely yields one, a safe tee shot over the lake corner at the 14th, and four points at the 15th, the toughest hole out there. A double bogey 7 at the 16th ended it.

His lead in the 2026 TEG Trophy grew from five points to ten.

## The TEG 19 Wooden Spoon tightens as John Patterson's 36 points meets David Mullin's 30, the worst score of the day
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

The Monday round at Monte Rei narrowed the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon considerably. John Patterson, who has held last place since the ninth hole of Round 2, posted 36 points — eleven better than the 25 he managed in Round 2 — while David Mullin returned 30, the lowest score in the field of five on the day.

Patterson's turnaround came after a wobble. A triple bogey at the 6th, where vast bunkers guard an offset green, began a run of four holes dropped to the card before he birdied the 10th. Mullin's damage came later and cost more: triple bogey at the 13th, then bogey, bogey, double and another triple through the 17th, ten shots over par across five holes, with a birdie at the last for consolation.

Both men signed for 97 on a course that ranks among the three hardest the group has played. For Patterson that was fourteen strokes better than his only previous visit. He remains bottom, but the gap to fourth has shrunk from nine points to three.

## The TEG 19 Green Jacket cut from four shots to two by a birdie at the last, while the Trophy lead doubled to ten
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei, third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated, did its work on Gregg Williams in the middle of the Monday round. He dropped nine shots in four holes from the 9th — a triple bogey at the short 9th, the one par 3 on the course that is supposed to offer respite, then doubles at the 10th, 11th and 12th. That let Jon Baker into the TEG 19 Green Jacket for the first time: a par at the 15th, stroke index 1, drew him level. Williams edged back in front with a bogey at the 16th, Jon Baker levelled again with a bogey at the 17th, and then Williams birdied the par-5 18th, over the water, to finish the round two clear rather than four.

The TEG 19 Trophy moved the other way. Jon Baker took the outright lead with a par at the 8th and never gave it back, stretching five points to ten. His 87 was his best round at Monte Rei.
