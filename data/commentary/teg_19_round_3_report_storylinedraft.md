# Forty-Three Points and a Ten at Monte Rei

## Alex Baker's 43 points, the best score of the day, assembled either side of a ten at the 2nd and a nine at the 12th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker's 43 points was the best score of the day in a field of five, and it was assembled around two craters. At the 2nd he ran up a 10 for no points. At the 12th — the horseshoe par 4 that bends around water, one of eleven holes here where water is in play — he took nine, and again walked off with nothing.

Everything in between covered for them. Holes 6 to 9 brought 13 points: a par at the 6th worth four, then bogeys at the 7th and 8th and a par at the 9th worth three apiece. On the third-hardest of the 28 courses the group has rated, that is a serious stretch of scoring.

The 43 was 14 points better than the 29 he posted in Round 2, and his gross 98 bettered his previous Monte Rei best by 16 strokes — a personal best at the course. It still left him third in the 2026 TEG Trophy, with Jon Baker's lead out from five points to ten.

## John Patterson's 36 points and 97 gross, a fourteen-shot course best that cuts the Wooden Spoon gap from nine to three
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's Monday round at Monte Rei began the way most of his tournament had: doubles at the 2nd, the 3rd and the 4th, six shots gone in three holes, with the damage on the 3rd coming at one of the hardest holes on the course. A triple bogey at the 6th followed, then bogeys at the 7th — the long par 3 with its narrow channel and deep hollows — the 8th, and the easiest hole on the card, the 9th. The bleeding stopped at the 10th, where he made birdie for five points.

From there he held on for 36 points and a gross 97. On a Nicklaus parkland that punishes this field harder than almost anywhere TEG visits, that was 14 strokes better than his only previous round here, a course best. It also moved the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon within reach of escape: his deficit to David Mullin, nine after two rounds, is now three.

## Jon Baker's record-equalling 87 stretches the Trophy lead to ten while Gregg Williams loses nine shots in four holes and keeps the Jacket by two
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Monday round at Monte Rei, a course that ranks among the three hardest the group plays, produced Jon Baker's best golf of the trip: an 87 that equalled the course record and beat his own previous best there by a shot. The spine of it was five straight pars from the 11th, worth 16 Stableford points, the last of them on the 15th — the hardest hole on the card — and that par drew him level with Gregg Williams at the top of the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. The run ended immediately with a double bogey 7 at the 16th.

Williams had his own trouble, losing nine shots in four holes from the 9th: a triple bogey on the short 9th, the easiest hole out there, then three consecutive doubles. He answered on the 18th, where a birdie took the Jacket lead back outright, and he holds it by two. In the 2026 TEG Trophy, Jon Baker's advantage grew from five points to ten.
