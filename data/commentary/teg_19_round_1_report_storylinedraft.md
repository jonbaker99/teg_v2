# Records Fall at Quinta da Ria

## Alex Baker's 62 points at Quinta da Ria, the highest Stableford round in TEG's 360 rounds, built on a fast start and a devastating run from the 10th to the 16th
<!-- storyline: round -->

The opening round at Quinta da Ria, on the Saturday, produced the best Stableford round in TEG history to date: 62 points from Alex Baker. It was quick out of the gate — 23 points across the first six holes, including a birdie at the par-5 1st and another at the 4th, worth five points, which lifted him past David Mullin into the outright lead in the 2026 TEG Trophy.

The damage came later. From the 10th to the 16th he took 29 points, starting with a par on the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, and peaking with an eagle two at the 14th for six. He gave up no gross shots at all from the 14th through the 16th before a double bogey at the 17th ended the par-or-better run.

His gross 77, five over, was the third-best gross round in TEG history. It led the 2026 TEG Green Jacket from the 5th until Jon Baker overhauled him at the last. The Trophy margin was nine points.

## Every player in the field made an eagle or better, five different holes, from David Mullin's two at the par-five 1st to John Patterson's two at the par-four 18th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Every one of the five players in the field made at least an eagle in the opening round at Quinta da Ria, and no two of them did it on the same hole. David Mullin went first and went furthest: an albatross two at the 1st, worth six Stableford points and enough to put him top of both the 2026 TEG Trophy and the 2026 TEG Green Jacket after a single hole — though the 1st is stroke index 16, one of the easiest on the course. Jon Baker's eagle at the par-5 11th and Gregg Williams's at the par-5 16th each returned five points. Alex Baker's two at the par-4 14th brought six, as did John Patterson's two at the last, which came on stroke index 3, one of the hardest holes at Quinta da Ria. Patterson's finish did not shift the standings at the top, however: the gross lead changed hands at the 18th regardless, Jon Baker moving ahead of Alex Baker to close the round on 76.

## John Patterson bled seven gross shots in three holes from the 3rd, dropped to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race at the 4th, and finished with an eagle two at the 18th for 47 points
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's Saturday at Quinta da Ria came apart early. A double bogey at the 3rd, a triple at the 4th and another double at the 5th cost him seven gross shots in three holes and returned just two Stableford points. The triple at the 4th also sent him to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings, having been third-worst in the field a hole earlier.

He answered immediately. Bogeys at the 6th and 7th were followed by pars at the 8th and 9th for 13 points across the four holes, enough to hand the Spoon lead back to Gregg Williams by the turn. The finish was stranger still: bogey, bogey, double bogey through the 17th, then an eagle two at the 18th, one of the hardest holes on the course, worth six points on its own.

It left him on 47 points, third in the 2026 TEG Trophy, but 86 gross — the highest score in the field, and last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket.

## Gregg Williams's 84 was the third-lowest gross round of his 68 TEG appearances and still left him bottom of the Wooden Spoon race by a single point
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

Gregg Williams's Saturday at Quinta da Ria was a round of two ledgers. A bogey at the 2nd — the easiest hole on the course — dropped him to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race as early as the second hole, and although John Patterson relieved him of it at the 4th, another bogey at the 9th put him back there for good. He stayed bottom through to the 18th.

In between there was real golf. Back-to-back pars at the 3rd and 4th cost him nothing in gross terms. Three straight bogeys from the 7th and a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, were answered by a birdie at the 11th. Then four more dropped shots across the 12th to the 15th, +5 in all, before an eagle at the 16th.

It added up to 84, twelve over, third of the five in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. His 41 Stableford points left him last in the 2026 TEG Trophy, one point behind David Mullin.

## Alex Baker led the gross count from the 4th hole to the 17th and lost the TEG 19 Green Jacket lead on the final green to Jon Baker, whose 76 is the lowest gross round in TEG history
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Saturday round at Quinta da Ria set the TEG 19 Green Jacket race off in a straight line and then bent it at the last moment. David Mullin held the gross lead after the opening hole, Alex Baker drew level with a birdie at the 4th, and by the 5th he was out in front on his own — a position he kept for the next twelve holes.

Jon Baker kept pulling alongside without ever getting past: an eagle at the par-5 11th drew him level, a par at the 13th did the same. The decisive stretch was 16 to 18, where he made a birdie at the par-5 16th and parred in, taking 11 Stableford points and dropping no gross shots. The par at the last put him ahead outright for the first time all day.

His 76, four over par, is the lowest gross round in TEG history; Alex Baker's 77 was a shot behind. Jon Baker's 53 points is the second-best round recorded, though Alex Baker's 62 leads the TEG 19 Trophy.
