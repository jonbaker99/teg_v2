# One Point in It at Quinta da Ria

## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best score of the opening day, salvaged from a three-hole wobble at the 8th, 9th and 10th
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points was the best score of the opening day at Quinta da Ria, three clear of the 31 that propped up the field, and it did not arrive tidily. He moved to the top of the 2026 TEG Trophy as early as the 4th, with nothing more exotic than a par, and was back behind Alex Baker a hole later.

Then came the damage. Doubles at the 8th and 9th were followed by a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course — seven shots gone in three holes and a single point from each, on a course that gives up little to golfers of this standard.

The repair was immediate. Pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th, where the layout opens out towards the sea, brought nine points and the lead back at the 12th. Alex Baker nudged ahead again at the 17th before Jon's par at the 18th, worth four points, settled it at 39 to 38. Gregg Williams leads the TEG Green Jacket on 87.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 462-yard par four with water and out of bounds, took a nine, an eight and a seven from the field
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th is the signature hole and, by stroke index, the hardest on a course that already asks more of this field than most: 462 yards of par four with out of bounds all down the left and a lake guarding the green from 110 yards out. A par there is worth celebrating. Nobody managed one.

Alex Baker took nine, a quintuple bogey worth no Stableford points at all. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and the damage moved him from second to last in the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race — bottom of the five. Jon Baker escaped with a seven, but only as the end of a slide that had begun two holes earlier: doubles at the 8th and 9th, then the triple at the 10th, seven shots gone in three holes.

Between them, the three handed back 12 shots to par on a single hole. The course picks up momentum after the turn, as advertised.

## John Patterson's 105, built from two nines and a ruinous finish, with the best four-hole scoring run of the day in the middle of it
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

John Patterson's round at Quinta da Ria added up to 105, 33 over par and the highest score in the field on a course that gives a mid-handicapper very little for free. The damage came in two bursts. From the 5th he went triple bogey, double bogey, quintuple bogey — the last of those a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes out there — and ten shots were gone in three holes.

Then came the best passage of his day: par, bogey, bogey, bogey from the 8th through the 11th, three Stableford points on each, 12 in four holes, one of them at the 10th, the hardest hole on the card.

The finish erased it. A 9 at the 14th, the par 4 that doglegs around a lake, a triple at the 15th and another at the par-5 16th between the big lakes: 11 shots in three holes. The 14th also dropped him alone to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He closed on 31 points, level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG Trophy, and last in the Green Jacket.

## Alex Baker led the Trophy standing on the 18th tee and finished a point short, while Gregg Williams's 87 opened a three-shot Green Jacket lead
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker's opening round at Quinta da Ria, a course that gives up little to golfers of this standard, turned on two short stretches. Three bogeys and a par across holes 3 to 6 brought him 13 Stableford points and the outright lead in the 2026 TEG Trophy at the 5th. Then came the turn: a quintuple bogey 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, followed by a double bogey and a triple at the 12th — ten gross shots gone in three holes, and the lead handed back to Jon Baker. Alex Baker drew level again at the 14th, went ahead outright at the 17th, and lost it on the last, finishing on 38 to Jon Baker's 39.

Gregg Williams, meanwhile, took the lead in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket with a par at the 3rd and never gave it up, holding firm through pars at the 12th and 13th. His 87 was three clear of Jon Baker's 90, and his 38 points tied Alex Baker for second in the Trophy.
