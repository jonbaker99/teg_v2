# Meller Holds On as Mullin Breaks the Course Record

## Henry Meller survives a late Trophy scare
<!-- storyline: race -->

Henry Meller closed out the 2014 TEG Trophy at Praia D'El Rey, leading after every round of the final day and eventually winning by 7 on net-versus-par, but the final round turned into a genuine scare. Meller's overnight cushion had been 15 shots, and by the finish it had been cut to 7.

The chief threat came from Gregg Williams, who trailed by as much as 20 during the round before posting a personal-best -12, also his best-ever gross round at +20, to close to within 7. Stuart Neumann matched that final gap after trailing by 17 at one stage, turning around a poor third round of +4 with a -12 of his own. David Mullin, separately confirmed as Green Jacket winner, also charged from 15 back to finish 8 off Meller's pace.

None of it was quite enough. Meller's lead never actually changed hands, but a day that began as a procession ended with three separate players closing hard on him at once.

## Jon Baker's back-to-back blow-ups at the 10th and 11th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Jon Baker's Sunday at Praia D'El Rey began quietly enough. After his opening round of the week had already put him second in the TEG 4 Trophy and first in the TEG 4 Green Jacket, he built through the front nine with four straight holes, 2 through 5, without dropping so much as a gross shot, gaining five strokes to par along the way.

Then came the 10th and 11th. A sextuple bogey 11 on the par-5 10th — his worst hole of the tournament — was followed immediately by a quadruple bogey 7 on the short 11th. Two holes, eleven shots over par between them, undid the round in a stretch.

The damage carried through to the overall standings: Jon Baker slipped from second to fifth in the Trophy race, and from first to fourth in the Green Jacket, a three-place fade in both after leading the way through round one. Round four at Praia D'El Rey turned from foundation to collapse in the space of two holes.

## Alex Baker's chaotic final round confirms the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Alex Baker's final round at Praia D'El Rey confirmed what had already been shaping up over the week: the TEG 7 Wooden Spoon was his. He had gone into the round eight shots adrift of safety and finished no closer, the gap holding at eight after a round that swung between disaster and brief recovery.

The trouble started early, with a quadruple bogey 7 on the short 3rd, and built through the back nine into a pair of quintuple bogey 9s — first at the 6th, then again at the 16th. In between there was a strange pocket of composure: a birdie at the 14th halted a run of bogeys and doubles, and for three holes from the 13th he actually gained ground, picking up five shots against par across that stretch. It wasn't enough to change the outcome. The round did produce one small consolation — his best score yet at the course this TEG — but the damage elsewhere was too great, and the Spoon was his for the taking.

## David Mullin's course-record 83 at Praia D'El Rey
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin shot 83 gross at Praia D'El Rey in round 4, a new course record, beating the previous best of 84 set across six prior visits. It was also the second-best gross round in TEG history to date, ten shots better than par.

The round was not without incident. Mullin bogeyed the 9th and then ran up a triple bogey at the par-5 10th before halting the damage with a birdie at the 11th. That birdie launched a run of four shots gained to par across holes 11-13, following an earlier stretch in which he had already gained five shots to par from the 4th to the 8th. The steady form did not last to the finish, though: a double bogey at the short 14th ended his run of pars.

The 83 improved Mullin's own prior best at the course, 89, by six shots, and stands as his best score there through this TEG. He remained the Green Jacket leader after the round, with his overall lead over the field narrowing slightly to 15.
