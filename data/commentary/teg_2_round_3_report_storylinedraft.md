# Mullin's Double At Boavista

## David Mullin completes the TEG 2 Trophy and Green Jacket double
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin completed the TEG 2 Trophy and Green Jacket double at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort, the first double in TEG history.

Mullin had already been sitting on the Jacket lead, and Round 3 settled the Trophy too. He took the outright lead on the third hole, a par-5 where a bogey was still enough to move him to the top of the Net VP standings, overturning Jon Baker's overnight advantage. By the end of the round Mullin led the Trophy by seven, having erased Baker's three-shot cushion, while his Jacket lead stretched from eight to eleven.

The round also marked a personal high point at the course: an 89, his best gross score there across five visits through this TEG, ten strokes better than his previous visit and a new course best for him this TEG. With both prizes now in his hands, Mullin's position looked close to unassailable heading into the closing rounds.

## Henry Meller's best round of the week arrives one round too late
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Henry Meller produced the round of his week at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort on Monday, but it came far too late to change his fortunes. His gross 20 was the second-best gross round recorded in TEG history to date, a stunning reversal after a miserable Round 2 that had left him near the bottom of the field — the swing from a 13 to a 2 stands as one of the sharpest turnarounds of the week. The turnaround included a two-hole stretch without dropping a shot, birdieing the par-4 5th before parring the 6th.

It was also a course personal best for Meller at Boavista, on his fifth visit to the venue, eleven strokes better than his previous visit there. Yet none of it moved him out of last place in the Wooden Spoon race — he remained the laggard both before and after the round, the gap to safety only narrowing from 16 to 12.

## Jon Baker's front nine implosion
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Jon Baker began round three of TEG 5 holding the Trophy lead by three shots, but a horror start at Boavista put the race out of reach. He opened with a double bogey on the 1st, followed by another double at the 2nd, before running up an 8 on the par-5 3rd. A triple bogey followed at the par-3 4th — one of the hardest holes on the course — and a fifth straight blemish, a double bogey at the par-4 5th, completed a stretch in which he went five holes without a net par and shipped 12 gross shots.

The damage did not end there. On the back nine Baker added a quadruple bogey 8 at the par-4 15th. Between the two collapses, his round unravelled enough to hand the initiative to David Mullin, who took over the Trophy lead and turned Baker's three-shot advantage before the round into a seven-shot deficit by the close of play.

## David Mullin's closing round at Boavista, the best recorded in TEG history to date
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin produced the best round recorded in TEG history on Boavista's third round, a net -2 that also stands as the best gross round ever posted, an 18-over-par 89. It was a new course record for Boavista, beating the previous mark of 91 set across ten prior visits, and it improved Mullin's own best on the course by two strokes.

The round transformed the leaderboard. Mullin arrived at Boavista in second place in the TEG Trophy, three behind Jon Baker, and left it seven clear at the top after the swing from worst to first among the field of five. He also held the Green Jacket lead throughout, extending his advantage there to eleven. Taken together, it is a double — Mullin's first, with no prior instances recorded in TEG history.

The turnaround was made more striking by what preceded it: Mullin had shot an 8 in round two, one of the worst scores of the week, before flipping to a -2 the very next day, a swing of ten shots. Henry Meller remained anchored at the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, now twelve back from safety.
