# Two Spoons, Then This

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, 13 clear, and never headed after the opening afternoon

**Neumann's first Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the 16th-highest Trophy total ever recorded and the second-best of his own career. He has, at last, something to put on the mantelpiece that is not a joke.

The margin was built early and then simply not given back. Neumann led the Trophy after round two at Troia, still led after round three at Penha Longa, and carried it through the closing round at Estoril. Williams led the Jacket standings at each of those same checkpoints. For three days the two competitions ran alongside one another like separate wars in adjacent countries, neither man losing an inch of his own.

The final round was not flawless. At Estoril's 8th, a par 4 and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann took nine. A quintuple bogey. No points whatsoever. He had spent three days assembling a cushion precisely so that something like this could happen to him without consequence, and here it was, arriving on schedule. The cushion held. The scorecard looked untidy and the tournament did not care.

Estoril otherwise gave him his best golf of the four days. His 92 there was a course personal best, fourteen strokes better than the 106 he posted on his only previous visit in TEG 15. It was the one venue of the four where he improved on his own history, and he chose the day the Trophy was settled to do it.

## David Mullin: from the bottom of the Spoon race in R1 to the Estoril course record in R4

David Mullin finished a long way from where he began. At Oitavos Dunes in the opening round he ran up a triple-bogey seven at the 10th, a stroke index 4 par four, and the zero points dropped him from second to fifth in the Wooden Spoon race. Fifth of five. The bottom of the pile, occupied on day one, with three days still to be got through.

Round three at Penha Longa was worse in places. A quadruple-bogey eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, followed by pars at 17 and 18 that read less like recovery than like a man tidying up after himself. The round came to 30 points, and his 98 was well adrift of his previous best at the course.

Estoril, on the Tuesday, was a different matter entirely. Mullin birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then wobbled through bogey, bogey and double at the 4th, 5th and 6th before a birdie at the 7th stopped the slide. The back nine held. Nine points across the 11th, 12th and 13th, with pars at 12 and 13, and two more pars at 16 and 17 to close.

The total was 45 points, a 15-point improvement on round three. The gross number mattered more. His 80 set a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 established across six prior visits, and bettered his own best there, a 94 at TEG 15, by fourteen strokes. Four days after being last in the race for the Spoon, he owned a course record. Nobody quite knew where to look.

## The Baker brothers and Penha Longa's 12th / Alex Baker's third Wooden Spoon, back-to-back, 127 points — and two all-time course-worst records to go with it

Alex Baker's tournament began badly and then declined to change course. In the first round at Oitavos Dunes he took nine at the 1st, nine at the 11th and ten at the 17th, and signed for 113 gross. A new course-worst for Oitavos, beating the previous mark of 111 set across twelve prior visits. At Troia the following day the damage was more efficiently packaged: thirteen shots dropped over the five holes from the 13th to the 17th, three triples and then two doubles.

Round three took the field to Penha Longa, where both Bakers arrived at the same stretch of ground and both declined to leave it intact. Jon Baker made a double bogey six at the 11th, then ran up a ten at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey worth nothing, and followed it with an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes, and three holes without a net par. It was his only round of the tournament in which he failed to improve on a previous visit: 97 gross at a course where his best was 85.

Alex was on the same holes doing broadly the same thing, only at a marginally gentler gradient. Double bogey at the 11th, a nine at the 12th for quadruple bogey, a seven at the 13th and a seven at the 14th. Twelve shots dropped across four holes and, like his brother, a three-hole run from the 12th without a net par.

The final round at Estoril separated them again. Jon's single lapse came at the 11th, the hardest hole on the card, where he made an eight. Alex's round was made almost entirely of them. Nine at the 1st, eight at the 3rd, nine at the 6th, and fourteen shots bled across the six holes from the 5th to the 10th, every one a double or worse. Then the 12th, a par four, where he took eleven. A septuple bogey, and the worst score he has ever made on a par four in his life. The round came to 107 gross, a second course-worst of the trip, exceeding Estoril's previous high of 106 across six prior visits. He ended on 127 points and took the Wooden Spoon, his third, and the second in succession. Two courses now hold their all-time worst round in his name. There is a kind of consistency in that.

## Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket, +66, by 16 shots

Gregg Williams's second Green Jacket was never seriously threatened, and the final margin, 16 shots clear of David Mullin, confirms it. His gross total of +66 is the fifth-best in the event's history and comfortably the best of his own career.

It was not, however, tidy. Troia, a course nobody in the field had seen before, produced a 94 and two of his worst passages of the tournament: four holes from the 8th without a net par, then three straight doubles from the 13th, six shots gone.

Penha Longa on the Monday was similarly uneven. A triple at the 3rd and a double at the 4th were answered at once by a birdie at the short 5th, at which point a bogey, a triple and a double through the 6th, 7th and 8th dismantled the repair. He recovered on the back nine, playing the 11th through the 15th in level par and gathering nine points from the last three of those. His 87 was five worse than his previous visit.

Estoril produced both his best round and his loudest mistake. The 80 was a course best by six on his only prior visit. It came despite an eight at the par-four 14th, a quadruple bogey, and a double at the 9th that ended a run of three straight pars. Neumann had done his own damage at Estoril's 8th earlier in the day. Different hole, same course, same air of a man conducting business as usual with one pocket on fire.
