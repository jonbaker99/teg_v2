# A Record-Breaking Debut in the Algarve

## Three players post top-three all-time TEG rounds on Boavista's tournament debut, led by David Mullin's flawless card
<!-- storyline: round -->

Boavista Golf & Spa Resort saw one of the most remarkable opening rounds in TEG history on its tournament debut. David Mullin shot a flawless +0, a gross 20-over-par 91, that stands as the best round ever recorded, and the best gross round too. He was not the only one to produce something extraordinary. Jon Baker carded +4, the second-best round in TEG history, backed by a gross 103 that ranks as the third-best gross round ever played. Stuart Neumann was not far behind either measure: his +11 sits as the third-best round of all time, while his gross 100 ranks as the second-best gross round in TEG history.

Three players, three top-three all-time performances, all on the same course on the same afternoon. The field score of just five meant the day's spread was enormous, from Mullin's perfect card down to a worst score of 0, but the headline was set from the very first visit to Lagos: Boavista had produced history.

## Gregg Williams shares the outright lead through two holes, then loses 22 shots across a ruinous closing stretch
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams began TEG 1's opening round at Boavista in style, birdieing the par-3 2nd to draw level for the outright lead in the Green Jacket (Gross) standings, then parring the 3rd without dropping a shot across the pair. It was as good as his day would get.

From the 12th, his round collapsed entirely. Double bogeys at the 12th, 13th and 15th were followed by a sextuple bogey 9 at the par-3 14th, his worst hole against par of the round, and then a quintuple bogey 9 at the 17th. A further double bogey at the last completed a stretch of seven holes, 12 through 18, in which he shed 22 shots. Only the double bogey at the 15th came on a genuinely difficult hole, the par-4 1st stroke index; the rest arrived on holes rated among the easier ones on the course, making the collapse harder to explain away. A round that opened with him sharing the lead ended with him rooted to the bottom of the field.

## The Trophy lead changes hands four times before David Mullin's finish secures it, while Henry Meller is already isolated at the foot of the Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Trophy lead changed hands four times through the front nine at Boavista in Round 1 of TEG 1. Gregg Williams drew level with a birdie at the par-3 2nd, before Jon Baker took the outright lead at the 4th despite a bogey. David Mullin pulled level with a bogey of his own at the par-4 5th, and Baker shared the lead again with a par at the par-5 8th. From there it was Mullin's finish that settled the round, carrying him to the outright TEG 1 Trophy lead.

At the other end of the field, Henry Meller was already in trouble. A double bogey at the par-5 3rd dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 1 Wooden Spoon race, and he never recovered: a horror stretch from the 11th to the 15th saw him bleed 20 shots, including a quintuple bogey at the 14th and a quadruple bogey at the 15th, Boavista's hardest hole. By the end of the round he stood alone at the foot of the Spoon table.
