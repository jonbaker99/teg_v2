## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy — 156 points, led after every single round

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of runner-up Gregg Williams. It is the first Trophy of his career, a total ranking 16th on the all-time list and second-best of his own record. He did not sneak up on it. He simply stood at the front and declined to move.

The win was built on sustained control rather than a late charge, which in this company amounts to an act of restraint. Neumann held the Trophy lead after the second round at Troia, still had it after the third at Penha Longa, and carried it through the fourth at Estoril. Through the same stretch Gregg Williams led the Jacket. The two competitions ran on separate tracks, round after round, Williams ahead on the gross measure and Neumann ahead on the points.

The margin also meant Neumann could absorb damage that would have sunk a tighter lead. He arrived at the par-4 8th at Estoril in the fourth round, the hardest hole on the course at stroke index 1, with the tournament essentially in his pocket. He took a nine. The quintuple bogey returned no Stableford points whatsoever. Five shots handed back to the house for nothing at all, on a card being counted for the Trophy, and the final accounting barely noticed it had happened.

That is the shape of the four days: a leader who took the front early, never surrendered it across the closing three rounds, and finished 13 points ahead while also posting a hole that would have embarrassed a man learning the game.

## Estoril, R4: David Mullin turns 30 points into 45 and takes the course record with him

David Mullin's third round at Penha Longa was the sort of thing best filed and never reopened. He signed for 30 points, a total containing a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 16th, the hardest hole on the card, worth nothing. He did at least finish tidily, with gross pars at the 17th and 18th. There is something admirable in a man straightening the cushions on the way out of a burning building.

The fourth round at Estoril began no more promisingly. Mullin bogeyed the 4th and the 5th and then ran up a double bogey at the par-5 6th, three holes that yielded five points between them. The birdie at the 7th stopped the run. From there the round changed shape entirely.

The stretch from the 11th to the 13th brought nine points: a bogey at the toughest hole on the course, still worth three with his shots, followed by two pars. He then held par again at the 16th and 17th, going two holes late in the round without conceding a single gross shot.

The final numbers were 45 points, a gain of 15 on his third-round total, and a gross 80. That is a new course record at Estoril, beating the previous best of 86 set across six earlier visits.

The same round produced the other end of the scale. Alex Baker's 107 gross is a new Estoril course-worst, exceeding the previous mark of 106.

## The 12th hole and the Baker brothers

**The 12th hole and the Baker brothers**

The 12th at Penha Longa is a par 5 and the easiest hole on the card, stroke index 17. In the third round it took both Bakers apart. Jon Baker ran up a 10, a quintuple bogey, for no Stableford points. Alex Baker was only marginally better off with a 9, a quadruple bogey, also worth nothing. A hole offering no resistance whatsoever collected 19 shots from two men and gave back not one point.

Neither hole was an isolated incident. Both brothers had already dropped shots at the 11th, each making a double-bogey six, and both kept dropping them afterwards. Alex went 6-9-7-7 across holes 11 to 14, four holes costing him 12 shots to gross par and producing a single point. Jon's damage was more efficiently packaged, compressed into three holes: 6-10-8 through 11 to 13, 11 shots gone. Measured against their handicaps the picture is unchanged. Alex went three holes without a net par from the 12th to the 14th, Jon from the 11th to the 13th, each falling five shots short of the mark.

A different 12th, on a different course, had one more word to say in the fourth round at Estoril, a par 4 this time and stroke index 6. Alex Baker took 11 on it. It is the worst score he has recorded on a par 4. Jon, playing alongside, had already made an eight at the 11th, a quadruple bogey on the second-hardest hole on the course, and thus arrived as a spectator rather than a rival.

## Gregg Williams retains the Green Jacket at +66, by 16 — best gross in the field in all four rounds

Gregg Williams won the Green Jacket, finishing at +66 gross and beating runner-up David Mullin by 16 shots. The score ranks fifth on the all-time list for the event and stands as Williams's own best. He was the best gross player in the field in every single round, which is a sentence he has surely already had framed.

The margin flattered golf that was not without incident. At Troia in the second round he mislaid the middle of his card entirely, going four holes from the 8th to the 11th without a net par, a bogey followed by three straight double bogeys. He then dropped six gross shots in three holes from the 13th, another run of doubles at the 13th, 14th and 15th.

The third round at Penha Longa opened badly, with a triple bogey at the 3rd and a double at the 4th, before a birdie at the par-3 5th halted the slide. From there he found the steadiness that defined his four days: five holes from the 11th to the 15th without dropping a gross shot, including nine Stableford points across the 13th, 14th and 15th.

The final round at Estoril repeated the pattern. Williams took nine points from the 6th to the 8th, three straight pars, before a double bogey at the 9th ended the run. Worse came at the 14th, where he ran up an eight, a quadruple bogey, for no points. With a lead that size, it did not matter. He could have posted almost anything and still collected the Jacket, and at the 14th he set about testing the theory.

## Alex Baker takes back-to-back Wooden Spoons on 127 points — bottom of the table from the first round to the last

**Baker takes the Wooden Spoon**

Alex Baker finished the tournament with 127 Stableford points and collected the Wooden Spoon. His second in succession. He was bottom of the table from the first round to the last, which at least spared everyone the suspense.

The trouble started immediately. His opening round at Oitavos Dunes produced a 113 gross, a new course-worst there, beating the previous mark of 111 set across 12 prior visits. The round included a 10 at the par-4 17th, a sextuple bogey worth no points.

Round 2 at Troia brought a damaging closing stretch. Between the 13th and the 17th, Baker dropped 13 shots to par: three straight triple bogeys at the 13th, 14th and 15th, followed by doubles at the 16th and 17th. Those five holes returned five Stableford points in total.

The fourth round at Estoril followed a similar pattern, only earlier. Baker opened with a 9 at the par-4 1st, a quintuple bogey, and thereby settled the tone before anyone had warmed up. From the 5th through the 10th he dropped a further 14 shots: three holes without a net par, a double at the 5th, a quadruple bogey at the par-5 6th, another double at the 7th, and then four consecutive doubles running from the 7th to the 10th. The round totalled 107 gross, another course-worst, exceeding the previous 106 recorded across six prior visits.

Two course records in four days, both of them the wrong sort, and the Wooden Spoon to keep them company.
