# Jon Baker's Record Round Reshapes The Race

## Jon Baker's record-breaking 86 gross at Royal Óbidos, a new course best
<!-- storyline: round -->

At Royal Óbidos, Jon Baker carded a gross 86 in round three, breaking the course record of 87 that had stood across 18 prior visits to the venue. It also stood as his own best score there, arriving eight shots inside what he had managed on his last visit to the course.

The round was built on the back nine, where Baker gathered 20 Stableford points across holes 11 to 16, birdieing the par-5 11th and the par-3 13th. Within that stretch he strung together four holes, 13 through 16, without dropping a single gross shot, the lone blemish of the six-hole run coming with a bogey at the 12th, one of the harder holes on the course.

The round entrenched Baker's position in both of this year's TEG races. He was already leading the 2024 TEG Trophy and pushed his advantage from 2 points to 11, while in the Green Jacket race he moved past David Mullin, turning a share of the lead into an 8-point cushion heading into the next round.

## Alex Baker's catalogue of blow-ups produces the field's worst gross round
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Note there's another Baker in the field (Jon BAKER), so full names are needed throughout.

Alex Baker put together the worst gross round of the field at Royal Óbidos, a card littered with double-figure damage from start to finish. The trouble began early, with a quadruple bogey 8 on the 4th — the hardest hole on the course — followed immediately by a quadruple bogey 9 on the par-5 5th, part of a stretch across holes 4 to 7 that bled 12 shots.

Worse followed in the closing holes. Across holes 12 to 16, Alex Baker shipped 15 shots, a run that included a sextuple bogey 10 at the 14th — the round's low point. The 18th offered no respite, another quadruple bogey, this time a 9 on the par-5 closer.

The round finished well outside Alex Baker's own best figures at the course, with the field's worst gross score of the day at 38 recorded in the round-of-the-day comparison. Despite the carnage, it still counted as this TEG's best score so far at Royal Óbidos for Alex Baker, a modest floor given the round it followed.

## The Wooden Spoon changes hands as Gregg Williams collapses and John Patterson rallies
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

At Royal Óbidos, John Patterson followed a poor second round of 22 stableford points with a 41 in round three, a swing of 19 that stood out as a personal turnaround. He started dreadfully, with a double bogey at the 4th, a triple bogey at the 5th, and more doubles at the 6th and 7th, before a birdie at the 8th stopped the bleeding. He then found his best form, going through the 12th and 13th without dropping a gross shot, and piling up 13 points across holes 12 to 15, on his way to a course-best gross score this TEG.

Gregg Williams went the other way. A quadruple bogey nine at the par-5 5th was an early warning, and after a brief recovery at the 11th, he collapsed from the 12th: seven holes without a net par and 14 shots dropped over that stretch. A double bogey at the 14th confirmed his fall to the bottom of the 2024 Wooden Spoon race, swapping places with Patterson, who had occupied that spot before the round.

## David Mullin's fade lets Jon Baker escape in both the Trophy and the Green Jacket races
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin had held the lead in the 2024 TEG Green Jacket (Gross) for 35 holes, ever since the second hole of round one, when a double bogey at the first at Royal Óbidos handed it back to Jon Baker at the start of round three. The lead swapped twice more inside the round: Mullin retook it with a par at the sixth, only for Baker to draw level with bogeys at the second — one of the hardest holes on the course — and the seventh. By the close of play Baker held the Jacket outright, helped by Mullin's stableford total collapsing from 47 points in round two to 35, a swing of 12. Baker also carded a course personal best at Royal Óbidos, eight strokes better than his previous visit there.

The same round did the same work in the 2024 TEG Trophy, where Baker's overnight lead of two points stretched out to eleven as Mullin faded, leaving Baker in command of both races heading into the back half of the week.
