## David Mullin's 41-point, 85-gross round at Littlestone, a new personal best at the course by seven shots, built out of a scruffy front nine and a birdie finish
<!-- storyline: round -->

**David Mullin's 41 at Littlestone**

David Mullin posted the best score of the day at Littlestone. Forty-one points, from a gross 85. For the first six holes it resembled nothing of the sort.

He took a triple bogey seven at the 4th, one of the hardest holes on the course. He followed it with a double bogey at the 5th, then a bogey at the par-3 6th. By that stage he had gone a run of holes without making anything better than a bogey. Then the 7th arrived and he birdied it. He added a par at the 8th, as though the previous hour had been someone else's.

The middle of the round was steady rather than spectacular. Pars at the 10th, stroke index 1 and the hardest hole on the course, and at the 11th. Pars again at the 13th and 14th. The finish wobbled: bogeys at the 15th and 16th, then a double bogey five at the 17th. He birdied the last and shut it down.

The gross total is a Littlestone personal best. His only previous visit to the course had produced a best of 92, so this was an improvement of seven shots. It also stretched both of his leads. His advantage in the 2021 TEG Trophy grew from three points to nine. His lead in the 2021 Green Jacket went from five shots to twelve. Nine clear, with a seven on the card. Round here, that is what mastery looks like.

## Littlestone's revenge on the field — three of four players worse than their TEG 13 visit, and the day's modest definition of a good stretch
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Littlestone takes its due**

The Championship Links had only been seen once before, in TEG 13. It had evidently been keeping accounts. Jon Baker went round in 92, thirteen strokes worse than the 79 he posted here on the first visit. Alex Baker matched his TEG 13 figure exactly: 100, again. There is a certain integrity in that.

Alex Baker's card was undone by two holes. A quadruple bogey 9 at the par-5 5th, one of the easiest holes on the course, came first. Three holes later he made an 8 at the 8th, also a quadruple bogey. Neither hole returned a single Stableford point.

The day's best run of golf, on the gross figures, tells you most of what you need to know about conditions and form. It belonged to Jon Baker and it lasted two holes. A par at the 14th and a par at the 15th, the second-easiest hole on the course. Five points, and not a shot dropped to par across the pair. That was the summit of the entire day.

The consequence was a widening at both ends. David Mullin's lead in the 2021 Trophy grew from three to nine, and his Green Jacket margin from five to twelve. Jon Baker stayed bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, though his cushion to the player above shrank from four shots to two. The Links, having collected, closed its books.

## Alex Baker's six-point slide behind Mullin in the TEG 14 Trophy, and the narrowing of the Wooden Spoon gap between Jon Baker and Gregg Williams
<!-- storyline: race -->

**Alex Baker's four-hole collapse hands Mullin daylight**

The damage was done inside twenty minutes. Alex Baker played holes 2 to 5 at Littlestone in twelve shots over par. A triple bogey at the 2nd. A double at the 3rd. Another triple at the 4th, one of the hardest holes on the course. Then a quadruple bogey 9 at the 5th, which is one of the easiest. Four holes, three single points and a blank, five points short of net par over the stretch. The card offers no explanation and does not appear to want one.

That was enough to turn a close race into a comfortable one. David Mullin, who led the TEG 14 Trophy after round one, saw his advantage widen from three points to nine, and his lead in the TEG 14 Green Jacket grow from five to twelve. Mullin shot 85 at Littlestone, a course best and seven strokes better than his previous visit here. Alex Baker matched his 100 from that same first visit exactly.

At the other end, the TEG 14 Wooden Spoon is getting tighter rather than looser. Jon Baker still holds last place, as he did before the round. But the man immediately above him is now two points clear rather than four. Escape is closer than it was, which for Jon Baker counts as progress. Two rounds remain at a venue only being played for the second time, and neither end of the table is settled.
