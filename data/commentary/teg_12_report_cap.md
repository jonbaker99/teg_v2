# The 12 That Wasn't a Eulogy

The card says 12. Par 4, stroke index 2, the fourth hole of the entire tournament, eight over par in one box. When John Patterson signed for it, no player in TEG history had posted a worse hole relative to par. Anywhere. Ever.

He won the Trophy by ten.

That is TEG's third pilgrimage to Catalonia, and it wants unpicking, because none of it obeys the usual arithmetic of doom. The shape was simple enough: two days on the Tour Course, the gentler of the two Coles-Gallardo siblings, then two on the Stadium, which had declined to yield a round in the eighties across fifteen TEG visits. Gregg Williams led for two days. He led on the Tour. Both pots were settled on the Stadium, and the hinge was a single hole in Round 3.

## Round 1 — A 12, a 105, and a Leader Nobody Mentions

The Thursday round on the Tour Course produced two numbers everybody discussed and one nobody did.

Williams played the best gross round of his life: 84, twelve over, a personal best at the course by eight shots on his last visit. It put him five clear in the Green Jacket and one clear on the Trophy. In bar-conversation terms it ranked about fourth.

Because Patterson had his 12 at the 4th, the second-hardest hole on the property, and then did something odder than the 12. He carried on. Par, par, par, par from the 8th through the 11th, the humourless competence of a man who has decided not to discuss it, until a double at the 12th ended the run. He signed for 45 points, one behind Williams. Reduce the 12 to a bogey and he sleeps in front — a counterfactual he presumably ran several hundred times, and which cost him nothing whatever.

Stuart Neumann's afternoon was less retrievable. His 105 was a new worst gross round at the Tour Course across ten prior TEG visits, taking the record from 103. The foundations were sound: a sextuple bogey 10 at the 13th, the hardest hole out there, and a 12 at the par-5 15th. Alex Baker ran up a quintuple bogey 10 at the 10th and settled into the bottom of the Spoon table by the 14th, where — it takes some doing — he stayed for the best part of three days.

## Round 2 — Williams Holds, Patterson Hunts

Friday, same course, minimal movement. Williams held both leads by a single shot, despite a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th, a hole apparently collecting a toll from everyone who passed. Patterson kept pace and posted a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 15th, the only thing between him and parity. Neumann opened with an 8 at the 1st, getting the formalities out of the way.

David Mullin quietly returned an 84 of his own, a Tour Course personal best by a stroke. Two rounds gone and the leaderboard had barely twitched. The Tour Course had finished with the field. The Stadium hadn't begun.

## Round 3 — The Stadium Reshuffles Everything

Saturday. Everything that mattered happened inside four holes, most of it at one.

Patterson opened with a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 3rd, his third double-figure hole of the tournament, filed with the same absence of ceremony as the first two. Williams, playing steadily, drew level on Stableford with a par at that 3rd. Level, note, not ahead. It was his last sight of the summit.

Then the 4th. Patterson made bogey, took three points and the outright Trophy lead, and never gave it back. At the same hole Jon Baker also made bogey and, from fourth on gross, took the outright Green Jacket lead. Two competitions changed hands at one hole, on two bogeys. Williams made 9 there.

He was not finished. A septuple bogey 11 at the 13th — twelve over par across those two holes alone — removed him from the argument he had led since Round 1.

And Patterson, having done three days of damage in single spectacular instalments, spent the closing stretch doing the reverse. Nineteen points from the 12th to the 17th: par, bogey, bogey, a birdie at the 15th, bogey, bogey. No heroics. Six holes of a man giving nothing back.

He went to bed eight clear.

The Spoon race, indifferent to all this, passed itself round all day. Mullin dropped to the bottom with a double at the 9th, the hardest hole on the course. Alex Baker reclaimed it at the 11th, Mullin dropped again at the 14th, Alex Baker took it back at the 17th with a double. Three days holding the floor, one round left to lose it.

## Round 4 — Mullin's Sunday Morning, Baker's Sunday Afternoon

Sunday resolved everything, in order.

Mullin's 9 at the par-5 3rd dropped him to the bottom for the first time since the 14th hole of the previous round. The quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th confirmed the arrangement. He never climbed out, finishing on 139 points with a Wooden Spoon he has now held for a second consecutive TEG, after three rounds of watching another man mind it for him. Two holes was the whole handover.

Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, was about other business. His 89 was the first sub-90 round in fifteen TEG visits to the Stadium, beating a course record of 90 that belonged to him, from TEG 11, by one stroke. It sealed the Green Jacket at +63, eight clear of Mullin. He had been the third-most-discussed man on the property for two days and never appeared to mind.

Neumann, whose tournament began with the worst round the Tour Course has seen, eagled the par-5 15th — the easiest hole on the Stadium, and the only eagle anybody managed all trip.

Patterson signed for 40 and won by ten. He also set personal bests at both courses, 96 at the Tour and 95 at the Stadium, which is the flattest available sentence about a man who made three double-figure holes.

Williams finished runner-up in both competitions, with a personal-best gross total of +76 and individual bests at both venues — 84 at the Tour, 92 at the Stadium. He kept none of the silverware.

## The men, in brief

- **John Patterson** — Second Trophy, by ten, after sixth and fourth in his last two outings. Made 12, 10 and 10 in three consecutive rounds and won more comfortably than anyone in living memory. The 12 proved an anecdote, not an epitaph.
- **Gregg Williams** — Led both competitions for two rounds on the best gross round and gross total of his career, then lost both in one Stadium afternoon. Trophy runner-up for the third time. Two course records, no pots, and a 9 and an 11 to explain for years.
- **Jon Baker** — Took the gross lead with a bogey at the 4th in Round 3 and never returned it, then broke his own Stadium record with the venue's first 89. The Green Jacket by eight, won while everyone else discussed Stableford.
- **David Mullin** — Retains the Spoon on 139, having been granted a three-round reprieve and handed it back inside two holes on the final day. Jacket runner-up, and an 84 at the Tour that stands as his best there. An odd ledger, but honest.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Opened with 105, the worst round the Tour Course has recorded in ten TEG visits, and closed with the tournament's only eagle at the 15th. For the first time in two TEGs, the Spoon went elsewhere.
- **Alex Baker** — Held the bottom of the table from the 14th hole of Round 1 to the 17th of Round 3, then escaped on the final day. A Stadium personal best of 93, thirteen better than his last visit, and the strange dignity of earning the Spoon without receiving it.
