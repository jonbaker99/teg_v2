## Stuart Neumann's rollercoaster 35 points — the best round of the day, salvaged from two separate collapses
<!-- storyline: round -->

Stuart Neumann's round 2 at Troia opened as a disaster. Through nine holes he could not manage anything better than bogey. Double bogeys at the 1st, 3rd, 6th and 9th. A triple at the 4th, and another at the 8th. He was not so much playing the front nine as being processed by it. The bleeding finally stopped with a birdie at the 10th.

That birdie sparked the best spell of his day. Neumann took 11 points from three holes, adding a par at the 11th and another at the 12th. Across that run he dropped not one gross shot. For three holes he looked like a golfer. Then the 13th arrived and he took a triple bogey, and the stretch ended as abruptly as it had started. Further down the course he added a quadruple bogey at the 16th, in case anyone had grown attached to the idea of a recovery.

Two separate collapses, one on either side of the hot spell, and still 35 points. It was the best score of the day in the field of five. That is not a contradiction round here. It is the going rate. It kept him on top of the 2023 TEG Trophy standings, though his lead widened only from 5 to 7.

## Gregg Williams's horror round nearly hands Jon Baker the Green Jacket lead
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams took a three-stroke lead into the second round of the 2023 TEG Green Jacket at Troia Golf Championship Course. He then fell apart with real commitment. From the 8th he went four holes without a net par: a bogey at the 8th, then double bogeys at the 9th, 10th and 11th. The last of those came on the easiest hole on the course. There was a brief respite. It meant nothing. The collapse resumed at the 13th with three more consecutive double bogeys, six gross shots gone across holes 13 to 15, the par-5 14th and the second-hardest hole on the course included.

Jon Baker audited the wreckage in real time. He drew level for the Green Jacket gross lead with a bogey at the 11th, then drew level again with a par at the 14th, right in the teeth of the meltdown. Level was as far as he got. Williams held firm elsewhere on the card. By the close he had somehow stretched his Green Jacket lead to four shots. Some men lose ground while collapsing. Williams gained it.

## Alex Baker's Troia debut turns into a two-act horror show, but the Wooden Spoon gap tightens anyway
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Alex Baker's first visit to Troia came in two acts, both of them grim. The trouble began at the 4th with a triple bogey, which opened a stretch that shed 13 shots over six holes through to the 9th. That included double bogeys at the 1st and 6th stroke-index holes. Baker halted the run with a birdie at the 10th, one of only two net gains he made all round. He was allowed to enjoy it briefly.

From the 13th to the 17th another 13 shots leaked away. Triple bogeys at the 13th, 14th and 15th. Three holes running without a single net par, the 15th among the hardest on the course. The triple at the 13th also dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 16 Wooden Spoon race, which is the sort of thing a man notices.

And yet the damage did not widen the gap he needed to close. The margin to the next-worst player in the Spoon race shrank from four shots to three. Baker remains within touching distance, which is a peculiar thing to be told after a round like that. It was a small mercy from a miserable debut. The round stands as his only Troia score to date.

## David Mullin leapfrogs Gregg Williams into outright second as Stuart Neumann's Trophy lead stretches to seven
<!-- storyline: race -->

At Troia in round 2 of the 2023 TEG Trophy, David Mullin moved past Gregg Williams into outright second. Neumann's lead at the top widened from five shots to seven. Williams, meanwhile, was defending a Green Jacket lead that grew from three to four, and doing so through the middle of a genuinely miserable round. From the 8th to the 11th he went four holes without a net par, including three straight double bogeys from the 9th through the 11th. Six gross shots gone across those three holes. He then produced a second drought from the 13th to the 15th, three more double bogeys in a row, the last on the 15th, one of the hardest holes on the course. Six double bogeys in eight holes is a body of work.

None of it loosened his grip on the Jacket. It was, however, enough for Mullin to go past him in the Trophy. Neumann sits clear at the top after the second round, unbothered.
