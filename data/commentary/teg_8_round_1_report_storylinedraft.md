# New Course, Same Old Chaos

## Gregg Williams's stableford surge to 38 points, the best round of the opening day at Quinta da Marinha
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams produced the standout round of the opening day at Quinta da Marinha, carding 38 Stableford points to top the field by a comfortable margin over a worst score of 17. The round turned on a purple patch through holes 8 to 11, where Williams collected 14 points, capped by a birdie on the par-5 10th — a bogey on the first, the hardest hole on the course, followed by a par, that birdie, and another bogey. The birdie itself carried weight beyond the card, taking Williams to the outright lead in the 2015 TEG Trophy.

The round did not stay smooth. Over the next stretch, holes 12 to 15, Williams shipped ten shots, with double bogeys at the 12th, 13th and 14th before an 8 at the par-4 15th, a quadruple bogey that yielded no points at all. Even so, the damage was contained enough to leave the 38-point total untouched at the top of the day's leaderboard.

## Stuart Neumann's opening-round collapse, the worst Trophy round recorded in TEG history to date, which settles him at the foot of the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon table by a distance
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Quinta da Marinha's opening round produced one horror show that gave way to something worse. Henry Meller's quintuple bogey at the 3rd sent him to the bottom of the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon table after just three holes. He didn't stay there long: a triple bogey at the 4th put Stuart Neumann into last place instead, and from there his round fell apart entirely.

Between the 7th and 15th, Neumann bled 29 shots, going nine straight holes without a single net par. Quadruple bogeys at the 10th and 11th were followed by a quintuple bogey at the 12th, one of the hardest holes on the course, and another at the 14th. He closed with 17 points and a gross 43, both personal worsts - and, at the time, the worst TEG Trophy round recorded in TEG history. It left him marooned at the foot of the TEG 8 Wooden Spoon table by a distance.

## The opening skirmish for the TEG 8 Green Jacket, with David Mullin edging ahead of Jon Baker by a single shot
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Green Jacket lead changed hands three times before settling on the front nine's back end at Quinta da Marinha. Jon Baker moved level with a bogey at the par-5 6th, then David Mullin drew level in turn with a bogey of his own at the par-5 10th, before Gregg Williams shared the lead with a bogey at the 11th. None of it was pretty, but it set up the round's real turning point: a par from Mullin at the par-4 13th, one of the harder holes on the course, took him outright into first.

Baker's chance to answer disappeared at the very same hole. Moments after Mullin's par, Baker ran up a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 13th, the low point of his round. He steadied himself with bogey, double bogey, then a birdie at the 17th to stop the bleeding, but the damage was done. Mullin closed out the day a single shot clear of Baker for the TEG 8 Green Jacket lead.
