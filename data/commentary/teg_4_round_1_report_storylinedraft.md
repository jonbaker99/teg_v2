# No Bunkers, No Mercy

## Jon Baker's net 2 survives a quintuple bogey at the 14th to post the best Trophy score of the day
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's opening round at Royal Ashdown Forest in TEG 1 was a story of resilience. He traded the Trophy (Net VP) lead back and forth through the front nine and into the back, drawing level at the 5th, 7th, 13th and again at the 15th, never quite shaking free but never falling away either. Then, at the 14th, the hardest hole on the course, his round threatened to unravel entirely: a quintuple bogey 9 that could have ended his chances outright.

Instead he answered immediately, reeling off pars at the 15th and 16th without dropping a gross shot across the two holes to steady himself. That composure held to the finish, and Baker's net 2 for the round stood up as the best Trophy score of the day, ahead of a field of five in which the next-best score was 14. It was an afternoon that swung from disaster to the day's finest recovery.

## Gregg Williams's day of whiplash: a fleeting outright Trophy lead off a birdie at the 8th collapses into a nine-hole, 22-shot bleed and the worst gross score of the round
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams's opening round at Royal Ashdown Forest was a round of two extremes. He started roughly, dropping shots steadily through a stretch from the 2nd to the 6th that cost him 13 gross shots, including a quadruple bogey 8 on the par-4 4th — the easiest hole on the course. That run also left him three holes without a net par and dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 16 Wooden Spoon race.

Then came a turnaround. A bogey at the 7th broke the run of bogeys-or-worse, and a birdie at the 8th completed the recovery — good enough to send him to the outright lead of the TEG 16 Trophy on net scoring.

The lead did not last. From the 10th to the 18th, Williams bled 22 shots, with four doubles and three triples on the back nine, including bogey-or-worse on the hardest hole on the course, the 14th. He finished with the worst gross score of the round.

## A fractured leaderboard after round one: David Mullin claims the Green Jacket lead on twin birdie recoveries while Henry Meller's 12 at the short 11th plants him at the bottom of the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

Round one of the 2011 TEG at Royal Ashdown Forest's Old Course produced an early split between triumph and disaster. Henry Meller's day was defined by a 12 at the short par-3 11th, one of the hardest holes on the course at stroke index 3 — nine over par, and enough to stand as both his own worst hole and the worst posted anywhere in TEG history to that point. Coupled with a double bogey at the 7th that had already dropped him to the bottom of the 2011 TEG Wooden Spoon field, Meller closed the round anchored at the foot of that race.

David Mullin suffered his own rocky start, a triple bogey at the 5th sending him to the very bottom of the Spoon standings as well. But he answered twice, breaking bogey-or-worse runs with birdies at the 10th and the 13th, and by the close of play had pulled clear to lead the 2011 TEG Green Jacket. Jon Baker, meanwhile, sat atop the 2011 TEG Trophy standings.
