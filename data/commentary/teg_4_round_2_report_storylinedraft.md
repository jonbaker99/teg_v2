# Lingfield Draws First Blood

## Henry Meller's redemption round at Lingfield
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller arrived at Lingfield Park Golf Club looking to shake off a wretched opening round, and delivered exactly that. A quadruple bogey 8 at the 3rd temporarily dropped him to the bottom of the 2011 Wooden Spoon standings, last of the five players. He responded with a strong middle stretch, holding pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th to gain three shots against net par without dropping a single gross shot across those holes. That run ended with a triple bogey at the 12th, one of Lingfield's hardest holes, and later a quadruple bogey 9 at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course. Despite the blow-ups, the round marked a clear recovery: having taken 14 shots more than net par in Round 1, Meller was 12 shots better against net par this time, finishing just 2 over. By the close of play, it was Stuart Neumann, not Meller, sitting at the bottom of the Spoon race.

## Stuart Neumann inherits the Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Stuart Neumann's second round at Lingfield unravelled at the start. He opened with a triple bogey at the 1st, then a quadruple bogey 9 at the par-5 2nd, and a double bogey at the 3rd, dropping nine gross shots over the first three holes without a single net par among them. The 2nd, one of the easiest holes on the course at stroke index 16, proved the costliest, and it was there that Neumann slipped to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, a position he reinforced with further double bogeys at the 5th and 9th. Before the round Henry Meller had occupied the cellar spot; by the finish it was Neumann trailing, three shots off the next-worst in the race. It was a difficult reintroduction to Lingfield, a course he was visiting for the first time in the TEG, and his best gross score there, 99, came only after this rocky start had already done the damage.

## Lingfield punishes its first TEG field
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Round two of TEG 4 took the field to Lingfield Park Golf Club in Surrey for the first time, and the parkland course wasted no time making its mark. All five players in the field found themselves in trouble at some point, with Gregg Williams bearing the brunt of it. He carded a quintuple bogey 9 at the 3rd, one of the course's harder holes, then added quadruple bogeys at the 9th and the 8th for a torrid run of blow-up holes in the same round.

Jon Baker's title challenge also took a hit. Having led the TEG 4 Trophy going into the round, he ran up a 9 at the 17th, and by the close David Mullin had moved to the front, opening a two-shot gap. Mullin was not immune himself, closing his round with a quadruple bogey 9 at the 18th. Henry Meller and Stuart Neumann completed the carnage, both posting quadruple bogeys of their own, with Neumann's dropping him to the back of the field for the Wooden Spoon.

## David Mullin snatches the Trophy lead from Jon Baker
<!-- storyline: race -->

On Lingfield's back nine, the lead in the 2011 Trophy changed hands three times inside four holes. Jon Baker went ahead outright with a bogey on the 12th, one of the course's harder holes, before David Mullin twice drew back level, first with a bogey on the 10th and then another on the 13th.

The decisive blow came late. Baker ran up a quintuple bogey nine on the 17th, another of Lingfield's tougher holes, a collapse that turned into his worst round to date at +9. Mullin was far from chanceless himself, closing with a quadruple bogey nine of his own on the 18th, but it did not cost him: by the end of the round he had taken the outright Trophy lead from Baker, now two shots clear where Baker had led by three at the round's start. The Green Jacket picture was untouched, Mullin still out front there too, but his cushion in that race had stretched to seven.
