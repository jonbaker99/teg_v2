# Meller's Second Coming at Boavista

## Henry Meller and Jon Baker share the best trophy score of the day, but only one of them is making TEG history
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller and Jon Baker both carded -2 at Boavista, a score that ties as the 2nd-best round in TEG history to date. But it is Meller's version of it that carries the extra distinction. His gross 18-over is itself the 2nd-best gross round recorded in TEG history, and it doubled as a personal best at Boavista, taking his best score on the course down to 89 from a prior 91. Baker's -2 came home in 26-over gross, no such milestone attached.

Meller's round was also a striking reversal: having shot 8-over in the first round, his swing of ten shots between rounds was built on a stretch of four shots to par across the 7th to 9th before a double bogey at the 10th ended the run. Baker, despite his own historic score, endured a mid-round collapse of his own, dropping ten shots over five holes from the 10th to the 14th, all doubles.

Baker's round extended his TEG 12 Trophy lead to 13, while Meller's effort lifted him off the bottom of the Wooden Spoon table, with Stuart Neumann now occupying last place instead.

## Gregg Williams's day of visible carnage somehow produces a Boavista personal best
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams's second round at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort swung wildly between recovery and ruin. He opened strongly through holes 3 to 5, gaining four shots against par net, with a par salvaged on the par-5 3rd. The middle of the round then fell apart completely: across holes 8 to 11 he dropped 12 shots without a single net par, a stretch that included a quadruple bogey 8 at the 10th, one of the hardest holes on the course. A third collapse followed late, three holes from 15 to 17 costing him eight more shots, headlined by a triple bogey on the 15th, the hardest hole on the course. Somehow the wreckage still added up to progress. Williams's gross score was a personal best for Boavista, three shots inside his previous best of 105 and four shots better than his last visit there, achieved on his sixth trip to the course.

## Stuart Neumann's front-nine promise collapses into the Wooden Spoon cellar
<!-- storyline: race -->

Stuart Neumann had built a tidy run through the middle of his round at Boavista, holding par or better across the 4th, 5th and 6th — a stretch that included pars on the tricky par-3 6th, one of the harder holes on the course. That composure vanished at the 7th, where a double bogey ended the streak and swung him back over par for the round. It proved to be the start of a slide rather than a blip: a bogey at the par-5 8th, one of the easiest holes at Boavista, followed straight after, and by the end of the hole Neumann had dropped to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings, overtaking Henry Meller in last place with just one shot separating them.

It was a quiet kind of collapse — no blow-up hole, just three in a row that went the wrong way after three that had gone right. Neumann's overall total for the round still matched his personal best score at the course, set the previous year, but on this occasion it was enough only to leave him fielding the TEG 2 Wooden Spoon.
