# The 12 That Wasn't a Eulogy

The card says 12. Par 4, stroke index 2, the fourth hole of the whole tournament, and eight strokes over par in a single box. Nobody in TEG history had ever posted a worse hole relative to par. Anywhere, ever.

He won the Trophy by ten.

That is the story of TEG's third pilgrimage to Catalonia, and it wants unpicking, because none of it obeys the usual arithmetic of doom. The shape was simple enough. Two days on the Tour Course, the gentler of the two Coles-Gallardo siblings, then two on the Stadium, which had spent fifteen TEG visits declining to give up a round in the eighties. Gregg Williams led for two days. He led on the Tour. Both pots were settled on the Stadium, and the hinge was a single hole in the third round.

## Round 1 — A 12, a 105, and a Leader Nobody Mentions

The Thursday round on the Tour Course produced two numbers everybody discussed and one nobody did.

Williams played the best gross round of his life: 84, twelve over, a personal best at the course by eight shots on his last visit. It put him five clear in the Green Jacket and one clear on the Trophy. In bar-conversation terms it ranked about fifth.

Because Patterson had his 12 at the 4th, the second-hardest hole out there, and then did something rather more impressive than the 12: he carried on. Par, par, par, par from the 8th through the 11th, the flat competence of a man who has resolved not to discuss the matter, until a double at the 12th ended it. He signed for 45 points, one behind Williams. Turn the 12 into a bogey and he goes to bed in front. That is the counterfactual he presumably ran a few hundred times that evening, and it cost him nothing in the end.

Stuart Neumann's afternoon was less salvageable. His 105 was a new worst gross round at the Tour Course across ten prior TEG visits, taking the record from 103. The foundations were sound: a sextuple bogey 10 at the 13th, the hardest hole on the course, and a 12 at the par-5 15th. Alex Baker ran up a quintuple bogey 10 at the 10th and settled into the bottom of the Spoon table by the 14th, where — and this takes doing — he stayed for the best part of three days.

## Round 2 — Williams Holds, Patterson Hunts

Friday, same course, very little movement. Williams held both leads by a single shot, and managed it despite a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th, a hole apparently collecting a toll from everyone who passed. Patterson kept pace, his quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 15th the only thing between him and parity. Neumann opened with an 8 at the 1st. Formalities, dispatched early.

David Mullin quietly returned an 84 of his own, a Tour Course personal best by one stroke. Two rounds gone and the leaderboard had scarcely twitched. The Tour Course had finished with the field. The Stadium hadn't begun.

## Round 3 — The Stadium Reshuffles Everything

Saturday. Everything that mattered happened inside four holes, and most of it at one.

Patterson opened with a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 3rd, his third double-figure hole of the tournament, filed without ceremony alongside the other two. Williams, playing steadily, drew level on Stableford with a par at that same 3rd. Level, note. Not ahead. It was the last time he saw the summit.

Then the 4th. Patterson made bogey, took three points and the outright Trophy lead, and never gave it up. At the same hole Jon Baker also made bogey and, from fourth on gross, took the outright lead in the Green Jacket. Two competitions changed hands at one hole on two bogeys, which is either an indictment of the field or a fair account of the Stadium. Williams made 9 there.

He wasn't finished. A septuple bogey 11 at the 13th — twelve strokes over par across those two holes alone — removed him from an argument he had led since the opening round.

And Patterson, having spent three days doing his damage in single spectacular instalments, used the closing stretch to do the reverse. Nineteen points from the 12th to the 17th: par, bogey, bogey, a birdie at the 15th, then bogey, bogey. No heroics. Six holes of a man declining to give anything back.

He went to bed eight clear.

The Spoon race, indifferent to all this, spent the day handing itself around. Mullin dropped to the bottom with a double at the 9th, the hardest hole on the course; Alex Baker reclaimed it at the 11th; Mullin dropped again at the 14th; Alex Baker took it back at the 17th with a double. Three days holding the floor, one round left to lose it.

## Round 4 — Mullin's Morning, Baker's Afternoon

Sunday resolved everything, in order.

Mullin's 9 at the par-5 3rd dropped him to the bottom for the first time since the 14th hole of the previous round. His quadruple-bogey 8 at the 4th confirmed the arrangement. He never climbed out, and finished on 139 points with a Wooden Spoon he has now held for a second consecutive TEG, having watched another man mind it for three rounds. Two holes. That was the entire handover.

Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, was about other business. His 89 was the first sub-90 round in fifteen TEG visits to the Stadium, breaking by one stroke a course record of 90 that belonged to him, from TEG 11. It sealed the Green Jacket at +63, eight clear of Mullin. He had been the third-most-discussed man on the property for two days and never appeared to mind.

Neumann, whose tournament began with the worst round the Tour Course has ever seen, eagled the par-5 15th. Easiest hole on the Stadium, and the only eagle anybody managed all trip.

Patterson signed for 40 and won by ten. He set personal bests at both courses along the way, 96 at the Tour and 95 at the Stadium, which is the dullest available sentence about a man who made three double-figure holes.

Williams finished runner-up in both competitions with a personal-best gross total of +76 and individual bests at both venues — 84 at the Tour, 92 at the Stadium. He kept none of the silverware.

## The men, in brief

- **John Patterson** — Second Trophy, by ten, after sixth and fourth in his last two outings. Made 12, 10 and 10 in three consecutive rounds and won more comfortably than anyone in living memory. The 12 turned out to be an anecdote, not an epitaph.
- **Gregg Williams** — Led both competitions for two rounds off the best gross round and gross total of his career, and lost both in one Stadium afternoon. Trophy runner-up for the third time. Two course records, no pots, and a 9 and an 11 he'll be explaining for years.
- **Jon Baker** — Took the gross lead with a bogey at the 4th in Round 3 and never returned it, then broke his own Stadium record with the venue's first 89. The Green Jacket by eight, won while everyone else argued about Stableford.
- **David Mullin** — Retains the Spoon on 139, having been granted a three-round reprieve and handed it back in roughly twenty minutes on the final day. Jacket runner-up, and an 84 at the Tour that stands as his best there. A curious ledger, but honest.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Opened with 105, the worst round the Tour Course has recorded in ten TEG visits, and closed with the tournament's only eagle at the 15th. For the first time in two TEGs, the Spoon belonged to somebody else.
- **Alex Baker** — Held the bottom of the table from the 14th hole of Round 1 to the 17th of Round 3, then contrived to escape on the final day. A Stadium personal best of 93, thirteen better than his last visit, and the strange dignity of earning the Spoon without receiving it.
