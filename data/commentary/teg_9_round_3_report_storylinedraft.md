# Royal Óbidos Savages The Back Nine

## Alex Baker's 42-point round is the best of the day and the second-best of his own TEG career, arriving despite a quintuple bogey at the 14th and an eight-shot bleed through holes 10-12.
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker's third round at Royal Óbidos, played on the Sunday of TEG 3, produced 42 points on the Stableford card — the best score of the day and the second-best of his entire TEG career. It came in spite of a stretch that could have wrecked the round entirely. Between the 10th and 12th, Baker bled eight shots, carding triple bogeys on the 10th and the par-5 11th before a double bogey on the 12th, one of the harder holes on the course at stroke index 2. Two holes later, at the 14th, he ran up a quintuple bogey 9 for a blank on the Stableford sheet. Yet the rest of his card was strong enough to absorb both blows and still deliver a career-best-adjacent total. The round also gave Baker a course personal best at Royal Óbidos, 13 shots better than his last visit. In the Trophy race, John Patterson stayed on top, though the gap at the summit tightened from 10 shots to 3.

## Jon Baker's round split violently in two — a serene front nine undone by a ruinous stretch through holes 10-12.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Jon Baker's third round at Royal Óbidos read like two different rounds stitched together. He opened in fine form, going three holes without dropping a shot from tee to green through the 1st to the 3rd, and by the 5th had banked 16 stableford points, built on a birdie at the par-5 2nd with only a single blemish, a bogey at the brutal SI-1 4th.

Then came the 10th to 12th. A double bogey opened the sequence, followed by a quadruple bogey 9 on the par-5 11th, and another double bogey to close it out — three holes without a single net par, eight gross shots bled in all, on a stretch that included the SI-2 12th.

The collapse turned a round that had promised something special into a modest 34 points and +22 gross, with a full ten-point gap between his front and back nine — comfortably his most lopsided nine-hole split of the day.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket lead survives a quintuple bogey at the 10th, his worst hole of the tournament so far.
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

David Mullin's round 3 at Royal Óbidos had its rough patch, but it did not cost him his overall position. At the 10th, a par 4 with a stroke index of 8, he ran up a nine — a quintuple bogey and his worst hole of the tournament so far. It came without much warning, too: just earlier in the round he had put together his steadiest stretch of the week, playing the 2nd and 3rd without dropping a gross shot, a par followed by a birdie on the 3rd.

The blowup at the 10th did nothing to shift the wider picture. Mullin had carried a seven-shot lead into the round in the TEG 16 Green Jacket race, and he left it with the same seven-shot cushion intact. Whatever damage the nine did to his card, nobody behind him made the ground count.

## John Patterson's ten-shot Trophy cushion, built on a near-record 49-point round, shrank to just three after a 34-point comedown at Royal Óbidos.
<!-- storyline: race -->

John Patterson arrived at Royal Óbidos with a commanding ten-shot lead in the TEG 9 Trophy, built on a near-record 49-point round two. Round three brought him back to earth. He bled 8 shots over holes 2 to 4, taking a double bogey at the par-5 2nd and triple bogeys at the 3rd and 4th, the last of those on the course's hardest hole. Worse followed with two quadruple bogeys: an 8 at the 12th, one of the harder holes on the card, and a 9 at the par-5 18th to close out the round.

The damage added up to just 34 points, a 15-point fall from his round two total. It was still enough to keep him on top of the Trophy standings, but the cushion that had looked all but unassailable was down to three shots heading into round four. David Mullin, meanwhile, continued to lead the Green Jacket race, his advantage there untouched by Patterson's collapse.
