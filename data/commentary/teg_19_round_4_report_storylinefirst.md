# Sixteen Clear and Two to Spare

## Alex Baker takes the TEG 19 Trophy by 16, Jon Baker holds the TEG 19 Green Jacket by two, and Gregg Williams collects the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker finished the job at Monte Rei with 52 points, the third-best round in TEG history to date, and the TEG 19 Trophy by 16, 213 to John Patterson's 197. To all practical purposes it was settled by the fourth green. Fifteen points from the first four holes. A birdie at the 1st, a par at the 2nd, then three points even after a bogey at the 3rd, one of the hardest holes on the course. There was a great deal of golf left. None of it mattered. He closed with a par and a birdie at the last for an 87, two better than his previous best at Monte Rei.

His gross figures pointed the other way. Alex Baker's +15 was the worst of the five on the day, and he slid from second after Round 1 to fifth in the TEG 19 Green Jacket at +55. He won the Trophy by 16 while hitting more shots than anybody on the premises, which is roughly what winning looks like round here. That left Jon Baker holding the Jacket at +43, two clear of David Mullin and Gregg Williams on +45. Mullin had trailed by 11 at one stage, Williams by eight. Neither of them arrived. Jon Baker held on by doing nothing dramatic whatsoever, which in this field counts as a plan. Williams, meanwhile, inherited the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon from Mullin, ending on 168 and seven adrift.

## The Wooden Spoon changed hands three times in the first three holes at Monte Rei and then never moved again
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams and David Mullin arrived at Monte Rei on the Tuesday level at the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon. The first three holes of the final round decided which of them stayed there. Williams bogeyed the 1st and sat alone at the bottom. Mullin parred the par-5 2nd to draw back level, a tie rather than a change of occupancy. Then Williams took a double bogey at the 3rd, one of the hardest holes on the course, and was on his own again.

That was the end of the movement. Neither man troubled the other over the remaining fifteen holes. It took Williams three holes to acquire the Spoon and fifteen more to confirm he had no intention of handing it on. He finished the tournament on 168 Stableford points to Mullin's 175, a gap of seven where there had been none at the start of the day. The margin is the more awkward for its cause. Mullin's closing 78 was his best gross round at Monte Rei in three visits, beating his previous best there by seven strokes. Williams lost the Spoon to a man playing the golf of his life. No consolation was available, and none was offered.

## Four triple bogeys around Monte Rei, two of them on the 9th, the shortest hole and the easiest on the card
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Monte Rei's final round produced four triple bogeys, and two of them arrived at the same hole. The 9th is a par 3 carrying stroke index 18, the easiest on the card. It still took six from both David Mullin and John Patterson, for no points either time.

Mullin's was the more painful interruption. He had played the first eight holes in two under, six pars and birdies at the 6th and 8th, before the 6 at the 9th removed the outward nine's momentum in one unhurried transaction. John Patterson reached the same hole off a par, two birdies and another par. He walked off it in precisely the same condition.

Alex Baker's pair came back to back and earlier. A 6 at the short 5th, then an 8 at the 6th. One point between the two holes. He answered with a birdie 2 at the 7th for five points, as though nothing had occurred, and the subject was not raised again. Jon Baker waited longer: four straight pars from the 9th, then a 7 at the 13th.

## David Mullin's career-best 49 points and 78 gross at Monte Rei, the best round of the day on both counts
<!-- storyline: round -->

**Mullin saves his best for last**

The Tuesday round at Monte Rei gave David Mullin the best golf of his four days: 49 Stableford points and a gross 78, six over par. The points figure is a personal best. The 78 is his best score at Monte Rei on three visits, seven strokes better than the last time he played it and well inside his previous course-best 85. It did not win him the day. 52 points was the best Stableford return in the field. But nobody bettered his six over on gross.

The round was built on the front nine. Mullin played the first eight holes without dropping a gross shot: five pars, birdies at the 6th and 8th, and 26 points banked. The 9th has already been dealt with. He added nine more in pars at the 11th, 12th and 13th, and closed with three straight pars for another nine.

It left him tied second in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket on 45 over, two behind Jon Baker, and clear of the Wooden Spoon he had carried into the round. He had passed it to Williams on the 3rd. Williams gave it no opportunity to leave.
