# One Point at Monte Rei

## How Alex Baker turned a 43-point opening round at Quinta da Ria into a one-point win, surviving a 10 and an 8 in R4
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

Alex Baker won the TEG 19 Trophy with 157 points, one clear of Gregg Williams. He then spent the final round doing his level best to hand it back.

The foundation was poured at Quinta da Ria. He eagled the par-5 11th, then the par-5 16th, and took the outright lead at the ninth hole of the opening round. He held it for 62 holes. That run survived a sextuple-bogey 11 at the last in R2 and a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 3rd in R3, both at Monte Rei.

The final round was worse. Another 8 at the 3rd. Then eight shots dropped across holes 9 to 11, a 7, a 6 and a 5. Then eleven more across 13 to 15, including a septuple-bogey 10 at the par-3 14th. He arrived at that tee leading a golf tournament. He left it with a 10 and no visible change of expression. It was an act of magnificent indifference to his own interests.

Gregg Williams, who had trailed by as many as 11 points and sat fifth after R1, kept closing. At the 17th he made a par, and the lead was his outright at last. Alex Baker had taken an 8 there.

A birdie at the par-5 16th had already ended a long run of bogey-or-worse. At the last he made a par, worth three points, and took the lead back. He kept it. Gregg Williams finished on 156, second in the Trophy. It stands as the third-best Trophy total of Alex Baker's career.

## Seventeen eagles, all of them on par 5s, plus two all-time scoring records and a record-equalling birdie streak of two holes
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Seventeen eagles were recorded across the four days. Every one of them came on a par 5. The par 5s were the only holes surrendering anything, and they surrendered generously.

Quinta da Ria produced seven of them on the Saturday, most clustered on two holes. The par-5 11th yielded four: David Mullin, Alex Baker, Jon Baker and John Patterson all got down in three. The par-5 16th gave up three more, to Patterson, Alex Baker and Gregg Williams. Patterson's pair, the 11th and the 16th, two different holes on the same course, were the standouts of the opening round.

Monte Rei then took over. In Round 2 the par-5 2nd was eagled by Mullin and Jon Baker, the par-5 8th by Mullin, and the par-5 16th by Williams and Jon Baker. Round 3 added four more: Patterson and Mullin at the 16th, Williams and Mullin at the 8th. Williams eagled the Monte Rei 8th again in the final round, his third eagle on that hole in three days. The hole had stopped putting up a fight. Mullin finished with five eagles for the tournament, Williams four.

The short-game numbers followed. Williams recorded 13 birdies or better across TEG 19, an all-time TEG record. In the final round he also made birdies at the 5th and 6th, a two-hole run that equals the all-time TEG birdie-streak record. Patterson matched it later the same afternoon at the 16th and 17th.

## David Mullin sets all-time TEG records for eagles and birdies in one tournament and finishes fourth of five
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Mullin's record book and the holes that undid it**

David Mullin made five eagles across the four rounds of TEG 19. No player has managed that in a single TEG. He added 13 birdies or better, also an all-time TEG record. The first eagle came at the par-5 11th at Quinta da Ria in Round 1. He then took the par-5 2nd and the par-5 8th at Monte Rei in Round 2, and returned the following day to eagle Monte Rei's 8th again along with the par-5 16th.

It bought him fourth place out of five. Mullin finished on 150 Stableford points, seven clear of Jon Baker and seven adrift of Alex Baker's winning 157.

The other half of the card explains the gap. At Quinta da Ria's 15th in Round 1 he ran up an 11. Seven over par on a par 4. It is the worst hole against par of his career, and it dropped him to joint bottom of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon race alongside John Patterson. In the final round at Monte Rei he made a quadruple-bogey 7 at the par-3 9th, then another quadruple bogey, an 8, at the 15th. A different hole on a different course from the Round 1 disaster. The same zero points.

His gross golf still ranked second in the TEG 19 Green Jacket, 368 strokes, behind Gregg Williams.

## Monte Rei's par-3 7th, stroke index 8, quietly ruins four players across three consecutive rounds
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

Monte Rei's 7th is a par 3 with a stroke index of 8. Nominally a hole of middling difficulty. By the end of the trip it was the most destructive thing on the property.

Its most consequential work came in Round 2. Jon Baker arrived holding the TEG 19 Green Jacket lead, held outright since the 5th hole of Round 1 at Quinta da Ria, 20 holes in front. He took eight, a quintuple bogey, for no Stableford points. Gregg Williams took six, a triple, and that proved sufficient. The gross lead changed hands on a hole neither of them had played. Jon Baker took it back two holes later at the 9th, but the card remembered. Alex Baker, playing it in the same round, also made eight.

Round 3 offered no clemency. Alex Baker made seven, a quadruple bogey. Gregg Williams matched him with a seven of his own. Both walked off with nothing.

Round 4 completed the sequence. Alex Baker made eight again, his second quintuple bogey of the tournament at that hole, and a third consecutive blank there.

Across three rounds on the Monte Rei 7th, Alex Baker played the hole in 14 over par and scored no points at any of the three attempts. For a short hole rated only eighth-hardest on the card, it operated well above its grade.

## Gregg Williams goes +27, +14, +13, +12 to win the Green Jacket by fourteen after starting the tournament bottom of the field
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Williams turns a last-place start into a fourteen-shot Green Jacket**

Gregg Williams won the TEG 19 Green Jacket with 354 strokes, +66. He finished fourteen clear of David Mullin on +80. It is the best gross total of his career.

Nothing about the opening round suggested it. Williams went round Quinta da Ria in 99 on the Saturday. He ended the day last of the five in the Trophy standings and at the head of the Wooden Spoon table. From there the only available direction was upwards, and he took it at a walking pace.

The gross lead came to him at the 7th at Monte Rei in Round 2, ending a 20-hole run in front for Jon Baker, who had held it since the 5th in Round 1. Neither man earned it. Williams took a triple bogey at the par three, Jon Baker a quintuple. Jon Baker had it back by the 9th. Williams reclaimed the lead at the 2nd in Round 3 and was never headed again. His scoring came down with him: 85 in Round 3, then 84 on the final day, his best round of the four.

The gross total was built on par fives. He eagled the 16th at Quinta da Ria in Round 1. He eagled the 16th at Monte Rei in Round 2, a different hole on a different course. He then eagled the 8th at Monte Rei in both Round 3 and Round 4. In all he made 13 birdies or better across the tournament, an all-time TEG record, including back-to-back birdies at the 5th and 6th in the final round.

## Jon Baker eagles twice, posts the best round of R2, leads the Jacket, and still retains the Wooden Spoon by seven
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

Jon Baker's four days in the Algarve pulled in two directions at once. He led the TEG 19 Green Jacket for twenty straight holes, from the fifth at Quinta da Ria in the opening round to the seventh at Monte Rei the following afternoon. He also finished bottom of the TEG 19 Trophy on 143 Stableford points, collecting the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, seven behind David Mullin on 150.

The gross golf was genuinely good in places, which is the cruellest part of it. He eagled the par-5 11th at Quinta da Ria in the first round. He eagled twice more at Monte Rei in the second, the par-5 2nd and the par-5 16th, two different holes on the Nicklaus course. The 16th came inside a closing run of 19 Stableford points across holes 14 to 18: a birdie two at the short 14th, a bogey at the stroke index one 15th, the eagle three, then pars at the 17th and 18th. His 87 that day was his lowest gross round of the tournament.

The Jacket lead went at the par-3 7th at Monte Rei, where he took eight, a quintuple bogey, while Gregg Williams made six. Jon regained the lead two holes later and held it into the third round, losing it for good at the second hole there. He finished third in the Jacket on 380, 26 shots behind Gregg Williams.

Two eagles, the best round of R2, twenty holes at the head of the Green Jacket, and the Wooden Spoon by seven points. He has the receipts for a fine tournament and the trophy for the other kind.
