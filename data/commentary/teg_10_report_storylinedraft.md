# Two Records at Boavista: Alex Baker Wins the TEG 10 Trophy by Eleven

## How Alex Baker turned two runner-up finishes into the highest Trophy total in TEG history, taking the lead at Palmares and never handing it back
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**Alex Baker's record run**

Alex Baker won the TEG 10 Trophy with 184 points, 11 clear of Gregg Williams — the highest Trophy total recorded in TEG history, and comfortably his own best.

It was built round by round. At Palmares, a course new to the field, Baker posted a personal best of 45 points on the Praia/Alvor loop, with a personal-best gross round of +23. He went better the following day on Alvor/Lagos: 47 points and +22 gross, both personal bests again, and that despite a 10 at the par-five 13th, where he took five over par and scored nothing.

The lead changed hands during that third round. David Mullin had held the Trophy lead for 30 holes, from the 12th of round one, and lost it at the sixth at Palmares, where Baker's par drew him level. Baker was the leader after round three on points and did not fall behind again.

The final round at Boavista settled it. Baker scored 51 points, the best round anyone has posted in TEG to date, off a gross +17 that was another personal best. The middle of it did the damage: 21 points across holes five to ten, four pars and two bogeys against the strokes he was receiving. His gross total of +90 was also a personal best, closing a week in which almost every round he played was the best of his career.

## Stuart Neumann, holder of back-to-back Wooden Spoons, escapes a third only because John Patterson was worse, while posting 10s at the 18th in consecutive rounds
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Stuart Neumann arrived in the Algarve for TEG 10 with a decade of Boavista behind him — ten prior visits, a best gross of 82 set back in TEG 5 — and found the 18th hole an immovable problem. In round one at Boavista he ran up a 10 at the last, six over par at a stroke index 5 hole, for no points. Twenty-four hours later, on the first ever TEG visit to Palmares' Praia/Alvor loop, he did it again: another 10 at the 18th, another six over, another blank. Neither hole has an equal in his career. No single hole has ever cost him more shots against par than these two, and they came in consecutive rounds on different courses.

The Palmares round did not save its damage for the finish. He was five over at the 13th for a 10, five over again at the 14th for a 9, and three over at the 15th — thirteen shots bled in three holes, none of them yielding a net par. That was the more remarkable for what came immediately before it: par, bogey, par, par across the 9th to the 12th, 14 Stableford points in four holes, the sort of stretch that suggested the round was under control.

It closed at 105. His best of the week, a 98, waited until the return to Boavista in round four.

## Gregg Williams plays the best scoring golf of his TEG career, leads the Trophy outright in R3, and still finishes eleven points behind Alex Baker
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Gregg Williams arrived in the Algarve for TEG 10 and produced the best scoring golf of his career. His gross total for the week, +76, is the lowest he has recorded at a TEG.

The peak came in the second round, on the Praia/Alvor nines at Palmares — a course none of the field had played before. Williams signed for 48 points, a personal best, and his gross figure of +14 was also the best round of his career by that measure. The scoring was concentrated in a five-hole run from the 8th: pars at the 8th, 9th and 11th, a birdie at the 4th-stroke-index 10th and a bogey at the 12th brought him 18 points in five holes.

It had not started that way. On the opening day at Boavista he ran up a nine at the 18th, a par four, a quintuple bogey worth no points at all.

Boavista also produced the tidiest bookend. Williams had played the course ten times before this TEG, with a previous best gross of 97; in the final round he went round in 93, a course best, and ten strokes better than his last visit there.

His scoring in the TEG 10 Trophy was not, in the end, enough to beat Alex Baker.

## David Mullin's eighth Green Jacket, built on a Boavista course record in R1 and a gross lead Jon Baker twice out-scored but never seriously threatened
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**David Mullin takes the TEG 10 Green Jacket with the lowest gross total on record**

David Mullin won the TEG 10 Green Jacket at +51 gross, 12 clear of Jon Baker. It is the lowest gross total ever returned in a TEG, and Mullin's own best.

The tournament was effectively framed on the opening day. At Boavista, on his eleventh visit to the course, Mullin went round in 75 gross, +4 — the best gross round in TEG history and a new course record, beating the previous mark of 82 set across 50 prior visits. It was 23 strokes better than his last visit there. The same round produced 49 Stableford points, the second-best round in TEG history. Across the four days he recorded 6 birdies or better and 34 pars or better, both all-time TEG records.

The week was not flawless. Having scored 42 points in round two at Palmares - Praia / Alvor, Mullin dropped to 31 on the Alvor / Lagos loop the following day, a fall of 11, closing that round with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the par-four 18th.

Baker had the better of him once. His +10 gross at Palmares - Praia / Alvor in round two is the third-best gross round TEG has seen. Baker also finished with a personal-best gross total of +63, and set a Boavista personal best of 86 in round four, improving on his previous 90. Neither was enough to make the lead uncomfortable.

## The defending Trophy champion John Patterson finishes last in all four rounds and 32 points adrift, undone twice by the same hole at Boavista
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

John Patterson collected the TEG 10 Wooden Spoon, closing the week on 112 points after four rounds in the Algarve.

The damage came in single, outsized bursts rather than steady decline. Boavista's par-five 8th caught him twice: a 10 in the opening round, then an 11 in the final round, the highest score he has recorded on a par five. Both times he walked off with nothing on the card. In between, the 1st at Palmares — Praia / Alvor cost him a 10 in round two, and the 8th at Palmares — Alvor / Lagos another 10 in round three.

That third round contained the worst of it. Across holes 11 to 15 he went triple bogey, quintuple bogey, triple bogey, quadruple bogey, quadruple bogey — 19 shots dropped in five holes, five holes without a net par, and a single Stableford point from the whole stretch.

The return to Boavista offered no relief. Beyond the 11 at the 8th, he ran up an 8 at the 10th and a 9 at the closing hole, finishing on 21 points and 40 over gross. Both figures are the worst of his career to date, in a round and in gross terms.

His best gross of the week was the 102 he posted at Boavista in round one, on what was his first visit to a course the group has now played twelve times.
