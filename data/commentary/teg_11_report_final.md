# Baker v Baker: Jon Takes the Lot

The 13th at El Prat - Azul is a par 4, and on the Saturday afternoon Jon Baker made a par there. Nothing to report. A drive, a shot, two putts, the sort of hole a man plays without ever quite noticing he has played it.

With it, he lost a lead he had held for forty-five holes.

That is the arithmetic of a brother. Alex Baker, the defending Trophy champion, had drawn level at the 1st and gone outright ahead at the 13th while Jon was busy doing nothing wrong at all. The lead had stood since the 4th hole of the opening round on Thursday. It ended without a single misfired shot to blame it on.

What makes the moment worth dwelling on is who it happened to. Jon Baker arrived in Catalonia as the Green Jacket's most decorated runner-up: second in the gross in three of the last four TEGs, and never better than third on the Trophy in the same stretch. Standing in his way was David Mullin, eight Jackets to his name and back-to-back titles in hand. Mullin would go on to play the best raw golf of his TEG life over the four days. He would also finish the tournament holding the Wooden Spoon.

---

## Round 1 — Jon Baker Rewrites the Stadium

The Thursday round at PGA Catalunya - Stadium, TEG's second visit to the layout, was settled early and quietly. Jon Baker went to the top of both the Trophy and the Jacket at the 4th and stayed there, closing the day three points clear on Stableford and two shots clear on gross. Before that there had been four lead changes at the 3rd, 6th and 7th, with Mullin and John Patterson briefly level. Opening-hole jockeying, and nothing more.

The number that mattered was 90. It was worth 35 points and +18 gross, and it broke the Stadium course record of 94, set across five prior visits. Nine shots better than Jon had managed here last time. Records at TEG generally arrive by accident. This one looked researched.

Elsewhere the Stadium collected its usual dues. Gregg Williams made 8 at the short 5th, a quintuple bogey. Then the 13th began its work: Alex Baker took a quadruple-bogey 8 there, and Patterson followed him in with a 9. Patterson sank to the bottom of the Spoon race a hole later with a triple-bogey 7 at the 14th, and finished the day last on points, which by now he wears like a club tie.

---

## Round 2 — Two Course Records in Two Days

The Tour course is the Stadium's slightly more forgiving sibling, and on the Friday it duly gave up the best round of the tournament and the worst, within hours of each other.

Jon Baker shot 84: 41 Stableford points, +12 gross, and a new Tour course record, beating the 89 that had stood across five prior visits. Two days, two layouts, two records. He led the Trophy by two and the Jacket by three, and while nobody said so out loud, this was the round that paid for everything that came afterwards.

The par-5 15th was where the rest of the field went to be processed. Patterson made 11 there, a sextuple bogey and his worst hole of the tournament relative to par. Williams came through that same hole and made 10. Alex Baker, meanwhile, had already found a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 12th.

Patterson signed for 103. That exceeded the previous Tour course-worst of 101, set across five prior visits, which means the same course surrendered its record and its anti-record on the same afternoon.

Mullin, on 36 points, sat fourth and shot 85 — a personal best on the Tour, bettering the 89 he made in TEG 6. He was scoring beautifully. The table did not much care.

---

## Round 3 — Forty-Five Holes in Front, Then Along Came Alex

El Prat - Azul, Greg Norman's course out near Barcelona, was new ground for everyone. It was also the only day of the four on which anything genuinely changed hands, which is the sort of coincidence that makes a routing feel haunted.

The gross went first. Mullin drew level at the 3rd and took the lead outright at the 5th, where Jon made a perfectly serviceable par and lost the Jacket lead anyway, ending a 37-hole tenure that had run since Thursday's 4th hole. It lasted one hole. At the par-3 6th Jon made bogey and reclaimed it; Mullin made 7.

That quadruple bogey at the 6th is the hinge of the whole Green Jacket. Everything Mullin did afterwards, and he did a great deal, was spent getting back to level.

Jon then produced the best stretch of golf anyone played all trip: pars at the 7th and the 8th — the latter the hardest hole on the course — and a birdie at the par-5 9th, ten Stableford points in three holes. He gave a chunk back with doubles at the 11th and at the 12th, the easiest hole out there, and arrived at the 13th to find his brother waiting.

Alex went ahead. Jon parred. The 45-hole reign was over, and Alex took a two-point Trophy lead into the final day while Jon held the gross by three.

Mullin's own contribution at the 12th was a bogey, which dropped him from fourth to last in the Spoon race. He scored 29. He would not climb off the bottom again.

---

## Round 4 — Baker Beats Baker

Back at the Stadium on the Sunday, where the tournament had started and where Jon's 90 still stood as the course record, Mullin opened with three pars for nine points and drew level for the Jacket at the 3rd. For roughly forty minutes it was a proper contest.

Then Alex Baker dropped seven shots in three holes: double at the 4th, double at the short 5th, triple at the 6th. At that same 6th, Jon made a double bogey of his own and drew level on the Trophy. He never trailed again, and the manner of it is worth savouring — the tournament turned on a double bogey, because the alternative was worse.

Alex's afternoon then unravelled with a certain grim tidiness. A quadruple-bogey 8 at the 9th, the hardest hole on the course. Another 8 at the 17th. He signed for 27 points and +34, the worst round of his TEG career, and the two-point cushion he had carried overnight became a six-point defeat.

Jon, for his part, made 8 at the 13th — the hole that had already taken a quadruple bogey off Alex and a 9 off Patterson on the opening day. It cost him nothing whatsoever. He still returned 35 points and +19.

Mullin, needing three shots and getting none of them, dropped seven across the 15th, 16th and 17th: a triple bogey at the easiest hole on the course, then doubles at the last two. He shot 91, a personal best at the Stadium, bettering his 94 from TEG 6. He finished second in the Jacket by three and last on points with 125.

Williams made a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 10th and shot 99, fourteen shots better than his last visit here. Progress, at TEG, is often measured in wreckage avoided.

---

## How it was decided

- **Trophy (Stableford).** Jon Baker, 143 points, by six from Alex Baker on 137. Jon led from the 4th hole of Round 1, lost the lead to Alex at El Prat's 13th after forty-five holes in front, and drew level again at the Stadium's 6th in Round 4 with a double bogey while Alex was mid-way through dropping seven shots in three holes. He never trailed after that. Alex's closing 27 points was his worst TEG round to date.
- **Green Jacket (Gross).** Jon Baker, +70, by three from David Mullin. Jon led after every round, by 2, 3, 3 and 3, but the margin was built on two course records — 90 at the Stadium, beating the prior 94, and 84 on the Tour, beating the prior 89. The decisive hole was El Prat's par-3 6th in Round 3: Jon bogeyed it to retake the lead outright, Mullin made 7. It is Jon's first Green Jacket, and it ends Mullin's back-to-back run.
- **Wooden Spoon.** David Mullin, 125 points, from rounds of 29, 36, 29 and 31. Patterson was bottom after Rounds 1 and 2 and looked settled there. Mullin, fourth at halfway, dropped to last with a bogey at El Prat's 12th, the easiest hole on the course, and stayed there for the remaining twenty-four holes. Second in raw strokes, last on points: handicap arithmetic, coldly applied.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Jon Baker.** Three Jacket runner-up finishes in the last four TEGs, and never better than third on the Trophy in that time. He broke the course record at both PGA Catalunya layouts, surrendered a 45-hole lead to his own brother without hitting a bad shot, took it back with a double bogey, and left with the Trophy, the Jacket and both records. Bridesmaid no more.
- **Alex Baker.** The defending Trophy champion and the only man to take a lead off Jon all tournament. He held it into the final round by two, then made 8s at the 9th and the 17th on the way to 27 points, the worst round of his TEG career, and lost by six.
- **David Mullin.** Personal bests at both Catalunya courses, 85 on the Tour and 91 at the Stadium, and gross runner-up by three. He also finished dead last on points with the Spoon in his hands. Eight Jackets and counting has become eight Jackets and stopping.
- **John Patterson.** The reigning Spoon holder did everything a man could to keep it: an 11 at the Tour's 15th, three quintuple bogeys, and a 103 that set a new course-worst there. He was spared only by Mullin's decline, which is not the same as being spared by his golf.
- **Gregg Williams.** Three prior Trophy wins, and a quintuple bogey in three of the four rounds, including a 10 at the Tour's 15th. His closing 99 at the Stadium was fourteen shots better than his last visit to the course, which counts as a solid outing.
