# Four Ahead at the Third, Six Behind at the Last: The Baker Brothers in Catalonia

## How the TEG 11 Trophy changed hands twice between brothers: Jon Baker's 45-hole lead lost at El Prat, then Alex Baker's four-shot final-round lead surrendered in eleven points
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**The lead that moved, and moved back**

Jon Baker had held the TEG 11 Trophy lead since the fourth hole of the opening round — 45 holes in front, spanning three rounds — before it slipped away at El Prat's Azul course on the Saturday. Alex Baker drew level at the first, where even a bogey was worth three points, then took the lead outright at the 13th with the middle of a run of three straight pars for nine points across holes 12 to 14. Jon Baker was going the other way over the same ground: double, double, triple at 11, 12 and 13, three holes without a net par. The two finished the round tied at the top of the Trophy, with Jon Baker leading the TEG 11 Green Jacket.

The final round back at PGA Catalunya's Stadium course undid Alex Baker entirely. He posted 27 points, the worst round of his TEG career to that point, and 34 over gross. Seven shots went in three holes from the 4th, eight more from the 12th, and eight again over the closing three, including a quadruple-bogey eight at the 17th to match the eight he had taken at the 9th. Jon Baker had his own quadruple bogey, at the 13th, and it cost him nothing. He won the Trophy on 143 points by six.

## John Patterson's four rounds of demolition: two nines, an eleven, a course-worst 103, and a Spoon dodged by ten points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's week in Catalonia was defined by the holes that got away from him. The opening round on the PGA Catalunya Stadium course produced four of them: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 4th, a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 10th, another 9 at the 13th and an 8 at the 17th, none of which returned a Stableford point.

Round two on the Tour course was worse. At the par-five 15th he ran up an 11, a sextuple bogey and the highest score relative to par he has recorded in a TEG round. It contributed to a gross 103, a new course-worst at PGA Catalunya Tour, beating the previous mark of 101 set across five earlier visits.

El Prat's Azul course offered a mixed day. He took a 9 at the 16th, but earlier gathered nine points in three holes with bogeys at the 8th, 9th and 10th — his best sustained run of the week.

The closing round back on the Stadium course followed the same pattern. He dropped seven shots across the 7th, 8th and 9th without a net par among them, then eight more between the 12th and 14th, before finishing with back-to-back pars at the 16th and 17th for a 99. Despite it all, having trailed the TEG 11 Trophy lead by 16 points at one stage, he closed to within eight by the finish.

## Gregg Williams, fourteen shots better than his last visit to the Stadium and still detonating one hole per round
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Gregg Williams: better and still combustible**

Gregg Williams arrived at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course for his third visit and left with a course personal best. His 99 in the final round of TEG 11 was fourteen strokes better than the 113 he had posted there in TEG 6, the only previous time he had played it. On the Tour Course he matched rather than beat his history: 92 in round two, exactly the number he shot at his last visit. El Prat's Azul Course was new ground, and he got round it in 95.

What did not change was the one hole per round that went badly wrong. In round one at the Stadium he took eight at the par-three 5th, a quintuple bogey. On the Tour Course he ran up a ten at the par-five 15th. At El Prat he made eight at the par-four 5th. And in the final round, in the middle of that career-best 99, he took nine at the par-four 10th.

That last one came inside the worst passage of his week. From the 8th through the 11th at the Stadium on Sunday, Williams played four holes in eleven over par: double bogey, double bogey, the nine, then another double. Removing those four holes would have left him with a considerably lower number, but the improvement stands regardless.

## Jon Baker's gross week: two course records in two days, the Jacket lead held after every round, and the end of David Mullin's run of Green Jackets
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Jon Baker's gross week**

Jon Baker won the TEG 11 Green Jacket at +70, three clear of David Mullin, and he did it on the back of two days that rewrote the record books at Camiral. His opening 90 gross on the PGA Catalunya Stadium Course was a new course record, taking five strokes off the previous mark of 94 set across five prior visits. On the Friday he did it again on the Tour Course, going round in 84 to beat the standing record of 89. Both were personal course bests by a distance: nine strokes better than his previous best of 99 on the Stadium Course, six better than his 90 on the Tour.

The lead was not held throughout. Baker took it at the fourth hole of round one and kept it for 37 holes, across three rounds, before Mullin took over at the fifth on the Saturday at El Prat's Azul Course, a course neither man had played before. Baker's answer came immediately after: three holes without a dropped gross shot at seven, eight and nine, capped by a birdie at the par-five ninth, worth ten Stableford points.

Mullin opened the final round with three pars for nine points and posted 91 on the Stadium Course, his own best there, but the three strokes stayed with Baker. At +70 it is the third-best gross total of Baker's career.

## David Mullin takes the TEG 11 Wooden Spoon by ten points while finishing Jacket runner-up with new personal bests at two of the three courses
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

**David Mullin: the Spoon, and two course bests**

David Mullin finished bottom of the TEG 11 field, collecting the Wooden Spoon with 125 points across the four rounds in Catalonia.

The scorecard was not the flat line that a last place implies. On the Tour Course in round two he took 10 points from holes 8 to 10, closing the run with a birdie at the par-five 10th, and his 85 there was a personal best on the course, four strokes better than the 89 he had posted on his only previous visit in TEG 6. The Stadium Course produced the same pattern: a round-four 91, three shots inside the 94 he managed at the same venue in TEG 6, and another course best.

The damage came in bursts. El Prat's Azul Course, new to the group in round three, caught him at the par-three 6th, where a 7 went down as a quadruple bogey and a blank, and again over the stretch from 11 to 15, five consecutive holes without a net par that cost him seven points against his handicap. The final round followed the same shape in reverse: three straight pars from the 1st for nine points, then seven gross shots dropped over 15, 16 and 17 to finish. The Spoon was settled long before that.
