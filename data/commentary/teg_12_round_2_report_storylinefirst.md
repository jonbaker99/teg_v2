# Two Personal Bests, No Movement

## Stuart Neumann answers a ruinous opening round with 44 points and a personal best at PGA Catalunya Tour, the best round of the day by any measure on the Trophy metric
<!-- storyline: round -->

Stuart Neumann opened his second round at PGA Catalunya Tour with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st. The 1st is stroke index 14, which is to say one of the gentler holes on the property. He took an 8 there regardless.

What followed was less a recovery than a rebuttal. He gained 13 Stableford points across the 3rd to the 6th, capped by a birdie at the 6th that also completed two holes without a gross shot dropped. A second such stretch arrived at the 8th and 9th. The round then threatened to fall over again on 14, 15 and 16, where a double bogey, a triple and another double cost him seven shots and three points. It was not enough. The card had already gone too far to be argued with.

Neumann signed for 44 points, the best score recorded by anyone in the 2019 TEG Trophy's second round, and top of the field of six by some distance. The day's next-best was 36. It also stood as a course personal best for him at PGA Catalunya, improving his best gross score there this TEG to 93. Gregg Williams retained his lead in both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, untroubled by any of it.

## Alex Baker's round comes apart between the 6th and the 9th, eleven shots gone in four holes, and leaves him four adrift at the foot of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's second round at PGA Catalunya Tour began promisingly. He picked up 12 Stableford points across the 2nd to the 5th, built on pars at the 2nd and the 5th, with bogeys at the 3rd and the second-hardest hole on the course, the 4th. He was, briefly, playing golf.

Then the 6th. A double bogey there dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race, and the bottom proved comfortable. He went four holes without a net par. A triple at the 7th, a triple at the short 8th, a triple at the 9th. Eleven gross shots handed over across those four holes, without any noticeable change in his demeanour.

A birdie at the 10th finally stopped the bleeding. He found another decent spell later too, taking 9 points from the 13th to the 15th, including a bogey at the 13th, the hardest hole on the course. None of it counted for anything where it mattered. He stayed the race's laggard, and the gap to the next-worst player in the field grew from one shot to four. Some men lead from the front. Baker leads from the back, and he is pulling away.

## David Mullin posts the best gross round of the day, 84, his personal best at PGA Catalunya Tour by a single shot
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

David Mullin carded the best gross round of the day on Friday at PGA Catalunya's Tour Course. An 84, and a personal best at the venue, beating his previous mark of 85 by one shot across five visits. Five visits, one shot. The margins of a patient man.

The round turned on the 5th to the 8th. Four straight pars, not a gross shot dropped, 12 Stableford points collected, including a par at the 8th, one of the harder holes out there. The run ended at the 9th with a triple bogey and a blank Stableford return. The rest of the card held firm enough to deliver the low gross of the day, which after years of near-misses at this course counts as a debt settled.

## The TEG 12 Trophy lead changed hands five times across eighteen holes and finished exactly as it started, Gregg Williams one point clear of John Patterson, with the TEG 12 Green Jacket still deadlocked
<!-- storyline: race -->

The TEG 12 Trophy lead moved five times during round two at PGA Catalunya and settled nowhere. John Patterson drew level at the 4th. He took it outright at the 12th, was pegged back to a tie at the 13th, and lost it outright at the 15th. He levelled again at the 16th. He then closed with three straight bogeys worth 3 points each, nine points across the final stretch.

The 15th deserves its own mention. Patterson made a quintuple-bogey 10 on the par 5. It cost him the outright lead and, one suspects, some quiet interior scaffolding. All that movement, and the final margin did not shift by a single point. Gregg Williams ended the round exactly as he had started it, one clear of Patterson.

The Green Jacket behaved much the same way. Williams drew level on gross at the 10th with a birdie, and level again at the 18th with a closing par. The gap stood at zero, as it had all along. By the close of play Williams led both the Trophy and the Jacket, and the table looked precisely as it had that morning. Eighteen holes, six men, considerable exertion, no consequence.
