# Fifty, and Never Headed

Littlestone sits out on the Romney Marshes doing very little to advertise itself. No clubhouse grandeur, no Open Championship plaque, no reason for anyone to drive there on purpose. On the Saturday of TEG 13 it gave up the two best rounds of the tournament and one of the finest afternoons of scoring in the history of the thing.

Jon Baker went round in 79, eight over, the second-best gross round TEG has ever recorded. In the same weather, on the same marsh, John Patterson signed for 49 Stableford points, the third-best round in TEG history, off a gross of 83 and a personal-best +12.

The two of them finished the day fourteen shots apart in the Green Jacket.

That is the honest shape of this tournament, and it needs saying early: the golf produced numbers nobody had seen before, and none of it altered the result by a syllable. Baker took the Trophy lead at the 2nd hole of Round 1 and, apart from a two-hole excursion at Prince's, kept it until somebody handed him a trophy. Kent was TEG's first visit to the county. Four links, four inaugural outings for every man in the field, four blank pages in the record book with no course records on them to chase.

So he set a different one instead.

## Round 1 — Deal or No Deal: Baker Nine Clear, Patterson Signs for 108

Thursday at Royal Cinque Ports, two Open Championships on its CV and a stiff opinion about visitors. TEG's first ball here was struck at this venue and the scoreboard split in half almost immediately.

Baker played the opening three holes in par, par, birdie at the par-5 3rd: ten points from three holes, and the tone set before anyone had settled. He signed for 85, thirteen over, and 39 points. That was nine clear in the Jacket and six in the Trophy, and he had been in front since the 2nd.

Patterson opened with an 8 at the 1st. By the 2nd he was bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, which is a position that usually requires several hours of dedicated work. He added a 9 at the 7th and another at the par-5 16th, and his 108 was the highest gross round anyone posted all tournament.

Alex Baker's afternoon turned on the 11th, SI 3 and playing like it, where he took 8.

David Mullin began 7-5-6-5 — triple, bogey, bogey, double — seven over through four holes, and then birdied the par-5 5th, the second-hardest hole on the course. The eight-time Green Jacket winner was still in there somewhere.

## Round 2 — Baker Blinks at 16, Birdies the 18th

Friday at Prince's, Sandwich, in the Dunes/Himalayas configuration. This was the only genuine contest of the four days, and it lasted two holes.

Baker bogeyed the 4th, double-bogeyed the 5th, then birdied the par-5 6th and looked broadly untroubled. Gregg Williams, three Trophies already on the shelf and third in the Jacket in each of the last three TEGs, drew level at the 15th. At the par-3 16th, where Baker made par for three points, Williams went in front outright.

That ended a run of 32 consecutive holes in the lead, stretching back to the 2nd hole of the opening round. Williams held it for two holes.

Baker birdied the par-4 18th and took the lead back. He was never headed again.

The round gave him 36 points and an 88, sixteen over, worth a Trophy lead of one and a Jacket lead of seven. Patterson, meanwhile, dropped eleven shots across the first four holes: 8, 5, 8, 6, on a par-4, a par-3, a par-5 and a par-4 respectively. He later took 8 at the 17th and finished bottom of the Spoon race for a second consecutive round. Mullin took 8 at the 9th and slid, quietly, in a manner nobody was really watching.

## Round 3 — Littlestone, Little Resistance: 44 and 49 on the Marsh

Saturday on the marsh. Littlestone was laid out by William Laidlaw Purves, who also drew Royal St George's, and later tidied by Braid and MacKenzie. Between them they produced the most obliging golf course of the trip, and the field responded by having the round of its collective life.

Baker's 79 was the lowest gross of the tournament and the second-lowest anyone has ever returned in TEG. The heart of it ran from the 8th to the 11th: par, birdie at the par-3 9th, par at the 10th — SI 1, the hardest hole on the course — and par at the 11th. Thirteen points from four holes. He took 44 for the round.

Williams had drawn level at the 1st, the easiest hole on the course, and spent the rest of the day watching that arrangement dissolve. Baker finished five clear in the Trophy and fourteen clear in the Jacket. Williams shot 86, which on most days of most TEGs is a fine afternoon's work and here bought him nothing.

Then there was Patterson. Forty-nine points, the third-best round in TEG history, off 83 gross and a personal-best +12. This is the same man who had signed for 108 four days' golf earlier and been last in the Spoon race by the 2nd hole of the tournament. He is less a golfer than a weather front: you don't assess him, you take shelter and wait to see what he does to the coastline. He also took 7 at the par-3 17th, because the front had not entirely passed through.

At the other end, Alex Baker took 8 at the 2nd, added a double at the par-3 9th, and dropped to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race for the first time. He finished the round there.

## Round 4 — Shore Thing: Baker Doubles Up, Mullin Spoons at the 10th

Sunday, back to Prince's, this time on the stronger Shore/Dunes routing — the same venue as Round 2, a different and less forgiving lap of it.

Patterson opened with a 3 at the par-5 2nd. It was the only eagle recorded by anyone across the whole tournament. He later took 9 at the par-5 15th, restoring the balance of nature.

Baker's closing round was a professionally dull 85, thirteen over, for 39 points. He bogeyed the par-5 15th and double-bogeyed the 16th, one of the easiest holes on the course, then birdied the par-3 17th to tidy up. No lead change was recorded anywhere in the final round, in any competition, until the 10th.

That was Mullin's. A 9 at the par-4 10th, SI 3, and after three and a half rounds of finishing 3rd, 3rd and 4th on the Trophy metric, he was suddenly last for the first time in the entire tournament, with eight holes left to do something about it. He did not do something about it. The final ledger read 135 points: a third consecutive Wooden Spoon and a fourth in total, for the most decorated gross golfer TEG has ever produced.

Alex Baker, who had spent two rounds bleeding shots in clusters, closed with 89 — seventeen over, a personal best, and his finest round of the trip. It was the only meaningful piece of family business he was permitted all tournament.

Baker's 85 completed a gross aggregate of +50. There were no course records in Kent to aim at, because nobody had ever been to any of these places.

He beat the whole of TEG history instead.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy (Stableford).** Jon Baker, 158 points (39-36-44-39), six clear of Gregg Williams. He led at the end of all four rounds and held the lead from the 2nd hole of Round 1. Three lead changes, all in the middle two rounds: Williams level at Prince's 15th and in front at the 16th, Baker back in front with a birdie at the 18th two holes later, Williams level again at Littlestone's 1st. The birdie at Prince's 18th was the decisive takeover. Nothing changed hands after Littlestone.
- **The Green Jacket (Gross).** Baker, wire to wire, no lead changes at all: nine ahead after Deal, seven after Prince's, fourteen after Littlestone, eighteen at the finish. The 79 in Round 3 was the round that ended it. His +50 is the best gross aggregate in TEG history, and this is his third Green Jacket in succession.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** David Mullin, 135 points. Patterson was bottom after Rounds 1 and 2, Alex Baker after Round 3; Mullin arrived last only at the 10th hole of the final round, via a 9. Third consecutive Spoon, fourth overall.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Jon Baker** — Arrived on back-to-back Green Jackets and left with a third, plus the Trophy by six and the best gross total anyone has ever posted. Headed for two holes in four days, at Prince's 16th and 17th, and he fixed that himself with a birdie at the last.
- **Gregg Williams** — Three Trophies already, and third in the Jacket for three TEGs running. Here he was the only man in the field to get his hands on the lead, at Prince's 16th and again at Littlestone's 1st, and his reward was second place in both the Trophy and the Jacket. A promotion, technically.
- **John Patterson** — The defending Trophy champion produced the tournament's worst round (108 at Deal), its best (49 points at Littlestone, third-best in TEG history), a personal-best +12 gross and the only eagle anyone made. He was last in the Spoon race by the 2nd hole of the tournament and escaped it entirely.
- **David Mullin** — Eight Green Jackets on the shelf, and a slide of 3rd, 3rd, 4th, 5th that only became terminal at the 10th at Prince's Shore. The 9 there put him last for the first time all tournament, with eight holes to play, and he stayed put.
- **Alex Baker** — Two rounds spent haemorrhaging shots in clumps, including an 8 at Deal's 11th and another at Littlestone's 2nd, which left him bottom of the Spoon race after Round 3. He answered with a personal-best 89 at Prince's Shore, and watched his brother take everything else.
