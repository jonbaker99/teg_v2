# Mullin Completes the Double as Baker's Lead Turns to Dust

## David Mullin completes the Trophy-Jacket double as Jon Baker's seven-shot lead disintegrates and Gregg Williams inherits the Wooden Spoon.
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin completed the Trophy and Green Jacket double at TEG 4, only the second time in TEG history that one player has taken both titles in the same edition, after the first at TEG 2. The Trophy race still had a twist left before that was settled. Overnight leader Jon Baker took the lead further with a birdie on the par-3 second at Bletchingley, but it lasted only a hole: Mullin levelled with a bogey on the third that counted as net par, then reclaimed the lead outright at the par-3 12th — one of the easiest holes on the course — with a net birdie. He held it the rest of the way, finishing the round eight shots clear in the Trophy and nineteen ahead in the Jacket race.

At the bottom of the field, the Wooden Spoon changed hands. Henry Meller dropped to last place with a quintuple bogey on the tough par-5 11th, only for Gregg Williams to leapfrog him with a quadruple bogey on the sixth and a triple bogey on the 13th, inheriting the Spoon by five shots.

## Jon Baker eagles the 8th to lead the Trophy by seven, then loses it by the close.
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin completed the double at Bletchingley, taking both the 2011 TEG Trophy and the 2011 TEG Green Jacket — only the second such double in TEG history, the first having come at TEG 2. He was helped along by a spectacular collapse from Jon Baker.

Baker had taken the outright Trophy lead with a birdie at the 2nd, then extended it to seven shots with an eagle at the par-4 8th. It did not last. Between the 9th and the 15th he bled 27 gross shots, running up a sextuple-bogey nine at the par-3 12th — the easiest hole on the course — before topping that with an 11 at the par-5 15th, his worst hole of the tournament. Seven holes passed without a single net par along the way. Having arrived off a three-under round three, Baker closed with a nine-over round four, and the lead he had built vanished entirely. Mullin finished the day eight clear at the top of the Trophy.

## Gregg Williams's Wooden Spoon-clinching round is the worst gross round recorded in TEG history to date.
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Gregg Williams's round four at Bletchingley Golf Club produced the worst gross round recorded in TEG history to date: a 43-over-par horror show worth just 15 points, and it handed him the 2011 TEG Wooden Spoon. Williams's card collapsed in two separate stretches, bleeding 13 shots across holes 4 to 7, including quadruple bogeys at the 5th and the 6th, then bleeding a further 16 shots across the closing six holes, a run that featured a triple bogey, a quadruple bogey and four more dropped-shot holes in succession.

Henry Meller had troubles of his own on the same course. He ran up quintuple bogey 10s at both the 11th and the 15th, and went four holes without a net par from the 8th to the 11th. That collapse had dropped him to the bottom of the Spoon race, but Williams's even heavier round then overtook him. The laggard's spot changed hands, with Williams now sitting five shots adrift of the next player up.

## David Mullin's unremarkable final round is nonetheless the best round of the day, and the one that wins the tournament.
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin closed out TEG 4 by winning both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, a double that had happened only once before in TEG history, at TEG 2.

His final round at Bletchingley was nothing spectacular, but it was the best round of the day, with the field's best Trophy score of the day standing at 15. Mullin began the round level for the Trophy lead after a bogey at the par-5 3rd, then settled into pars at the 4th, 5th and 6th, including one at the 5th, the hardest hole on the course. That steady run ended abruptly at the par-3 7th, where a quadruple bogey wiped out three holes of gains in one stroke. It made no lasting difference. By the 12th he had drawn level again and moved into the outright Trophy lead with another par, one he did not relinquish. He finished the round eight clear in the Trophy standings, and with the Green Jacket already his, completed the double.
