# Two Tens and a Trophy: Williams Wins Lisbon by Twelve

The first came at Penha Longa's 12th, a par 5 rated stroke index 17, which is the course's polite way of saying it is one of the holes it intends to give away. Gregg Williams took ten. The second arrived the following afternoon at Quinta da Marinha's 11th — a different course, a different hole, an identical number in the box.

He won the Trophy by twelve.

That is the whole of TEG 8 in one sentence, and the rest of this report is an attempt to explain how it can possibly be true. The answer is that Stableford is a forgiving creditor. It sends the bailiffs round for one hole, seizes the contents, and then hands you a fresh account on the next tee with no questions asked. Gross keeps a full ledger and charges interest. Which is why David Mullin, the only man in the six who never once made a double-figure score across four days, won the Green Jacket, and why Williams, who made two of them, never came close to it.

Six players, three courses on the Lisbon Coast, and a tournament defined less by good golf than by the wipeout hole: the sextuples, the nines, the tens, arriving with the regularity of a public transport timetable. At the bottom of it all, Stuart Neumann began assembling the Wooden Spoon at the fourth hole of the tournament and never once put the work down.

## Round 1 — The Ledger Opens at Quinta da Marinha

Wednesday, on Robert Trent Jones Sr.'s parkland, a course no TEG player had seen. Within three hours the two defining trajectories of the trip were already fixed.

Williams posted 38 points, best in the field, and took the outright Trophy lead at the 10th. Note that hole. It was the only lead change the Trophy produced in 72 holes; for the remaining 62, nobody so much as drew level with him. He led by four at the close, which at the time looked like an opening skirmish.

Henry Meller, the defending Trophy champion, opened his account by making a 10 at the par-5 3rd. It was enough to install him, briefly, at the very bottom of the Spoon race, a neighbourhood in which he owns three previous properties. He was evicted at the 4th by Neumann, who moved in and stayed for the rest of the tournament.

Neumann's afternoon deserves the slow, careful treatment given to structural surveys. From the 7th to the 15th, nine consecutive holes, he shed 29 shots. Doubles at the 7th, the easiest hole on the course, and the 8th, the hardest. Then triple, quad, quad, quintuple, double, quintuple, double. He signed for 17 points and 114 gross, +43 — both the worst he has ever recorded, and the worst return anyone made at Quinta da Marinha across the two visits.

Alex Baker, arriving as the reigning Wooden Spoon holder, produced 30 points and a ten-point gap between his nines. He dropped 14 shots over the last four holes: double, triple, a 9 at the 17th and an 8 at the last.

The Jacket, meanwhile, changed hands four times before the round was out — Jon Baker level at the 6th, Mullin level at the 10th, Williams level at the 11th — all of it routine early jockeying between men still learning where the fairways were. Mullin took it outright at the 13th and led by one.

## Round 2 — Eleven Clean Holes, Then a Ten

Thursday in the Sintra hills, at Penha Longa, laid out by the son of the man responsible for Quinta da Marinha. Billed as the hardest course of the trip, up and down a mountain, it produced the lowest gross round anyone played all tournament.

Williams went eleven holes without dropping a single net shot. Bogey, bogey, bogey, a par at the SI 2 4th, bogey, par at the par-5 6th, par at the short 7th, a double at the 8th, bogey, par, a double at the SI 3 11th. It was controlled, unglamorous, entirely within himself.

Then he stood on the 12th tee, a par 5 the course was practically offering him, and made a 10.

He signed for 41 points. Best in the field, again. The lead went from four to eleven, and the tournament was, in all honest accounting, decided on a Thursday afternoon by a man who had just taken a quintuple bogey at one of the easiest holes on the property. Stableford charged him one blank box and moved on.

Mullin's answer was the round of the trip. Nine holes from the 4th to the 12th without conceding a net shot, including a birdie at the par-5 6th, the easiest hole on the course, and pars at the SI 2 4th and the 12th. He shot 86, +14, and led the Jacket by six at halfway. Nobody would beat that figure over the remaining two days.

## Round 3 — Everybody's Worst Day

Friday, back to Quinta da Marinha, now with the advantage of local knowledge. It made no difference whatsoever.

Neumann dropped 17 shots in the opening five holes. A 10 at the par-5 1st, doubles at the 2nd and 3rd, a 9 at the 4th, a triple at the short 5th. He later added an 8 at the par-3 9th and an 11 at the 10th, and finished with 20 points, bottom of the field, 14 adrift of Meller's 34. With one round to play, the Spoon had stopped being a contest and become an administrative formality.

Alex Baker's card is worth reading aloud. Triple, triple, triple at the 3rd, 4th and 5th. A bogey. A double. Then a 10 at the 8th, the hardest hole on the course. Double, triple, bogey, an 8 at the SI 2 12th, triple, bogey, double, triple at the 16th. At the 17th he made a birdie, his first hole better than bogey since the 6th, and somehow the whole ruinous exercise still came to 32 points.

It was three more than Williams managed. The leader dropped 14 shots in four holes — a triple at the SI 1 8th, a double at the short 9th, a 9 at the 10th and the second of his tens at the 11th — and posted 29, fourth of six. His lead fell from eleven to five.

That is the number to hold on to. Six players had their best chance of the tournament at a man playing his worst golf of it, and between them they clawed back six points.

Mullin, too, was suffering. His 26 points were the fewest he has scored in any TEG round, ten down on his Penha Longa 36, and included an 8 at the SI 2 12th. At that same hole Jon Baker drew level on gross, and at the 13th he took the lead outright, ending Mullin's run of 36 holes in front. Mullin levelled again at the 15th but Jon Baker closed out two clear, his 89 the best gross return anybody managed at Quinta da Marinha.

He had eighteen holes to enjoy it.

## Round 4 — Oitavos Dunes, and the Reckoning

Saturday on Arthur Hills's links beside the Atlantic, fully exposed, and the day produced a 25-shot spread between the best gross round and the worst. Both ends of it belong to this report.

Mullin retook the lead outright at the 2nd and that, effectively, was the Green Jacket. He made an 8 at the 11th and absorbed it without visible distress, bogeyed the 14th and 15th, then birdied the par-5 16th to end the argument. An 88, +17, eight strokes better than Jon Baker, who had thus been beaten by Mullin in three of the four rounds and finished second again. The Jacket went to Mullin at +77, six clear — a third in succession and a sixth in total, achieved without a single double-figure score anywhere in four days.

Williams simply refused to give anyone anything. He played the last ten holes without dropping a net shot: par at the short 9th, bogeys at the 10th and 11th, par at the 12th, bogey at the par-5 13th, pars at the 14th and at the 15th, the easiest hole on the course, bogeys at the 16th and 17th, and a double at the SI 2 last. Another 41. A third Trophy, by twelve.

Alex Baker went seven holes from the 8th to the 14th without conceding a net shot, then made a 9 at the 18th, because improvement in TEG is rarely tidy. He finished on 137 and second, having arrived holding the Spoon.

Meller's title defence expired somewhere around the 5th green. He dropped 22 shots in six holes: a double at the 1st, an 8 at the 2nd, a 6 at the short 3rd, a 10 at the 4th, a 9 at the SI 1 5th, another double at the 6th. He scored nothing at all at the 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 5th, four consecutive blank boxes. His 21 points were 13 fewer than his R3 34, and his 111 was the worst round of the day.

Neumann scored 32, comfortably his best of the trip, and was still last on the day. He was last on every day. The Spoon was confirmed at 100 points, eleven behind Meller, which is the cruellest part of it: Meller played worse than him on the final afternoon and still won by a distance.

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy (Stableford).** Williams took the outright lead at the 10th hole of R1 and led after every round: by four, eleven, five and twelve. It was the tournament's only Trophy lead change. He posted 38, 41 and 41, all best in field, either side of the 29 at Quinta da Marinha, and beat Alex Baker head-to-head in three of the four rounds. The decisive round was R2 at Penha Longa, where the 41 turned four into eleven. Final: 149 to Alex Baker's 137.
- **The Green Jacket (Gross).** Mullin led outright from R1 H13 for 36 holes, lost it to Jon Baker at R3 H13, and retook it outright at R4 H2 at Oitavos, which settled it. He won the final round by eight and the Jacket by six, +77 to +83, and was never worse than second on gross after any round. His 86 at Penha Longa was the lowest gross round of the tournament; Jon Baker's 89 in R3 was the best return at Quinta da Marinha.
- **The Wooden Spoon.** Neumann went to the bottom at the 4th hole of R1 and was bottom of the field in all four rounds. R1 gave him 17 points and +43, both career worsts. R3's 20 left him 14 behind Meller with one round remaining, and a 32 at Oitavos could not close it. Final: 100 points, eleven adrift.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Gregg Williams** — Two 10s, one wretched afternoon and a third Trophy, won by twelve. Three best-in-field rounds and a scoring system that billed him for exactly two holes and then reopened his account. Still nowhere on gross, which for him is now less a weakness than a settled way of life.
- **David Mullin** — Gross rounds of +20, +14, +26, +17, the Jacket at +77, and not one double-figure hole in 72. His R3 26 was the worst points round of his TEG career, and he responded by winning the final round by eight strokes. A third Jacket in a row, a sixth overall.
- **Alex Baker** — Arrived holding the Spoon, left as Trophy runner-up on 137. Between those two facts sit a 10 at the hardest hole on the course, an 8 at the 12th on the same afternoon, and a 9 at the last at Oitavos. Progress, of a sort.
- **Jon Baker** — Led the Jacket by two after R3, having taken it at the 13th, and lost it at the 2nd hole of the following morning. Beaten by Mullin in three of four rounds and second by six. His 89 at Quinta da Marinha was the best gross round anyone played there.
- **Henry Meller** — Defending Trophy champion. Opened with a 10 at the 3rd, scored 34 in R3 to look briefly respectable, then dropped 22 shots in the first six holes at Oitavos for 21 points and a 111. Finished eleven clear of the Spoon, which counts as a result.
- **Stuart Neumann** — Third on the Trophy in each of the last three TEGs; bottom of the field in every round of this one. Rounds of 17, 31, 20 and 32, a 29-shot bleed across nine holes in R1, and 17 shots gone in the first five of R3. The Spoon by eleven, and no dispute about it.
