# Mullin's Long Reign Ends at Boavista

## Henry Meller, anchored to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon table after two blow-up-heavy rounds, quietly produces the best golf of the day
<!-- storyline: round -->

Henry Meller arrived at TEG 3's third round anchored to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon table, his tournament so far defined by two blow-up-heavy rounds. On Tuesday at Boavista, he quietly put together the best golf of the day.

The turnaround was stark: a level-par round three followed a second round that had bled ten shots to par. The engine room was a run from the 3rd to the 5th, three holes played without dropping a single gross shot, each one a par worth a shot to his net score. It was the kind of stretch that had been entirely absent from his week so far.

It didn't last. A double bogey at the 6th, one of the harder holes on the course, ended the steady run as quickly as it had built. But the damage was contained, and the round stood as a rare bright spot. It narrowed his gap to the next-worst Spoon contender from fifteen shots to eight, without doing anything to shift him off the bottom of the table.

## Jon Baker and Stuart Neumann both close their rounds with identical quintuple-bogey nines at Boavista's 18th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Round three at Boavista finished with a strange piece of symmetry: Jon Baker and Stuart Neumann both closed with quintuple-bogey nines at the 18th, the two blow-ups mirror images of each other, right down to the identical result. Baker's was the second such nine of his round — he had already run one up at the 7th — a costly pair of collapses on a card that otherwise wasn't without promise. Neumann's arrived at the end of a wretched finish, having bled ten shots over the last three holes, with a double bogey at the 16th and a triple at the 17th setting up the disaster at the last. The 18th, a stroke index 5 hole, was no pushover, but two nines from two different players on the same green in the same round is still a rare coincidence. Neither man's history at Boavista suggests this was typical; both have shot far better gross totals here in the past. On this Tuesday, though, the closing hole had the final word for both of them.

## David Mullin's twenty-one-hole grip on the TEG 5 Trophy is broken by a three-hole capitulation, handing Gregg Williams a five-shot lead
<!-- storyline: race -->

David Mullin's hold on the TEG 5 Trophy lead had stood since the 18th hole of round one, twenty-one holes in front, but it unravelled in three holes at the start of round three. A triple bogey at the 2nd, then double bogeys at the 3rd and 4th, bled seven gross shots and left him without a net par across the stretch. Gregg Williams made his move at the 3rd, a routine par on the par-5 that was worth two shots net against par, and that was enough to take the lead outright.

By the end of the round Williams held the Trophy by five, having overturned a three-shot deficit, while Mullin's card never recovered — a quadruple bogey nine at the 13th compounded the damage on the way to his worst round of the TEG, twelve over. It followed a strong second round and marked a sixteen-shot swing in form. Mullin still leads the Green Jacket by fourteen, but for the first time all week he is chasing in the Trophy race.
