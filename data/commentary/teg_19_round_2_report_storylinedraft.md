# Records Fall on a New Course

## Gregg Williams's +3 gross at Monte Rei is the lowest gross round in TEG history to date, paired with a personal-best 50 points and a birdie at the 16th that took him to the top of the Green Jacket standings
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei, TEG's first visit to the Nicklaus course, belonged to Gregg Williams. His 75 was +3 gross — the lowest gross round in TEG history to date, and comfortably the best of the five scores returned, the worst of which was +18.

The middle of the front nine did most of the work: a birdie at the 5th, then an eagle three at the 6th, part of an 18-point haul across holes 4 to 8. He closed just as tidily, dropping no gross shots from the 13th to the 16th — including a par at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course — and picking up 13 points over those four holes.

The birdie at the 16th was the decisive one. It moved Williams from second to outright first in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, displacing Jon Baker, and he ended the day one shot clear at 15 over par to Jon Baker's 16. The Wooden Spoon place he held after Round 1 had already passed to David Mullin.

## John Patterson's 51 points, the third-best round in TEG history and the best of his own 34, built on an eagle two at the par-4 15th, Monte Rei's stroke index 1
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei gave John Patterson the third-best round in TEG history to date: 51 Stableford points, from a gross 82, ten over par.

The scoring came in two bursts. Through holes 10 to 12 he made three straight pars and collected 11 points, the handicap strokes turning steady golf into a haul. Then came the 15th, the hardest hole on the North Course, where he holed out for a two — an eagle on a par 4, worth six points on its own, and part of a two-hole stretch through 14 and 15 where he gained two shots on the card.

It was not enough to change the shape of the 2026 TEG Trophy. Patterson moved up to second at the 16th hole, but Alex Baker's lead grew from nine points to 13 across the round, and Patterson sits on 98, level with Jon Baker. Gregg Williams, meanwhile, posted a personal-best 50 points on the same day.

## What Monte Rei did to the three chasing positions: Jon Baker surrendered the Green Jacket lead by a single shot, David Mullin fell to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race, and Alex Baker's worst day of the week still widened his Trophy lead from nine to thirteen
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Sunday round at Monte Rei reshuffled every position behind the leader. Jon Baker had taken the 2026 TEG Green Jacket lead on the last green of R1, lost it at the 2nd when Alex Baker eagled the par-5, drew level again at the 3rd and was back in front outright with a birdie at the 5th, one of the easiest holes on the course. He held it until the 16th, when Gregg Williams went past — Williams had picked up three shots on par across the 4th to 9th, with a birdie and an eagle in that stretch. He now leads on 159 to Jon Baker's 160.

David Mullin dropped to the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon at the 2nd and stayed there. Fourteen points across 15-18, including a par at the hardest hole and a birdie at the last, still left him seven behind Williams.

Alex Baker's 49 points was 13 down on his R1 62, yet his 2026 TEG Trophy lead grew from nine to thirteen.
