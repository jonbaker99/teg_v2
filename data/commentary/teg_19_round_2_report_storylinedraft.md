# Monte Rei Gives With One Hand

## Jon Baker's 44 points at Monte Rei, the best round of the day, containing two eagles, a quintuple-bogey eight and a closing burst of 19 points
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's Sunday at Monte Rei was the best round of the day — 44 Stableford points, eight clear of the field's worst, and eleven better than the 33 he posted in the first round. It was also nothing like a steady one. An eagle three at the 2nd was followed immediately by seven gross shots dropped in three holes: doubles at the 3rd and 4th, a triple at the short 5th. Worse came at the 7th, where a quintuple-bogey eight handed the 2026 TEG Green Jacket lead to Gregg Williams after Jon Baker had held it for 20 holes since the fifth hole of Round 1. He was back in front by the 9th.

A birdie at the 8th had stopped the run, and a second birdie, at the 14th, began the closing stretch that made the round: 19 points across the last five holes, including a second eagle at the 16th and pars at the 17th and 18th.

## The par-3 7th at Monte Rei, where all five players failed to make par and gave up twenty shots between them
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The par-3 7th at Monte Rei carries stroke index 8 — nothing on the card marks it as a problem hole. On the Sunday round it took twenty shots off the field. Not one of the five made par, and not one of the five took a Stableford point from it. Jon Baker and Alex Baker both ran up 8s, quintuple bogeys apiece; John Patterson made 7; David Mullin and Gregg Williams both took 6.

For two players the hole was the end of a longer slide. David Mullin dropped 15 shots across holes 4 to 7, including an 8 at the par-3 5th; John Patterson dropped 13 over the same stretch.

It also changed a competition. Jon Baker had led the 2026 TEG Green Jacket since the 5th hole of Round 1, twenty holes in front, and his 8 handed the lead to Gregg Williams. He took it back two holes later, at the 9th, and finished the round two strokes clear.

## Five eagles on Monte Rei's par fives in a single round, two of them by David Mullin in the middle of an otherwise ruinous day
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Monte Rei's par fives gave up five eagles in the second round, and three of the five players in the field collected at least one. The 2nd and the 16th did most of the damage: Jon Baker holed out for a 3 at both, Gregg Williams matched him at the 16th, and David Mullin took a 3 at the 2nd, backing it up with a par at the 3rd, the second-hardest hole on the course.

Mullin's other eagle was the strangest of them. Between the 4th and the 7th he went quadruple bogey, quintuple bogey, triple bogey, triple bogey — an 8 at the par-4 4th, an 8 at the par-3 5th, fifteen shots dropped in four holes and not a single Stableford point banked. He then reached the 8th in one under two shots for his second eagle of the day.

It did not save the round. Mullin signed for 94, finished last in the TEG 2026 Trophy standings, and took over the Wooden Spoon by four.

## Alex Baker's ten-point Trophy cushion cut to four, and David Mullin's fall from second overnight to last and sole possession of the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: race -->

Alex Baker kept his grip on the 2026 TEG Trophy at Monte Rei, but not his cushion. He dropped nine shots in three holes early — a triple bogey at the 3rd, one of the hardest on the course, a double at the 4th, a quadruple bogey at the short 5th — added a quadruple bogey 8 at the 10th, and closed with an 11 at the last, six over and the worst he has scored relative to par on any hole. He also slid from second to fifth in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. Jon Baker, ten adrift at one point, finished the day four behind.

David Mullin fared worse. Second in the Trophy after the opening round, he played the 4th through the 7th in fifteen over — 8, 8, 8, 6 — and ended the day fifth. His quadruple bogey 8 at the 15th, the hardest hole on the course, dropped him to the foot of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race on his own, four clear of Gregg Williams, who had taken that place three holes earlier.
