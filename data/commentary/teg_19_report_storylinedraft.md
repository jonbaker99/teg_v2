# Jon Baker Takes the TEG 19 Trophy, Gregg Williams the Jacket

## Jon Baker leads the TEG 19 Trophy after every round, builds a ten-point cushion at Monte Rei, then holds on through a six-point Round 4 swing
<!-- storyline: trophy -->

**Jon Baker wins the TEG 19 Trophy on 159 points**

Jon Baker took the TEG 19 Trophy with 159 Stableford points, four clear of Gregg Williams. He did not have it all his own way: through the opening round at Quinta da Ria the lead passed back and forth between Jon Baker and Alex Baker four times, and in Round 2 Williams took it outright twice before Jon Baker reclaimed it for good at the 16th.

That he did so at Monte Rei, a course TEG rates among the three hardest it has played, made the margin look larger than it was. Jon Baker closed Round 2 with four straight pars from the 15th for 14 points, dropping nothing to the card. In Round 3 he parred five in a row from the 11th — 16 points, including the 13th, where even a short approach rarely yields one, and the lakeside 15th — before a seven at the par-five 16th ended the run.

The cushion reached ten points, and Round 4 took six of them back. Three consecutive double bogeys at the 7th, 8th and 9th cost six shots and returned just three points, and an eight at the 17th brought nothing at all. Williams closed to four but no further. The TEG 19 Green Jacket went the other way: Williams won the gross on 346.

## The seventh at Monte Rei, a par three of over 230 yards, takes a ten from Alex Baker in Round 2 and a ten from John Patterson in Round 4
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**The 7th at Monte Rei takes its toll**

Monte Rei's 7th is a par three on the card and very little like one on the ground: over 230 yards from the back tee, a narrow channel of fairway onto the green, deep hollows waiting either side. Across the three rounds played there it produced two tens.

The first belonged to Alex Baker in Round 2 — a septuple bogey, ten shots, no Stableford points, and the worst he has made on a par three. It came at the hole where Gregg Williams took outright lead of the TEG 19 Trophy from Jon Baker.

John Patterson matched it precisely in Round 4: ten again, seven over, nothing on the card. It was his worst on a par three too, and he had company. David Mullin took six at the 7th, the middle hole of a run from the 6th to the 8th without a single net par. Jon Baker made five, the first of three straight double bogeys through the 9th that cost him six shots to par.

For a course rated the third hardest TEG has played, that is the hole doing the heavy lifting. The two men who made ten there finished level in the TEG 19 Green Jacket, both on 420 strokes.

## Alex Baker, the defending Trophy champion, swings from 29 points to 43 between Round 2 and Round 3 with a ten on the card in each
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Alex Baker: two tens, and fourteen points between them**

Alex Baker began the TEG 19 Trophy in credit. At Quinta da Ria in the opening round he took 13 points from holes 3 to 6 — bogey, bogey, par, bogey — and that run carried him to the outright lead at the fifth. He was top again after the 17th, before Jon Baker retook it at the last.

Then came Monte Rei, a Nicklaus layout that gave the field almost nothing all trip. In Round 2 Alex ran up a 10 at the par-three 7th, a septuple bogey and the worst he has recorded on a par three. He rallied for nine points across 12 to 14, but the card closed at 29 points and a gross 114.

Round 3, same course, different hole: another 10, this time a quintuple bogey at the par-five 2nd. What followed was the reversal of the tournament for him — 13 points from holes 6 to 9 (par, bogey, bogey, par) and a round of 43 points, fourteen better than the day before. His gross came down to 98, sixteen strokes inside Round 2.

Round 4 offered a quieter illustration of the same control: back-to-back pars at 13 and 14 without dropping a gross shot.

He finished third in the Trophy on 143, and tied with John Patterson at the bottom of the TEG 19 Green Jacket on 420, 132 over par.

## Gregg Williams leads the TEG 19 Green Jacket from the third hole of Round 1, is caught twice in Round 3, and answers both times
<!-- storyline: jacket -->

**Williams leads the Jacket wire to wire, and never lets it go**

Gregg Williams took the lead in the TEG 19 Green Jacket at the third hole of the opening round at Quinta da Ria, and the gross standings never changed hands again. He finished at +58 — 346 strokes — twelve clear of Jon Baker on +70, and the lowest gross aggregate of his own TEG career.

It was not serene. The third round at Monte Rei, a course that ranks among the three hardest the group has played, took him apart in the middle. After a bogey at the par-five 8th he ran up a triple at the short 9th and then three straight double bogeys at the 10th, 11th and 12th: nine shots gone in four holes, and four holes without a single net par, five points short of his handicap allowance over that stretch. A birdie at the 13th ended the run, and the lead survived.

The final round was steadier. Three pars in a row at the 4th, 5th and 6th brought him nine Stableford points, and he closed with a par at the 3rd-stroke-index 17th and a birdie at the 18th, the water-guarded par five that invites the second shot and punishes it. That gave him 83, his best score of the three rounds at Monte Rei.

The TEG 19 Trophy went elsewhere: Jon Baker won it on 159 points to Williams's 155.

## John Patterson takes the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon on 123 points, last in three of the four rounds, with three tens and a Round 3 that only delayed matters
<!-- storyline: spoon -->

John Patterson took the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon with 123 points, seven behind David Mullin. The position changed hands four times before it settled: Patterson dropped to the bottom at the third hole of Round 1, Mullin took it over at the 10th, Patterson reclaimed it at the 14th, Mullin again at the second hole of Round 2 — and then Patterson went back to last at the ninth that afternoon and stayed there.

The damage was done in large single numbers. At Quinta da Ria he made nines at the 7th and the 14th, both par fours. At Monte Rei in Round 2, where water is in play on eleven of the eighteen holes and little comes cheaply, he took ten at the par-five 6th, leaked twelve shots across holes 11 to 15, and closed with another ten at the par-five 18th for a round of 25 points.

Round 3 was genuinely better: a birdie at the 10th ended a long run of bogeys or worse, and 36 points represented an eleven-point improvement and a gross 97, his lowest of the three Monte Rei rounds. It only delayed the outcome. In Round 4 he was eleven over par after four holes, including a nine at the 2nd, then took ten at the par-three 7th — seven over, the worst hole relative to par of his career. Three points apiece at the 11th, 12th and 13th were undone by nine dropped shots from the 15th.
