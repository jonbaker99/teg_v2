# Two Personal Bests, No Movement

## Stuart Neumann answers a ruinous opening round with 44 points and a personal best at PGA Catalunya Tour, the best round of the day by any measure on the Trophy metric
<!-- storyline: round -->

Stuart Neumann's second round at PGA Catalunya Tour began badly, with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st, one of the course's easier holes at stroke index 14. He answered immediately, gaining 13 stableford points across holes 3 to 6, capped by a birdie at the 6th that also completed two holes without dropping a gross shot. A second such stretch followed at 8-9. The round threatened to unravel again on 14-16, where a double bogey, triple bogey and double bogey cost him seven shots and three points, but it was not enough to derail the card. Neumann finished on 44 points, the best score recorded by anyone in the 2019 TEG Trophy's second round and the top of the field of six by some distance over the day's next-best 36. It also stood as a course personal best for him at PGA Catalunya, improving his best gross score there this TEG to 93. Gregg Williams retained his lead in both the Trophy and the Green Jacket.

## Alex Baker's round comes apart between the 6th and the 9th, eleven shots gone in four holes, and leaves him four adrift at the foot of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Alex Baker's second round at PGA Catalunya - Tour began promisingly enough. He picked up 12 Stableford points across the 2nd to 5th, a stretch built on pars at the 2nd and 5th sandwiching bogeys at the 3rd and the difficult, second-hardest 4th. But it unravelled immediately after. A double bogey at the 6th dropped him to the bottom of the TEG 12 Wooden Spoon race, and from there he went four holes without a net par, taking a triple bogey at the 7th, a triple at the short 8th, and another at the 9th — eleven gross shots bled across those four holes. A birdie at the 10th finally halted the run, and he found another good spell later, gaining 9 points from the 13th to the 15th including a bogey at the brutal, hardest hole on the course, the 13th. None of it was enough to climb off the bottom of the Spoon table. He remained the race's laggard, and the gap to the next-worst player stretched from one shot to four.

## David Mullin posts the best gross round of the day, 84, his personal best at PGA Catalunya Tour by a single shot
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

David Mullin carded the best gross round of the day on Friday at PGA Catalunya's Tour Course, an 84 that stands as his personal best at the venue, edging his previous mark of 85 by a single shot across five visits there.

The round turned on a four-hole stretch from the 5th to the 8th, where Mullin reeled off four straight pars without dropping a gross shot, picking up 12 Stableford points in the process — a stretch that included a par on the 8th, one of the harder holes on the course. That run of steady golf ended abruptly at the 9th, where a triple bogey broke the streak and cost him a blank Stableford return. Even with that blip, the rest of the card held up well enough to deliver the low gross score of the day, a fitting reward given Mullin's history of near-misses at this course.

## The TEG 12 Trophy lead changed hands five times across eighteen holes and finished exactly as it started, Gregg Williams one point clear of John Patterson, with the TEG 12 Green Jacket still deadlocked
<!-- storyline: race -->

The lead in the TEG 12 Trophy swung back and forth all through round two at PGA Catalunya, changing hands five times without ever settling. John Patterson drew level at the 4th, took it outright at the 12th, was pegged back to a tie at the 13th and lost it outright at the 15th, then levelled again at the 16th before closing with three straight pars for bogeys worth 3 points each, nine points across the closing stretch. In between, at the 15th, Patterson ran up a quintuple-bogey 10 on the par 5. None of it moved the final margin: Gregg Williams closed the round exactly as he started it, one point clear of Patterson.

The Green Jacket followed a similar pattern without ever truly separating, Williams drawing level on gross at the 10th with a birdie and again at the 18th with a closing par, leaving that race still deadlocked, gap zero, after the round. By the end of the day Williams led both the Trophy and the Jacket, with the table back exactly where it began.
