## Jon Baker's 42 points on TEG's first visit to Monte Rei, bookended by three opening pars and four closing ones, with Gregg Williams trading the Trophy lead with him all afternoon
<!-- storyline: round -->

The Sunday round took TEG to Monte Rei for the first time. It is a Nicklaus parkland in the East Algarve and it concedes nothing. Only two rated courses play harder for golfers of this standard. Jon Baker opened with three pars worth 10 Stableford points, the third of them a four-pointer at one of the hardest holes out there.

The 2026 TEG Trophy lead then changed hands repeatedly, with the solemnity of a key being passed between two caretakers who distrust each other. Gregg Williams went outright ahead at the 7th with a par. Jon Baker took it back at the 12th with another. Williams reclaimed it at the 13th despite a bogey. Jon drew level at the 15th, the mid-length par 4 that tracks a lake from tee to green and the hardest hole on the course, with a par worth four points. He moved clear at the 16th.

Then he parred the last four holes for 14 points, without dropping a gross shot. Finishing like that on a course like this is the equivalent of reversing a van into four consecutive bays and touching no kerbs. He signed for 42, the best Trophy score of the round. His overnight lead went from one point to five.

## Monte Rei's TEG debut: the third-hardest course TEG has rated taking its introductions from all five players in the form of nines, eights and sevens
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

The Sunday round was Monte Rei's first appearance in TEG. The Jack Nicklaus parkland on the East Algarve has water in play on eleven of its eighteen holes, and it introduced itself as the third-hardest of the 28 courses TEG has rated.

Alex Baker got the worst of it early. He made a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, then another 9 at the 2nd. A quadruple-bogey 7 followed at the 11th. Two holes into the course's TEG debut, his card already read like a utility bill after a cold January. Jon Baker was not spared either, taking a 7 at the par-3 7th.

David Mullin's trouble arrived in clusters. A 9 at the par-5 6th opened a run of three holes that cost him eight shots to par. Everything was played in the correct order and recorded neatly, and eight shots went missing regardless. A second 9 came at the 16th.

Mullin at least found an ending. He had gone a stretch without beating a bogey. He followed the 16th with a bogey at the 17th and then a birdie at the last, the short par 5 that bends round a lake. It was the only genuinely good news anyone got out of that stretch.

## The men who went backwards on Sunday: Alex Baker's 29 points and John Patterson's 25, which widened every gap in the TEG 19 standings without changing a single leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Monte Rei ranks third-hardest in TEG's rated set, and the bottom of the field felt all of it. Alex Baker was 14 over par after four holes: a 9 at the first, another at the second, then a triple and a double. At the 7th, the longest and meanest par 3 on the course, he signed for a 10. It is the worst he has made on a par 3. That was bad golf, played in the open, mostly near water. Three more holes from the 9th cost him nine shots. Then came a run of bogey-bogey-par from the 12th worth nine points in three holes, which was tidy, proper work and deserved better company. A quadruple bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes here, closed the door. He finished with 29 points.

John Patterson had 25, the lowest in the field. A 10 at the 6th started it. A triple at the 8th took him level with David Mullin at the foot of the TEG 19 Wooden Spoon, and a double at the 9th left him there alone. Five holes from the 11th leaked 12 shots, a rate of loss more usually associated with clearing a loft. He then filed a second 10 of the round at the 18th. No leader changed. The gaps did: Jon Baker's TEG 19 Trophy lead grew from one to five, and Patterson's Spoon margin from nothing to nine.
