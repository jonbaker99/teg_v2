For thirty-five holes Jon Baker held the Green Jacket lead. That is, from the 2nd hole of the tournament until the 1st hole of the third round, at which point he double-bogeyed a par 4 and handed the whole thing to David Mullin, a man with nine Jackets already hanging up.

Five holes later, at the par-3 6th at Royal Óbidos, Mullin made a par and went outright ahead.

It lasted one hole.

What followed was the best six-hole stretch of anybody's four days — 20 points from the 11th to the 16th — and an 86 that is the lowest gross score Royal Óbidos has ever surrendered. By the close on the Lisbon Coast, Baker had 166 Stableford points and the Trophy by 18, plus the Green Jacket at +67 by 13. Three TEGs running he had finished fourth on the Trophy. He did not finish fourth this time.

The other story ran along the same fairways in the opposite direction. Four rounds produced four course records: two of them the kind you mention at dinner, two the kind that appear in the book whether you mention them or not. Gregg Williams, the reigning Jacket holder and the last man to win the Trophy, arrived as the form pick and left with the Spoon.

## Round 1 — Twenty-Four Visits, One New Worst {.round1 .round}

The Saturday at Praia D'El Rey was TEG's fifth round at the venue, and familiarity is what gives the day its sting. Twenty-four prior player-visits had been logged here. The worst gross any of them had produced was 110.

Alex Baker signed for 114.

The damage was efficiently packaged. A quadruple-bogey 8 at the par-4 4th, a 10 at the 5th — SI 2, one of the hardest holes on the course — and a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 18th. Fifteen over par across three holes, and eleven shots worse than his own last visit here. The reigning Spoon holder, twice over, had begun in character.

He had company at the 5th, where Williams also took an 8, and Patterson opened his tournament with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 1st.

Elsewhere the day behaved itself. Mullin scored 33 with a thirteen-point split between his nines in favour of the back, the good half arriving in a run from the 12th to the 16th worth 16 points: par, birdie, par, bogey, par. Williams also took 33, sat third, and posted a 93 that was seven shots better than his last time round here — a detail worth holding on to.

Patterson led the Trophy by one from Jon Baker's 38. In the gross, Baker led from the 2nd hole until Williams drew level at the 17th, which was as close as anyone came to him for a day and a half.

## Round 2 — Mullin's 82, Patterson's 116 {.round2 .round}

West Cliffs, on the Sunday, was only TEG's second round on Cynthia Dye's 2017 links. Its record book was accordingly thin. Both ends of it were rewritten before the players had finished their sandwiches.

Mullin went round in 82. The previous record, 88, had stood across six prior visits; his own best there dropped by twelve shots in an afternoon. His scoring came in a four-hole burst from the 7th: bogey, par, birdie, birdie, 14 points. He then bogeyed the 11th and 12th, double-bogeyed the par-5 13th, bogeyed the 14th, and closed the sequence with a birdie at the par-5 15th.

At the other end of the same links, at the same time, John Patterson shot 116. The course's previous worst was 115. He was nineteen shots adrift of his TEG 15 visit here, and at +44 it was the highest gross round of his career.

The engine of it was eight holes from the 8th to the 15th, across which he dropped 27 shots. Double at the 8th. Triples at the 9th, 10th and 11th. Double at the par-3 12th. A quintuple-bogey 10 at the par-5 13th. A triple at the 14th. Then, at the par-5 15th, SI 16 and one of the easiest holes on the property, an 11 — his worst hole relative to par in TEG history. He had already taken an 8 at the 3rd, which is SI 1, so at least the difficulty ratings were being treated with even-handedness.

Meanwhile Jon Baker bogeyed the 1st, SI 17, and with that modest act took the outright Trophy lead. He did not surrender it for the remaining 71 holes. He then made 14 points in four holes — par, par at SI 1, birdie, par — and nine more from the 14th to the 16th, for a round of 44. His 2015 mark at West Cliffs was improved by fifteen shots.

Alex Baker, of all people, set a personal best here, built on 16 points from the 4th to the 8th: bogey, par, bogey, bogey, bogey. He also took quadruple-bogey 8s at the 10th and 11th, which is the sort of thing that happens on the way to a personal best when the personal best is yours.

Baker led the Trophy by two. Mullin drew level in the gross at the 18th. Level, not ahead.

## Round 3 — Lost at the 1st, Won by the 16th {.round3 .round}

Monday brought Royal Óbidos, Seve's last design, water where you would rather it were not, and TEG's fourth round on it. It is also where both trophies were settled.

Baker's double bogey at the 1st ended thirty-five holes of gross leadership. He drew level again at the 2nd. Mullin's par at the 6th put him outright in front. Baker was level again at the 7th, and in front of everybody from there to the end of the tournament.

Then, from the 11th: birdie at the par 5, bogey at the 12th (SI 2), birdie at the 13th (SI 18, the easiest hole out there), and pars at the 14th, 15th and 16th. Twenty points in six holes. He signed for 86 gross and 44 points.

Eighteen prior visits to Royal Óbidos had established the record at 87. Baker's 86 took it, and was also his own best here by three on his 89 from TEG 9, and eight shots better than his last visit. He led the Trophy by 11 and the Jacket by eight.

Behind that, Gregg Williams was compiling something remarkable in its own way. From the 12th he double-bogeyed seven consecutive holes to the last — 12th, 13th, 14th, 15th, 16th, 17th, 18th, fourteen shots gone at a rate of precisely two per hole. Not one of them varied. It had the air of an administrative process being seen through to completion.

The double at the 14th dropped him to the bottom of the Spoon race for the first time. He had already taken a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 5th. His round was 26 points, and he never got out of last place again.

Alex Baker managed four separate holes of 8 or worse: an 8 at the 4th (SI 1), a 9 at the par-5 5th, a 10 at the 14th and a 9 at the par-5 18th. His five holes from the 12th to the 16th cost fifteen shots.

Patterson took a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 11th, then found 13 points across the 12th to the 15th — par, par, bogey, par — and signed for 94, a personal best at Royal Óbidos. Two days after the highest gross West Cliffs has recorded, he played the best round he has ever played here.

## Round 4 — Nine, Nine, and Still Eighteen Clear {.round4 .round}

The Tuesday return to Praia D'El Rey was a formality, and Baker treated it accordingly. He double-bogeyed the 1st, bogeyed the 2nd and 3rd, birdied the 4th, and then contributed a quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 10th and another quadruple-bogey 9 at the par-5 17th, the hardest hole on the course. In between he took nine points from the 14th to the 16th. Forty points, +19, and he finished the day leading both competitions by more than he had started it.

Two nines in a round is normally a story. Here it was loose change.

The coastal dunes of the back nine were less forgiving to others. Alex Baker dropped 25 shots across nine holes from the 5th to the 13th: double, double, triple, triple, double, double, triple, then a 9 at the par-5 12th and an 8 at the 13th. It escapes being the defining collapse of the tournament only because of what Williams had done a day earlier.

Williams opened with a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st, scored 29, and finished on 123 points. Patterson closed with an 8 at the 1st, a sextuple-bogey 9 at the par-3 14th — SI 17, one of the easiest holes on the course, and his worst hole relative to par — a 10 at the 15th, and an 8 at the 18th.

## How it was decided

- **Trophy (Stableford).** Jon Baker won on 166 points, 18 clear of David Mullin. He was one behind Patterson after the opening 38, took the outright lead with a bogey at the 1st at West Cliffs, and held it for the remaining 71 holes. Back-to-back 44s at West Cliffs and Royal Óbidos turned two points into eleven; the closing 40 made it eighteen. The 166 is his personal best and the 9th highest total in TEG history.
- **Green Jacket (Gross).** Baker won at +67, 13 clear of Mullin. He led from the 2nd hole of Round 1, was drawn level twice, and lost the lead outright only at the Royal Óbidos 1st in Round 3, where a double bogey put Mullin ahead. Mullin's outright lead lasted from the 6th to the 7th. The course-record 86 gave Baker an eight-shot cushion, and the two nines in Round 4 cost him nothing that mattered.
- **Wooden Spoon.** Gregg Williams finished last on 123 points. Third after Round 1, fourth after Round 2, he fell to the bottom at the 14th at Royal Óbidos, mid-way through seven consecutive double bogeys, for a round of 26. A 29 in the final round confirmed it. It is his second career Spoon, and it arrives two TEGs after he won the Trophy.

## The men, in brief

- **Jon Baker** — Fourth, fourth and fourth on the Trophy in TEGs 14, 15 and 16, and then this: the double, a personal-best 166, the Jacket at +67, and the Royal Óbidos course record. Fifteen shots better than his last West Cliffs visit, too, in case anyone was still keeping a ledger against him.
- **David Mullin** — Runner-up in both, and the only man who ever had a hand on either. His 82 took twelve shots off his last West Cliffs round and six off the course record. He also led the Green Jacket outright, for one hole, on Monday.
- **Gregg Williams** — Arrived as the form man with a Trophy and back-to-back Jackets, and led the gross on level terms after the first evening. Seven doubles in a row at Royal Óbidos and a 9 to open the final round did the rest.
- **John Patterson** — Led the Trophy after Round 1 and set a personal best of 94 at Royal Óbidos, which deserves saying first. The 116 at West Cliffs, 27 shots dropped in eight holes, is the highest gross that course has recorded.
- **Alex Baker** — Escaped a third straight Spoon only because Williams sank faster. His 114 is the worst gross in twenty-four visits to Praia D'El Rey, he took four holes of 8 or more at Royal Óbidos, and he bled 25 shots in nine holes on the final day. He also set a personal best at West Cliffs, and nobody can take that away.
