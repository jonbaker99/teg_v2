# Patterson's 49 Turns the Trophy Upside Down

## John Patterson's 49-point round, the second-best total in TEG history to date, built on a front-nine rescue and a back-nine avalanche of points
<!-- storyline: round -->

John Patterson's second round at Praia D'El Rey produced 49 points, the second-best round total in TEG history to date, and it did not come easily. He opened with three straight bogeys through the first three holes, then compounded it with a run through holes 4-6 that cost him eight shots — a double bogey followed by two triple bogeys, including one on the 5th, one of the hardest holes on the course. That left him needing a response, and he found it immediately, birdieing the 7th to halt the bleeding.

From there the round turned into an avalanche. Patterson gathered 17 points across holes 9-13, including a birdie at the 12th, then closed with another 14 points over the final four holes. The finish transformed his tournament: having shot just 38 in Round 1, his 49 here was an 11-shot swing that vaulted him into the lead of the TEG 2 Trophy race, overtaking Jon Baker and opening a ten-point gap.

## Alex Baker, a day after his worst-ever gross round, posts a personal-best round, a personal-best gross score, and a new course record at Praia D'El Rey
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

A day after carding his worst-ever gross round, Alex Baker turned it around completely at Praia D'El Rey. Following a stableford total of 32 in Round 1, he jumped to 44 points in Round 2, a personal-best round, built on a gross score of +27, also a personal best for him.

The round was not without trouble. Baker bled 8 shots across holes 10 to 12, headlined by a quadruple bogey 9 at the par-5 10th, one of the easier stroke-index holes on the course, followed by a double bogey at the short 11th and another at the par-5 12th.

Despite that stretch, the rest of his card was strong enough to carry him to the best gross score of his three visits to Praia D'El Rey, nine shots better than his previous best here, and comfortably inside his best figures for the course this TEG.

## Praia D'El Rey's par-4 9th and short par-3 14th combine to wreck four separate scorecards across the field
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Round 2 at Praia D'El Rey turned into a horror show at two holes in particular, with four players wrecking their cards between the 5th and the 14th.

Gregg Williams took a sextuple bogey 10 at the par-4 9th, a stroke-index-4 hole that also caught out Stuart Neumann for a quadruple bogey 8. Neumann had already taken a quadruple bogey 8 at the 5th, one of the hardest holes on the course at stroke index 2, and his round unravelled to the point that he finished 21 strokes worse than his previous visit here. He remained anchored at the bottom of the 2016 TEG Wooden Spoon race, the gap behind him stretching from 8 shots to 14.

At the short par-3 14th, normally one of the easiest holes on the course at stroke index 17, both Alex Baker and Jon Baker ran up quadruple bogey 7s. For Jon Baker the damage was costly: he had led the Trophy race overnight but slipped 10 shots behind new leader John Patterson by the end of the round.

## Jon Baker's one-point overnight Trophy lead evaporates as a ragged middle nine drops him to third, ten adrift of the new leader
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker started the second round at Praia D'El Rey a point clear in the TEG 16 Trophy race, but the lead did not survive the middle of his card. From the 5th he went three holes without a net par, carding a double bogey at the 5th, another at the 6th, and a triple bogey at the par-5 7th — seven shots dropped in all. A brief recovery followed, three straight pars from the 11th, before a quadruple bogey at the short 14th ended that run as well.

While Baker unravelled, John Patterson took the outright Trophy lead with a bogey at the 1st and held it through to the end of the round. By the finish the gap between them had swung from Baker's one-point advantage overnight to a ten-point deficit, with Patterson now in front. Elsewhere David Mullin retained his Green Jacket lead, extending it from two points to seven over the same round.
