# The Dunes Take Their Cut

## Gregg Williams's 40 points and 88 gross at West Cliffs, the best round of the day by four points and six shots, on a course he had never seen
<!-- storyline: round -->

Gregg Williams produced the best round of TEG 15's second day, 40 points and a gross 88, on a West Cliffs layout he had never played before. He built the score on a strong middle stretch, running off four straight pars from the 4th through the 7th for 12 points, then adding two more pars at the 9th and 10th to extend his run without dropping a gross shot to six holes. The round was not spotless: he took a 10 at the par-5 15th, one of the easier holes on the course at stroke index 16, but recovered his composure to close out the best score of the day by four points and the best gross by six shots.

The round kept Gregg Williams on top of both the TEG 15 Trophy and the TEG 15 Green Jacket, a lead he had already held overnight. His advantage grew in both races, out to nine points in the Trophy and fourteen in the Jacket, with the round proving decisive in stretching gaps that had been far tighter before it.

## Stuart Neumann's back nine at West Cliffs, 26 points and 115 gross, taking the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon position off David Mullin at the 14th
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Stuart Neumann's second round at West Cliffs was a procession of blow-ups that cost him the bottom spot in the TEG 15 Wooden Spoon race. It began at the 4th, where he ran up a quadruple bogey 8, and worsened at the par-5 13th with a sextuple bogey 11. The damage that mattered most came at the 14th, one of the course's hardest holes, where a quintuple bogey 9 dropped Neumann to last in the Wooden Spoon standings, taking the unwanted position off David Mullin with the gap between them at zero. There was no recovery over the closing stretch: a double bogey at the 16th and another quintuple bogey, this time at the 17th, were followed by a double bogey at the 18th, nine shots bled across the last three holes. Neumann finished with 26 points, matching the worst Trophy score of the round, and a gross 115 in his first visit to West Cliffs.

## West Cliffs on its first appearance in TEG: the par-five 6th and the par-four 17th between them accounting for four double-figure scores, against one eagle
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

West Cliffs Golf Links, on Portugal's Lisbon Coast, made its TEG debut in round 2 of TEG 16, and two holes did most of the damage. The par-five 6th, stroke index 11, produced four blow-ups in one round: Alex Baker's 11 (a sextuple bogey, his worst score against par of the day), quintuple-bogey 10s from David Mullin and Stuart Neumann, and a quadruple-bogey 9 from John Patterson. Alex Baker's round unravelled further over the next two holes, a 9 and a 7 completing a stretch in which he dropped 13 shots without a single net par across holes 6 to 8.

The par-four 17th, stroke index 6, did similar damage later on. Alex Baker added a sextuple-bogey 10 there as part of an 11-shot bleed over the closing three holes, while Jon Baker went a shot worse with a septuple-bogey 11, his own worst hole of the round.

The only moment of real quality came at the par-five 15th, one of the easiest holes on the course, where David Mullin's eagle stood alongside a quintuple-bogey 10 from Gregg Williams on the same hole.

## John Patterson's chase of the TEG 15 Trophy: level with Gregg Williams after three holes, nine points adrift by the eighteenth, with David Mullin's Green Jacket deficit widening from eight to fourteen
<!-- storyline: race -->

John Patterson began the second round of TEG 15 five points behind Gregg Williams in the Trophy race, and made an immediate move to claw it back. A double bogey at the 1st and a bogey at the 2nd threatened another lost morning, but a birdie at the 3rd — the hardest hole on the West Cliffs course — drew him level with Williams for the Trophy lead.

It did not last. Holes 6 to 8 undid the recovery entirely, Patterson dropping eight shots across the three-hole stretch, including a quadruple bogey at the 6th, to hand the initiative straight back.

By the close of the round Williams still led both the Trophy and the Green Jacket, and the gaps had grown rather than shrunk: the Trophy deficit out to nine points, and David Mullin's Green Jacket gap widening from eight to fourteen. Patterson's brief share of the lead at West Cliffs proved the high point of an otherwise losing day.
