# Eighty-Two at Penha Longa: Williams Finally Gets the Jacket

Gregg Williams arrived on the Lisbon Coast with a ledger that read like a cruel joke. Three Trophies. Zero Green Jackets. Finishes of third, second, second and third in the last four TEGs, which is the golfing equivalent of always being asked to hold the coats. The Jacket itself had never been anywhere else: nine to David Mullin, three to Jon Baker, and a firmly closed door to everyone else who had ever swung a club in this tournament's name.

On the 1st tee at Estoril, on the opening morning, Williams made an 8.

What followed over the next four days was the quietest demolition in TEG history — 157 points, a Green Jacket by 29 shots, and a course record at Penha Longa that will take some shifting. At the other end, Alex Baker was already assembling something with a handle on it.

---

## Round 1 — Two Eights, Thirty-Nine Points: Williams Opens Five Clear

The Saturday round at Estoril took TEG to a new course, and not an unreasonable one: Portugal's first golf club, a short Mackenzie Ross parkland where the trees do the disciplining so the architect doesn't have to. The field met it with the patience of men who had been in a minibus.

Williams opened with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the par-4 1st. He closed with a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 18th, stroke index 16, one of the easiest holes out there. Between those two bookends he assembled 39 points and +17 gross, which is either a testament to the middle sixteen holes or an indictment of everyone else.

The early jockeying was exactly what early jockeying looks like. Mullin drew level at the 2nd in both competitions. Williams levelled the gross at the 4th and took the outright Trophy lead at the same hole. Jon Baker drew level on the Trophy at the 10th. Then, at the 11th, Williams went outright top of the gross list.

He stayed there for the remaining 61 holes of the tournament. Nobody knew that yet, including him.

Neumann's contribution was a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 15th, his worst score to par at that point in his career, and a hole he would have cause to remember when West Cliffs got hold of him. Williams finished the day five clear on the Trophy and eight on the Jacket. Alex Baker posted 30 points and was last on the round-by-round table, a position he would go on to occupy with the loyalty of a man who has paid for the seat.

---

## Round 2 — Three Elevens, Four Tens and One Eagle: West Cliffs Sorts the Field

The Sunday round at West Cliffs was also a TEG debut. Cynthia Dye's dunescape on the Silver Coast, voted World's Best New Course in 2017, an award presumably decided before this particular six-ball had been consulted. The field treated it as a punishment beach.

The par-5 6th alone was responsible for three separate incidents. Alex Baker took 11 there. Neumann took 10. Mullin took 10. Elsewhere, Neumann added an 11 at the par-5 13th, Jon Baker made 11 at the par-4 17th — the worst score to par of his recorded career — and Alex Baker made 10 at the same 17th. Three 11s, four 10s, and an afternoon that will be discussed in lowered voices for years.

Alex Baker's stretch through the sixth, seventh and eighth deserves its own paragraph, because it is a small masterpiece of accumulation: 11 at the par-5 6th, 9 at the par-5 7th, 7 at the par-4 8th. Thirteen shots dropped in three holes. He then played the rest of the round like a man who had left the gas on at home and could no longer concentrate on anything else.

The par-5 15th made the day's only eagle. Mullin holed out in 3 for five points, the single most competent thing anyone did all tournament.

Williams took 10 at that same 15th.

Patterson drew level at the top of the Trophy at the 3rd, which was the last time the leaderboard's front end had any business being interesting. Meanwhile the bottom of the table changed hands five times in eighteen holes — Mullin at the 2nd, Jon Baker at the 3rd, Jon Baker again at the 7th, Mullin at the 11th, Neumann at the 14th — a sort of hot-potato arrangement in which nobody wanted the potato and everybody kept catching it. Williams shot 40 points for +16, and moved to nine clear on the Trophy and 14 on the Jacket. Alex Baker scored 27 and was last for the round again.

---

## Round 3 — Eighty-Two: Williams Rewrites Penha Longa

Here is the thing about Penha Longa. TEG had been there before — the Monday round was the second TEG round at the venue, the previous visit being TEG 8 — and on that occasion the mountain course above Sintra had looked at Gregg Williams and handed him a 96. Not a disgrace. Just a number that said: this is not your place.

He shot 82.

That is a new Penha Longa course record, beating the prior record of 86 set across six previous visits. It was a personal-best gross round of +10. It was fourteen shots better than his last visit here. It was worth 44 Stableford points, which took his Trophy lead from nine to 18 and his Jacket cushion to 17, and it converted a golf tournament into a procession with fifteen holes still to play on the following day.

The mountain was in a giving mood generally. Jon Baker went round in 85, six better than TEG 8. Neumann managed 92, seven better than his previous effort. Mullin shot 85, a personal course best by exactly one stroke, which he will no doubt continue to describe as a personal course best.

Patterson found something too, and briefly it was magnificent. Across holes three to eight he collected 19 points: par at the 3rd, bogey at the 4th — stroke index 2, one of the hardest holes on the course — par at the 5th, par at the par-5 6th which is the easiest hole there, par at the par-3 7th for four points, and a bogey at the 8th. Six holes, 19 points, no visible explanation.

And at the very top of the round, on Penha Longa's par-4 1st, Alex Baker made a double bogey. It sounds like nothing. It was, in fact, the moment the Wooden Spoon was decided: it dropped him to outright bottom of the table, and he remained there for the last 53 holes of the tournament. He later took a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 14th and signed for 29 points, last for the round, again.

---

## Round 4 — Twenty-One Clear: Williams Completes the Double at Oitavos

With nothing left to settle at the top, the Tuesday round at Oitavos Dunes — the second TEG visit here, again after TEG 8 — turned its full attention to the people who were losing.

Patterson opened with a sextuple-bogey 10 at the par-4 1st. He followed it with a 6 at the 2nd and a 5 at the par-3 3rd, which is ten shots dropped in three holes and a first hour that cannot have improved the drive back. The man who had scored 19 points in six holes at Sintra had spent them all and could not raise a loan.

Mullin's day was worse, and historically so. The nine-time Green Jacket holder and defending Trophy champion posted 22 points, the worst round of his TEG career, and +33 gross, his worst gross round. The 104 was sixteen shots worse than the 88 he shot at Oitavos in TEG 8. There is no kind way to render this, so here it is plainly: the most decorated golfer in the tournament's history finished his week with the lowest round he has ever played.

Williams, wearing the mantle lightly, shot 91 for 34 points and +20 — an Oitavos personal best by two shots, which he collected almost absent-mindedly on the way to the car park. Jon Baker's 94 was also a personal best by two, and enough to secure the Green Jacket runner-up spot. Neumann was four shots worse than his TEG 8 round here and it did not matter in the slightest; his 136-point total was already good for second in the Trophy.

Alex Baker shot 103. It was a personal best at Oitavos by one stroke. It was also his fourth consecutive round at the foot of the table, and it completed a 114-point tournament and his second career Wooden Spoon. Both facts are true and only one of them will be mentioned at dinner.

The final margins: the Trophy by 21, the Green Jacket by 29.

---

## How it was decided

- **The Trophy (Stableford).** Williams led after every round — by 5, 9, 18 and 21 — for a total of 157 from rounds of 39, 40, 44 and 34. The four lead changes all came in the first two rounds and were all routine opening exchanges: Mullin level at R1 H2, Williams outright at R1 H4, Jon Baker level at R1 H10, Patterson level at R2 H3. The decisive round was the 44-point 82 at Penha Longa, which turned a nine-point lead into 18. Neumann finished runner-up on 136, the best finish of a TEG career that previously contained no wins and two Wooden Spoons.

- **The Green Jacket (Gross).** Williams won at +63 from rounds of +17, +16, +10 and +20, by 29 shots from Jon Baker. Three lead changes, all in R1, the last of them at the 11th at Estoril when Williams went outright top. He was never headed across the remaining 61 holes, the cushion going 8, 14, 17, 29. It is his first Green Jacket at the fifteenth attempt, and he is the first man to hold it who is not called David Mullin or Jon Baker.

- **The Wooden Spoon.** Alex Baker, 114 points, from rounds of 30, 27, 29 and 28 — last on every round-by-round table. Eight outright changes at the bottom, five of them in the R2 shambles at West Cliffs, but the one that stuck was his double bogey at Penha Longa's 1st in R3, which put him outright bottom for the final 53 holes. Second career Spoon.

## Player-by-player summary

- **Gregg Williams** — Three Trophies and never a Jacket, until now. Led both competitions from the 11th hole of the tournament to the last, won the Trophy by 21 and the Jacket by 29, and left a Penha Longa course record of 82 behind him — fourteen shots better than the 96 the same mountain gave him in TEG 8. He also made two quadruple-bogey 8s in his opening round and a 10 at West Cliffs' 15th, which tells you a great deal about everyone else.

- **David Mullin** — Arrived as defending Trophy champion with nine Green Jackets, left with neither and something worse. His week contained the tournament's only eagle, at West Cliffs' par-5 15th, a 10 at the par-5 6th on the same afternoon, and a Penha Longa personal best of 85 by a single shot. Then Oitavos gave him 22 points and +33 — the worst round of his TEG life, on both counts.

- **Stuart Neumann** — Two prior Spoons, no wins, and a 10 at Estoril's 15th and an 11 at West Cliffs' 13th inside the first two rounds. He finished Trophy runner-up anyway, with a Penha Longa personal best of 92 in the middle. The best finish of his career, achieved largely by remaining upright.

- **Jon Baker** — Came in holding the Spoon and spent parts of R2 holding it again, twice going outright bottom after an 11 at West Cliffs' 17th. He then shot 85 at Penha Longa, six better than TEG 8, added a personal-best 94 at Oitavos, and finished Green Jacket runner-up. A recovery, if a distant one.

- **Alex Baker** — TEG 14 runner-up, and here last on every single round-by-round table. Thirteen shots gone across three holes at West Cliffs, a 10 at Penha Longa's 14th, and outright bottom from the 1st hole of R3 onwards. His 103 at Oitavos was a personal course best, which is a strange thing to carry home with a Spoon.

- **John Patterson** — Drew level at the top of the Trophy at R2's 3rd hole and produced the purest golf anyone played all week: 19 points in six holes at Penha Longa. Oitavos then opened with a 10 at the 1st and took ten shots off him in three holes. He had given every one of those points back before most of the field had settled.
