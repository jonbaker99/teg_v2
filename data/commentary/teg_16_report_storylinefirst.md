# Sixth, Fifth, Third, Second, First: Neumann Never Gives It Back

## Stuart Neumann's first Trophy: 156 points, led after every round, won by 13

**Neumann takes the Trophy**

Stuart Neumann won the Trophy at TEG 16 on the Lisbon Coast with 156 Stableford points, finishing 13 clear of Gregg Williams. It is the first time he has won it. The total ranks 16th on the all-time list.

There was no late charge, because there was nothing to charge at. Neumann led the Trophy after the second round at Troia. He led it after the third at Penha Longa. He led it after the closing round at Estoril. He had taken possession of the lead the way a man takes possession of an umbrella left in a hallway, and at no point did anyone persuade him to give it back. Williams, for his part, headed the Jacket standings on gross at each of those same checkpoints, so the two competitions ran alongside one another for the four days without ever meeting.

The final round supplied the only genuine scare. At Estoril's 8th, a par 4 and the hardest hole on the card at stroke index 1, Neumann took nine. A quintuple bogey. Nought points. He then walked to the 9th tee still leading the tournament by a distance, which tells you rather a lot about the state of everyone else.

It was otherwise his best day of the four. His 92 at Estoril was a course personal best, 14 strokes better than the 106 he posted there at TEG 15. The other venues declined to cooperate: 97 at Oitavos Dunes against a previous best of 95, and 98 at Penha Longa against 92. At half the courses he was beaten by his own past self. The Trophy is decided on points, and on points he was never headed.

## Penha Longa, R3: the field comes apart on the mountain while two men keep their footing

**Round 3 — Penha Longa (Atlantic Course), Sintra**

Robert Trent Jones Jr.'s mountain parkland, on its third visit, dismantled the field through the middle of the back nine. Jon Baker suffered worst. A double bogey six at the 11th, then a 10 at the par-five 12th, a quintuple bogey on the easiest hole on the card at stroke index 17, then an eight at the 13th. Eleven shots gone in three holes. Not a net par among them. One Stableford point for the whole enterprise, which is the going rate up there.

Alex Baker took the identical route a few minutes behind, as though following signs. Six at the 11th, nine at the 12th, then sevens at the 13th and 14th: twelve shots dropped across four holes, with three consecutive holes from the 12th producing no net par. David Mullin's damage arrived later, an eight at the 16th, the hardest hole on the course at stroke index 1.

Gregg Williams walked through all of it without appearing to notice. He parred the 11th, 12th, 13th, 14th and 15th, five holes without dropping a gross shot, and the last three returned three points each, nine in all. His 87 was five strokes off his previous best here of 82. On this evidence it was more than sufficient.

The round left Stuart Neumann atop the Trophy standings, level on Stableford with the man immediately behind him, while Williams carried the Jacket lead into the closing round at Estoril.

## David Mullin: 30 points at Penha Longa, then the lowest round Estoril has ever seen

David Mullin arrived at Estoril off the back of a thoroughly unhelpful third round. Penha Longa gave him 30 points and a gross 98, thirteen strokes worse than his previous visit to the Sintra mountain parkland, with the damage concentrated at the 16th, where a quadruple-bogey eight on the toughest hole on the card returned nothing whatsoever. He closed with pars at the 17th and 18th, in the manner of a man tidying a room he has already set fire to.

The final round at Estoril, the Mackenzie Ross parkland being played for only the second time, was another business altogether. Mullin signed for 45 points, a swing of 15 on the previous day, and a gross 80. That is the lowest round anyone has recorded at Estoril, beating the previous mark of 86 set across six earlier visits. It is also comfortably his own course best, his only prior trip round here, at TEG 15, having produced a 94.

It was not flawless. He birdied the 2nd and parred the 3rd, then dropped shots at the 4th, 5th and 6th, the last a double bogey at the par five. The bleeding stopped at the 7th, where a birdie three brought four points. The middle of the back nine did the heavy lifting: a bogey at the 11th, the second-hardest hole, then pars at the 12th and 13th, worth nine points in three holes. Pars at the 16th and 17th saw him home.

## Gregg Williams's Green Jacket: +66, the best gross round in the field on all four days, won by 16

**Williams takes the Jacket by 16**

Gregg Williams won the Green Jacket at TEG 16 with a gross aggregate of +66, finishing 16 shots clear of runner-up David Mullin. It is the fifth-best Jacket score on record and the best Williams has posted, and the margin meant the thing was decided long before the final round: he already held the Jacket lead after the third, while Stuart Neumann led the Trophy on Stableford.

The rounds themselves were not uniformly tidy. Williams opened with 89 at Oitavos Dunes, two better than his previous best there, then 94 on his first visit to Troia. Penha Longa was his messiest day. A triple bogey at the 3rd and a double at the 4th, answered at once by a birdie at the par-three 5th, then a worse sequence still through 6 to 8: bogey, triple, double, three holes without a net par. He then played 11 to 15 in level par and collected nine Stableford points across 13, 14 and 15, having apparently decided the emergency was over. His 87 was five strokes above his previous best on the course.

The closing round at Estoril was his lowest of the four, an 80, six shots better than his only previous visit and a course best. He achieved it while also running up an eight at the 14th, a quadruple bogey on a stroke index 4 hole, an act of serene disregard for a lead he no longer needed to protect. The cushion absorbed it without comment.

## Alex Baker retains the Wooden Spoon — 127 points, bottom of the race from the first hole, and two all-time course-worsts

Alex Baker's tournament was settled early and never once looked like being unsettled. He finished on 127 Stableford points to collect the Wooden Spoon, and the tone was established on the very first hole of the trip: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 1st at Oitavos Dunes. Some men need nine holes to find their level. He needed one.

Another 9 followed at the 11th and a sextuple-bogey 10 at the 17th, and his 113 gross became a new Oitavos course-worst, exceeding the previous mark of 111 set across 12 prior visits. It was also ten strokes adrift of his own best there, a 103.

The middle rounds offered nothing resembling relief. At Troia he dropped 13 shots across holes 13 to 17, a run of three triple bogeys and two doubles. At Penha Longa he bled 12 shots over four holes from the 11th, including a quadruple-bogey 9 at the 12th, and travelled from the 12th to the 14th without a net par.

Estoril then produced the second course record of the wrong sort. Baker signed for 107, beating the prior worst of 106 from six previous visits, and eight strokes worse than the 99 he managed on his only earlier trip there. He opened with a 9 at the 1st, added an 8 at the 3rd and a 9 at the 6th, and leaked 14 shots between the 5th and the 10th. The 12th brought an 11, a septuple bogey and his worst return on a par 4. Two of the four courses now hold their worst-ever round in his name. He did not have to work especially hard for either.
