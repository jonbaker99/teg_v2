# The Stadium Gives In

## David Mullin, bottom of the Trophy and holding the Wooden Spoon at breakfast, shoots 84 gross for a new PGA Catalunya Stadium course record and 44 Stableford points
<!-- storyline: round -->

**David Mullin**

David Mullin took breakfast on Monday as the last-placed man in the 2025 TEG Trophy and the holder of the 2025 TEG Wooden Spoon. Two points of daylight stood between him and the player above. By the end of the afternoon at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course, he had rewritten the course's record book.

His 84 gross is the lowest score any player has shot here. The previous mark was 89, set at some point across 27 prior visits. It had survived a great deal. It did not survive Monday. The 84 is also comfortably Mullin's own best at the course, where his previous best was 91, and eight strokes better than he managed on his last visit.

The round was built not on brilliance but on abstinence. These were holes he simply refused to spoil. He opened with three straight pars, at the 1st, 2nd and 3rd, for nine points, and one of those was at the opening tee, among the hardest holes on the course. Pars at the 5th and 6th followed. The best of it came around the turn. A par at the short 8th, then a bogey at the 9th, the hardest hole out there and still worth three points off his handicap, then a birdie at the 10th. Ten points in three holes.

The 44 Stableford points were the best in a field of five, where the lowest return was 28. The Spoon duly changed hands. Jon Baker now holds it, 14 points adrift of the next man up.

## Alex Baker shoots 100 gross — seven worse than his last visit here — and still takes 44 points and extends his Trophy lead
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

**Alex Baker: a 100 that did the job**

Alex Baker went round PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course in 100 strokes on Monday. That is seven more than he took on his last visit here. He walked off with 44 Stableford points, the highest Trophy score returned by anyone in the five-man field in round three. The handicap strokes were asked to perform heavy manual labour, and they did so without complaint.

The damage came in the middle. Between the 6th and the 10th he dropped twelve shots in five holes. A triple bogey at the 6th. Doubles at the 7th, 8th and 9th, the last of those the hardest hole on the course. Another triple at the 10th. Those five holes yielded eight points between them, which is generous of the format.

Then it turned. From the 11th to the 16th he collected 21 points in six holes, built on pars at the 11th, 13th and 14th and bogeys at the 12th, 15th and 16th. In an entire round he managed two consecutive holes without dropping a gross shot. They were the 13th and the 14th.

The net effect was that the leader of the TEG 18 Trophy stayed precisely where he was, and then some. His advantage at the top grew from 11 points to 12.

## Europe's elite championship test hands out three personal bests and four scores of 43 points or more in a single afternoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

**Two course bests on the Stadium, built on the plainest golf imaginable**

The sixth visit to the Stadium Course at Camiral, on Monday 13 October, produced the lowest gross rounds either Gregg Williams or David Mullin has managed there. Williams went round in 85, seven strokes better than his previous visit and seven better than the 92 that had stood as his course best. Mullin's 84 was eight better than his last visit and seven inside his own previous mark of 91. In a field of five, the best return of the day in the 2025 TEG Trophy was 44 points. The worst was 28.

Neither course best came from anything spectacular. Williams's afternoon was set up by four consecutive pars from the 2nd to the 5th, three points apiece, twelve in total, not a gross shot dropped. Mullin did the same thing over a shorter span, opening with pars at the 1st, 2nd and 3rd for nine points. The first of those was on the 1st, one of the hardest holes on the course. Europe's elite championship test had prepared itself for heroics. It was undone by men declining to make mistakes.

The day reshaped two of the three races. Williams took over the lead in the 2025 Green Jacket, now eight clear. Jon Baker inherited the 2025 Wooden Spoon, 14 adrift of the next man.

## Jon Baker hands the TEG 18 Green Jacket lead to Gregg Williams at the 2nd and drops to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race on the same hole
<!-- storyline: race -->

**Two holes, two titles turned over**

Jon Baker arrived at PGA Catalunya's Stadium Course on Monday holding the TEG 18 Green Jacket lead by two. He lost it at the 2nd. A triple bogey 7 there returned no points at all. Gregg Williams made a routine par at that hole, and routine was sufficient. The gross lead changed hands outright. By the close of play Williams was eight clear.

That same hole cost Jon Baker at the other end of the table too. He passed David Mullin to take up last place in the TEG 18 Wooden Spoon race. He had been two points clear of it before the round. He ended the day holding it by 14.

It got worse before it got better. At the 4th he ran up a 9, a quintuple bogey, which requires a certain commitment. The finish offered no relief whatsoever. Four double bogeys from the 15th to the 18th. Eight shots dropped across the stretch and not a single net par among them. The last of the four came at the 18th, one of the hardest holes on the course, which at least allowed him to fail in appropriate surroundings.

The round totalled 28 points, down 11 from his 39 in round two, and the lowest score returned by any of the five players. His gross 100 was 11 strokes worse than his last visit here. Williams, meanwhile, went the other way. His 85 is a course best, beating his previous mark of 92.
