# Nine Ahead, Two Home: Mullin's Kent Double

## David Mullin wins the Trophy on 154 points by 2 — a lead built on the first two links and defended by fingernails at Prince's

**Mullin holds on for the Trophy**

David Mullin won the Trophy with 154 points, two clear of Alex Baker. It is the 16th-best total in TEG history and the third-best of his own career, and he earned every one of the points, including the ones he collected while apparently trying not to.

The foundation was laid on the first two links. At Royal Cinque Ports he went round in 87, seven strokes better than his previous visit, and the engine room was a four-hole run from the 9th: par, birdie at the 10th, then two more pars for 13 points. That sequence ended at the 13th with a double bogey, which is how these things generally end. At Littlestone he shot 85, again seven better than last time out, and another course best.

Prince's declined to co-operate. In Round 3 he stood on the par-five 8th and took nine. A quadruple bogey. Nothing on the card, no points, no explanation offered or required. He then took 13 points from the closing four holes, three pars and a birdie at the last, as though the 8th had happened to a different man in a different county. Round 4 produced a second quadruple bogey, an eight at the 10th. A man protecting a tournament lead does not usually budget for two of these. Mullin budgeted for two.

That opened the door for Baker, who had at one point trailed by nine. He answered with 10 points across the first three holes of the final round and 11 more from the 13th to the 15th, closing to within two. Then came the 16th, where he ran up a ten, and the door, having been held open with some courtesy, shut. His unravelling had in fact begun earlier, at Littlestone, where he dropped 12 shots across holes 2 to 5. He would have liked two of them back.

## Alex Baker and the 16th hole: two 10s in one TEG, and a Trophy left on the table

**Alex Baker and the 16th**

Two of Alex Baker's four rounds in Kent ended with a 10 written on his card, and on both occasions the damage was done at a hole numbered 16. They were different holes on different courses, which is the only mitigation available to him.

The first came in the opening round at Royal Cinque Ports: a quintuple bogey at the par-five 16th, stroke index 7, no Stableford points. The second, at Prince's in the final round, was worse in relative terms. A 10 at a par four. A sextuple bogey. On stroke index 17, the second-easiest hole on the card.

The Prince's ten hurt more because of the company it kept. Baker had played the 13th, 14th and 15th in par, par, par, three holes without dropping a gross shot, worth eleven Stableford points. The 16th erased the run at a stroke. He was not alone in mislaying himself there. Jon Baker arrived at the same hole having gone par-birdie-birdie-par-par from the 11th and made a double bogey for no points. The hole did not discriminate.

These were the headline numbers, not the complete set. There was also an 8 at the 11th at Royal Cinque Ports, a 9 at the 5th and an 8 at the 8th at Littlestone, and an 8 at the 10th at Prince's in Round 3: five further holes returning nothing whatsoever. For all that, his opening 98 at Royal Cinque Ports was four strokes better than his previous visit, which says a good deal about the previous visit.

## Gregg Williams plays the best gross golf of his TEG life and wins nothing

**Gregg Williams: the best gross golf of his TEG life, and nothing to show for it**

Williams finished TEG 14 with a Gross total of +76, a personal best. It was built on two course records of his own: 93 at Royal Cinque Ports, one better than his previous mark there of 94, and 86 at Prince's in Round 3, three strokes better than his last visit and inside his previous best of 89.

None of it was tidy. He opened at Deal with a 9 at the par-five 3rd, stroke index 4, a quadruple bogey worth nothing, the sort of start a lesser man would treat as information. Littlestone was worse in the wider sense: 97, eleven strokes adrift of the 86 he managed there before, and only 27 Stableford points.

Round three was the correction, and a proper one. He gained twelve points on the day, reaching 39, and the middle of it was genuinely good golf. Pars at the 9th, 10th and 11th for nine points, three holes without dropping a gross shot, then two more clean holes at the 15th and 16th. Between them sat an 8 at the par-five 12th, which ended the run.

The final round offered steadiness in patches: gross pars at the 5th and 6th, and again at the 14th and 15th. Having trailed the Green Jacket lead by 18 at one stage, he closed to within 8. Ten shots recovered. Eight shots short. He will be told this counts as progress.

## David Mullin's Green Jacket at +68, by 7 — his ninth

David Mullin took the Green Jacket at TEG 14 with a gross total of +68, seven clear of Jon Baker. His ninth. It is the sixth-best Green Jacket total on record and the fourth-best of his own career at the metric.

The number was built on consistency, a word that flatters several of the holes involved. Mullin set a personal best on every course he played: 87 at Royal Cinque Ports and 85 at Littlestone, each seven strokes better than his previous visit in TEG 13, and 91 at Prince's Shore/Dunes, one better than before. Not one of those is a score to hang above a fireplace. Together they were enough that nobody could reach him.

The chase behind him was real, and it was late. Jon Baker trailed by 15 at one point and finished seven back, the bulk of that work done in the final round at Prince's, where his 83 gross set a new course record. The previous mark of 85 had stood across five prior visits and belonged to Baker himself. Gregg Williams made up more ground still, 18 behind at his worst and eight behind at the finish, on a personal-best gross of +76, with his 86 at Prince's in Round 3 a course best of his own.

Neither got close enough. A margin of seven at the end understates a lead that was larger for most of the four days.

## Jon Baker, defending Trophy champion, collects the Wooden Spoon on 125 points — 12 adrift of the next worst

Jon Baker finished last in the Stableford count and took the Wooden Spoon with 125 points across the four rounds. The nearest man was 12 points away. That is not a scrap for last place. That is solitude.

The early links were unkind, though they had assistance. At Royal Cinque Ports his best gross was 92, seven strokes worse than the 85 he had managed on his previous visit. At Littlestone he posted 92 against a prior best of 79. Round 3 at Prince's brought 28 points, held down in part by a run of pars from the 14th that concluded with a triple bogey six at the par-three 17th.

Then, with the Spoon effectively engraved, he played the best golf of his tournament. He returned 39 points, an improvement of 11 on the previous day, and his 83 gross set a new course record for Prince's Shore/Dunes, beating the mark of 85 that had stood across five prior visits. It was also his personal best on the layout, two strokes better than his last time there. The scoring came in a burst: five holes without dropping a gross shot from the 11th to the 15th, back-to-back birdies at the 12th and 13th, a two-hole birdie streak equalling the all-time TEG record, and 14 points across holes 12 to 15. Somewhere around the 13th green he stopped being a man playing out a lost tournament and became a man with a record to protect.

The close undid some of it. A double bogey at the 16th ended the run, and he ran up an eight at the 18th. The record stood. So did the Spoon.
