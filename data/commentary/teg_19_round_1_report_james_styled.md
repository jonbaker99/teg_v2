## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in a field of five. It arrived in two halves that barely acknowledged one another. He had already held the 2026 TEG Trophy lead once, taking it outright with a par at the 4th, before Alex Baker reclaimed it a hole later. Then the tree-lined front nine closed in. Double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th, then a triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots gone in three holes, with no net par among them. For that spell he played like a man reversing a van into a garage he has never measured.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he steadied at once. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th, worth nine points, the 12th restoring his outright lead. He worked through them the way a good locksmith works: no fuss, each one shut. Alex Baker edged back in front at the 17th. Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good. He finished one clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams. A gross 90 gave him second behind Williams in the Green Jacket.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria swung violently in both directions. The first collapse ran three holes from the 5th. A triple bogey at the short hole, a double bogey at the 6th, then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone. The 9 was a quintuple bogey conducted with the orderliness of a man completing a form he got wrong at the top. Every stroke was duly entered in the correct box.

Then the opposite. From the 8th to the 11th he posted a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points, the bogey at the 10th earning three of them on the hardest hole on the course. Four holes of entirely sensible golf, played at a tempo suggesting nothing had happened.

The second collapse was worse. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake. A triple at the 15th. Another triple at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes, with one point salvaged. It was bad golf by any measure you care to apply. The 14th also took him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He finished with 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket, and 31 points, level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 462-yard par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th explains a great deal about the scorecards. It runs 462 yards, stroke index 1, with out of bounds down the whole of the left and a lake in front of the green from 110 yards out. A par there deserves applause. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine, five over, no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and it moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven, which only capped a wider slide, doubles at the 8th and the 9th either side of it and seven shots dropped across three holes.

Between them the trio gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. On a course that asks more of this field than most, none of it came as a shock.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

Quinta da Ria gives up strokes grudgingly. The Saturday round there left Alex Baker holding the 2026 TEG Trophy and then losing it inside three holes. He took the lead outright with a par at the 5th. He lost it over a wretched run on the back nine: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th, a triple at the 12th. Ten shots in three holes. The lead passed to Jon Baker with all the ceremony of a key left under a mat.

He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th. He went back ahead with a par at the 17th. He lost it for the last time at the 18th. Jon Baker closed on 39 points to Alex Baker's 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31. The two share the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
