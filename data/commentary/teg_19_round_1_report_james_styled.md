## Jon Baker's 39 points, the best round of the day, assembled out of a ruinous spell at the turn and three immaculate pars when the course opened out
<!-- storyline: round -->

Jon Baker's 39 points on the Saturday at Quinta da Ria was the best score in the field of five. It arrived in two halves that barely acknowledged one another. He had already led the 2026 TEG Trophy once, taking it outright with a par at the 4th. Alex Baker took it back a hole later.

Then the tree-lined front nine closed in. Double bogeys at the 8th and the 9th. A triple at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course. Seven shots gone in three holes, and not a net par among them. It was bad golf, conducted with the steady rhythm of a man losing his keys three times before breakfast.

From the 11th, where the course opens out towards the sea, he steadied at once. Pars at the 11th, the 12th and the 13th for nine points. The 12th restored his outright lead. He played those three holes like a man reconciling a bank statement that balanced first time.

Alex Baker edged back in front at the 17th. Jon Baker's par at the 18th, worth four points on one of the hardest holes here, took the lead back for good. He finishes one clear of Alex Baker and Gregg Williams. His gross 90 is second in the Green Jacket, behind Williams.

## John Patterson's round of violent extremes: nines at the 7th and the 14th, eleven shots gone in three holes, and in between a four-hole burst of twelve points
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

John Patterson's opening round at Quinta da Ria swung violently in both directions. The first damage came in a three-hole stretch from the 5th. A triple bogey at the short hole. A double bogey at the 6th. Then a 9 at the 7th, one of the hardest holes here. Ten shots gone, and no statement issued at the time.

Then the reverse, and it deserves saying plainly. From the 8th to the 11th he posted a par and three bogeys for twelve Stableford points. The bogey at the 10th earned three of them on the hardest hole on the course. Four holes of genuinely tidy work.

The second collapse was worse. A quintuple bogey 9 at the 14th, a par 4 doglegging around a lake. A triple at the 15th. Another at the 16th, the par 5 that threads between the two biggest lakes. Eleven shots in three holes. One point salvaged. A review has been ordered.

The 14th also took him past David Mullin to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon race. He finished with 105, 33 over, last in the 2026 TEG Green Jacket. His 31 points leave him level with Mullin at the foot of the Trophy.

## The 10th at Quinta da Ria, a 422-metre par 4 that took a nine, an eight and a seven from three of the five players in a single pass
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Quinta da Ria's 10th explains why the course bites. It runs 422 metres. Stroke index 1. Out of bounds the whole way down the left, and a lake sitting in front of the green from 100 metres out. A par there is worth celebrating. In a single pass, three of the five players failed to get within three shots of one.

Alex Baker took nine. Five over, no Stableford points. David Mullin took eight, also for nothing, and the damage moved him from second-worst to the bottom of the 2026 TEG Wooden Spoon standings. Jon Baker escaped with seven. Escape is a generous word: doubles at the 8th and 9th sat either side of it, seven shots dropped across three holes.

Between them the trio gave back twelve shots to par on one hole. It was not a surprise. This course asks more of this field than most.

## The three races as Quinta da Ria left them, told from the vantage of Alex Baker, who held the Trophy lead walking to the 18th tee and did not hold it walking off
<!-- storyline: race -->

The Saturday round at Quinta da Ria, a course that gives up strokes grudgingly, left Alex Baker holding the 2026 TEG Trophy and then not holding it. He took the lead outright with a par at the 5th. He lost it on the back nine with the calm of a department explaining a mislaid dataset: a 9 at the 10th, the hardest hole on the course, a double bogey at the 11th, a triple at the 12th. Ten shots gone in three holes. Lessons will be learned. The lead passed to Jon Baker.

He drew level again with a bogey at the 14th. He went back ahead with a par at the 17th. He lost it for the last time at the 18th. Jon Baker closed on 39 points, Alex Baker on 38, with Gregg Williams also on 38.

Williams had the TEG Green Jacket under control from the 3rd hole onwards and finished on 87. That is a lead taken early and then simply administered. At the bottom, David Mullin's bogey at the last dropped him level with John Patterson on 31. The pair share the TEG Wooden Spoon position overnight.
