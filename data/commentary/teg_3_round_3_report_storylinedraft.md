# Mullin Rewrites Boavista's Record Book

## David Mullin breaks the Boavista course record with a gross 85 while Jon Baker's -7 stands as the second-best net round in TEG history
<!-- storyline: round -->

David Mullin shot a gross 85 at Boavista Golf & Spa Resort, breaking the course record of 88 that had stood across 25 prior visits. His +14 gross is the best gross round recorded in TEG history to date, and his net -4 ranks as the third-best round in TEG history. Mullin was steady rather than spectacular in stretches, going through the 8th and 9th without dropping a shot.

Even that was overshadowed by Jon Baker, whose -7 stands as the second-best round in TEG history. Baker got there despite two significant blow-ups: a quintuple-bogey 9 at the 18th and a quadruple-bogey 8 at the 5th, both quickly absorbed into an otherwise brilliant card.

Neither man's position at the top changed. Baker's Trophy lead grew from 13 to 16, and Mullin's Green Jacket lead stretched from 5 to 9 — a round that hardened rather than reshaped the race.

## Gregg Williams's net -4 and Stuart Neumann's gross 18 rank third-best in TEG history to date, and both mark new personal bests at Boavista, yet neither performance troubles the top of either the Trophy or Green Jacket table
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

Gregg Williams and Stuart Neumann delivered the two outstanding rounds of the day at Boavista, and by TEG standards, of all time. Williams's net -4 stands as the third-best round recorded in TEG history, while Neumann's gross 18 points ranks third among all Gross rounds played. Both marked personal bests on the course: Williams's 97 beat his prior best of 102, and Neumann's 89 topped his previous mark of 92, continuing a course visited eight times by each.

Williams's card was not without blemish — a quadruple bogey 7 on the short par-3 2nd, one of Boavista's easier holes by stroke index, briefly threatened to derail things before he recovered.

Neither round troubled the leaders. Jon Baker's cushion atop the TEG 3 Trophy actually grew, from 13 to 16, and David Mullin's Green Jacket lead extended from 5 to 9. The one tangible benefit came in the Wooden Spoon race, where Neumann's score lifted him off the bottom, handing the unwanted spot to Henry Meller instead.

## Henry Meller's early collapse — a triple bogey at the 1st and a quintuple bogey at the 3rd — hands him outright last place in the TEG 3 Wooden Spoon race, with Stuart Neumann reprieved after occupying the spot himself
<!-- storyline: race -->

Henry Meller's round three at Boavista unravelled from the start. A triple bogey at the 1st, one of the easier holes on the course at stroke index 14, dropped him to third in the TEG 3 Wooden Spoon race. Two holes later he ran up a quintuple bogey 10 at the par-5 3rd, his worst hole against par of the tournament, and that was enough to send him to outright last place.

The damage kept coming before he could settle. A double bogey at the 5th and a quadruple bogey at the 6th extended the bleeding, and it wasn't until a birdie at the 11th that Meller finally arrested the slide.

The net effect was a reversal at the bottom of the Wooden Spoon standings. Stuart Neumann, who had held the last-place spot overnight, found himself reprieved as Meller took over the unwanted lead, and the gap back up to safety stretched from just one shot to five.
