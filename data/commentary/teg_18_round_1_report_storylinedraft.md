# Alex Baker's 46 Sets The Standard

## Alex Baker's opening 46 stableford points, the best round of the day and the third-best Trophy return of his TEG career
<!-- storyline: round -->

Alex Baker opened round one of TEG 16 with 46 stableford points, the best return of the day and the third-best Trophy round of his TEG career, despite two blow-up holes bookending an otherwise excellent card.

Baker took the outright lead in the Trophy twice early on, first through the 3rd and again through the 5th, before running into a quadruple-bogey 7 at the short 2nd. He recovered in style, reeling off 17 points across holes 8 to 12 and following it with 13 more from 14 to 16, including a birdie at the par-5 15th. That run ended when a double bogey at the 17th, one of the hardest holes on the course, broke his streak of pars and better. Baker then closed with a second quadruple bogey, a 9 at the par-5 18th.

Even with two blow-ups costing him eight shots against par, the strength of his stretches either side carried him to the best round of the day.

## David Mullin's sextuple bogey at the 15th, his worst score ever recorded on the hole, wrecked a card that had twice shared the Green Jacket lead
<!-- storyline: d0 -->

David Mullin's opening round at PGA Catalunya - Tour unravelled at the 15th, where a sextuple bogey 11 on the par-5 — his worst score ever recorded on that hole — undid a card that had twice been good enough to share the lead in the TEG 16 Green Jacket race. The damage was compounded by a quadruple bogey 8 at the 6th earlier in the round, leaving little room to recover.

The 15th proved decisive beyond the Jacket picture too: the blow-up dropped Mullin from second to fifth in the TEG 16 Wooden Spoon race, sending him to the bottom of that table. It capped a difficult return to a course he had visited four times before — his round here was 16 strokes worse than his last visit, and well short of his prior best gross of 84 at the venue. A round that had shown genuine promise finished instead as a lesson in how quickly a good card can disappear.

## Gregg Williams survived two quadruple bogeys and briefly shared the Green Jacket lead before a third disaster hole dropped him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon
<!-- storyline: d1 -->

Gregg Williams' opening round at PGA Catalunya was a story of two rounds in one. He began wildly, bleeding eight shots over three holes from the 3rd, running up back-to-back triple bogeys before a double bogey at the 5th left him without a net par for that entire stretch. Worse followed at the 7th, where a quadruple bogey 9 on the par-5 wiped out any momentum. Yet Williams steadied himself, closing the bleeding with a birdie at the 15th, and by the 16th he had clawed his way level for the outright lead in the TEG Green Jacket (Gross).

The recovery didn't last. A bogey at the 14th had already dropped him to the bottom of the TEG Wooden Spoon standings, and a second quadruple bogey, this time at the 17th, confirmed the damage. Two blow-up holes, two brief flashes of contention, and a finish at the wrong end of the field summed up a round that promised far more than it delivered.

## John Patterson's round swung from a 22-shot front-nine collapse to one of the field's hottest stretches coming home
<!-- storyline: d2 -->

John Patterson's opening round at PGA Catalunya - Tour swung wildly from disaster to brilliance. He ran up a quadruple bogey 8 at the 3rd, then kept bleeding shots through triples at the 4th, 7th and 9th and doubles at the 5th, 6th and 10th, dropping 22 shots across holes 3 to 10 and going seven holes without a single net par. The damage cost him at the top of the leaderboard too — a triple bogey at the 4th sent him to the bottom of the 2025 TEG Wooden Spoon race.

But the back nine told a different story. From the 13th to the 16th he piled up 13 stableford points, playing the par-4 13th — the hardest hole on the course — and the par-3 14th without dropping a gross shot at all. The turnaround produced a 14-point split between the nines, and a final round total of 34 stableford points that masked just how bad the start had been.

## Jon Baker's round split the TEG 18 competitions - best gross of the day put him top of the Green Jacket, while his stableford total left him bottom of the Trophy and Wooden Spoon standings
<!-- storyline: race -->

Jon Baker's opening round set the tone for a peculiar TEG 18, splitting his fortunes clean in two. On the par-5 5th, a bogey was still enough to move him into outright second place in the Green Jacket (Gross) race, and by the close of the day his card carried the best gross score of the field, enough to send him to the very top of the Jacket standings after Round 1.

But the same round did the opposite for his Stableford points. Holes 7 to 9 undid him twice over: three holes without a net par, and seven gross shots bled across a double bogey on the par-5 7th, a double on the par-3 8th, and a triple on the par-4 9th. A closing double bogey on the 18th then dropped him to the bottom of the Wooden Spoon race. By the end of the day Alex Baker led the Trophy, level on points at the top, while Jon Baker sat bottom of both the Trophy and the Spoon — and top of the Jacket.
